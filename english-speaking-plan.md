# English Speaking Plan

**Goal:** talk without thinking. Fast, relaxed casual conversation in front of people, with an American-style accent.
**Level:** B2 (CEFR), around 6 IELTS
**Time:** 2 hours every night
**Length:** 6 months (24 weeks), then a new plan

## Why this plan works

At home you use **passive** vocabulary: words you recognise and can reach slowly. Under pressure, your brain only has time for **active** vocabulary: phrases you've said out loud so many times they come out automatically. So the main job is not learning lots of new words. It's **saying the words you already know, out loud, under time pressure, again and again.**

"Talking without thinking" comes from three things, and every month trains all three:

1. **Chunks, not single words.** Native speakers don't build sentences word by word. They pull out ready-made pieces like "the thing is…" or "I was gonna say…". The more chunks you own, the less you have to build.
2. **Speed and pressure.** Every month gets a bit faster, less prepared and more public, so your brain stops translating and starts grabbing.
3. **Never stopping.** When you don't know a word, you go *around* it ("it's like a…, the thing you use to…") instead of freezing. That habit matters more than knowing every word.

## The 6 months at a glance

| Month | Focus | By the end of the month you can… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foundations**: stories, questions, small talk, vocabulary | tell a practised story using real connectors, with fewer pauses |
| 2 | **Speed**: same skills, faster, plus opinions and explaining | tell stories and give opinions at close to normal speed |
| 3 | **Spontaneity**: no preparation | talk for 2 minutes on a random topic with zero prep, and keep going when you're missing a word |
| 4 | **Real-life pressure**: groups, humour, calls, audiences | hold your own in a group chat and on the phone |
| 5 | **Work and complex topics** | run your part of a meeting and handle questions you didn't expect |
| 6 | **Consolidation**: no new material, maximum speaking | speak without translating in your head. Compare with your Day 1 recording |

## Daily routine

Every night has the same four blocks. As the months go on, **less studying and more speaking**:

| Months | Shadowing | Main skill | Speaking drill | Review | Live conversations per week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 20 min | 40 min | 45 min | 15 min | 1 |
| 3–4 | 15 min | 30 min | 60 min | 15 min | 2 |
| 5–6 | 15 min | 20 min | 70 min | 15 min | 3 |

**Every week, the same rhythm:**
- **Days 1–5:** one main-skill item per day (listed in each week's table). Use it straight away in that day's speaking drill.
- **Day 4:** the speaking drill is **voice mode with Claude**.
- **Day 6:** re-record an unprepared 2-minute talk, count your 3 numbers, and have a **live conversation** with a real person (meetup, language exchange app, colleague).
- **Day 7:** light day, 30 minutes only. Listen to a casual podcast while walking. Rest so your brain can lock in what you practised.

Live conversations can be with a colleague, at a meetup, on a language exchange app, or with an online tutor. In months 3–6, add the extra ones on any day you like.

## All-day habits (no extra time)

- **Think in English.** In your head, describe what you're doing: "OK, I'm gonna grab a coffee, then I need to reply to that email."
- **Talk to yourself** in the shower, while cooking, on the way to work. Out loud if you can.
- **Phone and apps in English.**
- When a phrase comes to mind in your own language, ask yourself, *"how would an American say that?"*, and write it down for tonight.

## Month 1: Foundations

**Speaking drill:** 4/3/2 with a new story each day.
**Shadowing:** one sound per day (flap T, reductions, linking, rhythm). In weeks 2–4, practise the same sounds with new clips.

### Week 1: Storytelling

| Day | Shadowing (20 min) | Main skill (40 min) | Speaking drill (45 min) | Review (15 min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | Flap T: "water" → "wadder", "get it" → "ged it" | Learn the 12 connectors, say each one 5× in a sentence | **Baseline recording** (2 min, unprepared), then 4/3/2 with a work story | Write down your 3 numbers + 5 phrases |
| **Day 2** | Reductions: gonna, wanna, kinda, "whaddya" | Reaction phrases (see Toolkit) | 4/3/2 with a trip or weekend story | Log 5 phrases, review Day 1's |
| **Day 3** | Linking: "pick it up" → "pi-ki-dup" | Questions: 50 direct questions out loud | 4/3/2 with a funny or embarrassing story, then 3 questions you'd ask the listener | Log 5 phrases |
| **Day 4** | Sentence rhythm: stressed words long, the rest squashed | Questions: follow-ups + indirect questions | **Voice mode with Claude**: 20 min casual chat. Tell a story, answer questions, ask questions back | Write down the mistakes that were corrected |
| **Day 5** | One full 2-minute clip, copy everything | 10 everyday idioms | 4/3/2 with an old story, using 3 of the idioms | Log 5 phrases |
| **Day 6** | Free choice | Re-record a 2-min unprepared story, count your 3 numbers | **One live conversation outside**: a meetup, language exchange, or a colleague you don't usually chat with | Send your numbers to Claude |
| **Day 7** | — | **Light day (30 min only)**: listen to a casual podcast while walking | — | Rest. Your brain consolidates what you practised. |

Week 1 gives you a first taste of questions and idioms. Weeks 2 and 4 go deeper into them.

### Weeks 2–4

Same table and same daily structure as week 1. **Only the main skill column changes**, as below. Whatever you learn in the main skill, use it in that day's speaking drill. For example, in week 3 every 4/3/2 story ends with you asking the listener a question.

#### Week 2: Questions
| Day | Main skill (40 min) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Direct questions: 50 out loud, using what/where/when/who/why/how, in past and present |
| 2 | Follow-ups: play a podcast, pause after each thing the speaker says, and ask 2 follow-up questions out loud |
| 3 | Indirect questions: turn 30 direct questions into "Do you know…" or "Can you tell me…" with the right word order |
| 4 | Question tags and short answers: "You work here, right?", "It's late, isn't it?", "Yeah, I did." |
| 5 | Interview yourself for 10 minutes, asking and answering out loud |
| 6 | Re-record and count your 3 numbers |

#### Week 3: Small talk and keeping a conversation going
| Day | Main skill (40 min) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Openers: "How's it going?", "What've you been up to?", and answers that go beyond "fine" |
| 2 | Reactions plus echo questions: "You went to Japan? No way!" |
| 3 | **Answer, add, ask back** (see Toolkit) |
| 4 | Natural time-buyers to replace "umm": "Let me think…", "I mean…", "Good question…" |
| 5 | Changing the topic and ending a chat: "Speaking of…", "That reminds me…", "Anyway, I'll let you go." |
| 6 | Re-record and count your 3 numbers |

#### Week 4: Vocabulary and idioms
| Day | Main skill (40 min) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 10 everyday phrasal verbs: end up, figure out, run into, catch up, come up… |
| 2 | Collocations: make a decision, take a break, have a chat, do a favour |
| 3 | Opinion softeners: "pretty good", "kind of", "honestly", "not a big fan of…" |
| 4 | 10 new idioms, then use them in your voice-mode chat with Claude |
| 5 | Talking about your job: what you do and a recent problem you solved |
| 6 | Re-record and count your 3 numbers, then compare with your Day 1 baseline |

## Month 2: Speed

**What changes:**
- **Speaking drill:** 4/3/2 becomes **3/2/1**.
- **Shadowing:** faster speakers. Try **speak-along** (see Toolkit) instead of pause-and-repeat.
- **Review:** say your phrases out loud from memory instead of reading them.

| Week | Main skill | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 (voice mode) | Day 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Better stories** | Setting the scene: "So this was last summer…" | Building suspense: "And then…", "Guess what?" | Casual reported speech: "I was like…", "she goes…" | Tell Claude 3 stories, get corrections | Retell a movie or series episode |
| 6 | **Opinions** | Giving opinions: "I feel like…", "Honestly, I think…", "If you ask me…" | Agreeing: "Totally", "Same here", "I know, right?" | Disagreeing politely: "I see what you mean, but…" | Debate 3 topics with Claude | Hedging: "I guess it depends", "Kind of, yeah" |
| 7 | **Explaining** | How something works: "First you…, then…" | Explaining a decision: "We went with X because…" | Comparing: "X is way better for…, but Y…" | Explain 3 things from your life or work to Claude | Checking understanding: "Does that make sense?", "You know what I mean?" |
| 8 | **Speed review** | Your 60-second answers: "Tell me about yourself", "What do you do?", "What do you do for fun?" | Rapid-fire: 30 questions, answer each in under 10 seconds | Mix month 1 + 2 phrases in one story | 30 min fast Q&A with Claude | Re-record your hardest story from month 1 |

## Month 3: Spontaneity

From here on, you **stop preparing**. This is the month where "talking without thinking" really starts.

**What changes:**
- **Speaking drill (60 min):** the **random-topic drill** (see Toolkit) on Days 1, 3 and 5. **Voice mode with Claude** on Days 2 and 4.
- **Live conversations:** 2 per week.
- **Shadowing (15 min):** speak along with whole clips, no pausing.

| Week | Main skill | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Day 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **Talking on the spot** | 1-min talks about everyday objects | …about places | …about people you know | Claude picks 10 random topics, you answer each one straight away | "Would you rather…?" questions |
| 10 | **Hypotheticals** | "If I could…, I'd…" | "If I hadn't…, I woulda…" | "I wish…" | 10 crazy "what if" questions with Claude | "Imagine if…" stories |
| 11 | **Never stopping** | Paraphrasing: describe 20 words without saying them | Smooth self-correction: "I mean…", "or rather…", "what I meant was…" | Talking about things you don't really know: "I'm not sure, but I think…" | Claude asks you hard questions, you never stay silent for more than 2 seconds | Summarising: "So basically what I'm saying is…" |
| 12 | **Role-plays** | Ordering food and complaining | Making plans with a friend | Meeting someone new at a party | Claude plays 3 characters, you improvise | Arguing a side you don't agree with |

## Month 4: Real-life pressure

Real conversations are messier than practice: several people, jokes, bad phone lines, people watching. This month trains for that.

**What changes:**
- **Live conversations:** still 2 per week, but try to make one of them a **group** (3+ people).
- **Shadowing:** podcasts with 2–3 hosts chatting and interrupting each other.

| Week | Main skill | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Day 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Group conversations** | Jumping in: "Oh, that reminds me…", "Can I just add…" | Handing over: "What about you?", "What do you guys think?" | Keeping your turn: "Hang on, let me finish" | Group role-play with Claude playing 2 people | Asking someone to repeat, naturally: "Sorry, say that again?", "Wait, you lost me" |
| 14 | **Humour and banter** | Sarcasm and tone: "Oh great, just what I needed" | Exaggeration: "It took like a million years" | Telling a funny story with timing: pause before the punchline | Trade funny stories with Claude | Reacting to jokes: "Haha, stop", "You're killing me" |
| 15 | **Calls (no body language to help)** | Making plans on the phone | Leaving a voice message | Bad connection: "You're breaking up", "Can you hear me now?" | A full phone-style call with Claude, eyes closed | Send a friend 3 English voice notes |
| 16 | **Speaking in front of people** | Introducing yourself to a group | Telling a story to 3+ people | A 2-minute toast or update | Claude asks you questions "in front of an audience" | Answering a question while others listen |

## Month 5: Work and complex topics

**What changes:**
- **Speaking drill (70 min):** mostly voice mode and live conversations.
- **Live conversations:** 3 per week. Use real work moments when you can, like meetings or coffee chats.
- **Shadowing:** work talk, interviews and tech podcasts.

| Week | Main skill | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 | Day 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | **Meetings** | Giving updates: "So, quick update…" | Asking for clarification: "Just to make sure I get it…" | Pushing back politely: "I hear you, but…" | Mock meeting with Claude | Summing up action items: "So, next steps…" |
| 18 | **Explaining your work** | What you do, to someone outside your field | Walking through a project from start to finish | Explaining trade-offs: "The downside is…, but…" | Claude asks "why?" after every answer | A work success or failure story |
| 19 | **Complex topics** | News and current events | Tech and AI | Culture: your country vs the US | Discuss with Claude, giving both sides | Balanced views: "On one hand…, on the other…" |
| 20 | **Thinking on your feet** | Questions after a talk | Questions you didn't expect | Defending an idea | Claude plays a tough boss or client | Giving and receiving feedback |

## Month 6: Consolidation

**No new material.** The goal is to make everything from months 1–5 automatic.

**Main skill (20 min):** open your phrase log and pick the **10 phrases you still don't use naturally**. Use each one at least twice in that day's speaking.
**Speaking drill (70 min):** mix everything: random topics, 3/2/1, role-plays, voice mode. Do the hardest situation for you first.
**Live conversations:** 3 per week, or more if you can.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 21 | Recycle month 1 + 4: stories, small talk, groups, humour |
| 22 | Recycle month 2 + 3: opinions, explaining, hypotheticals, talking on the spot |
| 23 | Recycle month 5: work, complex topics, tough questions |
| 24 | **Final test** (see below) |

### Final test (week 24)

1. Redo the **Day 1 baseline**: 2 minutes, unprepared, same kind of story. Count your 3 numbers.
2. Record a **30-minute** unscripted conversation, live or in voice mode.
3. Compare both with your Day 1 recording.
4. Write down the situations where you still had to think, and send it all to Claude to build the next 6-month plan.

**Target:** "umm"s and long pauses about **half** of your Day 1 numbers, and at least **8 different connectors** in 2 minutes.

## Monthly check

At the end of every month, re-record a 2-minute unprepared talk and compare it with the Day 1 baseline and last month's recording. If your pauses and fillers aren't going down, tell Claude and adjust the plan. Don't just push on.

## Toolkit

### The 4/3/2 drill (month 1)
Tell the same story in **4 minutes**, then **3 minutes**, then **2 minutes**, recording each one. Same content, less time, so your brain is forced to find words faster.

### The 3/2/1 drill (month 2 onwards)
Same idea, faster: 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute.

### The random-topic drill (month 3 onwards)
Write 50 random topics on cards or in a notes app (your first car, coffee, a bad boss, the ocean, a song you hate…). Pick one without looking and start talking **immediately**, for 1–2 minutes. No preparation, no stopping. Record it.

### Answer, add, ask back
Never give a one-word answer. **Answer** the question, **add** one detail, then **ask** something back.
- "Did you have a good weekend?" → "Yeah, it was nice! I finally tried that new ramen place. Have you been?"

### Paraphrasing (the "never stop" rule)
Missing a word? Don't stop. Go around it:
- "It's like a… kind of a…"
- "The thing you use to…"
- "You know, the place where…"
- "What's the word… anyway, it's basically…"

### The 3 numbers
1. How many "umm"s and "uhh"s
2. How many pauses longer than 2 seconds
3. How many different connectors you used (not just "and" and "so")

### Shadowing
Pick a casual American speaker *chatting* (not presenting) on YouTube or a podcast. Play 1–2 sentences, pause, and repeat them, copying the rhythm, speed and melody exactly, like an actor.

**Speak-along (month 2 onwards):** play the clip and talk *at the same time* as the speaker, without pausing. Hard at first. That's the point.

### 12 storytelling connectors
- So basically…
- The thing is…
- Anyway…
- And then out of nowhere…
- Turns out…
- Which was weird, because…
- I was like…
- And he goes… / And she goes…
- Long story short…
- The funny part is…
- At that point…
- In the end…

### Reaction phrases
- No way!
- Wait, really?
- That's rough.
- Fair enough.
- That makes sense.
- Oh, nice!
- I bet.

### Question types
- **Direct:** What did you do last weekend? / Where have you been?
- **Follow-ups:** How come? / How'd that go? / Wait, what happened? / What was that like?
- **Indirect:** Do you know what time it starts? / Can you tell me where it is?

**Watch out:** inside a sentence, use normal word order, not question order.
- ❌ What do I need is more practice.
- ✅ What I need is more practice.
- ❌ I don't know where is the office.
- ✅ I don't know where the office is.
