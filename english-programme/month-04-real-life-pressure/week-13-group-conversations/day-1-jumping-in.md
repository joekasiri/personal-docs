## Learning objectives

- Hear the "landing points" where a group conversation lets a new speaker in.
- Use 12 short entry chunks to take a turn without waiting for silence.
- Connect your point to what was just said, so your jump-in feels natural, not rude.
- Make back-channel sounds (mm-hm, right, yeah yeah) at natural moments, so people feel you are part of the group before you even speak.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: back-channels.** In a group, listeners are never silent. They make tiny sounds that mean "I'm with you, keep going": *mm-hm*, *uh-huh*, *right*, *yeah yeah*, *oh wow*, *totally*. They come in the small gaps, usually at the end of a phrase, and they are quick and soft. Making them is also how you "warm up" your voice before you jump in. People hear you, so your first real sentence is not a surprise.

How they sound:

| Sound | Respelling | Melody | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| mm-hm | "mm-HMM" | up on the second part | yes, I follow |
| uh-huh | "uh-HUH" | up on the second part | yes, keep going |
| right | "RIGHT" (short) | falling | I understand |
| yeah yeah | "yeah-YEAH" (fast, together) | falling | I know, I agree (eager) |
| oh wow | "oh WOW" | up then down | that's surprising |
| totally | "TOH-dly" (flap T) | falling | strong agreement |

Practice sentences. Say them at full speed, copying the melody. The back-channel is in brackets, as if a second person said it.

1. "So we get to the restaurant, (mm-HMM) and the line is out the door, (oh WOW) like, around the block."
2. "I was gonna take the train, (uh-HUH) but then I was like, nah, I'll just drive."
3. "And the whole TEAM was there, (RIGHT) even the people who never show up."
4. "It's kinda like what Sam was saying, (yeah-YEAH) you just gotta book early."
5. "So she texts me at, like, midnight, (NO way) asking if I'm still UP."
6. "We ended up at this tiny little bar, (mm-HMM) super cozy, (NICE) and the music was great."
7. "Honestly, the BEST part was the dessert. (TOH-dly)"
8. "Oh, that reminds me — didja guys ever try that taco place on Fifth?"
9. "Wait, can I just add something? 'Cause the same thing happened to ME."
10. "Oh, speaking of trips — I'm going to Denver next MONTH."

**Clip task:** play your multi-host podcast clip and **speak along** with one host for 3–4 minutes. Then play it again and this time *only* make the back-channel sounds in the gaps, as if you were a fourth host. Then speak along once more. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md) for the speak-along method.

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Jumping in

### Explanation

In a group, nobody says "Your turn now." If you wait for complete silence, you will wait forever, because a good group conversation never goes silent. Native speakers take turns in the tiny gaps between sentences, often less than half a second. That feels rude to many learners. It is not. In casual American conversation, **a quick, friendly jump-in is normal and welcome**, as long as you connect it to what was just said.

There are three steps:

1. **Hear the landing point.** A speaker is about to finish when: their voice falls at the end of a sentence; they laugh; they say a "closing" phrase ("…so yeah", "…which was crazy", "…anyway"); or someone else reacts ("No way!"). That is your door.
2. **Start with an entry chunk.** Do not start with your main idea. Start with 2–4 automatic words ("Oh, that reminds me…", "Wait, can I just add…"). The chunk does two jobs: it tells the group you are taking a turn, and it gives your brain one second to find the next words.
3. **Bridge, then say your point.** Connect to what they said ("…same thing happened to me", "…speaking of Denver…"), then say your idea in one or two sentences. Keep the first turn short. Once you are in, you can get a longer turn later.

Body and voice help too: lean in a little, raise your hand slightly or open your mouth with a small "Oh—", and say the entry chunk **a bit louder** than normal. In groups, the first words decide whether you get the turn.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Oh, that reminds me… | what they said makes you think of something connected | "Oh, that reminds me, my brother just got a puppy too." |
| Can I just add something? | you want to add a point to the topic | "Can I just add something? The parking there is terrible." |
| Sorry, just to jump in… | someone is still talking a bit and your point can't wait | "Sorry, just to jump in — is that the place near the station?" |
| Oh, speaking of [X]… | you take one word from their sentence and use it | "Oh, speaking of coffee, did you guys see the new café?" |
| Same thing happened to me! | you have a similar story | "Same thing happened to me! My flight got cancelled too." |
| Wait, I gotta say… | you have a strong reaction or opinion | "Wait, I gotta say, that movie was way too long." |
| That's so funny, because… | there is a fun coincidence | "That's so funny, because I was there last weekend." |
| Oh, on that note… | you add something on the same theme | "Oh, on that note, has anyone tried the new climbing gym?" |
| I was just gonna say… | you were about to say the same thing | "I was just gonna say, that sounds exactly like my old boss." |
| Quick thing — | you have a short, practical point | "Quick thing — are we splitting the bill or what?" |
| Oh, totally, and… | you agree and take the turn in one move | "Oh, totally, and the worst part is the noise." |
| Not to change the subject, but… | you want to move to a new topic politely | "Not to change the subject, but who's coming on Saturday?" |

### Examples

**Dinner with friends (four speakers).** Maya is telling a story. You find two landing points.

> **Maya:** …so we finally get to the cabin, and there's no Wi-Fi, no signal, nothing. So yeah.
> **Jordan:** Oh man.
> **You:** Oh, that reminds me — when we went camping last summer, my phone died on the first day.
> **Sam:** Wait, for the whole trip?
> **You:** The *whole* trip. Best weekend ever, honestly.
> **Maya:** Right? It's like a detox.
> **Jordan:** I could never. I'd lose my mind.
> **You:** Oh, totally, and the first night was hard. But by Sunday I didn't even miss it.

**Work lunch (three coworkers and you).** The conversation is fast. You use a "speaking of" bridge.

> **Priya:** The new office is nice, but the coffee machine is a disaster.
> **Leo:** It took me ten minutes to get a latte this morning.
> **You:** Oh, speaking of coffee — has anyone tried the place downstairs? It opened Monday.
> **Priya:** No! Is it good?
> **You:** It's pretty good. A little pricey, but way faster than the machine.
> **Leo:** Okay, I'm going after lunch.

**After a team call (you jump in while someone is still finishing).**

> **Dana:** …and the client wants the new screens by Friday, which, I mean, it's possible, but —
> **You:** Sorry, just to jump in — is that the onboarding screens or the settings ones?
> **Dana:** Onboarding.
> **You:** Okay, cool, then we're fine. Most of it's already done.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Excuse me, can I say something?"
✅ "Oh, can I just add something?"
*Reason: the first one sounds formal and stops the whole conversation. The second slides in.*

❌ Waiting for a long silence before speaking.
✅ Jumping in at a landing point (a falling voice, a laugh, "so yeah").
*Reason: good group conversations never go silent, so the silence never comes.*

❌ "That remembers me of my trip."
✅ "That reminds me of my trip."
*Reason: in English, something "reminds" you. "Remember" is what you do.*

❌ "Speaking about coffee…"
✅ "Speaking of coffee…"
*Reason: the fixed chunk is "speaking of".*

❌ "I want to add an information."
✅ "Can I just add something?" / "Can I add one thing?"
*Reason: "information" has no plural or "an" in English, and "I want to" sounds demanding.*

❌ Jumping in with a totally new topic and no bridge: "I bought new shoes."
✅ "Not to change the subject, but I finally bought those shoes."
*Reason: a jump-in without a bridge sounds like you were not listening.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill in "jump-in mode"** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Jump-in mode (40 min).** Each card below gives a topic and a line a friend "just said". Read the friend's line out loud in a different voice. Then jump in with an entry chunk from tonight and talk for **90 seconds** without stopping. Record every one. Do 10 cards (about 4 minutes each including setting up and a quick listen-back to the first 15 seconds).

| # | Topic | Your friend just said… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cooking | "I tried to make pasta from scratch last night and it was a total mess." |
| 2 | Moving apartments | "Moving is the worst. I'm never doing it again." |
| 3 | Phones | "I dropped my phone in the sink this morning." |
| 4 | Weekend plans | "We're thinking of going hiking on Saturday." |
| 5 | Bad weather | "It rained so hard yesterday, my shoes are still wet." |
| 6 | Work tools | "Our team just switched to a new design tool and nobody knows how it works." |
| 7 | Travel | "I've always wanted to go to Japan." |
| 8 | Gyms | "I signed up for a gym in January and I've been, like, twice." |
| 9 | Neighbours | "My neighbour plays the drums every night at ten." |
| 10 | Food delivery | "I ordered sushi and they brought me a pizza." |

Rules: (1) your first words must be an entry chunk, never "Umm"; (2) your second sentence must connect to the friend's line; (3) after that, keep going for the full 90 seconds using any Month 1–3 tools (connectors, paraphrasing, self-correction).

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Tell the story of the best group dinner, party or trip you've ever been part of. Who was there, what happened, and what made it fun?"** Tell it in 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each. In each version, include at least one moment where you say what someone else said and how you jumped in ("And then Jake goes… and I was like, oh, that reminds me…"). This recycles [casual reported speech](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-3-casual-reported-speech.md).
