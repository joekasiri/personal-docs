## Learning objectives

- Finish your turn clearly so the group knows you are done.
- Pass the turn to the whole group or to one person by name.
- Bring a quiet person into the conversation without putting them on the spot.
- Use a personal hook ("You lived in Lisbon, right?") so the next person has something easy to say.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: hand-over intonation and names.** When Americans pass the turn, the *name* and the *question* carry the melody. Open questions ("What about you?") usually **fall** at the end. Yes/no hand-overs ("You've been there, right?") **rise** on "right". Names at the start get a little stress and a tiny pause: "MAYA — what do you think?"

Practice sentences (speak at full speed, copy the melody marked with ↘ fall / ↗ rise):

1. "What about YOU? ↘"
2. "What do you guys THINK? ↘" ("whaddya guys think")
3. "JORDAN — you've been there, RIGHT? ↗"
4. "How 'bout you, SAM? ↘"
5. "Have you guys ever done that? ↗" ("have-ya guys")
6. "Am I the only one who HATES that? ↗"
7. "Anyway, enough about ME — what's new with YOU? ↘"
8. "I wanna hear what PRIYA thinks. ↘"
9. "Is it just me, or is it super cold in here? ↗"
10. "Sorry, LEO, you were saying? ↗"
11. "Does anyone else do that, or is it just me? ↗ … ↘"

**Clip task:** find a multi-host podcast where the host asks the guests questions (search "podcast hosts ask each other questions", "friends podcast what about you"). Speak along for 4 minutes, paying attention to how they say each other's names. Then pause on three hand-overs and say them yourself, copying exactly. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Handing over

### Explanation

Yesterday you learnt to take a turn. Tonight you learn to **give it away**. This matters for two reasons. First, if you never hand over, you either talk too long or you finish with an awkward silence where nobody knows if you are done. Second, handing over makes you look relaxed and in control, the person who keeps the group together. It also takes pressure off you: while someone else answers, you get a break.

A good hand-over has two parts:

1. **A clear finish.** End your turn with a closing signal: a summary word ("So yeah."), a laugh, or "Anyway…". Your voice falls.
2. **A pass.** Either to the **whole group** ("What do you guys think?") or to **one person** ("Sam, what about you?"). Passing to one person works better when the group is big or quiet, because a question to "everyone" often gets no answer.

The best passes have a **hook**: something you know about that person that makes answering easy. "Priya, you lived in Lisbon, right? Is it as nice as people say?" is much easier to answer than "Priya, what do you think about travel?"

**Quiet people.** In most groups, one person talks less. Bring them in gently: use their name, ask about something they know, and keep it light. Do not say "You're very quiet!" That puts them on the spot.

**"You guys"** is the normal American way to say "all of you", for any mix of people.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What about you? | you finish and pass to the person you were talking to | "I'm more of a beach person. What about you?" |
| What do you guys think? | you pass to the whole group | "I think we should just go Friday. What do you guys think?" |
| How about you, [name]? | you pass to one person | "I hated it. How about you, Leo?" |
| [Name], you've been there, right? | you know something about them (a hook) | "Maya, you've been to Mexico City, right?" |
| Have you guys ever…? | you invite everyone's stories | "Have you guys ever lost your passport?" |
| Am I the only one who…? | you want people to agree or disagree with a confession | "Am I the only one who reads the last page first?" |
| Is it just me, or…? | you share a small opinion and invite reactions | "Is it just me, or is this music really loud?" |
| Anyway, enough about me — | you have talked a lot and want to give space | "Anyway, enough about me — how was your trip, Sam?" |
| I wanna hear what [name] thinks. | you bring a quiet person in | "I wanna hear what Priya thinks. She's the expert." |
| Sorry, [name], you were saying? | you notice you or someone else cut them off | "Sorry, Jordan, you were saying?" |
| Does anyone else…? | you check if others share your habit | "Does anyone else set five alarms?" |
| You should tell them about… | you give a friend the stage for their story | "Sam, you should tell them about the bear." |

### Examples

**Group at a barbecue (four speakers, one quiet).** You notice Priya has said nothing.

> **Jordan:** …so we ended up driving six hours for one concert. Worth it, though.
> **Leo:** Dude, that's commitment.
> **You:** That's amazing. I've never done a road trip for a show. Priya, you go to a lot of concerts, right?
> **Priya:** Kind of, yeah. I saw three last month.
> **You:** Three? Okay, what was the best one?
> **Priya:** Honestly, this tiny jazz thing in a basement. It was so good.
> **Leo:** Wait, where was that?

**Coffee with two coworkers.** You finish and pass the turn clearly.

> **You:** So my weekend was basically just painting the living room. Very exciting. So yeah. How about you, Dana? Anything fun?
> **Dana:** Not really, I just slept.
> **You:** Honestly, that sounds better. Marcus, didn't you have a wedding?
> **Marcus:** Oh yeah, my cousin's. It was a whole thing.
> **You:** Okay, we need details.

**Saving a story that got cut off.**

> **Sam:** So the hotel calls me and —
> **Leo:** Oh, hotels are the worst. Last year I —
> **Leo:** (two minutes later) …anyway.
> **You:** Sorry, Sam, you were saying? The hotel called you?
> **Sam:** Yes! Thank you. So they call me and say my room doesn't exist.

### Common mistakes

❌ "What is your opinion?"
✅ "What do you think?" / "What do you guys think?"
*Reason: "What is your opinion?" sounds like a survey or a job interview.*

❌ "You have been to Lisbon, no?"
✅ "You've been to Lisbon, right?"
*Reason: in American English the tag is "right?", and the verb is contracted.*

❌ Asking the whole group "What do you think?" and waiting in silence.
✅ "Leo, what do you think?"
*Reason: a question to "everyone" can belong to no one. A name makes it easy.*

❌ "You are very quiet tonight!"
✅ "Priya, you went to that festival, right? How was it?"
*Reason: pointing out silence makes people feel watched. A hook makes them feel invited.*

❌ Handing over and then answering yourself: "What about you? I think it's…"
✅ "What about you?" (then stop and wait two seconds)
*Reason: you gave the turn away, so let them take it.*

❌ "Tell us about you."
✅ "Tell us about yours." / "What about you?"
*Reason: after your story, you hand over *their* version: their weekend, their trip, "yours".*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **voice mode with Claude playing two friends** ([guide](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)). You are the "host" of the conversation. Your job is to keep it going and make sure both friends get turns.

**Step 1 (2 min).** Open voice mode and paste or read this prompt:

```
Let's do a group conversation practice. You play TWO friends at the same time:
- DANI: chatty, tells long stories, sometimes talks over others.
- MARCUS: quiet and friendly, only speaks a lot when someone asks him directly.
Say each name before their line, like "Dani: ..." and "Marcus: ...".
We're at a café on a Saturday afternoon catching up. Talk naturally, casual American English, short turns.
My job is to keep the conversation going and HAND OVER the turn. Every few minutes,
let Dani dominate or let the conversation go quiet, so I have to bring Marcus in.
Don't correct me during the chat. After 20 minutes, when I say "feedback", tell me:
1) how many times I handed over and how (list the phrases I used),
2) times I missed a chance to bring Marcus in,
3) three natural hand-over phrases I should use more,
4) up to 5 grammar or word mistakes with corrections.
```

**Step 2 (20 min).** Have the conversation. Goals:
- Hand over at least **10 times**: at least 4 to Marcus by name, at least 2 with a hook ("Marcus, you just got back from Chicago, right?").
- Use "Anyway, enough about me —" at least once after you talk for a while.
- Use "Sorry, Marcus, you were saying?" if Dani cuts him off.

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Listen, and write down the phrases and corrections.

**Step 4 (25 min).** Run it again with a new setting. Say: *"Same two people, new scene: we're at a friend's birthday dinner and none of us know each other well. Start."* This time also use two jump-ins from [Day 1](day-1-jumping-in.md). Ask for feedback again at the end.

**Step 5 (8 min).** Listen back to 3 minutes of your recording (if your app records) or read the feedback again. Say each corrected sentence 3 times.
