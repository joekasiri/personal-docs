## Learning objectives

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: fast repair questions.** When native speakers miss something, the question is short, fast and light, often just two or three words. The **question word** gets the stress and a rising tone: "You went WHERE? ↗" "Wait, WHO? ↗". Many are reduced: "Say that again?" → "say-that-again", "What'd you say?" → "whadja say?", "Come again?" → "c'mon-again?" (two quick beats).

Practice sentences (stress in CAPITALS):

1. "Sorry, say that AGAIN? ↗"
2. "Wait, you LOST me. ↘"
3. "You went WHERE? ↗"
4. "Wait, WHO'S Kevin? ↗"
5. "Sorry, it's LOUD in here — whadja SAY? ↗"
6. "Hold on, back up a SEC. ↘"
7. "Sorry, I missed that last PART. ↘"
8. "Wait, what do you MEAN by 'the incident'? ↗"
9. "Sorry, I didn't catch his NAME. ↘"
10. "Wait, so you're MOVING? ↗"
11. "Come AGAIN? ↗"

**Clip task:** search "podcast hosts confused what", "wait what podcast moment" or "friends podcast misunderstanding". Speak along for 3 minutes. Notice how quickly people say "Wait, what?" and how the speaker then repeats only the key words. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Asking people to repeat

### Explanation

In groups you will miss things. People talk over each other, the music is loud, someone mentions a friend you have never met, or a word is new. **Native speakers miss things too, all the time**, and they ask without any embarrassment. The problem for learners is usually not the missing word. It is the fear that asking shows a "language problem", so they nod and pretend. Then they get lost, and stop talking.

The fix is to ask the way native speakers do: **fast, light and specific.**

- **Fast and light.** Two to four words with a relaxed face: "Sorry, say that again?" It sounds like a normal part of the chat, not a stop.
- **Specific.** Repeat the part you *did* understand and put a question word where the gap is: "You went *where* on Saturday?" "Your sister did *what*?" Now they repeat only one word, and the conversation keeps moving.
- **Blame the situation, not your English.** "Sorry, it's so loud in here." "Sorry, I zoned out for a sec." Both are true for everyone, and nobody thinks about language.
- **Missing context is normal.** In groups, people mention names and stories you do not know. Just ask: "Wait, who's Kevin?" "Sorry, what happened at the wedding?" People love explaining.
- **Check with a summary.** "Wait, so… you're quitting *and* moving?" This recycles [summarising](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-5-summarising.md) in a tiny, fast form.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sorry, say that again? | you missed everything | "Sorry, say that again? The music's loud." |
| Wait, you lost me. | you followed, then got confused | "Wait, you lost me. Who was driving?" |
| Sorry, I missed that last part. | you missed only the end | "Sorry, I missed that last part — after the party?" |
| You went where? | you missed one key word (echo + question word) | "You went where on Saturday?" |
| Wait, who's [name]? | they mention someone you don't know | "Wait, who's Kevin? Your roommate?" |
| Hold on, back up a sec. | the story jumped and you need the earlier part | "Hold on, back up a sec. Why were you at the airport?" |
| Sorry, it's so loud in here — what was that? | there is noise | "Sorry, it's so loud in here — what was that?" |
| Wait, what do you mean by [X]? | you heard the word but don't know what they mean | "Wait, what do you mean by 'the incident'?" |
| Sorry, I didn't catch his name. | you missed a name | "Sorry, I didn't catch her name. Jen?" |
| I think I missed something — | the group laughs or reacts and you don't know why | "I think I missed something — why is everyone mad at Kevin?" |
| Come again? | a playful, very short "what?" (between friends) | "Come again? You ate *twelve* tacos?" |
| Wait, so… [summary]? | you want to check you understood | "Wait, so you're moving to Austin in June?" |

### Examples

**Noisy bar, three friends and you.**

> **Jess:** …so Kevin shows up with the goat, and everyone just loses it.
> **Leo:** Classic Kevin.
> **You:** Wait, who's Kevin?
> **Jess:** Oh, sorry, my cousin. He's, like, the family clown.
> **You:** Okay. And sorry, it's so loud in here — he showed up with a *what*?
> **Jess:** A goat! A real goat. At my grandma's birthday.
> **You:** Come again? Why did he have a goat?
> **Leo:** Nobody knows. That's the best part.

**Work lunch: a word you don't know.**

> **Dana:** Honestly, the whole launch was a bit of a dumpster fire.
> **You:** Wait, what do you mean by "dumpster fire"? Like, it was a disaster?
> **Dana:** Yeah, exactly, total chaos. Nothing worked.
> **You:** Got it. So what went wrong first?

**You missed the end of a long sentence.**

> **Sam:** We're gonna leave around six, grab food on the way, and then meet Priya at the, uh, the trailhead by the lake.
> **You:** Sorry, I missed that last part — we're meeting Priya where?
> **Sam:** At the trailhead. By the lake.
> **You:** Okay, cool. So, six o'clock, food on the way, then the lake. Got it.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Repeat, please."
✅ "Sorry, say that again?"
*Reason: "Repeat, please" sounds like an order or a classroom.*

❌ "Pardon?"
✅ "Sorry?" / "Sorry, what was that?"
*Reason: "Pardon?" is fine in British English but sounds stiff and old-fashioned in casual American chat.*

❌ "I don't understand."
✅ "Wait, you lost me — who was driving?"
*Reason: "I don't understand" sounds like a language problem and stops everything. Be specific.*

❌ "What did you said?"
✅ "What did you say?" ("Whadja say?")
*Reason: after "did", use the base verb.*

❌ "Can you speak more slowly, please?" (in a group of friends)
✅ "Sorry, it's so loud in here — what was that?"
*Reason: in a group, asking for slower speech makes you the problem. Blame the noise.*

❌ Nodding and smiling when you are lost.
✅ "I think I missed something — what happened?"
*Reason: pretending works for 10 seconds. Then you are lost for 10 minutes.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, group role-play

Tonight Claude plays **two people** in a group and makes the conversation messy on purpose, so you have to ask for repeats, names and explanations. Play café background noise quietly if you have it. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read this prompt:

```
Group role-play. You play TWO people: NINA and TYLER, old college friends of mine.
I'm meeting them at a busy bar. Say each name before their line.
Make it realistic and a bit messy:
- talk fast, use casual American slang and some idioms I might not know,
- mention people, places and inside jokes I don't know (without explaining),
- sometimes say something unclear or cut your own sentence short,
- sometimes Nina and Tyler talk to each other and leave me out.
My job is to ask them to repeat, explain names and jokes, and check my understanding,
and also to jump in and hand over like a real group member.
Don't correct me during the chat. When I say "feedback", tell me:
1) every repair question I used and whether it sounded natural,
2) moments I should have asked but didn't,
3) up to 5 mistakes with corrections,
4) three slang words or idioms you used, with meanings.
Start with Nina greeting me.
```

**Step 2 (20 min).** Have the conversation. Targets:
- At least **8 repair questions**, using at least 5 different phrases from the table.
- At least **3 echo questions** with a question word ("You did *what*?").
- At least **2 summaries** ("Wait, so…?").
- Also keep using Days 1–3: jump in, hand over, hold your turn.

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Write down the corrections and new slang in your phrase log.

**Step 4 (25 min).** New scene. Say: *"Same people, new scene: we're planning a weekend trip together on a group video call and the line is a little bad. Nina keeps giving details fast: dates, times, prices. Start."* Focus on catching every detail. At the end, **summarise the whole plan** back to them in one go. Ask for feedback.

**Step 5 (8 min).** Repeat each correction 3 times. Then say your 5 favourite repair phrases as fast as you can, twice.
