## Learning objectives

- Keep your voice "open" so people can hear you are not finished.
- Hold your turn politely and with a smile when someone cuts in.
- Decide quickly when to hold your turn and when to let it go.
- Get back to your point after an interruption.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: unfinished intonation.** When native speakers are *not* finished, they keep their voice **level or slightly up** at the end of a phrase, and they often add a linking word ("and…", "so…", "but…") that they stretch a little. A falling voice says "I'm done." A level voice plus "and…" says "Wait, there's more." This is your first, invisible way to hold your turn.

Practice sentences (→ = keep the voice level, ↘ = fall). Stretch the bold word slightly.

1. "So we get there **and** → the door's locked **and** → nobody has a key ↘."
2. "I was gonna call you **but** → then I thought → nah, I'll just text ↘."
3. "It's not that I don't like it, it's **just** → it's kinda expensive ↘."
4. "Hang on, hang on → let me finish → it gets better ↘."
5. "Hold on → I'm getting there ↘."
6. "**So** → the thing is → we already paid for it ↘."
7. "Two secs → just let me finish this thought ↘."
8. "And — sorry — just to finish → the second option is cheaper ↘."
9. "Yeah, I'll get to that → but first → the hotel ↘."
10. "Okay, so, as I was saying → the train was two hours late ↘."

**Clip task:** search "podcast hosts interrupt each other funny", "talking over each other podcast" or "debate podcast casual". Find a moment when a host keeps talking while someone tries to cut in. Speak along with that host for 3 minutes. Copy how they keep the voice up and repeat words ("and — and —") to keep the floor. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Holding your turn

### Explanation

In groups, people interrupt all the time. Usually it is not rude: they are excited, they guess your ending, or they have a quick reaction. But if you always stop when someone cuts in, you will never finish a story, and you will start to feel that your English "isn't good enough" to hold attention. It is a skill, not a level.

You have three tools, from softest to strongest:

1. **Voice.** Keep the end of your phrase level and add "and…" or "so…" (see shadowing). Many interruptions never happen because people can hear you are not done.
2. **A quick holding phrase, said with a smile.** "Hang on, let me finish." "Two secs." "I'm getting there." Light and friendly. Often with a small hand gesture (palm out, or one finger up).
3. **A promise.** "Yeah, I'll get to that." "It gets better, I promise." This tells the interrupter their point is welcome *later*.

**When to let it go.** Hold your turn when you are in the middle of a story or a point. Let it go when the interruption is more important (someone needs to leave, the food arrives, someone has bad news) or when the other person has been waiting a long time. Letting go is also a phrase: "Sorry, go ahead." "No, you go." Then take your turn back later with "Okay, so, as I was saying…" or "Anyway, where was I?"

**Tone is everything.** "Let me finish" said flat and loud sounds angry. Said with a smile and rising energy ("Hang on, hang on, let me finish — it gets so much worse"), it sounds fun and keeps the group with you.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hang on, let me finish. | someone cuts in during your story | "Hang on, let me finish — it gets better." |
| Hold on, hold on — | you need to stop an interruption fast | "Hold on, hold on, I'm not done!" |
| Just one sec — / Two secs — | you need a moment to finish | "Two secs, let me just finish this." |
| Let me just finish this thought. | you are in the middle of an idea (also at work) | "Let me just finish this thought, then I want your take." |
| Wait, I'm getting there. | someone guesses the ending too early | "No, wait, I'm getting there!" |
| I'm almost done, I promise. | you have talked for a while | "I'm almost done, I promise — last part." |
| And — sorry — just to finish… | you were cut off and you return quickly | "And — sorry — just to finish, it was free." |
| Yeah, I'll get to that. | they ask about something you planned to say later | "Yeah, I'll get to that, but first…" |
| It gets better, I promise. | you want people to wait for the good part | "It gets better, I promise. So then…" |
| Let me just say this one thing. | you want one final point before you hand over | "Let me just say this one thing, then I'll shut up." |
| Sorry, go ahead. | you decide to let them speak | "Oh, sorry, go ahead." |
| Okay, so, as I was saying… | you take your turn back after an interruption | "Okay, so, as I was saying, the train was late." |

### Examples

**Three friends, you hold your turn twice.**

> **You:** So I get to the airport, and the guy at the desk looks at my passport and —
> **Leo:** It was expired.
> **You:** No! Hang on, let me finish. It gets better. He looks at my passport and says, "Is this you?"
> **Maya:** Oh no.
> **You:** Because the photo is from when I was, like, sixteen —
> **Leo:** Oh my god, the hair.
> **You:** Two secs! I'm getting there. So he calls his manager…
> **Maya:** (laughing) Okay, okay, go on.

**Letting go, then taking it back.**

> **You:** So the main problem with the new app is —
> **Sam:** Sorry, sorry, the pizza's here, I'll grab it.
> **You:** Oh, go ahead.
> *(one minute later)*
> **You:** Okay, so, as I was saying, the main problem with the app is that nobody can find the settings.
> **Priya:** Ugh, yes. I looked for, like, ten minutes.

**At a work lunch: holding your turn softly.**

> **You:** I think we should test it with real users first, because —
> **Marcus:** Yeah, but we don't have time.
> **You:** Let me just finish this thought — because if we don't, we'll fix the wrong thing. *Then* I'm totally open to a quick version.
> **Marcus:** Okay, fair.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Wait! I didn't finish!" (loud, flat)
✅ "Hang on, let me finish — it gets better." (smiling)
*Reason: the first sounds annoyed. The second keeps the fun and the turn.*

❌ "Let me to finish."
✅ "Let me finish."
*Reason: after "let me", use the verb without "to".*

❌ "Please don't interrupt me."
✅ "Two secs — I'm almost done."
*Reason: among friends, "don't interrupt me" sounds like a teacher. Save it for serious situations.*

❌ Stopping and never coming back to your point.
✅ "Okay, so, as I was saying…"
*Reason: if you never return, people learn that you can always be cut off.*

❌ Ending each phrase with a falling voice, then getting upset when people jump in.
✅ Keep your voice level and add "and…" when you are not finished.
*Reason: a falling voice tells the group you are done.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

### Part A: Interruption drill (30 min)

1. Set a timer app to beep at random every 20–40 seconds (or ask someone at home to say "Wait, what?" or "Oh, I know this!" at random moments).
2. Tell each of the stories below for **2 minutes**, recording. Every time the timer beeps, say a holding phrase **out loud and with a smile**, then carry on. Use a different phrase each time.
3. After each story, check: did you go back to your point every time?

Stories (one after another):

1. "The time you got lost in a city you didn't know."
2. "A meal that went wrong, at home or in a restaurant."
3. "The most annoying thing that happened at work this year."
4. "A time you met someone famous, or someone who *thought* they were famous."
5. "Your worst ever travel day."

Then do one **3/2/1-style squeeze** on the story you liked best: 2 minutes with beeps, then 1 minute with beeps. Use [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md) timing ideas.

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (30 min)

**Mission:** in a real conversation (ideally 3+ people), use this week's first three skills.

Try to:

1. Jump in at least **3 times** with an entry chunk from [Day 1](day-1-jumping-in.md).
2. Hand over at least **3 times**, once to someone by name with a hook ([Day 2](day-2-handing-over.md)).
3. Hold your turn at least **once**, with a smile, and then finish your point.
4. Tell one short story (1–2 minutes) from Part A.

**Backup plan (no partner available):** voice mode with Claude. Paste this:

```

so I can practise holding my turn. Casual American English, short turns, no corrections until I say "feedback".
At feedback, tell me how well I held my turn, which phrases I used, and up to 5 mistakes.
```

Afterwards, write in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): who you talked to, how many times you jumped in, handed over and held your turn.
