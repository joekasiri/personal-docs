## Learning objectives

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: agree-and-add chains.** When people build on each other, the agreement word is short and quick, and the stress jumps to the **new** information. "Yeah, and the PARKING's terrible." "Right, right, and it's FREE on Sundays." The first words are almost squashed together: "yeah-and", "right-right-and", "exactly-and-that's-why".

Practice sentences (stress the CAPITALS, squash the rest):

1. "Yeah, and on TOP of that, it's cheaper."
2. "Right, right, and the other thing is the TIMING."
3. "Exactly — and that's WHY nobody goes there."
4. "Ooh, and what if we brought a SPEAKER?"
5. "Yeah, plus it's, like, ten minutes from my PLACE."
6. "Building on what SAM said — we could just rent a VAN."
7. "Going back to what MAYA said about the BEACH…"
8. "To piggyback on THAT — we should book TONIGHT."
9. "And honestly, it's not just the PRICE, it's the VIBE."
10. "I'd take it even FURTHER — let's make it a whole WEEKEND."

**Clip task:** search "friends planning podcast", "hosts brainstorm ideas podcast" or "comedy writers room chat". Find a part where speakers keep adding to one idea. Speak along for 4 minutes. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Building on others' ideas

### Explanation

In a group, the most liked person is often not the one with the best ideas. It is the one who makes *other people's* ideas better. Building on someone's idea is also the easiest way to talk without thinking, because half of your sentence is already there: they just said it.

Think of improv theatre's famous rule: **"Yes, and…"**. You accept what the other person said ("yes") and add something ("and"). Compare:

- A: "We should do a picnic on Saturday."
- B: "Yeah, but it might rain." (the idea stops)
- B: "Yeah, and if it rains, we can move it to my place." (the idea grows)

You can still disagree (you learnt that in [Week 6 · Day 3](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-3-disagreeing-politely.md)). But in casual groups, "Yeah, and…" keeps the energy up, and you can add your concern *inside* the build: "Yeah, and let's have a plan B in case it rains."

**Five ways to build:**

| Way | What you do | Example (after "Let's do a picnic") |
|---|---|---|
| Add more | add another detail or reason | "Yeah, and the park's got grills now." |
| Give an example | make it concrete | "Totally, like that spot by the lake." |
| Add a twist | change one part to make it better | "Ooh, and what if it's a potluck?" |
| Push it further | make it bigger | "I'd take it even further — let's camp overnight." |
| Connect two people | link two ideas from the group | "That kinda fits with what Sam said about the music." |

**Use names.** "Building on what Maya said…" or "Going back to Leo's idea…" shows you were listening, and people love hearing their idea mentioned.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Yeah, and on top of that… | you add another point | "Yeah, and on top of that, it's free." |
| Building on what [name] said… | you develop someone's idea (casual or work) | "Building on what Priya said, we could test it next week." |
| Right, and the other thing is… | you add a second reason | "Right, and the other thing is, nobody wants to drive." |
| Exactly — and that's why… | you agree and draw a conclusion | "Exactly — and that's why we should book early." |
| Ooh, and what if we…? | you add a fun twist | "Ooh, and what if we make it a costume party?" |
| Going back to what [name] said about… | you return to an earlier idea | "Going back to what Sam said about the budget…" |
| Yeah, plus… | quick, casual addition | "Yeah, plus my car fits six." |
| To piggyback on that… | you add directly to the last idea | "To piggyback on that, the venue has a projector." |
| It's kinda like [X]… | you give an example or comparison | "It's kinda like that trip we did to Austin." |
| And honestly, it's not just [X], it's [Y]. | you widen the point | "And honestly, it's not just the price, it's the location." |
| I'd take it even further — | you make the idea bigger | "I'd take it even further — let's do it every month." |
| That fits with what [name] said. | you connect two people | "That fits with what Leo said about leaving early." |

### Examples

**Three friends and you plan a birthday.**

> **Maya:** Okay, Jordan's birthday. Ideas?
> **Sam:** Maybe just dinner somewhere nice?
> **You:** Yeah, and on top of that, we could get the private room at Bella's. It's not even that expensive.
> **Leo:** Ooh, and what if we all wear something ridiculous? He'd hate it.
> **Maya:** He would *hate* it. I love it.
> **You:** I'd take it even further — we make him a terrible crown.
> **Sam:** Okay, but going back to the money thing — how much is the room?
> **You:** Like fifteen bucks each. That fits with what you said, Sam, nothing too crazy.

**Design team brainstorm (work, casual).**

> **Priya:** Users keep missing the "save" button.
> **Marcus:** We could make it bigger.
> **You:** Yeah, and the other thing is, it's grey. Building on what Marcus said, we could make it bigger *and* use the main colour.
> **Dana:** Ooh, and what if it saves automatically?
> **You:** Exactly — and that's why autosave might be the real fix. It's kinda like how notes apps work. Nobody presses save there.
> **Priya:** Okay, I like that. Let's test both.

**Turning a "but" into an "and".**

> **Leo:** Let's go to the beach on Sunday.
> **You (old habit):** Yeah, but the traffic is terrible.
> **You (new habit):** Yeah, and if we leave by eight, we'll beat the traffic.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Yes, but…" in response to every idea.
✅ "Yeah, and…" (then add your concern inside the build)
*Reason: "but" stops the idea; "and" grows it, and people stay open to your point.*

❌ "Like Sam said, we should go to the beach." (and nothing more)
✅ "Like Sam said, the beach — and we could bring the grill."
*Reason: repeating without adding is not building. Always add one new thing.*

❌ "I agree with you." (full stop)
✅ "Totally — and the timing works too."
*Reason: "I agree" alone sounds formal and ends your turn with nothing new.*

❌ "In addition to that, moreover…"
✅ "Yeah, plus…" / "And on top of that…"
*Reason: written connectors sound like an essay in casual speech.*

❌ "To piggyback of that…"
✅ "To piggyback on that…"
*Reason: the fixed chunk uses "on".*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill in "build mode"** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Build mode (40 min).** Each card gives a topic and two ideas that two friends "just said". Read both lines out loud. Then talk for **90 seconds**: start by building on one of the ideas, connect it to the other one at some point, and keep going. Record each one. Use a different building phrase to start each card.

| # | Topic | Friend 1 said… | Friend 2 said… |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A team night out | "We should do bowling." | "Somewhere with good food, please." |
| 2 | Making the office nicer | "We need more plants." | "The lighting is so depressing." |
| 3 | A weekend trip | "Let's go somewhere we can drive to." | "I just want to sleep and eat." |
| 4 | Saving money | "I'm gonna stop buying coffee out." | "I need to cancel some subscriptions." |
| 5 | A friend's farewell | "We could make a video with messages." | "Let's do something outdoors." |
| 6 | Learning a skill | "I wanna learn to cook properly." | "Online classes never work for me." |
| 7 | A better morning | "I should wake up earlier." | "My phone is the problem." |
| 8 | A group vacation | "Somewhere warm, for sure." | "Not too expensive." |
| 9 | Improving an app | "It needs a dark mode." | "The sign-up takes forever." |
| 10 | A neighbourhood party | "Everyone brings one dish." | "We need music, but not too loud." |

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Tell the story of a plan your friends or your team made together that went better (or worse) than expected. Whose idea was it, who added what, and what happened?"** 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each. In each version, say at least two times how someone built on an idea ("And then Leo goes, 'Ooh, and what if…'").
