# Week 13 exercises: Group conversations
[Week 13 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-jumping-in.md) · [Day 2](day-2-handing-over.md) · [Day 3](day-3-holding-your-turn.md) · [Day 4](day-4-asking-people-to-repeat.md) · [Day 5](day-5-building-on-others-ideas.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** everything is **out loud**. Never just read or write. When a line belongs to another speaker, say it in a different voice (higher, lower, faster) so your brain hears a "group". Record when the exercise says so. Answer keys give *model* answers: yours can be different and still correct. If yours is different, say the model once too, so you own both.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Find the landing point
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Each friend's turn below has three possible places to jump in, marked **(A)**, **(B)** and **(C)**. Usually only one is a real landing point (the speaker is finishing). The others are in the middle of a thought.

1. Read the turn out loud in the friend's voice, with natural intonation.
2. Decide which letter is the landing point.
3. Read the turn again up to that letter, then **jump in** with an entry chunk and one connected sentence.
4. Check the answer key.

1. **Maya:** "So we booked this Airbnb (A) and the photos looked amazing, but when we got there (B) it was, like, half the size. So yeah. (C)"
2. **Leo:** "I've been trying to run every morning, (A) which is hard because (B) I'm not a morning person at all. Like, at all. (C)"
3. **Priya:** "The concert was incredible. Honestly, one of the best nights of my life. (A) The only thing was (B) the tickets were (C) so expensive."
4. **Sam:** "My landlord finally fixed the heating, (A) after, what, (B) three weeks? Which was crazy. (C)"
5. **Jordan:** "We're thinking of getting a dog, (A) but my partner wants a big one and I want (B) a small one, so (C)…"
6. **Dana:** "Work's been insane. We launched the new app on Monday, and it crashed, like, twice. (A) Anyway. (B) I need a vacation. (C)"
7. **Marcus:** "I finally watched that show everyone's talking about, (A) and honestly (B) I don't get the hype. (C)"
8. **Jess:** "So my sister calls me at 2 a.m., (A) and I'm thinking something terrible happened, (B) and she just goes, 'Do you have Mom's pasta recipe?' (C)"

### Exercise 1.2: Bridge builder
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then record the last five

For each line, jump in using the **entry chunk given**, then build a **bridge** to what they said, then add **one sentence** of your own. Aim for under 5 seconds before you start speaking.

| # | Friend says… | Use this entry chunk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I just got back from Chicago. It was freezing." | Oh, that reminds me… |
| 2 | "We're gonna try that new Korean place on Friday." | Oh, speaking of… |
| 3 | "My train was cancelled again this morning." | Same thing happened to me! |
| 4 | "I think the new office is way too noisy." | Can I just add something? |
| 5 | "I've started reading before bed instead of scrolling." | That's so funny, because… |
| 6 | "The client wants the logo bigger. Again." | Wait, I gotta say… |
| 7 | "Has anyone been to the farmers' market downtown?" | Oh, on that note… |
| 8 | "My roommate keeps eating my food." | I was just gonna say… |
| 9 | "So, the restaurant is at eight, right?" | Quick thing — |
| 10 | "Winter here is just so long." | Oh, totally, and… |
| 11 | "I finally finished that huge puzzle." | Not to change the subject, but… |
| 12 | "And then the speaker fell off the stage — he was fine, though." | Sorry, just to jump in… |

### Exercise 1.3: Three-second jump-ins
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** record

Set a timer. Read each line in a "friend" voice. You have **3 seconds** to jump in with *any* entry chunk and *one* connected sentence. Do not plan. If you miss the 3 seconds, say "Oh—" and go anyway. Do the list twice: the second time, use a different chunk for each line.

1. "I think I'm gonna cut my hair really short."
2. "Did you see how many people were at the gym today?"
3. "My mom just learnt how to send voice notes. She sends, like, ten a day."
4. "I'm so done with rainy weekends."
5. "We should all go bowling sometime."
6. "My laptop died in the middle of a presentation."
7. "I tried oat milk for the first time. It's not bad."
8. "I've been learning guitar. Very slowly."
9. "The movie was three hours long."
10. "I got lost driving to my own office today."
11. "Somebody brought a dog to work. Best day ever."
12. "I spent way too much money this weekend."
13. "I'm thinking about going back to school."
14. "The new phone update moved all my apps."
15. "I've never been to a baseball game."
16. "My neighbour gave me a whole bag of lemons."
17. "I fell asleep on the bus and missed my stop."
18. "We're finally painting the bedroom."
19. "I can't believe it's almost the end of the year."
20. "I signed up for a cooking class."

### Day 1 answer key

**Exercise 1.1** (landing point + a model jump-in):

1. **C.** "So yeah" + falling voice. Model: "Oh, that reminds me, the same thing happened to us in Miami. The pool in the photos was basically a bathtub."
2. **C.** The repeated "Like, at all" closes the thought. Model: "Same thing happened to me! I lasted four days."
3. **A.** A strong, complete sentence with a falling voice (B and C are mid-sentence). Model: "Wait, I gotta say, I'm so jealous. Who did you see?" (If you miss A, you can also jump in after "so expensive".)
4. **C.** "Which was crazy" is a closing phrase. Model: "Three weeks? Oh, that reminds me, our elevator's been broken for a month."
5. **None is perfect, but C is best.** "So…" trailing off often invites help. Model: "Oh, totally, and medium dogs exist! Problem solved."
6. **C** is the clearest, but **B** works too ("Anyway" is a closing word). Model: "Oh, speaking of vacations, are you still thinking about Portugal?"
7. **C.** Model: "Wait, I gotta say, same. I stopped after episode two."
8. **C.** The punchline is the end. Model: "That's so funny, because my brother did the exact same thing last week."

### Exercise 2.3: Three-voice script
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud (all voices)

Read the whole scene out loud. Use a different voice for each friend. Fill each gap (1–8) with a hand-over phrase. Then read it once more, faster.

> *Setting: a picnic. You, Leo (chatty), Priya (quiet), Sam (funny).*
>
> **Leo:** …and that's why I'm never flying that airline again. Never.
> **Sam:** Noted.
> **You:** Ugh, I've had a few of those. **(1)** _______ — you fly a lot for work, ______?
> **Priya:** Yeah, like twice a month.
> **You:** **(2)** _______ ever had a flight that bad?
> **Priya:** Once. I got stuck in Denver for a whole night.
> **Leo:** Denver! Okay, so last year in Denver, I —
> **Sam:** Here we go.
> **You:** Wait, Leo, hang on. **(3)** _______, Priya, you were saying? A whole night?
> **Priya:** Yeah, they gave us a blanket and a sandwich. That's it.
> **You:** That's rough. **(4)** _______ who actually likes airport sandwiches?
> **Sam:** Honestly? Me.
> **You:** No way. Okay, Sam, **(5)** _______ — what's the weirdest place you've slept?
> **Sam:** A bowling alley. Long story.
> **You:** Okay, we need that story. **(6)** _______ about the bowling alley.
> **Sam:** So it was my cousin's bachelor party…
> *(later)*
> **You:** …anyway, that's my plan for the summer. **(7)** _______ — what's everyone doing in July? **(8)** _______, Priya, you said something about Greece, ______?

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Interrupted scripts
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, then record

In each script, a friend interrupts you. Read the friend's lines in another voice. At every **[HOLD]**, say a holding phrase (different each time), with a smile. At **[BACK]**, take your turn back. Then record yourself doing script 4 without looking.

**Script 1: The broken elevator**
> **You:** So I'm in the elevator at work, on my way to the big meeting, and it just stops between floors —
> **Leo:** Oh no, you were stuck for hours.
> **You:** **[HOLD]** — it gets better. So it stops, and the lights go off, and the only other person in there is —
> **Leo:** Your boss!
> **You:** **[HOLD]** It's the CEO. And she's holding a giant cake.
> **Leo:** Why does she have a cake?
> **You:** **[HOLD]** So we're stuck for twenty minutes, and she just opens the box and goes, "Well, we might as well."

**Script 2: The wrong wedding**
> **You:** Okay, so my cousin's wedding was at this huge hotel, and I get there a little late, and I walk into the ballroom and —
> **Maya:** Wrong wedding.
> **You:** **[HOLD]** I walk in, I find a seat, I'm clapping for the couple —
> **Maya:** Oh my god, it's the wrong wedding.
> **You:** **[HOLD]** And I'm looking for my aunt, and I can't find anyone I know…
> **Sam:** Sorry, sorry — is that my phone? Hang on.
> **You:** Oh, go ahead.
> *(Sam comes back)*
> **You:** **[BACK]** I was at the wrong wedding. For forty-five minutes.

**Script 3: The work idea**
> **You:** I think we should launch the feature to a small group first, because —
> **Marcus:** We don't have time for that.
> **You:** **[HOLD]** — because if something breaks, only a few people see it.
> **Marcus:** And what about the deadline?
> **You:** **[HOLD]** But first, think about the support team. They're already overloaded.
> **Dana:** Okay, let him finish.
> **You:** Thanks. **[HOLD]** If we test with two hundred people, we're done in a week.

**Script 4: The neighbour's parrot**
> **You:** So my new neighbour has a parrot, right? And every morning at six, it starts —
> **Jess:** Singing!
> **You:** **[HOLD]** It starts saying my name.
> **Jess:** What? How does it know your name?
> **You:** **[HOLD]** So I'm thinking, okay, the neighbour must say my name a lot, which is weird —
> **Omar:** Wait, is the neighbour cute?
> **You:** **[HOLD]** Turns out the neighbour's *dog* has the same name as me.
> **Omar:** Oh, that's even better.

### Exercise 3.2: Hold it or let it go?
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

For each situation, decide: **hold** your turn or **let it go**? Then say the exact phrase you would use. If you let it go, also say how you would take the turn back later.

1. You're in the middle of a funny story. A friend guesses the ending (wrong).
2. You're telling a story. The waiter arrives to take the order.
3. You're explaining an idea at lunch. A coworker says "But that's too expensive" before you've finished.
4. You've been talking for four minutes. A quiet friend finally starts to say something.
5. You're telling a story. Your friend's phone rings and it's her mom in hospital.
6. You're giving directions to the group. Someone asks, "Wait, which station?"
7. You're about to say the funniest part. Your friend says, "Oh, I know, I know!"
8. You're halfway through a point. Someone says, "Sorry, I have to leave in two minutes, can I just say bye?"
9. You're telling a story. Someone asks a detail you planned to explain later.
10. You're in a group debate. Two people start talking at the same time as you.

### Exercise 3.3: Keep the door open
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

**Part A.** Say each starter with a **level voice** at the end and a stretched linking word, then finish it with your own words. Do each one twice.

1. "So I get to the station **and**…"
2. "I was gonna order the pasta **but**…"
3. "It's not that I don't like him, it's **just**…"
4. "We were halfway up the mountain **when**…"
5. "The thing is, we already paid, **so**…"
6. "I called the landlord **and** he said **and** — get this — …"
7. "My boss walks in **and**…"
8. "It was supposed to be a quick trip **but**…"
9. "I tried the new café **and** honestly…"
10. "So I open the box **and**…"

**Part B.** One long chain. Tell this as one story without ever letting your voice fall until the very end. Use "and…", "so…", "but then…" between each part:

> lost my keys → called my roommate → she was at the gym → waited outside → started raining → neighbour invited me in → neighbour makes amazing cookies → now we're friends.

### Day 3 answer key

**Exercise 3.1** (models; any friendly holding phrase works):
- Script 1: "Hang on, let me finish" / "Hold on, hold on — it's even weirder" / "I'm getting there!"
- Script 2: "No, wait — two secs" / "Hang on, it gets worse" / [BACK] "Okay, so, as I was saying —"
- Script 3: "Let me just finish this thought" / "Yeah, I'll get to that" / "Let me just say this one thing"
- Script 4: "Hang on, let me finish — it's worse" / "I'm getting there, I promise" / "Two secs!"

**Exercise 3.2** (models):
1. **Hold.** "Nope! Hang on, let me finish."
2. **Let go.** "Oh, go ahead, let's order." Later: "Okay, so, as I was saying…"
3. **Hold.** "Let me just finish this thought — then I want your take on the cost."
4. **Let go.** "Sorry, go ahead." (Four minutes is long enough.)
5. **Let go, completely.** "Oh no, take it. Go." (Don't take the turn back: ask if she's okay.)
6. **Answer quickly, then hold.** "Central Station. And — sorry — just to finish, then we walk five minutes."
7. **Hold.** "No, no, wait, I'm getting there!"
8. **Let go.** "Oh, of course, go ahead." Later: "Anyway, where was I? Oh right…"
9. **Hold with a promise.** "Yeah, I'll get to that, I promise."
10. **Hold or share.** "Okay, okay — one at a time! Let me just say this one thing, then Sam."

**Exercise 3.3 Part B** (model): "So I lost my keys, **and** I called my roommate, **but** she was at the gym, **so** I waited outside, **and then** it started raining, **so** my neighbour invited me in, **and** — this is the best part — she makes amazing cookies, **so** now we're friends ↘."

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Echo the gap
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence has a part you "didn't hear", shown as **[mumble]**. Ask a natural **echo question**: repeat what you did hear and put the right question word (who / what / where / when / how much / how long) in the gap. Stress the question word. Then do the list again, faster, in under 3 seconds each.

1. "We're going to **[mumble]** for New Year's."
2. "My sister just bought a **[mumble]**."
3. "I'm meeting **[mumble]** after work."
4. "The tickets cost **[mumble]** each."
5. "We waited for **[mumble]** at the airport."
6. "I ran into **[mumble]** at the supermarket."
7. "The party starts at **[mumble]**."
8. "He quit his job to become a **[mumble]**."
9. "We're moving on **[mumble]**."
10. "She ate **[mumble]** tacos."
11. "My boss said we should **[mumble]**."
12. "They found a **[mumble]** in the kitchen."

### Exercise 4.2: Who, what, why?
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

In each group moment, you are missing context: a name, an inside joke, a slang word or the reason everyone is laughing. Ask one natural question.

1. Everyone laughs when someone says "Remember Cancún?"
2. **Leo:** "Classic Kevin." (You don't know Kevin.)
3. **Jess:** "That place is so bougie now."
4. **Omar:** "We're doing the thing on Friday, right?" (You don't know what "the thing" is.)
5. **Maya:** "She ghosted him after the third date."
6. **Sam:** "I can't, I'm on call this weekend."
7. **Dana:** "We all know what happened at the last offsite." (Everyone groans.)
8. **Priya:** "Tyler's doing his whole bit again."

### Exercise 4.3: Check it with a summary
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then record

A friend gives you a fast plan or a lot of news. Read it out loud quickly (fast voice!). Then check with **"Wait, so…?"** and a one-sentence summary. Get every detail.

1. **Sam:** "Okay so, we're meeting at Lena's at seven, then we're all getting a cab to the concert, it starts at eight-thirty, and Jake's bringing the tickets but he'll be late, so we wait for him outside."
2. **Maya:** "Big news. I got the job in Seattle, I start in March, and we're selling the car, and also I'm getting a cat. Her name is Biscuit."
3. **Leo:** "The restaurant called, they can't do Friday, so it's Saturday now, same time, but it's the other location, the one on Fifth, not the one by the park."
4. **Dana:** "So the client meeting moved to Thursday at two, Marcus is presenting, you're doing the demo, and I'll send the deck tonight."
5. **Jess:** "My flight lands at eleven-fifteen at night, terminal B, but my phone's gonna be dead, so can you just wait by the coffee place near baggage claim?"
6. **Omar:** "We're splitting the cabin, it's four hundred and eighty for the weekend, there are six of us, so that's eighty each, and I need it by Wednesday."

### Exercise 5.3: Brainstorm script
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud (all voices), then improvise

Read the scene, filling gaps 1–6 with building phrases. Then keep the brainstorm going on your own for 3 minutes: play all four people and add at least six more ideas, each one building on the one before.

> *Setting: four friends planning a surprise for their friend Ana, who is moving to Canada.*
>
