## Learning objectives

- Project your voice so a whole room can hear you, without shouting.
- Introduce yourself in 10, 30 and 60 seconds with a clear structure.
- Adapt your introduction to the group (meetup, new team, class, wedding table).
- End with a "hook" that gives people something to talk to you about later.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: projection.** Projecting means making your voice carry across a room. It does **not** mean shouting. Shouting comes from the throat and sounds angry. Projection comes from the **belly** (your breath) and sounds confident.

How to project:
1. **Stand up straight**, feet apart, shoulders relaxed.
2. **Breathe low**: put a hand on your belly. When you breathe in, the hand moves out.
3. **Aim your voice** at the person farthest away, like you're throwing a ball to them.
4. **Keep the end of sentences strong.** Many learners drop their voice on the last words. In a room, those words get lost. Keep the volume to the very last syllable.
5. **Open your mouth more** than usual. Big vowels carry.

Practice sentences. Stand up, aim at the far wall, and keep the LAST word strong:

1. "Hi everyone, I'm ALEX."
2. "I work in product DESIGN."
3. "I'm originally from overseas, but I've been here for about three YEARS."
4. "Outside of work, I'm really into CLIMBING."
5. "Fun fact about me: I've never seen SNOW." (or something true for you)
6. "I'm here because I wanna meet more people in the CITY."
7. "If anyone's into board games, come find ME."
8. "That's me! Nice to meet you ALL."
9. "Should I go NEXT?"
10. "I'm looking forward to TONIGHT."

**Clip task:** search "meetup introductions go around the room", "first day class introductions video", "conference speaker introduction". Speak along with someone introducing themselves to a room, **standing up**, at their volume. Then record yourself saying sentences 1–8 and listen: can you hear the last word of every sentence clearly? Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Introducing yourself to a group

### Explanation

"Let's go around and introduce ourselves." For many learners, this is the scariest sentence in English. You have 20–30 seconds, everyone is looking, and you are also listening to the people before you (and comparing yourself). The fix is a **structure you own**, so you don't have to build it while your heart is beating fast.

**The 4-part group introduction (about 30 seconds):**

| Part | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Name | clear and strong | "Hi everyone, I'm Alex." |
| 2. Link | why you're connected to *this* group | "I'm a product designer, and I'm new to the team." / "I'm a friend of the bride from college." |
| 3. One interesting detail | something people can remember you by | "Outside of work, I'm really into climbing. Badly, but with passion." |
| 4. Hook / close | invite contact, hand over | "So if anyone knows a good climbing gym, come find me! That's me." |

**Adapt to the group.** The "link" changes everything. At a language meetup: "I'm here to practise my English and meet people." At work: your role and team. At a wedding table: how you know the couple. In a cooking class: your cooking level (a great place for a self-deprecating joke from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md)).

**Three lengths.** Have a 10-second version (name + link), a 30-second version (all four parts) and a 60-second version (add a little story or a reason). Listen to how long the people before you speak, and match them.

**Don't apologise.** "Sorry, my English is not good" makes people listen for mistakes. Just speak. If you want to mention it, make it positive: "I'm here to practise my English, so be nice!"

**Body.** Stand or sit straight, look at three different people (left, centre, right), smile at the start and at the end.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hi everyone, I'm [name]. | you start (strong, clear) | "Hi everyone, I'm Alex." |
| I work in [field] / I'm a [role] at… | you give your work link | "I'm a product designer at a fintech startup." |
| I'm here because… | you explain why you came | "I'm here because I want to practise speaking." |
| I'm originally from…, but I've been in [place] for… | your background | "I'm originally from overseas, but I've been in Chicago for two years." |
| Outside of work, I'm really into… | your hobby | "Outside of work, I'm really into baking." |
| A little bit about me: … | you start your interesting detail | "A little bit about me: I have three plants and they're all alive. Somehow." |
| Fun fact about me: … | a light, memorable detail | "Fun fact about me: I can name every country in Africa." |
| I'm looking forward to… | you finish positively | "I'm looking forward to getting to know you all." |
| If anyone's into [X], come find me. | a hook for later chats | "If anyone's into hiking, come find me." |
| That's me! | a simple, clear ending | "And that's me!" |
| Should I go next? / Who's next? | you start or hand over the round | "Okay, who's next?" |
| Nice to meet you all. | polite final line | "Nice to meet you all!" |

### Examples

**Language meetup (30 seconds).**

> "Hi everyone, I'm Alex. I'm a product designer, so I spend my days arguing about buttons. I'm here because I want to speak more English outside of work, and honestly, to meet some new people in the city. Outside of work, I'm really into climbing, very badly but with a lot of passion. So if anyone climbs, or wants to try, come find me! That's me. Nice to meet you all."

**New team at work (30 seconds, round the table).**

> **Manager:** Let's go around. Alex, wanna start?
> **You:** Sure! Hi everyone, I'm Alex. I'm the new product designer on the payments team. Before this I worked at a travel startup for four years, mostly on mobile apps. A little bit about me: I'm a huge coffee snob, so if you see me at the machine looking sad, that's why. I'm really looking forward to working with you all. Okay, who's next?

**Wedding table (10 seconds, then a question back).**

> **Guest:** So how does everyone know the couple?
> **You:** I'm Alex, I went to college with Sara. We were roommates for two years, so I have *stories*. How about you guys?

### Common mistakes

❌ "My name is Alex and I have 30 years."
✅ "I'm Alex, I'm 30." (and usually you don't need your age)
*Reason: English uses "be" for age. And in group intros, age is rarely needed.*

❌ Reading your whole CV: degrees, every job, years.
✅ Name, link, one interesting detail, hook.
*Reason: people remember one detail, not ten.*

❌ "I am working here since two years."
✅ "I've been working here for two years."
*Reason: from the past until now = present perfect + "for" with a length of time.*

❌ "Sorry, my English is not so good…"
✅ Just start: "Hi everyone, I'm Alex…"
*Reason: apologising makes people listen for mistakes. Most won't notice any.*

❌ Looking at the floor and dropping your voice at the end of sentences.
✅ Look at three people, keep the last word strong.
*Reason: the last word is often the important one (your name, your job).*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill, standing up, at "audience volume"** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Audience rules:** stand in your standing spot. Phone at face height, recording (video if possible). Imagine 10 people sitting in front of you. Look left, centre and right during each talk. Keep the last word of each sentence strong. Start every talk with "Hi everyone —" or "Okay, so —".

**Part A: Talks to the room (40 min).** Pick a topic at random and talk for **90 seconds** as if the group asked you about it. Do all 10. After every third talk, watch 20 seconds of the video: are you looking up? Can you hear the end of your sentences?

1. The city you live in now
2. A hobby you'd recommend to everyone here
3. The best advice you've ever received
4. Your favourite place to eat
5. A skill you're learning
6. Why you chose your job
7. A place everyone should visit once
8. Something most people don't know about your home country
9. A book, show or podcast that changed how you think
10. What a perfect weekend looks like for you

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Introduce yourself to a new group, and tell them about one thing you're passionate about and why."** 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute. Standing, projecting, recording. The 1-minute version should sound like a real self-introduction at a meetup: name, link, one detail with a short reason, hook, "That's me!"
