## Learning objectives

- Take a short, calm pause before answering instead of rushing or freezing.
- Give the short answer first, then one reason or example.
- Talk to the whole group, not only the person who asked.
- Check you answered, and hand the floor back.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: the thinking pause.** Confident speakers don't answer instantly. They often **repeat a key word of the question**, say a short time-buyer, **pause for one breath**, then answer. It sounds thoughtful, not lost. The voice on the time-buyer is relaxed and a bit slower; the answer starts clear and strong.

Practice sentences (/ = one-breath pause):

1. "Ooh, good question. / So the short answer is: yes."
2. "What do I miss most about home? / Honestly? / The food."
3. "Hmm, let me think how to put this. / It's kinda like…"
4. "So, two things. / First, the weather. / Second, the people."
5. "For me, personally, / I'd say it was the best decision I've made."
6. "I'm not totally sure, / but I think it's about three hours by train."
7. "That's a whole other story, / but in short: / I got lost."
8. "Does that answer your question? / Cool."
9. "Anyone else? / I don't wanna hog the mic."
10. "Why design? / Okay, so. / When I was a kid, I used to redraw the menus at restaurants."

**Clip task:** search "audience Q&A casual panel", "podcast live show audience questions", "interview answers thoughtful pause". Speak along with someone answering a question in front of people. Notice what they say in the first three seconds. Copy it. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Answering questions in public

### Explanation

Answering one friend's question is easy. Answering the same question when ten people go quiet and turn to look at you is not. Your heart speeds up, your brain goes blank, and you either freeze or start talking very fast without a clear point. The cure is a tiny routine you can run on autopilot:

**The PASH routine:**

| Step | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **P**ause (and repeat) | repeat a key word of the question or use a time-buyer; breathe | "What do I miss most? Ooh, good question." |
| **A**nswer short | give the short answer in one sentence first | "Honestly? The food." |
| **S**upport | one reason, example or tiny story | "Like, my mom's soup on Sunday. Nothing here comes close." |
| **H**and back | check, or pass the floor | "How about you guys? What do you miss when you travel?" |

**Short answer first.** Many learners start with background ("So, when I was young, my family…") and the group loses the point. Give the answer, then explain. It's like a newspaper headline.

**Talk to the room.** Look at the person who asked when you start, then look around the group while you explain, and back to the asker at the end.

**If you don't know:** say so briefly and offer something ([Week 11 · Day 3](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-3-talking-when-youre-unsure.md)): "I'm not totally sure, but I think…" or "No idea, honestly! Maybe Sam knows?"

**If the question is too personal:** smile and deflect lightly: "Ha, that's a story for another day!" Then give a small, safe answer or change the topic.

**If you didn't hear it:** ask, like in [Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/day-4-asking-people-to-repeat.md): "Sorry, could you say that again? It's a bit loud."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ooh, good question. | buy one second, show interest | "Ooh, good question. Let me think." |
| So the short answer is… | you give the headline first | "So the short answer is: no, but I'd like to." |
| Honestly? … | a short, honest answer | "Do I like it here? Honestly? I love it." |
| Let me think how to put this. | you need a moment to phrase it | "Let me think how to put this. It's not easy, but it's worth it." |
| So, two things. | you organise your answer | "So, two things. First, the price. Second, the location." |
| For me, personally,… | your own view | "For me, personally, the best part is the people." |
| I'd say… | a softer answer | "I'd say about three years." |
| I'm not totally sure, but… | you don't know for certain | "I'm not totally sure, but I think it opens at nine." |
| That's a whole other story, but in short… | the full answer is too long | "That's a whole other story, but in short: I missed the flight." |
| Ha, that's a story for another day! | you don't want to answer | "Why did I leave that job? Ha, that's a story for another day!" |
| Does that answer your question? | you check | "So yeah, that's why. Does that answer your question?" |
| Anyone else? I don't wanna hog the mic. | you hand the floor back | "Anyway, that's me. Anyone else? I don't wanna hog the mic." |

### Examples

**At a language meetup, the host asks you in front of the group.**

> **Host:** Alex, you've lived here two years now. What surprised you most?
> **You:** Ooh, good question. / So the short answer is: how friendly people are with strangers. Like, the first week, a guy in the supermarket told me his whole life story. In the cereal aisle.
> *(laughs)*
> **You:** At home, that would never happen. So yeah, that's the big one. How about you guys? Anyone else had that?

**At a dinner, a question you don't want to answer fully.**

> **Uncle Ray:** So, Alex, when are you getting married?
> **You:** Ha, that's a story for another day! / For now, I'm married to my job. And my plants.
> *(laughs)*
> **You:** But speaking of weddings, Mia, how was your cousin's?

**In a class, a question you're not sure about.**

> **Teacher:** Does anyone know how long it takes to get to the coast from here?
> **You:** I'm not totally sure, but I think it's about two hours by car. Maybe three with traffic. Does anyone drive there often?
> **Classmate:** Yeah, two and a half, usually.
> **You:** Okay, so I was close!

**Two-part answer to a "why" question.**

> **Guest:** Why did you move here?
> **You:** So, two things. / First, work. I got a job offer at a design studio. And second, honestly, I wanted an adventure. I was a bit bored at home. / So yeah, job and adventure. Does that answer your question?

### Common mistakes

❌ Answering instantly at top speed, with no point.
✅ Pause, repeat a key word, then give the short answer.
*Reason: one second of pause sounds confident; rushing sounds nervous.*

❌ Starting with long background: "So when I was a child, my father worked in…"
✅ "So the short answer is: my dad. He was a carpenter." Then explain.
*Reason: groups need the headline first.*

❌ Looking only at the person who asked.
✅ Start with them, look around the group, finish with them.
*Reason: everyone is your audience.*

❌ "I will answer to your question."
✅ "To answer your question…" / just answer.
*Reason: "answer" takes no "to" ("answer the question").*

❌ "Sorry, sorry, I don't know, my English…"
✅ "I'm not totally sure, but I think…" / "No idea, honestly!"
*Reason: not knowing a fact isn't a language problem. Keep it light.*

❌ Talking for five minutes and never handing back.
✅ "Anyone else? I don't wanna hog the mic."
*Reason: a clear hand-back makes you look relaxed and generous.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, questions "in front of an audience"

Tonight Claude plays a **host and an audience**. You stand up and answer questions as if a room is watching. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read, then stand up:

```
Let's practise answering questions in front of an audience. You are the HOST of a small
community meetup ("New in the City" night, about 20 people). I'm the guest of the evening:
someone who moved to this city two years ago and works as a product designer.
Describe the room briefly at the start (people sitting, a microphone), then interview me.
After a few host questions, let different audience members ask questions (give each one a name
and a personality: shy, funny, nosy, super detailed). Include:
- 3 easy questions, 3 "why" questions, 2 questions I probably can't answer (facts),
- 1 slightly too personal question, 1 question that's long and confusing,
- 1 question asked quietly that I might not hear.
Sometimes describe the audience reacting (laughing, nodding, silence).
Don't correct me until I say "feedback". Then tell me: did I pause before answering,
give the short answer first, add one example, and hand back? Up to 5 language mistakes.
```

**Step 2 (25 min).** Answer **at least 10 questions** with the PASH routine (Pause, Answer short, Support, Hand back). Stand up, project. Targets:
- A pause or time-buyer before every answer (never "umm" first).
- The short answer in the first sentence every time.
- "I'm not totally sure, but…" for the fact questions.
- "Ha, that's a story for another day!" for the personal one.
- A clarification for the confusing or quiet question ("Sorry, could you say that again?" / "So you're asking if…?").

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Write it down.

**Step 4 (20 min).** Round two. Say: *"Now I'm at a dinner party with 8 people, and the host keeps turning to me with questions in front of everyone: about my job, my country, my opinion on the food, and one awkward question. Faster pace."* Same routine.

**Step 5 (8 min).** Ask Claude: *"Give me 5 more questions, fast, one after another. I'll answer each in under 30 seconds."* Do it standing up.
