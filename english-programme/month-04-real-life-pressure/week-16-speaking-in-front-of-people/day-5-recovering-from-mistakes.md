## Learning objectives

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: a steady voice after a slip.** When speakers make a mistake in public, the mistake isn't the problem. The **change in their voice** is: they speed up, go quiet, or their voice goes high and shaky. Confident speakers do the opposite: after a slip, they **pause, breathe, and go a little slower and lower** for the next sentence. Practise that reset.

Practice sentences (say the slip, then **pause** /, then the fix slower and steadier):

1. "We went there on Tuesday — / sorry, Thursday. Thursday."
2. "It was really embarrassed — / embarrassing. Words are hard tonight."
3. "The project took three years — / three *months*. Felt like years, though."
4. "Okay, my brain just left the building. / Where was I? / Oh, right — the hotel."
5. "Let me start that sentence again. / So, the main reason is…"
6. "Ha, that came out wrong. / What I mean is: I love your cooking."
7. "Thank you — / yes, that's the word. 'Commute'."
8. "Bear with me a sec. / Okay. / So."
9. "Okay, let's pretend that didn't happen. / Anyway!"
10. "So, the point is: / it was worth it."

**Clip task:** search "speaker forgets what to say on stage", "news presenter mistake recovery", "live TV blooper recovery" (friendly ones). Watch how professionals recover. Then speak along with a calm speaker for 3 minutes, focusing on keeping your voice low and steady. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Recovering from mistakes

### Explanation

Everyone makes mistakes when speaking in front of people. Native speakers say the wrong word, forget names, lose their train of thought, and mispronounce things all the time. **The audience remembers how you recover, not the mistake.** A calm, quick recovery makes you look *more* confident than if you'd made no mistake at all.

The biggest danger for learners is not the mistake but the **spiral**: you make one mistake, you think "my English is terrible", you get nervous, you make more mistakes, and you stop. Tonight you learn to break the spiral in one sentence.

**Five types of public mistake, and what to do:**

| Mistake | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong word / wrong fact | correct quickly, keep going | "On Tuesday — sorry, Thursday." |
| Grammar slip | usually ignore it! Only fix if it changes the meaning | (Nobody noticed "he go". Keep going.) |
| Blank mind | name it lightly, pause, go back | "Okay, my brain just left the building. Where was I? Oh, right…" |
| Can't find a word | paraphrase ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-1-paraphrasing.md)) or ask the room | "The thing you… what's the word… the long chair? Sofa! Thank you." |
| Something came out wrong / awkward | laugh lightly, rephrase | "Ha, that came out wrong. What I mean is…" |

**Someone corrects you.** Say thanks and use it: "Oh, *commute*, thank you. Yeah, my commute is terrible." Don't argue, don't apologise five times. A correction from a friend is help, not an attack.

**People laugh at your mistake.** Laugh with them. It's usually friendly. One light line ("Okay, let's pretend that didn't happen") and move on. This uses your [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/day-4-teasing-and-taking-a-joke.md) skill of taking a joke.

**The reset.** After any mistake: **pause, breathe, slower, then "So…"** and your main point. "So, the point is…" is a great way to get back on track because it skips the confusing part.

**What not to do:** don't stop and say "Sorry, sorry, my English is so bad." It turns a one-second slip into a two-minute topic, and the room starts worrying about you.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sorry — let me say that again. | you said it badly | "Sorry — let me say that again. The meeting is at two." |
| Wow, okay, words are hard tonight. | a light joke after a slip | "It was very… embarrassed? Embarrassing. Wow, words are hard tonight." |
| Where was I? Oh, right — | you lost your place | "Where was I? Oh, right — the hotel." |
| Let me start that sentence again. | your sentence got tangled | "Let me start that sentence again. So the main idea is…" |
| Okay, my brain just left the building. | your mind went blank (with a smile) | "Okay, my brain just left the building. One sec." |
| Thank you — yes, that's the word. | someone gives you the word | "The… the… 'commute'! Thank you — yes, that's the word." |
| Ha, that came out wrong. What I mean is… | you said something awkward | "Ha, that came out wrong. What I mean is, I'm *impressed* you cooked." |
| Bear with me a sec. | you need a moment | "Bear with me a sec, I had a great example…" |
| Anyway! | a cheerful reset after a stumble | "Anyway! Back to the story." |
| Okay, let's pretend that didn't happen. | a funny recovery after a clear mistake | "I mean Ana, not Anna's dog. Okay, let's pretend that didn't happen." |
| Good catch, thanks. | someone points out a mistake | "Oh, good catch, thanks — it's fifteen, not fifty." |
| So, the point is… | you skip back to your main idea | "So, the point is: book early." |

### Examples

**Wrong fact during a toast.**

> "…and I've known Sara for ten years — / sorry, twelve. Twelve years. Sara, don't look at me like that. / Anyway! Twelve years, and she's never once been late. Except today."
> *(laughs)*

**Mind goes blank answering a question at a meetup.**

> **Guest:** What's the best thing about your job?
> **You:** Ooh, so the best thing is… / okay, my brain just left the building. / Bear with me a sec. / Right — the people. Honestly, the people. My team's amazing. So, the point is: the job's hard, but the people make it fun.

**Someone corrects you, and you use it.**

> **You:** My, uh, my daily travel to work is, like, an hour.
> **Jess:** Your commute?
> **You:** Yes! Commute. Thank you — yes, that's the word. My commute is an hour, which, honestly, is too long.

**Something comes out wrong.**

> **Host:** So, how was my lasagna?
> **You:** It was surprisingly good!
> *(silence, laughs)*
> **You:** Ha, that came out wrong. What I mean is, it was *amazing*. I was surprised because you said you can't cook. / Okay, let's pretend that didn't happen. It was the best lasagna I've had this year.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Sorry, sorry, sorry, my English is so bad, sorry…"
✅ "Sorry — let me say that again." (once, then continue)
*Reason: one short fix ends the mistake; many apologies make it the topic.*

❌ Standing in silence for ten seconds, panicking.
✅ "Bear with me a sec." / "Where was I? Oh, right —"
*Reason: saying something (even a time-buyer) keeps the room with you.*

❌ Explaining the mistake: "I said Tuesday because in my language the days are…"
✅ "Thursday, sorry." and keep going.
*Reason: nobody needs the reason. They just need the right word.*

❌ Arguing with a correction: "No, I think 'daily travel' is also correct…"
✅ "Oh, commute — thank you."
*Reason: accept, use, move on. Discuss words later if you're curious.*

❌ Fixing every small grammar slip in public.
✅ Fix only mistakes that change the meaning (wrong day, wrong number, wrong name).
*Reason: constant fixing breaks your flow. Most small slips go unnoticed.*

❌ Speeding up after a mistake.
✅ Pause, breathe, go slower and lower.
*Reason: speed shows panic; a steady voice shows control.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill with planted mistakes** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Planted mistakes (40 min).** Before each topic, roll a dice (or pick a number 1–6 without looking) to choose a **planted mistake**. Somewhere in your 90-second talk, make that mistake **on purpose**, then recover with a phrase from tonight and carry on. Standing, projecting, recording. Do all 10 topics.

| Dice | Planted mistake |
|---|---|
| 1 | Say a wrong day or number, then correct it. |
| 2 | Stop mid-sentence as if your mind went blank, for 3 seconds. Recover. |
| 3 | "Forget" a common word and paraphrase it. |
| 4 | Say something that "comes out wrong", then rephrase it with a laugh. |
| 5 | Lose your place completely, then use "Where was I? Oh, right —". |
| 6 | Imagine someone corrects you. Say "Oh, [word] — thank you" and use the word. |

Topics:
1. Your first day at a new job
2. A city you'd love to live in
3. A meal you can cook well
4. Something you changed your mind about
5. A friend who influenced you
6. The best event you've been to
7. A tradition from your family or country
8. Something you'd like to learn this year
9. A place where you feel calm
10. What you've learnt about yourself this month

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Tell the story of the most embarrassing moment you've had in front of other people, and how you got through it."** 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute (standing, recording). Use timing from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/day-3-funny-story-timing.md) and group-story phrases from [Day 2](day-2-telling-stories-to-a-group.md). If you make a real mistake while telling it, even better: recover and keep going.
