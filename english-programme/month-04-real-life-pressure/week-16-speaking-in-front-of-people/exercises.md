# Week 16 exercises: Speaking in front of people
[Week 16 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-introducing-yourself-to-a-group.md) · [Day 2](day-2-telling-stories-to-a-group.md) · [Day 3](day-3-toasts-and-updates.md) · [Day 4](day-4-answering-questions-in-public.md) · [Day 5](day-5-recovering-from-mistakes.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** this week, **stand up** for everything, in your standing spot. Speak at "room volume" ([projection](day-1-introducing-yourself-to-a-group.md#1-shadowing-15-min)), aim at the far wall, and look at three imaginary people (left, centre, right). Record video when you can: watching yourself once is worth ten readings. Model answers use the practice identity "Alex", a product designer who moved to a new city two years ago. Make your own answers true for you.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Three lengths
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** write keywords, then say out loud and record

1. Fill in the planning frame with **keywords only** (no full sentences).
2. Say your **10-second**, **30-second** and **60-second** introductions out loud, standing, recording each.
3. Do each length three times. By the third time, don't look at your notes.
4. Compare with the models.

| Part | Your keywords |
|---|---|
| Name | |
| Link (to this group) | |
| One interesting detail | |
| Extra story or reason (60s only) | |
| Hook / close | |

**Model, 10 seconds:**
> "Hi everyone, I'm Alex. I'm a product designer, and I'm new to the group. Nice to meet you all!"

**Model, 30 seconds:**
> "Hi everyone, I'm Alex. I'm a product designer at a fintech company, so I spend my days arguing about buttons. I'm here because I want to meet people outside of work. Outside of work, I'm really into climbing — badly, but with a lot of passion. So if anyone climbs, come find me! That's me."

**Model, 60 seconds:**
> "Hi everyone, I'm Alex. I'm originally from overseas, but I've been in the city for about two years now. I work as a product designer at a fintech company, which basically means I try to make banking apps less painful. A little bit about me: I started climbing last year because a coworker dragged me to the gym, and now I go three times a week. I'm still terrible, but I've made most of my friends here through climbing, so it's kind of my thing now. I'm here tonight because I want to meet people outside of work and outside of the gym! So if anyone wants a climbing buddy, or just knows a good coffee place, come find me. That's me. Nice to meet you all."

### Exercise 1.2: Change the room
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, standing, 30 seconds each

Give a 30-second introduction for each group. Change the **link** and choose a **detail** that fits the room.

1. A language exchange meetup (most people are learning languages).
2. Your first day on a new team at work, around the meeting table.
3. A cooking class for beginners.
4. A wedding table where you don't know anyone (you're a friend of the groom).
5. A running club's first group run.
6. An online community call for designers (camera on, 15 people).

### Exercise 1.3: Fun-fact bank
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

A good "interesting detail" is short, true and easy to ask about. Answer each prompt out loud with a **one-sentence fun fact** about you, starting with "Fun fact about me:" or "A little bit about me:". Choose your best three and log them.

1. Something unusual you can do.
2. A place you've been that most people haven't.
3. A strange job or task you've done.
4. Something you collect or used to collect.
5. A food you love that others find weird.
6. A goal you have this year.
7. Something you're terrible at (self-deprecating!).
8. A famous person you've met or seen.
9. A hobby you started recently.
10. A habit your friends tease you about.
11. The number of countries or cities you've lived in.
12. Something surprising about your hometown.

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Group-ify the story
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, standing, record the final version

This story is written for one listener, in a flat way. Tell it to an imaginary group of four (Leo, Maya, Sam, Priya). Add: a group opening, a "So picture this", two pauses for effect, a name ("Sam, you were there"), a "Hold on, it gets worse", a clear ending and a pass. Then compare with the model.

> "Last summer I went camping with Sam. We forgot the tent poles. We tried to make a tent with sticks, but it didn't work. Then it started raining. We slept in the car. In the morning, a family next to us offered us breakfast. They had a huge tent with a kitchen. We stayed with them for two days. Now we're friends with them."

### Exercise 2.2: Handle the room
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, 3-second limit

You're telling a story to a group. Someone says each line below. Answer within 3 seconds, then **continue the story** with one more sentence (any story you like).

1. "Wait, is this the same guy from last time?"
2. "Oh no, I know where this is going."
3. "Hang on, my food's here — keep going, I'm listening!"
4. "That happened to me too!"
5. *(Two people start a side conversation.)*
6. "No way. You're making this up."
7. "Where was this?"
8. *(Everyone laughs loudly at something you said.)*
9. "Okay, but how did it end?"
10. "Wait, who's Marco?"

### Exercise 2.3: Land it and pass it on
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

Each story ending is weak. Say a strong ending (a final line with a pause before it) and a pass to the group.

1. "…and then we got home. And that's it."
2. "…so, yeah, I lost my phone. Umm. Yeah."
3. "…and he still doesn't know it was me. So… yeah… okay."
4. "…in the end the cake was fine. I think that's all."
5. "…then my boss laughed and it was okay. So that's my story."
6. "…and I never went back to that hairdresser. Anyway, umm."
7. "…and now I always check the date on tickets. The end, I guess."
8. "…and the dog ate the whole sandwich. So."

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Read the model toasts
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud, standing, cup in hand

Read each toast out loud **twice**: once to learn it, once with pauses (/), a smile in your voice, and a slow final line. Notice the five parts (attention, connection, quality + story, occasion, wish + glass).

**Model toast A: the team after a launch**
> "Okay, everyone, can I have your attention for a sec? I'll keep this short, I know the pizza's getting cold. / Six months ago, this app was a sketch on a whiteboard. A very ugly sketch. Mine. / And this team made it real. I'll never forget the night before the launch, when Marcus found a bug at 11 p.m., and instead of panicking, Priya just said, 'Okay. Who wants coffee?' And we fixed it by midnight. / That's this team. Calm, kind, and a little bit crazy. / So please, raise your glasses — or your pizza. / To the team! Cheers!"

**Model toast B: parents' anniversary (family dinner)**
> "Hi everyone, can I say a few words? / Thirty years ago today, my mom and dad got married. In the rain. With a borrowed car and a very small cake. / One thing everyone knows about my parents is that they still laugh at each other's jokes. Even the bad ones. Especially the bad ones. / Mom, Dad, thank you for showing us what a team looks like. We love you. / So please, raise your glasses. / To Mom and Dad! Happy anniversary!"

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Short answer first
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, standing, 30 seconds each

Imagine the room goes quiet and everyone looks at you. Answer each question with **PASH**: Pause (repeat a key word or use a time-buyer), Answer short (one sentence), Support (one reason or example), Hand back. Then read the model for the first six and compare.

1. "What do you miss most about home?"
2. "Why did you choose design?"
3. "What's the best thing about living here?"
4. "What's something you'd change about your city?"
5. "What's the hardest part of learning a language?"
6. "If you could live anywhere, where would it be?"
7. "What's the best trip you've ever taken?"
8. "What's something you're proud of this year?"
9. "What do you do to relax?"
10. "Would you ever go back to live in your home country?"
11. "What's the most useful app on your phone?"
12. "What's one piece of advice you'd give someone new to the city?"

### Exercise 4.2: Tricky questions
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud, standing

These questions are hard in different ways. Answer each one in front of your imaginary audience.

1. (A fact you don't know) "What's the population of your home city?"
2. (Too personal) "How much do designers earn, like, exactly?"
3. (Long and confusing) "So, I was wondering, because my cousin, well, she's thinking about moving here, and she's not sure about, like, the neighbourhoods, or whether it's better to rent first, or, you know, how did you decide?"
4. (Quiet, you didn't hear) "*(mumbled)* …the best place for…?"
5. (Two questions in one) "What's your job and do you like it?"
6. (A yes/no that needs more) "Do you like it here?"
7. (Opinion about the host) "So, honestly, what do you think of my cooking?"
8. (A bit challenging) "Isn't design just making things pretty?"

5. "What's your dream job?"

8. "What's a skill you wish you had?"
9. "What's your favourite season and why?"

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Recovery scenarios
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, standing

You're speaking to a group. Each mistake happens. Say your recovery (one short sentence), **pause, breathe**, then continue with one more sentence in a slower, steadier voice.

1. You say "fifty people" but you mean "fifteen".
2. Your mind goes completely blank in the middle of a sentence.
3. You can't remember the word for "ladder".
4. You call your friend Mia "Maya" in a toast.
5. You say "I'm so exciting" instead of "I'm so excited". People smile.
6. You say "Your presentation was surprisingly good" to a colleague. It sounds rude.
7. You lose your place in a story because someone interrupted.
8. You mispronounce a word and someone laughs kindly.
9. You start a sentence, it gets too complicated, and you're lost in it.
10. You say the wrong city: "When I lived in Boston — no, Chicago…"
11. You knock over your glass during a toast.
12. Someone says, "Sorry, I didn't understand that," after your answer.

### Exercise 5.2: Blank-mind drill
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, standing, recording

Start each sentence below, then **stop at the "…"** and stay silent for **3 full seconds** (count in your head). Then recover with a phrase from [Day 5](day-5-recovering-from-mistakes.md) and finish the idea. The goal is to get used to the silence, so it doesn't scare you in real life.

1. "The best thing about my job is…"
2. "When I first moved here, the hardest thing was…"
3. "My favourite place in the city is…"
4. "The reason I started learning English seriously was…"
5. "If I could change one thing about my week, it would be…"
6. "The funniest person I know is…"
7. "Last weekend I…"
8. "Something people don't know about me is…"
9. "The most important thing in a team is…"
10. "Next year, I really want to…"

### Exercise 5.3: Accept the correction
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud (two voices)

A friend corrects you in front of the group. Say thanks, **use the correct word in a new sentence**, and continue.

1. **You:** "I'm very boring at work today." **Friend:** "Bored?"
2. **You:** "We went to the beach in Sunday." **Friend:** "On Sunday."
3. **You:** "My daily trip to work is long." **Friend:** "Your commute?"
4. **You:** "She's a very sympathetic person, always smiling." **Friend:** "Oh, you mean nice? Friendly?"
5. **You:** "I made a party for my birthday." **Friend:** "You had a party? You threw a party?"
6. **You:** "The meeting is at fifty past two — I mean, the fifteenth." **Friend:** "Wait, the date or the time?"
7. **You:** "I'm living here since two years." **Friend:** "You've been living here for two years!"
8. **You:** "I have to take a decision about the job." **Friend:** "Make a decision?"

2. Say one toast final line: "So please, raise your glasses to…"
3. Say "So the short answer is…" and answer: "What did you do this month?"
4. Say three recovery phrases: "Sorry — let me say that again.", "Where was I? Oh, right —", "So, the point is…"
5. Remind yourself: the audience remembers how you recover, not the mistake.
