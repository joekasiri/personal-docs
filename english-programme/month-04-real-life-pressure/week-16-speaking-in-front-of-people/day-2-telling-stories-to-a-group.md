## Learning objectives

- Use pauses for effect so a group stays with you.
- Include the whole group: eye contact, questions, mentioning people who were there.
- Handle reactions and interruptions from several listeners.
- Finish clearly and pass the stage to someone else.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: pausing for effect.** Good group storytellers pause in three places:

1. **After the opening hook** ("So, you guys know I hate flying, right? / Okay.") — to get everyone's attention.
2. **Before the big moment** (the beat from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/day-3-funny-story-timing.md)).
3. **After the big moment** — to let people react (laugh, gasp) before going on.

A pause feels very long to the speaker (it's usually only one or two seconds). To the listeners, it feels exciting. Don't fill it with "umm". Breathe, look at the group, and wait.

Practice sentences (/ = pause; project your voice like Day 1):

1. "Okay, so you guys know I hate flying, right? / Okay."
2. "So picture this. / Two a.m. / An empty airport."
3. "And then the pilot comes out / and he says / 'Is there a designer on the plane?'"
4. "And I'm sitting there like / ...me?"
5. "Hold on, it gets worse. /"
6. "And this is the best part — / he knew my mom."
7. "Sam, you were there — / back me up."
8. "Anyway. / That's why I don't fly at night anymore."
9. "So yeah. / That was my weekend. / Who's got a better one?"
10. "You had to be there. / But trust me. / It was amazing."

**Clip task:** search "live storytelling event funny", "story slam audience laughing", "telling a story at a dinner party". Speak along with a speaker telling a story to a live audience, and **stop where the speaker pauses** for a laugh. Count: how many seconds does each pause last? Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Telling stories to a group

### Explanation

Telling a story to one friend is a conversation. Telling it to four or five people is a small performance. A few things change:

1. **Get the floor.** In a group, you need a clear start so everyone turns to you: "Okay, so you guys are gonna love this." "Has anyone ever been stuck in an elevator? Okay, so…" (This is a bigger version of your [Week 13 jump-ins](../week-13-group-conversations/day-1-jumping-in.md).)
2. **Look at everyone.** Move your eyes around the group. Many people tell the whole story to one person and the others feel left out.
3. **Include people.** Mention people who were there ("Sam, back me up"), ask small questions ("You know that bridge downtown?"), and react to reactions ("Right?!").
4. **Handle several reactions.** People will laugh, guess, ask questions, or start side comments. Short replies keep you going: "Ha, I wish!", "I'm getting there!", "Good question — so…". Use your [Week 13 holding phrases](../week-13-group-conversations/day-3-holding-your-turn.md).
5. **Shorter is better.** In groups, attention is shorter. Aim for 1–2 minutes. Cut details that don't help the story.
6. **Land it and pass it on.** End clearly ("So yeah. That was my weekend.") and invite others: "Okay, who's got a worse one?" This keeps the group going, and makes people like your stories even more.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Okay, so you guys are gonna love this. | you take the floor for a story | "Okay, so you guys are gonna love this. Remember Leo's car?" |
| Has anyone ever…? Okay, so… | you open with a group question | "Has anyone ever been locked out on a balcony? Okay, so…" |
| So picture this… | you set a scene for everyone | "So picture this: 6 a.m., pouring rain, no umbrella." |
| You guys know [X], right? | you check shared knowledge | "You guys know that taco truck by the station, right?" |
| Hold on, it gets worse. | you protect the build | "Hold on, it gets worse. Then my phone dies." |
| Okay, okay, I'm getting there. | people want the ending now | "Okay, okay, I'm getting there!" |
| And this is the best part — | you signal the big moment | "And this is the best part — he was the manager." |
| [Name], you were there — back me up. | you bring a witness in | "Sam, you were there — back me up. It was huge." |
| You had to be there, but… | the story is funnier in real life | "You had to be there, but it was the funniest thing ever." |
| Anyway, that's why I don't… | a neat ending with a lesson | "Anyway, that's why I don't go camping anymore." |
| So yeah. That was my weekend. | a simple, clear ending | "So yeah. That was my weekend. How about you guys?" |
| Okay, who's got a better one? | you pass the stage | "Okay, that's my worst date. Who's got a worse one?" |

### Examples

**Dinner with four friends (you, Leo, Maya, Sam, Priya).**

> **You:** Okay, so you guys are gonna love this. Has anyone ever been locked out on a balcony?
> **Maya:** No…
> **You:** Okay, so picture this. Saturday morning. I'm on my balcony in my pajamas, coffee in my hand, feeling amazing. And the door — / *click*.
> **Leo:** No!
> **You:** Locked. From the inside. Phone inside. Keys inside.
> **Priya:** Oh my god, what did you do?
> **You:** Okay, okay, I'm getting there. So I'm on the third floor, right? And my neighbour downstairs is outside, watering her plants. And this is the best part — / she's never spoken to me. In two years.
> **Sam:** (laughing) Oh no.
> **You:** So I have to shout, "Excuse me! Hi! I'm your neighbour! I'm stuck!" In my pajamas. Sam, you've seen those pajamas — back me up.
> **Sam:** They have ducks on them.
> **You:** They have ducks on them. / Anyway, she called the building manager, and now she waves at me every day. So yeah. Best thing that ever happened to me. Okay, who's got a worse one?
> **Maya:** Okay, so one time…

**A meetup: the host asks you to share.**

> **Host:** Alex, you said you had a crazy travel story?
> **You:** Oh, yes. Okay. So, you guys know how airports announce "last call"? / I've never heard my own name in one before. Until Lisbon.
> *(laughs)*
> **You:** Hold on, it gets worse. I'm running, I get to the gate, and the gate is… / empty. They changed the gate. And they didn't announce it. In English.
> **Guest:** Did you make it?
> **You:** Good question. So I ask a cleaner, and she just points and says "Run." / And I ran. Made it with, like, thirty seconds. Anyway, that's why I now sit *at* the gate two hours early. Has anyone else had that?

### Common mistakes

❌ Telling the whole story to one person while the others watch.
✅ Move your eyes around everyone; ask the group small questions.
*Reason: people who aren't looked at stop listening.*

❌ Filling every pause with "umm".
✅ Pause in silence, look at the group, breathe.
*Reason: a silent pause sounds confident; "umm" sounds lost.*

❌ Ignoring reactions and talking straight through the laughs.
✅ Wait for the laugh to finish, react ("Right?!"), then go on.
*Reason: if you talk over the laugh, people miss your next line.*

❌ A five-minute story with every detail.
✅ 1–2 minutes: hook, build, big moment, ending.
*Reason: groups lose attention faster than one listener.*

❌ Ending with "…and that's it." (flat, quiet)
✅ "So yeah. That was my weekend. Who's got a worse one?"
*Reason: a clear ending tells people they can laugh, react, or take a turn.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, an audience of three

Tonight Claude plays **three listeners** in a group. They react, interrupt, ask questions and sometimes start side chats. You tell stories and keep everyone with you. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Stand up during the stories.

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read:

```
You're three friends listening to me tell stories at a dinner: MIA (laughs loudly, reacts a lot),
JAKE (guesses the ending and interrupts), and RENEE (asks detail questions). Say each name before their line.
Let me tell a story, and react like a real group: laugh, gasp, interrupt 2-3 times per story,
ask questions, and sometimes have a short side comment between yourselves so I have to bring everyone back.
After each story, one of you should say "Okay, my turn" and tell a SHORT story (30 seconds) so I can react too.
Don't correct me until I say "feedback". Then tell me: did I include everyone, did I pause for effect,
did I handle interruptions, did I end clearly? Plus up to 5 language mistakes.
I'll start with my first story.
```

**Step 2 (20 min).** Tell your **two prepared stories** (1–2 minutes each), then **two unprepared ones** from this list (choose quickly):
- "The worst trip of your life"
- "A time you met someone in a strange place"
- "The most awkward thing you've said at work"
- "Something that went wrong at a party"

Targets for each story: a group opening, at least two pauses for effect, at least one "Hold on, it gets worse" or "I'm getting there", one name mentioned ("Mia, you'd love this part"), a clear ending, and a pass ("Who's got a better one?").

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Write it down.

**Step 4 (25 min).** Round two. Say: *"Same three people, but now we're at a work lunch with my new team, and you don't know me well yet."* Tell two stories that suit a work lunch (a travel story, a funny moment from a past job). Notice how you change your tone and topics for people you don't know well.

**Step 5 (8 min).** Record your best story of the night once more, standing up, outside voice mode, in under 2 minutes.
