# Week 16: Speaking in front of people
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 4: Real-life pressure](../README.md) · [← Week 15](../week-15-calls/README.md) · [Week 17 →](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-17-meetings/README.md)

## This week's goal

This is the highest-pressure week of the month: everyone is looking at you, and it's your turn. Going around the table to introduce yourself, telling a story while five people listen, raising a glass for a friend's birthday, answering a question at a meetup while the room goes quiet, and making a mistake in the middle of it all. This week you train a **stronger voice** (projection), **pauses that make people listen**, short **structures** you can use on autopilot, and calm ways to **recover** when something goes wrong. The goal is not to give perfect speeches. It is to stay relaxed and keep talking when people are watching. At the end of the week you do the **Month 4 check**.

## How this builds on last week

[Week 15](../week-15-calls/README.md) took away faces and gestures. This week gives you *many* faces, all looking at you. The clear, slightly slower voice you used on the phone for names and numbers is the start of a good "group voice". You will also bring together the whole month: group skills from [Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/README.md) (handing over, holding your turn), timing and humour from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md), and older tools like your [sixty-second answers](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-1-sixty-second-answers.md) and [self-correction](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-2-self-correction.md).

## Before you start

- **Find an audience.** For Day 3 and Day 6 you need a situation where you speak to 3+ people at once. Ideas: a language exchange or meetup where people introduce themselves; a dinner where you can raise a glass; a team lunch where you give a short update; a club or class; an online group call. Ask a group of friends to be your "audience" for 10 minutes if nothing else works.
- **A standing spot.** You'll practise standing up, at full volume. Choose a room where you won't feel shy. Put your phone on a shelf at face height and record video if you can: you'll see your eye contact and posture.
- **Shadowing clips:** search "wedding toast best man speech funny" (pick friendly ones), "short toast at dinner", "storytelling event live audience", "meetup speaker introduction", "Q&A after a talk audience questions". You want live speakers with a real audience.
- Have your Month 3 numbers and your Week 1 baseline ready for the [monthly check](../../toolkit/monthly-check.md).

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes, then rest.** Watch or listen to a live storytelling event or a talk with a real audience (search "live storytelling event true stories", "story slam", "short talk with audience Q&A"). Your task: notice **three pauses** a speaker uses (before a funny line, before an important point, after a question) and **one moment** when the speaker made a small mistake or lost their place, and how they carried on. Write them in your phrase log. Then rest: you've finished Month 4.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Introduce yourself to a group in 30 seconds, with a strong voice and one interesting detail.
- [ ] Tell a story to 3+ people, include them, and let reactions land.
- [ ] Give a 1–2 minute toast or update with a clear structure and a final line.
- [ ] Answer a question in public: pause, short answer first, one example, hand back.
- [ ] Recover from a mistake in one short sentence and carry on.
- [ ] Complete the Month 4 check and compare with your baseline.
