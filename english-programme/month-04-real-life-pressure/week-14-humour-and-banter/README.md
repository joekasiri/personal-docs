# Week 14: Humour and banter
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 4: Real-life pressure](../README.md) · [← Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/README.md) · [Week 15 →](../week-15-calls/README.md)

## This week's goal

Humour is where many confident B2 speakers suddenly feel like beginners. Someone says "Oh, *great*" and you are not sure if it is good news. Friends tease each other and you do not know if you should laugh or defend yourself. A funny story falls flat because the punchline came too fast. This week you learn the everyday, friendly humour of American casual conversation: **sarcasm and tone**, **exaggeration**, **timing in funny stories**, **teasing and taking a joke**, and **reacting to jokes**. The goal is not to become a comedian. It is to understand the jokes in real time, laugh along naturally, and add a little humour of your own without thinking. All humour in this week is kind: about situations and ourselves, never about who people are.

## How this builds on last week

[Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/README.md) gave you the group moves: jumping in, handing over, holding your turn, asking for repeats and building on ideas. Banter *is* fast group talk, so you will use all of those moves this week. Building on ideas ("Yeah, and…") becomes building on a joke. You will also recycle storytelling from Month 2, especially [endings and punchlines](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-4-endings-and-punchlines.md) and [building suspense](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-2-building-suspense.md), and the intensifiers from [Week 4 · Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md).

## Before you start

- Book live conversation #1 (Day 3) and #2 (Day 6). Try for one group again: a games night, a pub quiz or a hobby meetup is perfect for banter.
- Find shadowing clips for the week: search "comedy podcast friends laughing", "sitcom-style improv chat", "funny storytime podcast", "sarcastic comedian chat show clip", "friends roasting each other podcast" (pick friendly, clean ones). You want people laughing *while* they talk.
- Have one or two real funny stories from your life in mind (a travel fail, a cooking disaster, a work mix-up). You will use them on Days 3 and 4.
- Recording app, [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md), [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), [voice mode](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes, then rest.** Listen to a light comedy podcast or a "storytime" episode where friends tell funny stories (search "funny storytime podcast", "comedians tell embarrassing stories"). Your only task: **catch the sarcasm and the pauses.** Each time you hear sarcasm, say quietly "sarcasm". Each time a storyteller pauses just before the funny line, notice how long the pause is. Write down one sarcastic line and one exaggeration you heard, in your own words, in your phrase log. Then rest.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Tell from tone whether someone means "great" or "not great", and answer the right way.
- [ ] Use light sarcasm and exaggeration about everyday situations.
- [ ] Tell a short funny story with a clear pause before the punchline, then stop.
- [ ] Take a friendly tease with a laugh and a comeback instead of a serious explanation.
- [ ] React to a joke in under two seconds with a natural phrase.
- [ ] Say when you don't get a joke, with a smile, without killing the mood.
