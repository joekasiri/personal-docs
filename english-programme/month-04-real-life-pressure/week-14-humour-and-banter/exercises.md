# Week 14 exercises: Humour and banter
[Week 14 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md) · [Day 2](day-2-exaggeration.md) · [Day 3](day-3-funny-story-timing.md) · [Day 4](day-4-teasing-and-taking-a-joke.md) · [Day 5](day-5-reacting-to-jokes.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** humour lives in the voice, so everything is **out loud** and with **tone**. A sarcastic line said in a normal voice is not sarcastic. An exaggeration said quietly is not funny. Overdo it: it's better to sound like a bad actor tonight than flat in a real conversation. All jokes in these exercises are friendly and about situations or ourselves. Answer keys give model answers; yours can be different.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Literal or sarcastic?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Read the situation. Decide if the line is **literal** (they mean it) or **sarcastic** (they mean the opposite). Then (1) say the line in the right tone, and (2) answer it the right way.

1. Your friend's flight is delayed for four hours. She says: "Oh, perfect."
2. Your friend just got tickets to her favourite band. She says: "Oh, perfect!"
3. It's snowing in April. Your coworker looks out of the window: "Spring is my favourite season."
4. A friend tastes your homemade soup and says: "Wow, this is really good."
5. The Wi-Fi drops for the third time during a call. Your teammate says: "I love technology."
6. Your roommate sees the huge pile of dishes: "Wow, thanks for cleaning up."
7. Your friend opens a birthday gift she really wanted: "No way, I love it!"
8. The 7 a.m. meeting is cancelled. Your coworker: "Oh no. What a shame."
9. Your friend has to work all weekend: "Living the dream."
10. Your phone falls in a puddle. Your friend: "Well, that went well."
11. Your friend's team won the game: "Best night ever!"
12. Your boss says the project deadline is now two days earlier. Your coworker whispers: "Great. Just great."

### Exercise 1.2: Make it sarcastic
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then record the last five

For each annoying situation, say a sarcastic line from the [Day 1 key phrases](day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md#key-phrases) (or your own). Use a **flat** or **over-the-top** tone. Then add one sincere sentence ("Honestly, though, it's fine.").

1. You get to the train station and the train left one minute ago.
2. You spill coffee on your white shirt before a job interview.
3. Your neighbour starts drilling at 7 a.m. on Saturday.
4. Your laptop starts a 45-minute update just before your presentation.
5. It starts raining the moment you leave the house without an umbrella.
6. Someone tells you "Just relax" when you're stressed.
7. You finally sit down to eat and your phone rings.
8. The self-checkout says "unexpected item in bagging area" for the fourth time.
9. Your friend says "I'll be there in five minutes" — for the third time in an hour.
10. You open the fridge and your roommate ate your leftovers.

### Exercise 1.3: Answer the sarcasm
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, 3-second limit

A friend says each line sarcastically. Answer within 3 seconds by **joining the joke** or **showing sympathy**. Do not take it literally. Use a different answer each time.

1. "Oh great, another meeting that could've been an email."
2. "Wow, I love waiting in line for forty minutes."
3. "My favourite thing: assembling furniture with no instructions."
4. "Oh sure, because the bus is *always* on time."
5. "Fantastic. My phone just died."
6. "What a surprise, the printer's jammed."
7. "Could this day get any better? I just locked myself out."
8. "Love that for me. My order got cancelled."
9. "Living the dream. Third night of overtime."
10. "Yeah, the diet's going great." (holding a giant slice of pizza)

### Exercise 2.3: Story upgrade
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then record

Read this flat story out loud. Then tell it again from memory with **at least five exaggerations** and **two sarcastic lines** (from [Day 1](day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md)). Record the upgraded version. Then read the model and compare.

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Mark the beat
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Each mini story is written with no pause. Find the place for the **beat** (one-second pause) just before the punchline. Say the story out loud with the beat, then say the punchline slower and lower. Then say it once more without looking.

1. "I finally tried online dating. First date, I walk into the café, and it's my dentist."
2. "I wanted to impress my new coworkers, so I baked cookies. Nobody ate them. Turns out I used salt instead of sugar."
3. "My dad bought a smart speaker. On the first day, he asked it to play jazz. It ordered him a jacket."
4. "I spent two hours making the perfect playlist for the road trip. We got in the car. The speaker was broken."
5. "I told my friend I'd been going to the gym every day. She asked where it is. I said, 'Next to the pizza place.' I know it so well because of the pizza place."
6. "I practised my presentation for a week. I walked in, opened my laptop, and the screen said 'Hello, Grandpa!' It was my grandpa's laptop."

### Exercise 3.2: Cut the set-up
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then record

These set-ups are far too long. Retell each one in **four sentences or fewer**, keeping only what the punchline needs. Then add the beat and say the punchline.

1. "So this was, I think, in 2019, or maybe 2020, no, it was before the pandemic, so 2019. In June. I was living in my old apartment, the one with the blue door, and I had a roommate called Ben, he was a teacher, really nice guy. And one morning I woke up early, around six, and I decided to make pancakes for both of us. I got the flour, eggs, milk, everything, and I made like ten pancakes. **Punchline:** Ben had moved out the day before."
2. "Okay so my company had this big summer party. It was at a hotel downtown, really fancy, with a band and a dance floor and these little sandwiches. I wore my blue suit, which I bought for my cousin's wedding. My boss was there, and the CEO, and everyone from marketing. I went to get a drink at the bar. **Punchline:** I said 'Love you, bye' to the CEO."
3. "So my friend Leo, who I met in college, we studied design together, he's from Chicago originally, he's really into cooking now, invited me over for dinner. He said he made a special recipe. He lives on the fourth floor with no elevator. **Punchline:** It was cereal. With a candle in it."
4. "I was at the airport in Denver, waiting for my flight home. The flight was at 8:40. I was early, so I went to get coffee and a muffin, and I sat down near the gate, and I put my headphones on, and I was listening to a podcast about productivity. **Punchline:** I missed the flight."

### Exercise 3.3: Rule of three
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Complete each list with **two normal things and one surprising thing**. Pause one beat before the third. Make the surprise the last word.

1. "For the trip, I packed my passport, my charger, and…"
2. "My perfect Sunday: sleep late, big breakfast, and…"
3. "In my first week at the job, I learnt three things: where the coffee is, how to book a room, and…"
4. "My roommate's three hobbies are yoga, cooking, and…"
5. "To get ready for the interview, I ironed my shirt, practised my answers, and…"
6. "My grandma has three rules: eat your vegetables, call your mother, and…"
7. "On the first date, we talked about music, travel, and…"
8. "My smart watch tracks my steps, my sleep, and…"
9. "At the party there was music, great food, and…"
10. "Things I lost this week: my keys, my wallet, and…"

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Safe or not?
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

Is this tease **safe** (friendly banter between friends) or **not safe** (could really hurt)? Say "safe" or "not safe" and say why in one sentence. For each "not safe" one, say a safe tease you could use instead.

1. "Look who finally showed up!" (to a friend who's always late)
2. "Have you put on weight?"
3. "Since when are you a wine expert?" (to a friend who suddenly talks about wine)
4. "Your accent is so funny."
5. "Oh, here we go again. Another photo of your cat."
6. "You're broke again? Ha."
7. "You took 200 photos of one sandwich."
8. "Nice try" (when a friend says they'll "definitely" go to the gym tomorrow)
9. "Is your family always that weird?"
10. "You're the worst at board games." (to a friend who loses every week and jokes about it)
11. "Your English is cute." (to a new coworker)
12. "You own more sneakers than a shoe store."

### Exercise 4.2: Take the joke, three ways
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, 3-second limit

A friend teases you. Answer **three different ways**: (a) laugh and admit, (b) exaggerate or fake offence, (c) gentle tease back. Use the [Day 4 key phrases](day-4-teasing-and-taking-a-joke.md#key-phrases).

1. "Late again? Did you get lost in your own street?"
2. "Is that your fourth coffee today?"
3. "You moved that button two pixels again, didn't you?"
4. "You've been 'about to start running' for a year."
5. "Did you seriously bring snacks to a restaurant?"
6. "You're always on your phone."
7. "Remember when you sang at karaoke? Our ears remember."
8. "Did you make this cake or did the cake make you?"
9. "You plan your vacations like military operations."
10. "You fell asleep at the movie. Again."

### Exercise 4.3: Comeback ping-pong
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud (two voices)

Read each exchange. Give the friend a playful voice. Fill your lines (**You:**) with a light answer; keep it the same size as the tease. Then do it again without looking.

**1. Board-game night**
> **Jess:** Okay, who wants to lose to me tonight?
> **You:** _______
> **Jess:** Bold words from someone who lost three times last week.
> **You:** _______

**2. The coffee snob**
> **Omar:** Let me guess: oat milk, extra hot, poured by a poet?
> **You:** _______
> **Omar:** You'd die if they gave you instant coffee.
> **You:** _______

**3. The sunburn**
> **Lin:** You look like a tomato. Did you forget sunscreen *again*?
> **You:** _______
> **Lin:** You said "I never burn" this morning.
> **You:** _______

**4. Your cooking**
> **Leo:** So what's for dinner, chef? Toast again?
> **You:** _______
> **Leo:** Says the person who set the microwave on fire.
> **You:** _______

5. **Safe.** It's about a habit they love.
6. **Not safe.** Money problems can be real and painful. Instead: "You and your online shopping."
7. **Safe.**
8. **Safe.**
9. **Not safe.** Family is personal. Instead: "Your family game nights sound intense."
10. **Safe** (because they already joke about it).

12. **Safe.**

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Two-second joke reactions
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, 2-second limit, then record round 2

Read each joke or funny line in a friend's voice (or ask someone to read them to you). React **within two seconds**. First, decide what type it is (funny story, pun, self-joke, tease, clever line), then choose a fitting reaction from the [Day 5 table](day-5-reacting-to-jokes.md#key-phrases). Round 1: one reaction each. Round 2: reaction + a follow-up question or a short riff.

1. "My phone and I have a lot in common. We both die at twenty percent for no reason."
2. "I tried to organise my desk. Now it looks amazing and I can't find anything."
3. "My cat walked on my keyboard and sent my boss 'jjjjjjj'. Honestly, the clearest email I've sent all week."
4. "Our team's new office plant is doing great. It's really growing on us."
5. "The waiter said 'Enjoy your meal' and I said 'You too.' He was not eating."
6. "Sam's been 'five minutes away' since lunch. It's six p.m."
7. "I joined a running club. So far I've mostly run late."
8. "I'm so bad at bowling, the ball came back to me out of pity."
9. "My smart fridge told me I'm out of milk. I knew. I didn't need the judgement."
10. "My GPS said 'make a U-turn' four times. Even my car thinks I make bad choices."
11. "The bakery near my place closed. I guess they couldn't make enough dough."
12. "I bought a book about time management. I haven't had time to read it."
13. "Leo brought his own hot sauce to a restaurant. In his backpack. Like a cowboy with a bottle."
14. "My grandpa thinks 'brb' is a type of sandwich. He tried to order one."
15. "The client asked for 'the same, but different'. So I renamed the file 'different'."
16. "I asked the designer why she's so calm. She said she just goes with the flow. The user flow."

### Exercise 5.2: Riff on it
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Add a short **riff** (one sentence) on top of each joke. Use exaggeration, a "what if", or a callback. Start with "Yeah, and…", "Okay, and next…" or "Wait till…".

1. "My mom just discovered voice notes. She sent me eleven this morning."
2. "Marcus wore sunglasses to the meeting. It was on video. Inside."
3. "My neighbour's dog barks at the mail carrier like it's a personal war."
4. "My roommate labels everything in the fridge. Even the ice."
5. "My little brother says he's going to be a professional gamer. He's eight."
6. "Our office coffee machine has more meetings in its calendar than I do."
7. "Jess named her plant 'Kevin'. Kevin is doing better than all of us."
8. "My dad's been 'fixing' the garden gate for three summers."

### Exercise 5.3: Didn't get it / not funny
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

What do you say? Keep the mood light.

1. Everyone laughs at a joke about an old American TV show you never saw.
2. A friend tells a pun, and you understand the words but not the joke.
3. A coworker makes a joke in a meeting that isn't very funny. Everyone gives a small smile.
4. A friend says "That's so Ohio" and the group laughs.
5. You laughed at a joke, but now someone asks you "Right? Wasn't that the best part?" and you didn't really get it.
6. Someone makes a joke that's a bit unkind about a person who isn't there.

### Day 5 answer key

1. Self-joke / clever line → "Ha! Same. Mine dies at fifty."
2. Self-joke → "Haha, stop. That's my whole apartment."
3. Funny story → "I'm crying. Did your boss reply?"

5. Self-joke (story) → "Haha, stop! We've all done that."

15. Clever line → "Ha, nice one. Did they like it?"

### Five-minute warm-up
1. Say 5 sarcastic lines with a deadpan voice and 5 with an over-the-top voice.
2. Tell yourself your funny story in 1 minute, with the beat.
3. Say "Haha, stop", "You're killing me" and "I can't with you" while actually laughing. Yes, it feels silly. Do it anyway.
