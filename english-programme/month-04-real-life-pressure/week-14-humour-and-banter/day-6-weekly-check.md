# Week 14 · Day 6: Weekly check
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 4](../README.md) › [Week 14](README.md) · [← Previous: Reacting to jokes](day-5-reacting-to-jokes.md) · [Next: Week 15 · Making plans on the phone →](../week-15-calls/day-1-making-plans-on-the-phone.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 15 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 15 min

## Before you start

- Live conversation #2 booked (ideally a group: games night, pub quiz, dinner with friends).
- [Nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) open with last week's numbers and your baseline.
- Recording app and timer. [The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) if you need a reminder.

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Redo the **hardest clip of the week**, probably the deadpan sarcasm clip from Day 1 or the laughing-while-talking clip from Day 5. Speak along twice. The second time, exaggerate the tone even more than the speaker. Then tell yourself one sarcastic line and one exaggeration about your day, out loud. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**Prompt (unprepared, 2 minutes):**

> "Tell me about the funniest person you know. What do they do or say that makes people laugh? Tell at least one funny thing that happened with them."

1. Read the prompt once. Start recording immediately. No notes.
2. Talk for 2 minutes. Try to include at least one exaggeration and one pause before a punchline, but don't stop to plan.
3. Count your 3 numbers ([how](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)): umms/uhhs, pauses over 2 seconds, different connectors.
4. Write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
5. Bonus: did your punchline land? Listen as if you were a friend. Did you laugh, even a little?

## 3. Skill check

Rate yourself: **1** = I can't yet, **2** = with effort, **3** = automatic.

| Day | Skill | "I can…" | Rating (1–3) | If you scored 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Sarcasm and tone](day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md) | hear sarcasm from tone and context, answer it correctly, and use it about situations | | Redo [Exercise 1.1](exercises.md#day-1-exercises) and shadow 5 min of a deadpan clip |
| 2 | [Exaggeration](day-2-exaggeration.md) | exaggerate with numbers, extremes, comparisons and "forever", with the right stress | | Redo [Exercise 2.1](exercises.md#day-2-exercises), saying each line with a big voice |
| 3 | [Funny story timing](day-3-funny-story-timing.md) | tell a funny story with a short set-up, a beat and a short punchline, then stop | | Do the Day 3 1-minute version of your story 3 more times |
| 4 | [Teasing and taking a joke](day-4-teasing-and-taking-a-joke.md) | take a tease with a quick, light answer, and tease safely | | Redo [Exercise 4.2](exercises.md#day-4-exercises) with a 3-second limit |
| 5 | [Reacting to jokes](day-5-reacting-to-jokes.md) | react in under 2 seconds with a fitting phrase, riff, or ask when I don't get it | | Redo [Exercise 5.1](exercises.md#day-5-exercises) twice |

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

**Mission: be fun to talk to.** Humour in a real conversation, ideally with 3+ people. Use the mission card in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials).

Try to:

1. **React to every joke or funny story** in under two seconds (at least 5 reactions, at least 3 different phrases).
2. **Tell one funny story** with timing: short set-up, beat, punchline, stop.
3. Use **at least two sarcastic lines** and **three exaggerations** about situations.
4. **Take one tease** with a light answer (if someone teases you), or tease a good friend once about something safe.
5. Try **one riff or callback** on someone else's joke.

**Backup plan:** a 40-minute voice session with Claude as a group. Paste:

```
Let's hang out as a group of three friends at a pub quiz: you play KIM (sarcastic, deadpan),
DEV (exaggerates everything) and ROSA (laughs at everything, teases me a little).
Say each name before their line. Make jokes, tease me lightly about safe things (being competitive,
my coffee habit, being late), tell short funny stories, and leave space for me to react and tell mine.
No corrections until I say "feedback". Then rate me 1-3 on: understanding sarcasm, exaggerating,
story timing, taking a joke, reacting to jokes. Give examples and up to 5 mistakes.
```

See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md). Write down afterwards: what made people laugh? Which joke did you not get?

## 5. Review and report (15 min)

1. Spaced review out loud: 5 phrases from this week, 5 from Week 13, 5 from Month 3 ([phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md)).
2. Send Claude your report. Copy and fill in:

```
Week 14 (humour and banter) report.
Re-record numbers: umms [ ], long pauses [ ], connectors [ ].
Last week: umms [ ], long pauses [ ], connectors [ ]. Baseline: umms [ ], long pauses [ ], connectors [ ].
Skill check (1-3): sarcasm [ ], exaggeration [ ], story timing [ ], teasing/taking a joke [ ], reacting [ ].
Live conversation: [who / group size]. Jokes I reacted to: [ ]. My funny story: [landed / didn't land].
A joke I didn't understand: [write it as well as you remember].
Please: 1) explain that joke if you can, 2) give me one humour habit to keep in Week 15 (phone calls),
3) suggest 3 short funny lines I can use when a call goes wrong.
```

3. **Carry into next week:** Week 15 is phone and video calls. No faces, no gestures. Humour is still useful there: a light sarcastic line when the connection drops ("Ah, technology. Love it.") relaxes everyone. Keep your reaction phrases ready.
