## Learning objectives

- Understand friendly teasing (banter) as a sign of closeness, not an attack.
- Take a joke about yourself with a laugh and a quick comeback.
- Tease friends lightly about safe topics.
- Set a boundary politely if a joke goes too far.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: playful vs serious tone.** The same words can be friendly or rude. The difference is the voice. A **playful** tease is lighter and higher, a bit faster, often with a smile you can hear (the vowels get wider), and sometimes a little laugh at the end. A **serious** line is lower, slower and flat.

Say each line twice: once **playful** (smiling, light), once **serious** (flat). Feel how different they are. Only use the playful version with friends.

1. "Look who finally showed UP!"
2. "Oh, here we go AGAIN."
3. "Says the guy who got lost in his own BUILDING."
4. "Since when are YOU a chef?"
5. "Nice TRY."
6. "Rude!" (laughing)
7. "Wow. I feel so attacked right now."
8. "Guilty as CHARGED."
9. "You're never gonna let me forget that, are you?"
10. "Hey, I'm working on it!"

**Clip task:** search "friends teasing each other podcast", "friendly roast podcast hosts" or "best friends banter interview" (choose friendly ones). Speak along for 4 minutes. Notice the laughs inside the sentences and how quickly the teased person answers. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Teasing and taking a joke

### Explanation

In American friendships (and many work teams), **friendly teasing is a sign that people like you**. People tease friends about small habits and harmless choices: always being late, being obsessed with coffee, being terrible at board games, taking 200 photos of every meal. If someone teases you, it usually means "you're one of us now."

For a learner, the danger is not teasing. It is **reacting too seriously**. If someone says "Look who finally showed up!" and you explain for one minute why the train was late, the fun stops. The natural reaction is short, light and quick.

**Taking a joke: four easy moves.**

| Move | What you do | Example (after "Look who finally showed up!") |
|---|---|---|
| Laugh and admit | agree, with a smile | "Guilty as charged." |
| Exaggerate it | make the tease bigger | "I know. I'm basically a legend at being late." |
| Fake offended | pretend to be hurt (clearly joking) | "Wow. I feel so attacked right now." |
| Tease back (gently) | small, safe comeback | "Says the guy who forgot his own party." |

**Teasing others: the safe zone.** Tease only:
- people you know and who already joke with you;
- about **small, chosen things**: habits, hobbies, taste in music, being a coffee snob, terrible at cooking, always on their phone;
- in a group where they can laugh along.

**Never tease about:** bodies, looks, weight, health, money, family problems, religion, nationality, accent, or anything they can't change or might be sensitive about. And never tease someone you just met.

**If a joke goes too far** (for you or someone else), say it lightly but clearly: "Okay, not that one, though." "Ha, okay, let's leave my ex out of it." Good friends will stop. That's normal, not dramatic.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Look who finally showed up! | teasing someone who's late (friendly) | "Look who finally showed up! We almost ordered without you." |
| Oh, here we go again. | someone starts their favourite topic again | "Oh, here we go again. Another sourdough story." |
| Says the guy/girl who… | a comeback: they did something worse | "Says the guy who brought a salad to a barbecue." |
| Since when are you a [X]? | someone suddenly acts like an expert | "Since when are you a wine expert?" |
| Nice try. | someone tries to trick you or avoid something | "You'll pay next time? Nice try." |
| Guilty as charged. | you admit the tease is true | "Always on my phone? Guilty as charged." |
| Okay, fair. | the tease is true and you accept it | "Okay, fair. I did eat all the fries." |
| Hey, I'm working on it! | they tease you about a habit | "Late again?" "Hey, I'm working on it!" |
| Wow, I feel so attacked right now. | fake offended (laughing) | "Wow. I feel so attacked right now." |
| Rude! | fake offended, very short (laughing) | "Your cooking's... interesting." "Rude!" |
| You're never gonna let me forget that, are you? | they bring up your old mistake | "The karaoke night? You're never gonna let me forget that, are you?" |
| Okay, not that one, though. | a joke went too far; set a boundary lightly | "Ha, okay, not that one, though." |

### Examples

**Arriving late to dinner with three friends.**

> **Leo:** Look who finally showed up!
> **You:** I know, I know. Guilty as charged.
> **Maya:** What was it this time? Traffic? Aliens?
> **You:** Honestly? I couldn't find my keys. They were in my hand.
> **Sam:** (laughing) Classic.
> **You:** Hey, I'm working on it! Says the guy who left his car running at the supermarket.
> **Sam:** Okay, fair.

**At work: the "pixel-perfect designer".**

> **Marcus:** Let me guess, you moved the button two pixels to the left again.
> **You:** Three pixels, thank you very much.
> **Dana:** He's never gonna stop.
> **You:** You'll thank me when it looks amazing.
> **Marcus:** Since when do users notice three pixels?
> **You:** Since I started working here.

**Setting a boundary (lightly), then moving on.**

> **Omar:** Remember your ex who only talked about crypto?
> **You:** Ha, okay, not that one, though.
> **Omar:** Fair, fair. Sorry.
> **You:** All good. Anyway, Jess, how was the climbing trip?

### Common mistakes

❌ Getting defensive with a long serious explanation: "No, I am not late, actually the train was delayed because…"
✅ "Guilty as charged. The train hates me."
*Reason: a serious defence kills the joke and makes everyone feel bad.*

❌ Teasing someone you just met: "Wow, you're really bad at this game."
✅ Wait until they joke with you first.
*Reason: teasing needs a relationship. With strangers it sounds rude.*

❌ "You are so stupid." (said as a "joke")
✅ "You're ridiculous." (laughing, about something silly they did)
*Reason: insults about intelligence aren't banter. "Ridiculous" or "you're the worst" (laughing) are the friendly versions.*

❌ "I'm offended." (said seriously)
✅ "Wow. I feel so attacked right now." (laughing) or "Rude!"
*Reason: fake offence must sound fake: big, playful, with a smile.*

❌ Teasing back much harder than the original tease.
✅ Match the size: small tease, small comeback.
*Reason: a bigger comeback turns a game into a fight.*

❌ Teasing about someone's accent, body or money.
✅ Tease only about small choices and habits.
*Reason: those topics can really hurt, even if the person laughs.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, trade funny stories

Tonight you **trade funny stories with Claude**, and Claude teases you a little (friendly) along the way. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read:

```
Let's hang out as old friends. You're JAMIE: warm, funny, loves banter.
We trade funny stories, taking turns. When I tell a story, react naturally, interrupt a little,
and tease me in a friendly way about small things (being clumsy, being late, my coffee habit,
being too perfectionist at work). Never tease about appearance, accent, money, family or anything serious.
I'll practise taking the joke (admitting, exaggerating, fake offence, gentle comebacks) and teasing you back.
Your stories should be short, with a pause before the punchline, so I can practise reacting.
Use some sarcasm and exaggeration too. Don't correct me until I say "feedback".
Then tell me: how I reacted to teasing (too serious? good comebacks?), whether my story timing worked,
and up to 5 language mistakes.
You start: tease me for being late to our call, then tell me a funny story.
```

**Step 2 (25 min).** Trade at least **four stories each**. Targets:
- Tell your Day 3 story again, with better timing.
- Tell at least two more of your own (use the 3/2/1 ideas from [Day 3](day-3-funny-story-timing.md) if you need them).
- Take at least **five teases** using all four moves (admit, exaggerate, fake offence, tease back).
- Tease Jamie back at least **three times** about safe things.
- Use your [Week 13 holding phrases](../week-13-group-conversations/day-3-holding-your-turn.md) if Jamie interrupts your punchline.

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Write down what Claude says.

**Step 4 (20 min).** Round two. Say: *"Now let's add a third friend, ALEX, who's a bit competitive and always has a 'better' story. Same rules."* Now you are in a group of three again: jump in, hand over, build on jokes.

**Step 5 (8 min).** Ask Claude: *"Give me 5 teases a friend might say to me and I'll answer each one in under 3 seconds."* Do it twice.
