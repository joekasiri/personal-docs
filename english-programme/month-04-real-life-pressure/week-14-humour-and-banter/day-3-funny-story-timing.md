## Learning objectives

- Keep the set-up of a funny story short.
- Pause for one beat before the punchline, then say it short and clear.
- Stop after the punchline (don't explain it), and let people laugh.
- Use the "rule of three" and a "callback" to add extra laughs.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: the pause before the punchline.** Comedians call it "the beat": a short silence (about one second) just before the funny line. It makes people lean in. After the beat, the punchline is said **slightly slower, a bit lower, and short**. Then the speaker stops.

Practice (/ = one-second pause; say the punchline slower):

1. "So I open the door, and standing there is / my boss. In a bathrobe."
2. "I spent three hours on the cake, and the dog ate it in / four seconds."
3. "I finally get to the front of the line and the guy goes / 'Sorry, we're closed.'"
4. "I practised my speech all week. Walked on stage. Opened my mouth. And said / 'Good morning.' It was eight p.m."
5. "And that's how I found out / I'd been calling her the wrong name for two years."
6. "Turns out the 'quick hike' / was eleven miles."
7. "So I'm waving at this guy for, like, a full minute / and he's waving at the person behind me."
8. "I bought a plant to make my apartment feel alive. / It died in a week. Now my apartment feels guilty."
9. "Wait for it… / he brought his mom."
10. "Of course, / it was the wrong airport."

**Clip task:** search "storytime funny pause punchline", "comedian telling a story to friends" or "funny dinner party story". Speak along with one story. Mark (mentally) where the storyteller pauses. Then tell the same story in your own words, copying the pauses. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Funny story timing

### Explanation

A funny story told with bad timing is not funny. The same story with good timing gets a big laugh. Timing is mostly about four things:

1. **Short set-up.** Give only the details the punchline needs. Not the date, not what you had for breakfast. Three or four sentences, max. If the set-up is too long, people stop listening before the funny part.
2. **The build.** Make the problem grow. Use "And then…", "Of course…", "And to top it all off…". Three steps are perfect (the rule of three, below).
3. **The beat + punchline.** Pause one second. Then say the funny line in as few words as possible, a bit slower. Put the funniest word **at the end**. "And standing there was my boss. In a bathrobe." (not "My boss, who was wearing a bathrobe, was standing there.")
4. **Stop.** Let them laugh. Don't explain the joke. If you want, add a short "tag" line after the laugh ("Ten out of ten, would not recommend.").

**The rule of three.** Lists of three feel complete. Make the first two normal and the third one surprising: "I packed my passport, my charger, and… my roommate's shoes."

**The callback.** Later in the conversation, mention the funny thing again: if someone mentions shoes an hour later, you say "Careful, I might pack them." People love callbacks because they feel like an inside joke.

**In a live group**, people may react or guess during your build. Use your [Week 13 holding phrases](../week-13-group-conversations/day-3-holding-your-turn.md) ("Hang on, it gets better") to protect your punchline.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So get this… | you start a funny story | "So get this. I go to the wrong wedding." |
| And I kid you not… | the next part sounds unbelievable | "And I kid you not, the goat followed me home." |
| You're not gonna believe this, but… | you build up to something surprising | "You're not gonna believe this, but it was the same guy." |
| Wait for it… | you make people wait for the punchline | "And then he brings out — wait for it — a trumpet." |
| Of course… | the next bad thing was "typical" | "And of course, it started raining." |
| And to top it all off… | the third and worst thing | "And to top it all off, I locked my keys in the car." |
| Plot twist: | a surprising change | "Plot twist: it was his birthday too." |
| And that's when I realised… | the moment you understood the funny truth | "And that's when I realised I was on the wrong train." |
| Needless to say… | the obvious result (with a straight face) | "Needless to say, I didn't get the job." |
| Long story short… | you skip to the end | "Long story short, I now own a cat." |
| Ten out of ten, would not recommend. | a funny "tag" after the punchline | "Food poisoning in Paris. Ten out of ten, would not recommend." |
| And that's the story of how… | a neat ending | "And that's the story of how I got banned from karaoke." |

### Examples

**A model funny story (with timing marks).**

> "So get this. Last month I'm at the gym, right? First time in, like, a year. I'm trying to look like I know what I'm doing. So I get on the treadmill, put my headphones in, start running, feeling great. And of course, I'm not looking at the buttons. I'm looking at myself in the mirror, being cool. And I hit the wrong button. Speed: maximum. / And that's when I realised… / the treadmill doesn't care how cool you are."
> *(laughs)*
> "Ten out of ten, would not recommend."

Notice: four sentences of set-up, a build ("And of course…"), a beat, a short punchline, a tag, stop.

**In a group: someone interrupts, you protect the punchline.**

> **You:** So my sister calls me, super serious, and says, "I need to tell you something about Mom."
> **Omar:** Oh no.
> **Jess:** Is she okay?
> **You:** Hang on, it's good, I promise. So I'm panicking, I'm driving over there, I run into the house, and Mom is sitting at the table with… / a laptop. She started a cooking channel.
> **Jess:** (laughing) No way!
> **You:** Forty subscribers. She calls them "my fans".

**Rule of three + callback.**

> **You:** I was so tired this morning. I put salt in my coffee, I put my shirt on backwards, and I said "Love you" to the bus driver.
> **Leo:** (laughs) What did he say?
> **You:** He said "Love you too." Best part of my day.
> *(Later, Leo gets on a bus to go home.)*
> **You:** Say hi to my boyfriend.

### Common mistakes

❌ Starting with the punchline: "I got stuck on a treadmill on max speed. So I was at the gym…"
✅ Build first, punchline last.
*Reason: once people know the ending, there's nothing to wait for.*

❌ A long set-up: "So it was Tuesday, the 14th, no, wait, the 15th, and I'd had a sandwich, a turkey one…"
✅ "So get this. Last Tuesday I'm at the gym…"
*Reason: every extra detail is a chance for people to stop listening.*

❌ Rushing the punchline at full speed, with no pause.
✅ Pause one beat, then say it slower.
*Reason: the pause tells people "this is the good part".*

❌ Explaining the joke: "…and it was funny because, you know, he was my boss and the bathrobe was, like, unusual."
✅ Stop after the punchline. Add a short tag only if you want.
*Reason: an explained joke is a dead joke.*

❌ Laughing so much before the punchline that nobody understands it.
✅ Smile, but hold the laugh until after.
*Reason: a little smile helps; full laughing hides your words.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

### Part A: Comedy 3/2/1 (30 min)

Use your **real funny story** (chosen above). If you don't have one, choose one of these: "the time you got on the wrong bus, train or plane", "a cooking disaster", "a time you waved at someone who wasn't waving at you", "the worst haircut you ever had".

1. **3 minutes** (record): tell it with all the details you want.
2. **2 minutes** (record): cut the set-up to four sentences, add a clear build with "And of course…", and put a one-second beat before the punchline.
3. **1 minute** (record): set-up in two sentences, rule of three in the build, beat, punchline, tag, stop.
4. Listen to the 1-minute version. Is the funniest word at the very end of the punchline? If not, rephrase and record it once more.

See [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (30 min)

**Mission:** in a real conversation, tell your funny story with timing, and use this week's first skills.

Try to:

1. Tell your funny story (the 1–2 minute version). Pause before the punchline. Stop after it.
2. Use at least **two sarcastic lines** about a situation (Day 1).
3. Use at least **three exaggerations** (Day 2).
4. If someone tells a funny story, react and ask one follow-up question.
5. Bonus: make one **callback** later in the conversation.

**Backup plan:** voice mode with Claude. Paste:

```
Let's have a casual chat as two friends at a bar. You're SAM, relaxed and funny.
I want to practise telling funny stories with good timing. I'll tell you one story;
react like a real friend (laugh, interrupt a little, ask questions).
Then you tell me one of yours, and I'll react. Use some sarcasm and exaggeration too.
No corrections until I say "feedback". Then tell me: did my punchline land, was my set-up too long,
did I pause before the punchline, and up to 5 language mistakes.
```

Log the conversation in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): did people laugh? What would you change?
