## Learning objectives

- React to a joke or funny story in under two seconds with a natural phrase.
- Match the reaction to the type of joke (a story, a pun, a self-joke, a tease).
- "Riff": add a small joke on top of someone else's joke.
- Say you didn't get a joke, or respond to a joke that isn't funny, without killing the mood.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: laughing while talking.** Native speakers often keep talking *through* a laugh. The words get breathy and broken, the voice goes up, and there are little "ha" sounds in the middle: "Ha-ha, stop, I can't — ha — I can't breathe." This sounds warm and real. Practise it so your reactions don't sound read from a book.

Practice sentences (laugh at the "ha"s; keep the words clear):

1. "Ha-ha, STOP."
2. "You're KILLING me. Ha."
3. "Oh my god, I'm — ha — I'm CRYING."
4. "I can't with you. I CAN'T."
5. "Ha! Okay, that was GOOD."
6. "Ugh, that's so BAD. Ha. It's so bad it's good."
7. "Stop it, ha, I can't BREATHE."
8. "Wait — ha — wait, he did WHAT?"
9. "Ha, nice one. I'm STEALING that."
10. "Okay, okay — ha — I need a minute."

**Clip task:** search "podcast hosts can't stop laughing", "friends laughing podcast funny moment" or "comedy podcast laughing fit". Speak along for 3–4 minutes, **including the laughs**. It will feel silly. That is fine. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Reacting to jokes

### Explanation

When someone makes a joke, there is a tiny window (about one or two seconds) for a reaction. If you are silent for three seconds while you decide if it's funny, the moment is gone and the joker feels awkward. A quick reaction, even a small one, keeps the energy up and makes people feel good about talking to you.

**Match the reaction to the joke:**

| Type of joke | Good reaction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Funny story | big laugh + a phrase + a follow-up | "Ha-ha, stop! What did your boss say?" |
| Pun / wordplay (bad on purpose) | a playful groan | "Ugh, that's terrible. Ha." |
| Self-deprecating joke ("I'm so bad at this") | laugh + kind reassurance | "Ha, stop, you're fine!" |
| Tease at someone else | laugh + maybe add to it | "Ha, he's not wrong." |
| A clever one-liner | appreciation | "Ha, nice one." / "Okay, that was good." |
| A really funny moment | over-the-top | "I'm crying. I can't breathe." / "Dead." |

**Riffing** means adding a small joke to someone's joke, like [building on ideas](../week-13-group-conversations/day-5-building-on-others-ideas.md) but funny. "Yeah, and next week he'll bring a trumpet." Riffs are usually short and use exaggeration or a callback.

**When you don't get the joke:** don't pretend (you might say something that shows you didn't get it). Smile and ask: "Wait, I don't get it. Explain!" or "Okay, I need a second on that one." People enjoy explaining their jokes to someone who is curious, and it often makes a second laugh. Puns and cultural references are hard even for native speakers from other countries.

**When the joke is not funny:** a small, polite smile and "Ha, okay." or "Ha, nice." That's enough. Don't fake a big laugh, and don't say "That's not funny" unless the joke is really unkind.

**Formal or casual?** "Dead", "I'm crying" and "I can't with you" are for friends. In a work meeting, use "Ha, that's great", "Ha, nice one" or just a real laugh.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Haha, stop. | something is very funny (friends) | "Haha, stop. He did not." |
| You're killing me. | someone keeps making you laugh | "You're killing me. Okay, then what?" |
| I can't with you. | someone is ridiculous in a funny way | "You ate the whole cake? I can't with you." |
| That's hilarious. | a clear, safe, all-purpose reaction | "That's hilarious. I'm telling my sister." |
| I'm crying. / I'm dying. | extremely funny (exaggeration) | "I'm crying. The goat?!" |
| Dead. | super short, very casual "too funny" | (After a funny photo) "Dead." |
| Okay, that was good. | a clever joke deserves credit | "Okay, that was good. I'm annoyed I didn't think of it." |
| Ugh, that's so bad. | a pun or "dad joke" (playful groan) | "Ugh, that's so bad. I love it." |
| Ha, nice one. | a quick, polite reaction (work-safe) | "Ha, nice one, Marcus." |
| I'm stealing that. | you love the line and want to use it | "'Professional overthinker'? I'm stealing that." |
| Stop, you're fine! | a friend makes a joke about themselves | "Stop, you're fine! Everyone's bad at bowling." |
| Wait, I don't get it. | you missed the joke | "Wait, I don't get it. Why is 'Bob' funny?" |

### Examples

**Group chat at dinner (four speakers).**

> **Jess:** So my dad finally learnt to use emojis. He sent the family group a birthday message with a crying face. He thinks it means "so happy".
> **You:** Haha, stop. To who?
> **Jess:** My grandma. On her ninetieth.
> **Omar:** I'm dying.
> **You:** Okay, and next year he'll send her the angry face. "Happy birthday, Mom!"
> **Lin:** (laughing) You're killing me.
> **Jess:** Don't give him ideas.

**A pun at work.**

> **Marcus:** I was gonna tell you a joke about the new design system, but it's not consistent yet.
> **You:** Ugh, that's so bad. Ha.
> **Dana:** Marcus, please.
> **You:** Okay, it was a little good. I'm stealing it for the demo.

**Not getting it, and saving the moment.**

> **Tyler:** It's like a Monday that thinks it's a Friday.
> **Nina:** (laughing) So true.
> **You:** Ha, wait — I don't get it. Explain!
> **Tyler:** Like, it acts all fun, but it's still Monday.
> **You:** Oh! Okay, yes. That's my whole job, actually.
> **Nina:** Ha!

### Common mistakes

❌ "It's funny." (flat, with no laugh)
✅ "Haha, that's so funny."
*Reason: said flat, "it's funny" sounds like you don't find it funny at all.*

❌ "You are killing me." (slow, serious, every word clear)
✅ "You're killing me." (fast, laughing)
*Reason: said slowly it sounds like a real complaint. Contract it and laugh.*

❌ "Dead." / "I'm crying." in a formal meeting with a client.
✅ "Ha, that's great."
*Reason: these are casual, internet-style reactions for friends.*

❌ Pretending to get a joke, then saying something that shows you didn't.
✅ "Wait, I don't get it. Explain!"
*Reason: asking is charming; pretending is risky.*

❌ Saying "Ha. Ha." like reading it.
✅ A real, short laugh: "Ha!" or "Haha!" with breath.
*Reason: a laugh is a sound, not two words.*

❌ Silence for three seconds while you think.
✅ Any quick sound first ("Ha!" "Oh my god"), then a phrase.
*Reason: a fast small reaction beats a slow perfect one.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill with a "funny angle"** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Funny angle (40 min).** Pick a topic without looking. Talk for **90 seconds**, recording. Rules: use at least **one sarcastic line**, **one exaggeration**, and **end with a punchline** (pause before it). After each recording, listen back to the last 15 seconds and react out loud to your own punchline as if a friend said it ("Ha, okay, that was good" or "Ugh, that's so bad"). Do all 10.

1. Your first job
2. Learning to drive
3. Your worst haircut
4. Cooking for other people
5. Pets (yours, or other people's)
6. Family group chats
7. Trying to exercise
8. Getting lost
9. Your morning routine
10. Assembling something from a box

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Tell the funniest thing a friend or family member ever did."** 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each. Use everything from this week: sarcasm, exaggeration, a short set-up, a beat before the punchline, and a tag line. In each version, include how *you* and others reacted ("And I was like, 'Stop, I can't breathe'").
