## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: sarcastic intonation.** Sarcasm means saying the opposite of what you mean, and the **tone** carries the real meaning. American sarcasm usually sounds one of two ways:

1. **Flat and slow (deadpan):** almost no melody, low voice, words a bit slower. "Oh. Great." "Wow. Perfect."
2. **Over-the-top:** too much melody, stretched vowels, like a bad actor. "Oh, *greeeat*." "Sooo fun."

Compare (say each pair out loud):

| Sincere (happy) | Sarcastic (annoyed / joking) |
|---|---|
| "Oh, GREAT! ↗ You got the job!" (high, bright, quick) | "Oh, great. ↘ The printer's broken again." (flat, low, slow) |
| "That's PERFECT, thanks!" | "Well, that's just… perfect." (pause, flat) |
| "I LOVE that!" | "Wow. Love that for me." (deadpan) |
| "That's so HELPFUL!" | "Thanks, that's *super* helpful." (stretched "super", eyes rolling) |

Practice sentences. Say them **sarcastically**, copying the style in brackets:

1. "Oh, great. Just what I needed." (flat)
2. "Wow. Love that for me." (deadpan)
3. "Yeah, that went *really* well." (stretched "really")
4. "Oh, *sure*, because that always works." (over-the-top "sure")
5. "Could this day get any better?" (flat, slow)
6. "Well, that's just… perfect." (pause before "perfect")
7. "Living the dream." (flat, small smile)
8. "What a *surprise*." (low, slow)
9. "Oh no. What a shame." (over-the-top, when a boring meeting gets cancelled)
10. "Nice. Very nice." (flat, looking at your soaking wet shoes)

**Clip task:** speak along with a deadpan or sarcastic speaker for 4 minutes. Then pick three sarcastic lines and say each one twice: once sarcastic, once sincere. Feel the difference in your voice. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Sarcasm and tone

### Explanation

Americans use light sarcasm all the time, especially to complain about small things in a funny way: traffic, weather, Monday mornings, broken printers, their own bad luck. It is a way of saying "this is annoying, but I'm not going to be dramatic about it." It also builds connection: when you are sarcastic about the rain together, you are on the same team.

**How to recognise sarcasm.** Ask: *does the situation match the words?* If someone says "Oh, perfect" while it starts pouring, the situation is bad, the words are good, so it is sarcasm. Then listen for the tone (flat or over-the-top) and look at the face (eye roll, small smile, raised eyebrows).

**How to answer sarcasm.** Do not take it literally. Join the joke or show sympathy:

- Share the feeling: "Ugh, I know." "Right?" "Tell me about it."
- Add your own sarcasm: "Yeah, best day ever."
- Show you get it, with a laugh: "Ha, classic."

**When to use it yourself:**
- ✅ About **situations**: the weather, delays, technology, your own mistakes.
- ✅ With people who already joke with you.
- ❌ Not about a person's work, looks, or choices (it can hurt, and a learner's tone can be misread).
- ❌ Not in writing to people you don't know well (no tone, so it reads as rude).
- ❌ Not when someone shares real bad news.

If someone does not get your sarcasm, just say: "I'm kidding!" or "That was sarcasm, sorry." No problem.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Oh great, just what I needed. | something annoying happens | (Phone at 1%) "Oh great, just what I needed." |
| Well, that's just perfect. | a plan goes wrong | (Train cancelled) "Well, that's just perfect." |
| Wow, love that for me. | a small bad thing happens to you | (Coffee spills on your shirt) "Wow. Love that for me." |
| Living the dream. | someone asks how you are on a boring/busy day | "How's it going?" "Oh, you know. Living the dream." |
| Yeah, that went well. | something clearly went badly | (After you fall off a chair) "Yeah, that went well." |
| Thanks, that's super helpful. | a joke reply to useless advice (only with friends) | "Just don't be tired." "Thanks, that's super helpful." |
| Oh, sure, because that always works. | someone suggests something that never works | "Just restart it." "Oh, sure, because that always works." |
| Could this day get any better? | many bad things in one day | (Rain, late bus, no umbrella) "Could this day get any better?" |
| What a surprise. | something bad but expected happens | (The bus is late again) "Wow. What a surprise." |
| Oh no, what a shame. | something you didn't want gets cancelled (happy really) | "The 8 a.m. meeting's cancelled." "Oh no. What a shame." |
| Big fan of that. | you dislike something (flat) | (Neighbour's drilling at 7 a.m.) "Big fan of that." |
| I'm kidding. / Just kidding. | you want to make clear it was a joke | "I'm kidding! It's actually really nice." |

### Examples

**At a bus stop with two friends.**

> **Leo:** The app says the bus is "arriving now". It's been "arriving now" for fifteen minutes.
> **You:** Oh, great. Love that for us.
> **Maya:** And it's starting to rain.
> **You:** Well, that's just perfect. Could this day get any better?
> **Leo:** (laughing) Right? Living the dream.
> **Maya:** Oh, look — the bus! It's real!
> **You:** What a miracle.

**At work, Monday morning.**

> **Dana:** Morning! How was your weekend?
> **You:** Good, thanks! Too short, though.
> **Dana:** Same. So, fun news: the system's down again.
> **You:** Oh, what a surprise. Big fan of Mondays.
> **Dana:** Ha, right? IT says "an hour". So, like, three hours.
> **You:** Tell me about it.

**When sarcasm is misread, and you fix it.**

> **Sam:** How was the dentist?
> **You:** Oh, amazing. Best afternoon of my life.
> **Sam:** Really? That's great!
> **You:** Ha, no, I'm kidding. Two fillings. It was awful.
> **Sam:** Oh no! Okay, I didn't get that.

### Common mistakes

❌ Saying "Oh great, just what I needed" in a bright, happy voice.
✅ Saying it flat and low, or over-the-top and stretched.
*Reason: without the tone, people think you mean it.*

❌ A: "Oh great, the printer's broken again." B: "That's good!"
✅ B: "Ugh, again? What a surprise."
*Reason: answering the words instead of the meaning. Check: does the situation match the words?*

❌ Using sarcasm about someone's work: "Wow, nice slides." (when they're bad)
✅ Use it about the situation: "Wow, this projector really hates us today."
*Reason: sarcasm about a person can hurt, and your tone may be read as serious.*

❌ Using sarcasm when a friend shares real bad news: "Oh, great."
✅ "Oh no, I'm so sorry. That's rough."
*Reason: sarcasm is for small annoyances, not real problems.*

❌ Texting sarcasm to a new coworker: "Wow, thanks for the super clear email."
✅ Keep writing sincere with people you don't know well.
*Reason: in text there is no tone, so sarcasm looks rude.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill, "everyday annoyances" edition** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Everyday annoyances (40 min).** Pick a topic without looking (write them on paper or shuffle in a notes app). Talk for **90 seconds**, recording, and include **at least two sarcastic lines** with the right tone, plus **one sincere line** at the end (what you actually think). Do all 10.

1. Phone updates
2. Monday mornings
3. Waiting rooms
4. Slow Wi-Fi
5. Self-checkout machines at the supermarket
6. Group chats with 40 unread messages
7. Assembling furniture
8. Airport security lines
9. Umbrellas that break
10. Passwords you have to change every month

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Tell the story of a day when everything went wrong, one thing after another."** 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each. Add at least three sarcastic comments in each version ("…and then, of course, it started raining. Perfect."), and use your [storytelling connectors](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).

Listen to one version back and check: does each sarcastic line *sound* sarcastic? If you can't tell, the listener can't either. Exaggerate the tone more.
