## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: spelling names on the phone.** Letters are hard to hear on the phone, especially **B/D/E/P/T/V/Z**, **M/N**, **F/S**, and the vowels **A/E/I**. Americans fix this with "**[letter] as in [word]**", using any common word (there's no single official list in daily life). Say the letter clearly, then the word.

| A | as in apple | N | as in Nancy / nose |
| B | as in boy | O | as in orange |
| C | as in cat | P | as in Peter / pizza |
| D | as in dog | Q | as in queen |
| E | as in egg | R | as in Robert / red |
| F | as in Frank | S | as in Sam / sugar |

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Calling businesses

### Explanation

Calling a business is a short, goal-focused call. The other person (receptionist, agent, waiter) does this all day. They want three things from you: **who you are, what you want, and the details**. If you give those clearly, the call is easy.

**The shape of a business call:**

1. **Greeting + goal**: "Hi, I'd like to book a table for Friday." (one sentence, straight away)
2. **Answer their questions**: name (spelled), phone number, date, time, number of people, order number.
3. **Ask your questions**: price, cancellation policy, parking, what to bring.
4. **Confirm**: read back the booking (Day 1 skill): "So that's Friday the 15th at eight, for four people, under Moreno."
5. **Close**: "Great, thanks for your help. Have a good one!"

**Polite but clear.** Use "I'd like…", "Could you…?", "Would it be possible to…?" These are polite without being long. Then say exactly what you need.

**Complaints on the phone** (building on [Week 12 · Day 1](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-1-ordering-and-complaining.md)): stay calm, give the facts (order number, date), say what you want ("a refund", "a replacement", "someone to come and fix it"), and ask what happens next. Remember the person on the phone didn't cause the problem.

**Phone menus and hold.** "Press 1 for…": listen for the key word. If a machine asks you to "say" something, speak slowly and use short words: "Billing." "Speak to an agent." When they say "Can you hold for a moment?", answer "Sure, no problem." When they come back: "Thanks for holding." → "No worries."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hi, I'd like to make a reservation / an appointment. | you open with your goal | "Hi, I'd like to make a reservation for Friday night." |
| I'm calling to check on my order. | you follow up on something | "I'm calling to check on my order. It was supposed to arrive Monday." |
| Do you have anything available on…? | you ask about free times | "Do you have anything available on Thursday afternoon?" |
| What's the earliest you have? | you want the first free slot | "What's the earliest you have next week?" |
| It's [name], [letter] as in [word]… | you spell your name | "It's Moreno. M as in Mary, O-R-E-N as in Nancy-O." |
| My order number is… | you give a reference | "My order number is four-four-seven-one." |
| Sure, I'll hold. | they ask you to wait | "Sure, I'll hold. No problem." |
| Could I speak to someone about…? | you need the right person | "Could I speak to someone about my bill?" |
| Would it be possible to…? | a polite request | "Would it be possible to move it to Saturday?" |
| Is there a cancellation fee? | you check the rules | "If I need to cancel, is there a cancellation fee?" |
| Could you send me a confirmation? | you want it in writing | "Could you send me a confirmation by email?" |
| Thanks for your help — have a good one! | you close politely | "Perfect. Thanks for your help — have a good one!" |

### Examples

**Booking a restaurant.**

> **Host:** Good evening, Bella's, this is Tony speaking.
> **You:** Hi, I'd like to make a reservation for Friday night, please.
> **Host:** Sure. For how many?
> **You:** Four people. Around eight, if possible.
> **Host:** Let me check… I have seven-forty-five or eight-thirty.
> **You:** Eight-thirty works.
> **Host:** Great. And the name?
> **You:** Moreno. M as in Mary, O, R, E, N as in Nancy, O.
> **Host:** And a phone number?
> **You:** Five-five-five, oh-one-nine-two.
> **Host:** Perfect.
> **You:** So just to confirm: Friday at eight-thirty, four people, under Moreno?
> **Host:** You got it.
> **You:** Great. Oh, and is there parking nearby?
> **Host:** There's a garage on the corner.
> **You:** Perfect, thanks for your help — have a good one!

**Checking on a late order (and complaining calmly).**

> **Agent:** Thanks for calling Home Basics, this is Kara. How can I help?
> **You:** Hi, I'm calling to check on my order. It was supposed to arrive last Monday, and it's still not here.
> **Agent:** I'm sorry about that. Can I get your order number?
> **You:** Sure, it's four-four-seven-one.
> **Agent:** Four-four-seven-one… and the name on the order?
> **You:** Alex Moreno.
> **Agent:** Thank you. Can you hold for a moment?
> **You:** Sure, I'll hold.
> **Agent:** Thanks for holding. It looks like it was sent to the wrong address.
> **You:** Oh no. It should be forty-eight Pine Street, Apartment three-B. B as in boy.
> **Agent:** I'm so sorry. I can send a new one, arriving Thursday.
> **You:** Would it be possible to get free shipping, since it was a mistake?
> **Agent:** Absolutely, I'll waive it.
> **You:** Great. Could you send me a confirmation by email?
> **Agent:** Of course.
> **You:** Thanks so much for your help.

**Changing a doctor's appointment.**

> **Receptionist:** Dr. Patel's office.
> **You:** Hi, this is Alex Moreno. I have an appointment on Thursday the twelfth at ten-thirty, and I need to change it.
> **Receptionist:** No problem. What day works better?
> **You:** What's the earliest you have next week?
> **Receptionist:** Monday the sixteenth at nine.
> **You:** Sixteen — one-six?
> **Receptionist:** Yes, one-six.
> **You:** Perfect. Is there a fee for changing it?
> **Receptionist:** Not with more than 24 hours' notice.
> **You:** Great. So Monday the sixteenth at nine. Thank you!

### Common mistakes

❌ "I want an appointment."
✅ "I'd like to book an appointment." / "Could I make an appointment?"
*Reason: "I want" sounds like a demand to a stranger.*

❌ "A reservation for four persons."
✅ "A reservation for four people." / "A table for four."
*Reason: "persons" is used in legal language, not in daily speech.*

❌ Spelling with letters only: "M-O-R-E-N-O" fast.
✅ "M as in Mary, O, R, E, N as in Nancy, O."
*Reason: M/N, B/V/D, F/S are easy to confuse on the phone.*

❌ "Is it possible to change the hour?"
✅ "Would it be possible to change the time?"
*Reason: in English, an appointment has a "time", not an "hour".*

❌ When put on hold: "I'm waiting." / silence.
✅ "Sure, I'll hold." / "No problem."
*Reason: a quick, friendly "sure" is the expected answer.*

❌ Getting angry at the agent: "This is your fault!"
✅ "I understand it's not your fault, but I really need this fixed."
*Reason: calm and clear gets better results on the phone.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, a full phone-style call, eyes closed

Tonight Claude plays **three different businesses**. You close your eyes for every call. No text on the screen: just voices. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read (then close your eyes):

```
Let's do realistic phone calls to businesses. I'll keep my eyes closed, so everything is just voice.
My practice identity: Alex Moreno, phone 555-0192, email alex.moreno@example.com,
address 48 Pine Street, Apartment 3B.
Play 3 businesses, one at a time:
1) a busy restaurant (I want a table for 6 on Saturday; you only have awkward times),
2) an internet company (my Wi-Fi has been down for 3 days; start with a phone menu, put me on hold once, transfer me once),
3) a hair salon or dentist (I need to change an appointment).
Before each call, tell me only: the business name and my goal. Then answer the phone like a real employee.
Ask me to spell my name and give numbers. Mishear one letter or number per call so I must correct it.
Speak at natural speed. Don't correct me until I say "feedback". After each call, give me feedback:
was I clear and polite, did I confirm the details, and up to 3 mistakes.
```

**Step 2 (35 min).** Three calls, with feedback after each one. Targets for every call:
- Goal in the first sentence.
- Name spelled with "as in" for difficult letters.
- At least one question from you (price, fee, parking, confirmation).
- A read-back before you close.
- One calm correction of a misheard detail.

**Step 3 (15 min).** Ask for one more call: *"Now a harder one: call a hotel to complain that my booking for March fifteenth to eighteenth disappeared. The agent is friendly but slow, and the line is a bit bad."* Use [Day 3](day-3-bad-connections.md) phrases if the "line breaks".

**Step 4 (8 min).** Open your eyes. Write down the three corrections you got most. Say each correct version three times.
