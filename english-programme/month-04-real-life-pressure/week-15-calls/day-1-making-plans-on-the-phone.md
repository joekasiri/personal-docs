## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: times and dates on the phone.** On the phone, times and dates are where mistakes happen. Americans usually say times as two numbers ("seven-thirty", "two-fifteen") and dates as "the fifteenth", "March third". Say the number clearly and **stress it**, and put a tiny pause before it.

| Written | Said | Respelling |
|---|---|---|
| 7:30 | seven-thirty | "SEV-en THUR-dee" (flap T) |
| 2:15 | two-fifteen / a quarter after two | "two fif-TEEN" |
| 6:45 | six forty-five / a quarter to seven | "six FOR-dee-five" |
| 12:00 p.m. | noon / twelve | "NOON" |
| Thu 14th | Thursday the fourteenth | "THURZ-day the for-TEENTH" |
| Mar 3 | March third | "MARCH THIRD" |

Practice sentences (stress the CAPITALS):

1. "Does THURSDAY work for you?"
2. "How about SEVEN-THIRTY? At Luca's?"
3. "Wait, is that the FOURTEENTH or the FORTIETH— I mean, the fourteenth, right?"
4. "Let's say a QUARTER to seven, just to be safe."
5. "Okay, so just to confirm: SATURDAY, TEN a.m., at the PARK."
6. "I'm free any time after FIVE."
7. "Can we do it the week AFTER? Like, the TWENTY-second?"
8. "Hey, it's SAM. Got a sec?"
9. "Is now a BAD time?"
10. "Okay, I'll let you GO. Talk SOON!"

**Clip task:** speak along with a phone-call clip for 4 minutes. Then close your eyes and listen once more without speaking: can you write down every time and day mentioned? Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Making plans on the phone

### Explanation

A phone call has a shape. If you know the shape, you don't have to think about *what comes next*, only about *what to say*.

1. **Open.** Say who you are (even if they have your number: "Hey, it's Sam!"), then check timing: "Got a sec?" / "Is now a good time?"
2. **Reason.** Say why you're calling in one sentence: "So I'm calling about Saturday."
3. **Plan.** Suggest, ask, agree (you know this from [Week 12](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-2-making-plans.md)). On the phone, give **one detail at a time** and wait for "okay" before the next one.
4. **Read back.** This is the most important step on the phone. Repeat the plan in one sentence: "Okay, so just to confirm: Thursday, seven-thirty, at Luca's." It catches every mistake.
5. **Close.** Signal the end ("Okay, cool…", "Alright, I'll let you go"), say the next step ("I'll text you the address"), and say goodbye ("See you then! Bye!"). Americans often say "bye" two or three times: "Okay, bye! Bye-bye!"

**Why reading back matters:** on the phone, *fifteen* and *fifty*, *Tuesday* and *Thursday*, *Fifth Street* and *Fifth Avenue* are easy to confuse. Reading back takes three seconds and saves you from standing at the wrong restaurant.

**Video calls** follow the same shape. The opening is often "Hey! Can you hear me okay?" (more on that on [Day 3](day-3-bad-connections.md)).

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hey, it's [name]. Got a sec? | you open a call with a friend | "Hey, it's Maya. Got a sec?" |
| Is now a bad time? | you check if they can talk | "Is now a bad time? I can call back." |
| So I'm calling about… | you give the reason | "So I'm calling about Jake's party." |
| Does [day] work for you? | you suggest a day | "Does Thursday work for you?" |
| What time were you thinking? | you ask them to suggest a time | "Sure! What time were you thinking?" |
| Let me just check my calendar. | you need a moment | "Let me just check my calendar… okay, I'm free." |
| Okay, so just to confirm — | you read back the plan | "Okay, so just to confirm — Thursday at 7:30, at Luca's?" |
| Wait, was that [X] or [Y]? | you check a detail that's easy to confuse | "Wait, was that Tuesday or Thursday?" |
| Can I call you back in five? | you can't talk right now | "I'm just getting on the train. Can I call you back in five?" |
| I'll text you the address. | you move details to text | "I'll text you the address, it's kind of hard to find." |
| Sounds good — see you then! | you agree and close | "Sounds good — see you Thursday!" |
| Okay, I'll let you go. | a polite way to end the call | "Okay, I'll let you go. Talk soon!" |

### Examples

**Making dinner plans with a friend (phone).**

> **You:** Hey, Jess! It's me. Got a sec?
> **Jess:** Hey! Yeah, I'm just walking home. What's up?
> **You:** So I'm calling about dinner this week. Does Thursday work for you?
> **Jess:** Thursday… let me check. Yeah, I'm free after seven.
> **You:** Cool. How about seven-thirty at Luca's? The Italian place on Fifth?
> **Jess:** Fifth Street or Fifth Avenue?
> **You:** Fifth Street. Near the library.
> **Jess:** Oh, okay, perfect.
> **You:** Okay, so just to confirm — Thursday, seven-thirty, Luca's on Fifth Street.
> **Jess:** Yep. Should I book?
> **You:** No, I'll do it. I'll text you the confirmation.
> **Jess:** Amazing. Thanks!
> **You:** Okay, I'll let you go. See you Thursday! Bye!
> **Jess:** Bye! Bye-bye!

**Rescheduling with a coworker (video call).**

> **Dana:** Hey! Can you hear me?
> **You:** Yep, loud and clear. Is now still a good time?
> **Dana:** Sure, I've got ten minutes.
> **You:** Great. So I'm calling about our review on the fifteenth. Something came up — could we move it?
> **Dana:** Sure. What were you thinking?
> **You:** Maybe the week after? Like, Tuesday the twenty-second?
> **Dana:** The twenty-second… let me just check my calendar. I can do two or four.
> **You:** Four works. So just to confirm: Tuesday the twenty-second, four p.m.
> **Dana:** Perfect. I'll move the invite.
> **You:** Thanks so much. Okay, talk soon!

**Bad timing: calling back.**

> **Leo:** Hey, what's up?
> **You:** Hey, it's me. Is now a bad time?
> **Leo:** Kinda — I'm literally in line at the pharmacy. Can I call you back in five?
> **You:** Sure, no rush.
> *(ten minutes later)*
> **Leo:** Okay, sorry! I'm free. What's up?

### Common mistakes

❌ "Hello, here is Sam." / "Hello, I am Sam."
✅ "Hey, it's Sam." (formal: "Hi, this is Sam.")
*Reason: on the phone, English uses "it's" or "this is", never "here is".*

❌ "We see us on Thursday."
✅ "See you Thursday!"
*Reason: this is a fixed chunk. No "we" and no "on" is needed in casual speech.*

❌ "I call you back."
✅ "I'll call you back." / "Can I call you back?"
*Reason: a decision made now about the future needs "will" ('ll).*

❌ "On 7:30."
✅ "At 7:30." (and "on Thursday", "in March")
*Reason: "at" + clock time, "on" + day, "in" + month.*

❌ Saying goodbye without confirming any details.
✅ "Okay, so just to confirm — Thursday, 7:30, at Luca's."
*Reason: on the phone, mishearing is normal. Reading back fixes it.*

❌ Ending with a sudden "Okay. Bye." in the middle of the plan.
✅ "Okay, cool. I'll let you go. See you Thursday! Bye!"
*Reason: calls need a "closing signal" so the other person isn't surprised.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill in "phone mode"** (40 min), then **one 3/2/1 round** (20 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Phone mode rules:** hold your phone to your ear (or wear earphones) as if you're on a call. Eyes closed or looking at a wall. No hands: don't gesture. Start each topic with a call opening ("Hey, it's me — got a sec? So…") and end with a closing ("Anyway, I'll let you go. Talk soon!").

**Part A: Phone-mode topics (40 min).** Pick at random, talk for **90 seconds** each, record. Imagine you are calling a friend to tell them about it.

1. A new café or restaurant you found
2. A problem with your apartment
3. A movie or series you just watched
4. Your plans for next weekend
5. Something funny that happened at work
6. A gift you need help choosing
7. A trip you want to plan together
8. Something you bought online that went wrong
9. Your thoughts about moving to another city
10. A sport or game you watched recently

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (20 min).** Prompt: **"Explain on the phone how to get from the nearest train or bus station to your home: streets, turns, landmarks, how long it takes."** No hands, no map, eyes closed. 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute. In each version include at least **two numbers** (minutes, house number, bus number) and end with a read-back: "So, just to recap: bus 15, get off at…"
