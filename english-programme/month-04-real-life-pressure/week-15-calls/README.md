# Week 15: Phone and video calls
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 4: Real-life pressure](../README.md) · [← Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md) · [Week 16 →](../week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md)

## This week's goal

On the phone, you lose everything that usually helps you: faces, gestures, lips to read, a menu to point at. The sound is worse, there is a small delay, and numbers and names get lost. That is why many people who are fine in face-to-face English dread calls. This week you train for exactly that: **making plans on the phone**, **leaving voice messages**, **handling bad connections**, **calling businesses**, and **sending casual voice notes to friends**. You will learn to open and close calls smoothly, say and check numbers, times and spellings, and keep going when the line is bad. By Day 5 you will have sent real English voice notes to a real friend.

## How this builds on last week

[Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/README.md) taught you to ask for repeats, and [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md) gave you light sarcasm and humour. On calls you will use both all the time: "Sorry, you cut out — say that again?" and "Ah, technology. Love it." You will also recycle the role-plays from Month 3, especially [making plans](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-2-making-plans.md) and [ordering and complaining](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-1-ordering-and-complaining.md). Those were face-to-face. This week the same tasks happen with no faces.

## Before you start

- **Find your "call people".** You need at least one friend, colleague or language partner who will take a 10–15 minute phone or video call (Day 3, Day 6) and swap voice notes with you (Day 5). Message them now.
- **Shadowing clips:** search "phone call English conversation real", "customer service call example", "voicemail examples", "video call podcast remote interview", "radio call-in show". You want audio where the sound is a little worse than a studio.
- Put your phone in a separate room from your laptop for drills: tonight's rule is **eyes closed or looking away**. No lip-reading.
- Recording app, [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md), [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), [voice mode](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Voice mode is already a "call" with no face, so it's perfect practice.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes, then rest.** Listen to a radio call-in show or a podcast where listeners phone in (search "call-in radio show advice", "listener calls podcast"). Your task: listen to the **first 10 seconds and the last 10 seconds** of each call. How do callers open? How does the host close the call? Write down one opening and one closing you liked. If you get a voice note from your friend, reply to it in English. Then rest.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Open a call, make a plan and confirm every detail (day, time, place) by reading it back.
- [ ] Leave a clear 30-second voicemail with your name, reason, number (said twice) and next step.
- [ ] Handle a bad line or a frozen video calmly with the right phrases.
- [ ] Book, check or complain about something by phone, spelling names and giving numbers clearly.
- [ ] Hear and say "fifteen" vs "fifty" correctly.
- [ ] Send relaxed, one-take English voice notes to a friend.
