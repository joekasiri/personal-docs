## Before you start

- Finish Days 1–4.
- Tell one or two friends (or a language partner) that you will send them **three English voice notes tonight** and that they can reply in English if they want. Use any messaging app.
- Quick refresh (do not re-study): casual reported speech ("I was like…", "she goes…") from [Week 5 · Day 3](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-3-casual-reported-speech.md), and changing topic from [Week 3 · Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md). Voice notes are full of both.

## Learning objectives

- Record a relaxed, one-take voice note without re-recording.
- Open and close a voice note naturally.
- Reply to someone else's voice note point by point.
- Keep a voice note short and easy to listen to.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: relaxed voice-note tone.** Voice notes sound like talking to a friend who is right there: relaxed, a bit fast, with natural hesitation ("like", "you know", "I mean", which you practised in [Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-4-staying-on-track.md)), laughs, and background life ("Sorry, I'm walking"). This week's numbers practice continues: numbers in casual speech are often rounded ("like, twenty minutes", "around six-ish").

Practice sentences (relaxed, smiling, a little fast):

1. "Okay, so, quick update…"
2. "Sorry, I'm walking, so it's kinda windy."
3. "Okay, I just listened to yours, and — ha — oh my god."
4. "Wait, I have to tell you something."
5. "Not gonna lie, I was kinda nervous."
6. "It was, like, twenty minutes from my place. Maybe twenty-five."
7. "Let's say six-ish? Six-thirty-ish?"
8. "Anyway, I'm rambling. Sorry."
9. "Oh, and one more thing —"
10. "Lemme know what you think!"
11. "Okay, that's it. Bye! Love you, bye!"

**Clip task:** search "vlog talking to camera casual", "voice memo style podcast", "walking vlog chatting". Pick someone who talks to the camera like a friend. Speak along for 4 minutes. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Voice notes to friends

### Explanation

Voice notes are half phone call, half text message. They're perfect practice for "talking without thinking" because there's no one to interrupt you, but there *is* someone who will hear you, so it's real.

The biggest problem for learners is **perfectionism**: recording a 40-second voice note eight times because of one small mistake. Native speakers make mistakes in voice notes all the time and just keep going. Tonight's rule: **one take only**. If you make a mistake, fix it in the moment ("I mean…", which you learnt in [Week 11 · Day 2](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-2-self-correction.md)) and keep going.

**The shape of a voice note:**

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Open (react or start) | "Okay, so, quick update…" / "Okay, I just listened to yours and…" |
| Main thing (one or two topics) | "So the interview was today, and honestly it went pretty well…" |
| Ask or invite | "What about you? How was the trip?" / "Lemme know if Saturday works." |
| Close | "Anyway, I'll let you go. Talk soon! Bye!" |

**Replying to a voice note.** Go point by point, and say which point you're answering: "Okay, so first, the cat — oh my god. Second, yes, Saturday works. And third…" This is easy for the listener to follow.

**Length.** Aim for 30 seconds to 2 minutes. Longer than that? Say sorry with a smile ("Sorry, this is a podcast now") or call instead.

**Background life is okay.** "Sorry, I'm on the bus." "Hang on, the dog's barking." It makes voice notes feel friendly.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Okay, so, quick update… | you start with news | "Okay, so, quick update on the apartment hunt…" |
| Okay, I just listened to yours and… | you reply to their voice note | "Okay, I just listened to yours and I'm dying." |
| Wait, I have to tell you something. | you have exciting news | "Wait, I have to tell you something. You'll never guess who I saw." |
| Not gonna lie… | an honest, casual comment | "Not gonna lie, I was kinda jealous." |
| So first… and second… | you answer several points | "So first, congrats! And second, yes, I'm in." |
| Sorry for the long voice note! | you talked too much | "Sorry for the long voice note! I got excited." |
| Anyway, I'm rambling. | you realise you've gone off topic | "Anyway, I'm rambling. The point is: I'm free Friday." |
| Oh, and one more thing — | you add something at the end | "Oh, and one more thing — bring a jacket." |
| Lemme know what you think. | you ask for their opinion | "Here's the plan. Lemme know what you think." |
| Call me when you're free. | it's easier to talk | "It's a long story. Call me when you're free." |
| No need to reply, just thought you'd laugh. | sharing something light | "No need to reply, just thought you'd laugh." |
| Okay, that's it. Bye! | you close | "Okay, that's it. Talk soon. Bye!" |

### Examples

**Model voice note 1: a quick update.**

> "Okay, so, quick update. The interview was today — the design job I told you about? And not gonna lie, I was super nervous, like, my hands were shaking. But it went pretty well, I think? They asked me to explain a project and I just… talked. Like, for ten minutes. In English. No freezing! Anyway, they'll let me know next week. What about you? How was the dentist? Okay, that's it. Bye!"

**Model voice note 2: replying point by point.**

> "Okay, I just listened to yours and — ha — the goat. I can't. So first, *of course* Kevin brought a goat. That's so Kevin. Second, yes, Saturday works for me, but can we do, like, seven instead of six? I have a thing in the afternoon. And third, I'll bring dessert. Not the salty cookies, I promise. Lemme know about the time. Bye!"

**Model voice note 3: a funny moment (no reply needed).**

> "Wait, I have to tell you something. I'm on the train right now, sorry if it's loud, and the guy next to me has a parrot. On his shoulder. Like a pirate. And the parrot just said 'Next stop' before the announcement. It's a better driver than the driver. Anyway — no need to reply, just thought you'd laugh. Bye!"

### Common mistakes

❌ Re-recording the same voice note ten times.
✅ One take. If you make a mistake: "…on Thursday — I mean, Tuesday — …" and keep going.
*Reason: voice notes are casual. Re-recording trains you to freeze, not to flow.*

❌ "Dear Sam, I hope you are well. I am writing to you to…"
✅ "Hey Sam! Okay, so…"
*Reason: a voice note isn't a letter. Talk like you would face to face.*

❌ "I send you a voice."
✅ "I'll send you a voice note." / "I'll send you a voice memo."
*Reason: the noun is "voice note" or "voice message"; and you need "I'll" for a decision.*

❌ A six-minute voice note about everything.
✅ 30 seconds to 2 minutes, one or two topics.
*Reason: people listen to voice notes on the go. Long ones get skipped.*

❌ Answering a voice note with no structure, mixing all their questions.
✅ "So first… second… and third…"
*Reason: point by point is easy to follow.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: the **random-topic drill as voice notes** (30 min), **one 3/2/1 round** (15 min), then **send three real voice notes** (15 min). See [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) and [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part A: Random-topic voice notes (30 min).** Pick a topic at random. Record it as a **voice note to a friend** (60–90 seconds), one take only. Open with a voice-note opening, end with a closing. Do 10.

1. A place you went this week
2. Something you're excited about
3. A small annoying thing that happened today (use sarcasm from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md))
4. A recommendation (a show, a café, an app)
5. A question you want your friend's advice on
6. Something funny you saw on the street
7. Plans for next month
8. A memory you share with this friend
9. Food you cooked or ate recently
10. A reply to this message from a friend: *"Hey! Long time no talk. What's new with you??"*

**Part B: one 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Prompt: **"Tell a friend about your week: one good thing, one bad thing, one funny thing."** 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute (recording). The 1-minute version should sound exactly like a voice note you'd really send.

**Part C: Send three real voice notes (15 min).** Send **three English voice notes** to a real friend, family member or language partner (each 30–90 seconds, one take):

1. **A life update:** your week (use your 1-minute 3/2/1 version).
2. **A plan:** suggest meeting or calling, with a day and a time.
3. **Something light:** a funny moment, a recommendation, or a question.

If they reply, answer their voice note point by point tomorrow. Write in your nightly log who you sent them to and how many takes you needed (target: one each).
