## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: fifteen vs fifty.** On a bad line, the "-teen" and "-ty" numbers are the most confused words in English. The difference is **stress** and the **end sound**:

| -teen (13–19) | -ty (30–90) |
|---|---|
| fif-TEEN: stress on the end, long "eeen", clear T | FIF-dee: stress on the start, short "dee" (flap T) |
| thir-TEEN | THIR-dee |
| four-TEEN | FOR-dee |
| six-TEEN | SIX-dee |
| eigh-TEEN | AY-dee |
| nine-TEEN | NINE-dee |

When in doubt, Americans clarify by spelling the digits: "fifteen — one-five" or "fifty — five-oh".

Practice sentences:

1. "It's FIF-teen — sorry, fif-TEEN. One-five."
2. "The bus is at six-FIF-dee… I mean, six-fif-TEEN."
3. "There'll be about THIR-dee people. Three-oh."
4. "Gate four-TEEN. One-four."
5. "It costs NINE-dee dollars. Nine-oh."
6. "Sorry, you're breaking up. Did you say fif-TEEN or FIF-dee?"
7. "Can you hear me NOW?"
8. "You cut out for a SEC — the last thing I heard was 'Friday'."
9. "I think you're on MUTE."
10. "You're FROZEN. Oh, okay, you're back."
11. "Let me call you BACK, the line's really bad."

**Clip task:** search "teen vs ty numbers pronunciation American", or use a numbers listening clip. Then search "phone call bad connection funny" and speak along with a real call for 3 minutes. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Bad connections

### Explanation

Bad connections happen to everyone: native speakers say "You're breaking up" all the time. So a bad line is not your fault and not a language problem. The skill is to name the problem quickly, calmly and with the right phrase, then fix it. If you just stay silent or say "What?" five times, the other person gets confused about whether *you* understood.

**Three levels of problem:**

| Problem | What happens | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| Small: you missed a word | a crackle, a car, a dog | "Sorry, you cut out for a sec. You said Friday at…?" |
| Medium: the line keeps breaking | every sentence has gaps | "You're breaking up a bit. Can you hear me okay?" / "Can you say that again, slowly?" |
| Big: you can't continue | constant gaps, a frozen video, a long delay | "The line's really bad. Let me call you back." / "I'm gonna turn my video off." / "I'll text you the details." |

**Say what you last heard.** "The last thing I heard was 'the restaurant on…'." This is like the echo questions from [Week 13](../week-13-group-conversations/day-4-asking-people-to-repeat.md): the other person repeats only the missing part.

**Video calls** add new problems: frozen screens, mute, echo, talking over each other because of the delay. When two people start talking at the same time, say "Sorry, go ahead" (you learnt this in [Week 13 · Day 3](../week-13-group-conversations/day-3-holding-your-turn.md)) or "No, you go."

**Humour helps.** A light sarcastic line from [Week 14](../week-14-humour-and-banter/day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md) relaxes everyone: "Ah, technology. Love it." "Okay, my Wi-Fi hates me today."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| You're breaking up. | their voice comes in pieces | "Sorry, you're breaking up a little." |
| Can you hear me now? | you moved or fixed something | "Okay, I moved to the window. Can you hear me now?" |
| Sorry, you cut out for a sec. | you missed a short part | "Sorry, you cut out for a sec. What time?" |
| I lost you there. | you missed a longer part | "I lost you there. The last thing I heard was 'Saturday'." |
| The last thing I heard was… | you say where the gap started | "The last thing I heard was 'the train leaves at…'." |
| You're frozen. | their video stopped | "Oh, you're frozen. Wait… okay, you're back." |
| I think you're on mute. | they're talking but there's no sound | "I think you're on mute — I can see you talking." |
| There's a bit of a delay. | the delay makes you talk over each other | "There's a bit of a delay, so sorry if I talk over you." |
| Sorry, go ahead — we talked over each other. | you both started at once | "Sorry, go ahead — we talked over each other." |
| Can you hear me okay? | you check your own sound | "Can you hear me okay? My headphones are acting weird." |
| Let me call you back, the line's really bad. | the call can't continue | "Let me call you back, the line's really bad." |
| I'm gonna turn my video off. | video makes the connection worse | "I'm gonna turn my video off, it might help." |

### Examples

**A friend calls from the train.**

> **Leo:** Hey! So I'm — *(crackle)* — station and — *(crackle)* — Friday.
> **You:** Sorry, you're breaking up. I lost you there. The last thing I heard was "station".
> **Leo:** Okay — can you hear me now?
> **You:** Yeah, better.
> **Leo:** I said I'm at the station and I'll be in town on Friday.
> **You:** Oh, nice! What time do you get in?
> **Leo:** Like six-fifteen.
> **You:** Six-fifteen — one-five — or six-fifty?
> **Leo:** One-five. Fifteen.
> **You:** Okay, got it. Six-fifteen on Friday. Text me when you're close, the line's really bad.

**A video call with a delay.**

> **Priya:** So I think we should —
> **You:** So for the slides —
> **Priya:** Oh, sorry —
> **You:** Sorry, go ahead — we talked over each other. There's a bit of a delay.
> **Priya:** No worries. I was saying we should start with the user research.
> **You:** Agreed. Oh — wait, you're frozen.
> **Priya:** …hello?
> **You:** Okay, you're back. I'm gonna turn my video off, it might help.
> **Priya:** Good idea. Ah, technology.
> **You:** Love it.

**Someone on mute.**

> **You:** Marcus, I think you're on mute. I can see your mouth moving.
> **Marcus:** — sorry! Can you hear me now?
> **You:** Yep, loud and clear.
> **Marcus:** Classic. Okay, so…

### Common mistakes

❌ "You are cutting."
✅ "You're cutting out." / "You cut out for a sec."
*Reason: the phrasal verb is "cut out".*

❌ "I can't listen to you."
✅ "I can't hear you."
*Reason: "hear" is the sound reaching your ears. "Listen" is paying attention.*

❌ "Your voice is broken."
✅ "You're breaking up."
*Reason: "breaking up" is the fixed phrase for a bad line.*

❌ "You are in mute."
✅ "You're on mute."
*Reason: "on mute" (like "on hold", "on speaker").*

❌ Saying "What? What? What?" louder and louder.
✅ "Sorry, I lost you. The last thing I heard was…"
*Reason: being specific saves time and stress for both of you.*

❌ Pretending you heard "fifteen" and arriving 35 minutes late.
✅ "Fifteen, one-five, or fifty, five-oh?"
*Reason: numbers are the most confused words on a bad line. Always check.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

### Part A: "Bad line" drill (30 min)

1. Put a fan, a running tap or a "bad connection sound effect" video near your phone or speaker.
2. Play a podcast or YouTube clip (any casual chat) **quietly** behind the noise, so it's hard to hear.
3. Every 30–60 seconds, pause it and react out loud as if it were a call: say what you heard, what you missed, and ask for the missing part using tonight's phrases ("Sorry, you cut out — the last thing I heard was…"). 10 minutes.
4. Then do a 3/2/1-style round without the noise: **"Tell a friend about a call or video call that went really wrong (a frozen screen, a mute moment, a terrible line)."** 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute, recording. Use at least three phrases from tonight inside the story. See [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md). 20 minutes.

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (30 min): a real call

**Mission:** make a **real phone or video call** in English (10–20 minutes) with a friend, colleague or language partner. No face-to-face tonight.

Try to:

1. **Open** with "Hey, it's [name]! Got a sec?" or "Can you hear me okay?"
2. **Make a plan** together (a meet-up, a call next week, a recommendation) and **read it back** ([Day 1](day-1-making-plans-on-the-phone.md)).
3. Use at least **two bad-connection phrases** (even if the line is fine, you can check: "Can you hear me okay?", "Sorry, you cut out for a sec").
4. Check **one number** with "fifteen, one-five?"
5. **Close** with "Okay, I'll let you go. Talk soon!"

**Backup plan:** voice mode with Claude. Paste:

```
Let's do a phone call. You're my friend NOOR, calling me from a train with a bad connection.
Every few sentences, "break up": replace part of your sentence with "...(crackle)..." so I miss
a key detail (a time, a day, a place, a number like fifteen/fifty). Sometimes "freeze" and say nothing
for a moment, then come back. We're trying to plan a dinner for 6 people next week.
I need to ask you to repeat, say what I last heard, check numbers, and read back the plan.
No corrections until I say "feedback". Then tell me how I handled the bad line and up to 5 mistakes.
```

Afterwards, write in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): who you called, how long, what was hard without a face.
