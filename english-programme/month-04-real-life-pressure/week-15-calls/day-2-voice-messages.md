## Learning objectives

- Leave a voicemail with a clear four-part structure.
- Say phone numbers in natural groups, slowly enough, and repeat them.
- Sound relaxed and friendly, even when the beep surprises you.
- Leave both casual (friend) and polite (business) voicemails.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: phone numbers in groups.** Americans say phone numbers **digit by digit, in groups**: 3 + 3 + 4. There's a small pause between groups and the voice goes **up** at the end of each group (meaning "more is coming") and **down** at the very end. Zero is usually "oh" (sometimes "zero"). Double numbers are usually said as two separate digits: "five-five" (some people say "double five", but it's less common in the US).

| Written | Said |
|---|---|
| (555) 019-2834 | "five-five-five ↗ / oh-one-nine ↗ / two-eight-three-four ↘" |
| 555-0147 | "five-five-five ↗ / oh-one-four-seven ↘" |
| ext. 204 | "extension two-oh-four" |
| #4471 (order number) | "four-four-seven-one" or "forty-four seventy-one" |

Practice sentences:

1. "My number is five-five-five ↗, oh-one-nine ↗, two-eight-three-four ↘."
2. "That's five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four. Again: five-five-five…"
3. "You can reach me at extension two-oh-four."
4. "Hi, this is Alex Moreno calling for Dr. Patel."
5. "I'm calling about my appointment on Thursday the twelfth."
6. "Could you give me a call back when you get a chance?"
7. "Hey, it's me! Call me when you get this."
8. "No rush. Or just text me, whatever's easier."
9. "I'll try you again later this afternoon."
10. "Okay, thanks so much — bye!"

**Clip task:** search "voicemail greeting examples", "leaving a voicemail English", "how to leave a professional voicemail". Speak along with two or three example voicemails. Copy the speed: a little slower than chatting, especially for names and numbers. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Voice messages

### Explanation

The beep is scary. There's no one to help you, you can't see a face, and you know you're being recorded. Most people panic, say "Umm, hi, uh, so…", and give the important details too fast. The fix is a **fixed structure** that you can say on autopilot, so your brain only needs to fill in the details.

**The four-part voicemail (about 30 seconds):**

| Part | What you say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Who | your name (and who you're calling, if needed) | "Hi, this is Alex Moreno, calling for Dr. Patel." |
| 2. Why | the reason, in one sentence | "I'm calling about my appointment on Thursday the twelfth." |
| 3. Details / ask | what you need | "I need to move it to the following week, if possible." |
| 4. Next step + number twice + bye | how to reach you, slowly | "Could you call me back at five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four? Again, that's five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four. Thanks so much. Bye!" |

**Casual voicemails** (to friends) are shorter and lighter: "Hey, it's me! Just calling about Saturday. Call me when you get this. Or just text. Bye!" No need to say your number.

**Business tips:** give your full name, and spell it if it's unusual (practise that on [Day 4](day-4-calling-businesses.md)). Say the date of anything you're calling about. Say numbers slowly, twice. Say when you'll try again, if relevant.

**If you mess up:** don't stop. Say "Sorry, let me say that again —" and repeat the detail. (Many phones also let you press a key to re-record, but don't count on it.)

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hi, this is [name], calling for [name]. | formal opening | "Hi, this is Alex Moreno, calling for Dr. Patel." |
| Hey, it's me! | casual opening (they know your voice) | "Hey, it's me! Guess what happened." |
| I'm calling about… | you give the reason | "I'm calling about the apartment on Oak Street." |
| Just wanted to check if… | a soft, friendly reason | "Just wanted to check if we're still on for tonight." |
| Could you give me a call back at…? | you ask for a callback | "Could you give me a call back at 555-0147?" |
| Again, that's… | you repeat the number | "Again, that's five-five-five, oh-one-four-seven." |
| I'll try you again later. | you'll call again | "I'll try you again later this afternoon." |
| No rush. | it isn't urgent | "No rush, whenever you get a chance." |
| Or just text me. | text is fine too | "Call me back, or just text me." |
| Call me when you get this. | casual ask | "Call me when you get this. It's good news!" |
| Sorry, let me say that again — | you made a mistake on the recording | "That's five-five-five, oh-one-nine… sorry, let me say that again." |
| Thanks so much — bye! | friendly close | "Thanks so much, have a good one — bye!" |

### Examples

**Model voicemail 1: rescheduling the dentist (polite).**

> "Hi, this is Alex Moreno, calling for Dr. Patel's office. I'm calling about my cleaning appointment on Thursday the twelfth at ten-thirty. Something's come up at work, so I'd like to move it to the following week, any morning is fine. Could you give me a call back at five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four? Again, that's five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four. Thanks so much. Bye!"

**Model voicemail 2: a friend's birthday (casual).**

> "Hey Jess, it's me! Happy birthday! I can't believe you're thirty. Okay, I can. Anyway, I hope you're having the best day. Just wanted to check if we're still doing drinks on Saturday, and what time. Call me when you get this, or just text me. Love you, bye!"

**Model voicemail 3: about an apartment (polite, with a question).**

> "Hi, my name's Alex Moreno. I'm calling about the one-bedroom apartment on Oak Street that I saw online. I wanted to ask if it's still available, and if I could come and see it this week. I'm free most evenings after six. My number is five-five-five, oh-one-four-seven. Again, five-five-five, oh-one-four-seven. I'll also try you again tomorrow morning. Thanks, bye!"

**Model voicemail 4: recovering from a mistake.**

> "Hi Mark, this is Alex from the design team. Just calling about the files for Monday's meeting. Could you send them by Friday? You can call me at five-five-five, oh-one… sorry, let me say that again. Five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four. Thanks, bye!"

### Common mistakes

❌ "Umm… hi… uh… so… it's… umm…" (panic at the beep)
✅ "Hi, this is Alex Moreno, calling about…" (your autopilot opening)
*Reason: practise the first sentence until it's automatic. The rest follows.*

❌ Saying the number once, fast: "555-019-2834-bye."
✅ In groups, slowly, twice: "five-five-five, oh-one-nine, two-eight-three-four. Again, that's…"
*Reason: the listener is writing it down. Give them time.*

❌ "I wait your call." / "I am waiting for your call."
✅ "Talk soon!" / "Hope to hear from you soon." / "Thanks, bye!"
*Reason: "I wait your call" is a direct translation; "I'm waiting for your call" sounds impatient.*

❌ "My number is five hundred fifty-five, nineteen…"
✅ "Five-five-five, oh-one-nine…"
*Reason: phone numbers are said digit by digit, in groups.*

❌ Not saying your name ("Hi, call me back, thanks!")
✅ "Hi, this is Alex…"
*Reason: they may not know your number.*

❌ A two-minute voicemail with your life story.
✅ About 30 seconds, four parts.
*Reason: long voicemails don't get listened to fully.*

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min): voice mode, the voicemail game

Tonight Claude plays an **answering machine** and the people who call you back. You leave messages, and they return your calls with questions. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 (2 min).** Paste or read:

```
Let's play "the voicemail game". You'll set up phone situations for me.
For each one: 1) tell me in ONE sentence who I'm calling and why (for example: "You're calling your dentist to cancel Friday's appointment"),
give me the key details (names, dates, times, a 555 phone number), then say "BEEP".
2) I leave a voicemail. 3) Then you play that person CALLING ME BACK: ask me 2-3 questions,
mishear one detail on purpose (a number or a day) so I have to correct it, and close the call naturally.
Mix casual situations (friends, family) and polite ones (doctor, landlord, a shop, a new colleague).
Don't correct me until I say "feedback". Then tell me: did my voicemails have all four parts
(who, why, details, next step + number twice)? Were my numbers clear? Up to 5 language mistakes.
Start with the first situation.
```

**Step 2 (25 min).** Do at least **6 situations**. Targets:
- Every voicemail under 40 seconds, with all four parts.
- Every phone number said in groups, twice.
- On each callback, catch the misheard detail and correct it: "Sorry, no — it's the *fifteenth*, one-five."
- Use Day 1's read-back on each callback ("So just to confirm…").

**Step 3 (5 min).** Say "feedback". Write down the corrections.

**Step 4 (20 min).** Harder round. Say: *"Same game, but now: give me the details only once, talk a bit faster, and in the callbacks make the conversation longer, with a change of plan."*

**Step 5 (8 min).** Record yourself (outside voice mode) leaving the best voicemail of the night one more time, perfectly. Listen back. Is the number clear enough to write down?
