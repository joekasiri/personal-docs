# Week 10: Hypotheticals
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 3: Spontaneity](../README.md) · [← Week 9: Talking on the spot](../week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md) · [Week 11: Never stopping →](../week-11-never-stopping/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you learn to talk about things that are **not real**: what you would do, what would have happened, what you wish, what might happen next. Hypotheticals are everywhere in casual conversation. Friends ask "What would you do if you won the lottery?", colleagues say "If we'd launched a week earlier, we would've been fine", and everybody talks about plans with "I'm thinking of…" and "I'll probably…". These questions come with **no warning**, and they are hard because the grammar feels long. By Day 6 you will answer any "what if" question straight away, using short, spoken forms like **I'd**, **woulda**, **shoulda** and **gonna** without building the sentence in your head first.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 9](../week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md) you learned to talk on the spot about real things: objects, places, people, experiences and "would you rather" choices. Last Friday's "would you rather" questions were your first taste of hypotheticals. This week goes deeper: you get the full set of patterns (second, third and mixed conditionals, wishes, what-ifs and the future) and you use them with zero preparation, in the same random-topic and voice-mode drills you used last week.

## Day 7: light day

30 minutes only. Put on a casual chat podcast with two or more people (search for "friends podcast what would you do", "hypothetical questions podcast" or "two friends chat would you rather") and listen while you walk. Your one job: **count the hypotheticals**. Every time you hear "I would've…", "if I had…", "I wish…" or "what if…", tap your leg. After the walk, say out loud the best one you heard, in your own words, and how you would answer it. Then rest. No recording, no logging.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Answer "What would you do if…?" in under 2 seconds with "I'd probably…" or "Honestly, I'd…".
- [ ] Talk about a past situation that went differently with "If I hadn't…, I would've…" and say it as "woulda".
- [ ] Mix past and present: "If I'd studied design earlier, I'd be a lead by now."
- [ ] Talk about wishes and regrets in a relaxed way, without sounding dramatic.
- [ ] Ask other people what-if questions and keep the conversation going for 5+ minutes.
- [ ] Talk about plans and predictions using gonna / will / might / probably / I'm thinking of, choosing the right one without thinking.
- [ ] Use at least 3 hypotheticals in a real live conversation.
