# Week 10 · Day 5: Future plans and predictions
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 3](../README.md) › [Week 10](README.md) · [← Previous: What-if questions](day-4-what-if-questions.md) · [Next: Weekly check →](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Main skill 30 min · Speaking drill 60 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- This week you imagined things that are not real. Tonight you talk about things that **might** be real: plans and predictions. It is the "possible" side of hypotheticals, and it comes up in almost every conversation ("Any plans for the weekend?", "Do you think it'll rain?", "Where do you see yourself in five years?").
- You already use "gonna" from [Week 1 shadowing](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md) and contractions like "I'll" from [Week 8](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-4-contractions-in-your-own-speech.md). Tonight is about **choosing** the right future form without thinking.
- Have ready: topic cards from section 3, recording app, timer, [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).

## Learning objectives

- Choose between **gonna / will / might / probably / I'm thinking of / I'm -ing** by how sure you are and when you decided.
- Use a "certainty scale" so your predictions sound natural, not too strong.
- Talk about plans that are not fixed yet: **I'm thinking of…, I might…, I'm hoping to…**
- Talk about the future for 1–2 minutes on a random topic with no preparation.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: I'll / it'll / gonna / might / probably.**

- **I'll** → sounds like "all" or "ahl". "I'll call you" → "ahl CALL you".
- **It'll** → "IDD-l" (flap T + dark L). "It'll be fine" → "IDD-l be FINE".
- **That'll** → "THAD-l". "That'll work" → "THAD-l WORK".
- **Going to** → "gonna" ("GUH-nuh"). With "I'm": "I'm gonna" → "ahm-uh-nuh" in very fast speech.
- **Might** → the T is a flap before a vowel: "might as well" → "my-duz-WELL"; before a consonant it's a tiny stop: "might go" → "my' GO".
- **Probably** → "PRAH-bly" (the middle "ab" almost disappears).
- **I'm thinking of** → "ahm THINK-in-uv".

**Practice sentences:**

1. AHL text you when I get THERE.
2. Don't worry, IDD-l be FINE.
3. I'm GUH-nuh grab some FOOD.
4. It'll PRAH-bly rain, so bring a JACKET.
5. I my' go to the GYM later. Not SURE yet.
6. Ahm THINK-in-uv moving next YEAR.
7. THAD-l work for ME.
8. We my-duz-WELL just ORDER pizza.
9. I'm PRAH-bly gonna stay HOME tonight.
10. IDD-l be DONE by Friday, I THINK.
11. I'm SEEing my parents on SUNday.

**Clip to find:** an American vlogger talking about their plans or goals for the year, or a weekly "plan with me" video. Search YouTube for "**my plans for next year vlog**", "**weekly plan with me chat**" or "**goals for the new year chatty**". Speak along with 2–3 minutes, twice, no pausing. Notice how often they say "probably" and "I'm thinking of". Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Future plans and predictions

### Explanation

English has many ways to talk about the future. You don't need grammar rules. You need **two questions**:

**Question 1: Is it a plan or a prediction?**

| | Plan (you decide) | Prediction (you guess) |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed, arranged (time and place) | **I'm -ing**: "I'm meeting Sarah at 7." | — |
| Already decided | **I'm gonna**: "I'm gonna paint my room this weekend." | **It's gonna**: "It's gonna rain — look at those clouds." (you see evidence) |
| Deciding right now | **I'll**: "Oh, you're busy? I'll do it." | **It'll / will**: "It'll be fine." (your opinion) |
| Not decided yet | **I'm thinking of -ing / I might / I'm hoping to** | **It might / It could** |

**Question 2: How sure are you?** Use the certainty scale. Most casual predictions sit in the middle, not at the ends:

| How sure | Phrase | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 100% | definitely / for sure | "I'm definitely gonna be there." |
| 80% | probably / I'll probably | "I'll probably be a bit late." |
| 70% | I bet… / I'm pretty sure… | "I bet it'll sell out." |
| 50% | might / maybe / could | "I might go, I might not." |
| 30% | I doubt… / probably not | "I doubt they'll finish it on time." |
| 0% | no way / definitely not | "There's no way it's gonna be ready." |

**Word order of "probably"** (a very common mistake):

- With **will**: probably goes **after** will → "I'll **probably** go." / Negative: "I **probably won't** go." (before won't)
- With **gonna**: "I'm **probably** gonna go." / "I'm **probably** not gonna go."

**Vague plans are normal.** When an American asks "Any plans for the weekend?", a perfect plan isn't needed. "Not really, I'm probably just gonna chill. I might see a movie." is a great answer. Then [add and ask back](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md).

**Bigger futures.** For "Where do you see yourself in five years?" questions, mix plans and hedges: "I'm hoping to…", "Ideally, I'd…", "If everything goes well, I'll…", "Who knows, maybe…". This connects back to Day 1's "I'd".

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I'm gonna… | you've already decided | "I'm gonna start running again." |
| I'm -ing (+ time) | it's arranged | "I'm flying to Chicago on Friday." |
| I'll… (decision now) | deciding in the moment, offering | "Oh, I'll grab the check." |
| I'm thinking of -ing… | a plan you're considering | "I'm thinking of taking a pottery class." |
| I might… | 50% plan | "I might stop by later." |
| I'm hoping to… | something you want but can't control | "I'm hoping to get promoted next year." |
| I'll probably… / I probably won't… | 80% / 20% | "I'll probably stay in. I probably won't go out." |
| I bet… | confident prediction, casual | "I bet he's gonna be late again." |
| I doubt… | you think it won't happen | "I doubt it'll be finished by Monday." |
| It's gonna be… | prediction with evidence | "Look at that line. It's gonna be a long wait." |
| Ideally, I'd… | the perfect version of your future | "Ideally, I'd be running my own studio." |
| We'll see. / Who knows? | ending a vague prediction | "Maybe I'll move. Who knows?" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: Friday at the office**

> A: Any plans for the weekend?
> B: Not much, honestly. I'm seeing my sister on Saturday, and then I'm probably just gonna chill. What about you?
> A: I'm thinking of going hiking, if the weather's OK.
> B: Ooh, I think it's gonna rain on Sunday, though. I saw the forecast.
> A: Ugh, really? Then I might go Saturday instead. I'll check tonight.

**Dialogue 2: a five-year question**

> A: So where do you see yourself in five years?
> B: Good question. Ideally, I'd be leading a small design team. I'm hoping to move into a lead role in the next year or two, so we'll see.
> A: Would you stay at the same company?
> B: Probably, if things keep going well. But I might try a startup at some point. I'm thinking of doing some freelance stuff on the side first, just to test it. Who knows?

**Model answer (1 minute): random card "Your next vacation"**

> OK, so my next vacation… I'm not a hundred percent sure yet, but I'm thinking of going to Portugal in the spring. My friend's getting married there in April, so I'm definitely gonna go to the wedding, that part's booked. And then I'll probably stay an extra week and travel a bit, maybe Porto and then down to the beaches. I might rent a car, I might just take trains, I haven't decided. I bet it's gonna be super crowded around Easter, though, so I should book soon. Honestly, I doubt I'll do much sightseeing. I'll probably just eat and sleep. We'll see!

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I will meet Sarah tomorrow at 7. | I'm **meeting** Sarah tomorrow at 7. | Arranged plans sound natural with -ing. |
| I probably will go. | I'll **probably** go. | "Probably" goes after "will". |
| I will probably not go. | I **probably won't** go. | Negative: probably + won't. |
| I think to move next year. | I'm **thinking of moving** next year. | "Thinking of" + -ing. |
| Phone rings — "I'm gonna get it!" (OK) / "I get it." | "I'**ll** get it!" | A decision right now = I'll. |
| It will definitely surely happen. | It's **definitely** gonna happen. / It'll **probably** happen. | Pick one level of certainty. |
| When I will arrive, I'll call you. | When I **arrive**, I'll call you. | After when / if / as soon as, use the present. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is a **random-topic drill** with "future" cards, plus one 3/2/1 round. Rules: [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md). No preparation. Start talking within 2 seconds.

**Step 1 — Warm-up (5 min).** Answer these out loud, 20 seconds each: "Any plans for tonight?", "What are you gonna do this weekend?", "Do you think it'll be busy at work next week?", "What's the next thing you're gonna buy?", "Are you going anywhere this year?".

**Step 2 — Random-topic rounds (35 min).** Mix these 15 cards, pick one without looking, record **1–2 minutes**. Every answer must use at least **three different future forms** (for example gonna + probably + I'm thinking of) and one hypothetical from this week (I'd… / If I could…).

1. Your next vacation
2. Your city in 20 years
3. Your job in 5 years
4. What phones will be like in 10 years
5. Your plans for next weekend
6. A skill you're gonna learn this year
7. Will people still work in offices in 2040?
8. The next big change in your life
9. Your next home
10. What you're gonna cook this week
11. Will AI design apps by itself one day?
12. Your English in 3 months
13. Your next birthday
14. Where your best friend will be in 10 years
15. The future of travel

**Step 3 — One 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Use card 3, "Your job in 5 years" (or the card you found hardest). Do the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md): 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute, recording each. In the 3-minute version, mix future forms with Day 1–3 patterns: "Ideally, I'd…", "If I'd started earlier, I'd…", "I wish I could…". In the 1-minute version, keep the best parts only.

**Step 4 — Listen back and count (5 min).** Listen to the 1-minute version. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md). Also check: did you use "probably" in the right place?
