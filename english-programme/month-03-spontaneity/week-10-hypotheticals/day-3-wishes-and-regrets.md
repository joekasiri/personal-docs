## Learning objectives

- Talk about the present you'd like to change: **I wish I had / I wish I could / I wish it was…**
- Talk about past regrets: **I wish I'd… / I shoulda… / I shouldn't have…**
- Complain lightly about other people: **I wish he'd stop… / I wish they wouldn't…**
- Keep regrets light and casual, the way Americans do in small talk, not dramatic.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: shoulda / shouldn'ta / I wish I'da.**

Same reduction as last night, now with "should":

- should have → **shoulda** ("SHOOD-uh")
- shouldn't have → **shouldn'ta** ("SHOOD-n-uh")
- I wish I had → **I wish I'd**, which in fast speech sounds like "I wish I-d" (the D almost disappears before a consonant: "I wish I'd known" → "I wish-eye NOHN")
- I wish I could → "I wish I kud" (very short "could")

"Wish" is usually **stressed**, and so is the important verb. The rest is quick:

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| I should have gone. | I shoulda GONE. |
| I shouldn't have eaten that. | I shouldn'ta EATEN that. |
| I wish I had known. | I WISH I'd KNOWN. |
| I wish I could stay. | I WISH I could STAY. |
| You should have seen it! | You shoulda SEEN it! |

**Practice sentences:**

1. I shoulda left an hour EARLIER.
2. Ugh, I shouldn'ta had that THIRD coffee.
3. I WISH I'd KNOWN about this place sooner.
4. You shoulda SEEN his FACE.
5. I WISH I could stay LONGER.
6. We shoulda BOOKED it last week.
7. I WISH I had more TIME, honestly.
8. He shouldn'ta SAID that in the MEETING.
9. I WISH it wasn't so EXPENSIVE.
10. I shoulda just ASKED.

**Clip to find:** a relaxed American vlogger talking about things they'd do differently. Search YouTube for "**things I wish I knew before moving**", "**things I wish I knew in my twenties**" or "**design career advice I wish I had**". Choose a chatty 2–3 minute section. Listen once, then speak along twice without pausing. Copy the stressed "WISH" and the lazy "shoulda". Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Wishes and regrets

### Explanation

"I wish" moves the verb **one step back in time**, just like "if" does. That's the whole trick.

| You want to talk about… | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The present (not true now) | I wish + past simple | I wish I **had** a bigger apartment. / I wish I **lived** near the beach. |
| An ability you don't have | I wish I **could** + verb | I wish I **could** cook like my mom. |
| The past (a regret) | I wish I**'d** + past participle | I wish I**'d** started earlier. / I wish I **hadn't** said that. |
| Someone else's annoying habit | I wish + person + **would / wouldn't** | I wish my neighbor **would** turn the music down. / I wish they **wouldn't** schedule meetings at 5. |

For regrets, Americans use **"I should've…"** even more than "I wish I'd…". They mean nearly the same:

- I wish I'd bought the tickets earlier. = I should've bought the tickets earlier.
- I wish I hadn't said that. = I shouldn't have said that.

**"I wish I was" or "I wish I were"?** In writing and careful speech, "were". In casual American speech, you will hear both. Use whichever comes out first. Nobody cares.

**Keep it light.** In small talk, regrets are usually small and funny: the extra slice of pizza, the trip you didn't book, the jacket you didn't bring. If you go too deep too fast ("I wish I'd had a different childhood"), people don't know what to say. Save deep regrets for close friends. A good light formula is:

1. The regret: "I really should've brought a jacket."
2. A tiny detail: "I literally checked the weather and still didn't bring one."
3. A laugh or a lesson: "Classic me. Next time, I'm bringing two."

**"If only…"** is a stronger "I wish". Use it for fun drama: "If only I'd bought Bitcoin in 2012!" Don't overuse it; one per conversation is plenty.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I wish I could… | you want an ability or freedom you don't have | "I wish I could take a month off." |
| I wish I had more… | something is missing now | "I wish I had more time to read." |
| I really should've… | light past regret | "I really should've booked earlier." |
| I shouldn't have… | you did something and regret it | "I shouldn't have stayed up so late." |
| I wish I'd known… | you didn't have information | "I wish I'd known it was this cold." |
| Looking back, I wish I'd… | a bigger life or career lesson | "Looking back, I wish I'd learned to code." |
| I kinda wish… | soft, unsure wish | "I kinda wish we'd stayed one more day." |
| I wish they'd just… | mild complaint about others | "I wish they'd just tell us the deadline." |
| If only… | playful drama | "If only I'd listened to my mom." |
| No regrets. / I don't regret it at all. | you're happy with your choice | "It was expensive, but no regrets." |
| Lesson learned. | ending a regret story | "I lost all my files. Lesson learned: back up." |
| It is what it is. | accepting something you can't change | "We missed the flight. It is what it is." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: after a weekend trip**

> A: How was the camping trip?
> B: Fun, but oh my God, so cold. I really should've brought a warmer sleeping bag.
> A: Didn't you check the weather?
> B: I did! It said ten degrees. I just thought, eh, it'll be fine. It was not fine.
> A: (laughs) Rookie mistake.
> B: I know. Lesson learned. Next time I'm bringing, like, three blankets. I kinda wish we'd stayed in a cabin, honestly.

**Dialogue 2: work wishes**

> A: How's the new project going?
> B: It's OK. I just wish we had more time for research. We're kinda designing in the dark.
> A: Yeah, that's tough.
> B: And I wish the stakeholders would make decisions faster. We waited two weeks for one answer.
> A: Did you push back?
> B: I should've, honestly. Looking back, I wish I'd set a deadline for feedback at the start.

**Model answer (1 minute): "Is there anything you wish you'd done differently?"**

> Hmm, yeah, a few things, but one big one. I wish I'd started speaking English way earlier. Like, I studied it for years in school, but I never actually *spoke* it. I just did grammar exercises. If I'd started talking to people back then, I think I'd be super relaxed by now. But it is what it is. And honestly, I'm kinda doing it now, so… better late than never, right? Oh, and a small one: I really should've bought a better laptop last year. Mine is so slow. I wish I could just throw it out the window sometimes. What about you? Any regrets?

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I wish I have more time. | I wish I **had** more time. | Wish about now = past form. |
| I wish I would speak French. | I wish I **could** speak French. | Your own ability = "could", not "would". |
| I wish I didn't eat so much last night. | I wish I **hadn't eaten** so much last night. | Past regret = had + past participle. |
| I should go to the party last week. | I **should've gone** to the party last week. | Past regret needs "should've" + participle. |
| I regret to not study. | I regret **not studying**. / I wish I'd studied. | "Regret" + -ing; or just use "I wish I'd". |
| I wish you will come. | I **hope** you'll come. | For real future possibilities, use "hope", not "wish". |

### Part B — Live conversation #1 (30 min)

Use the tips in [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: get the other person to share a light regret, and share two of yours.**

Things to try:

1. Open with something easy, then ask: "Is there anything you wish you'd done differently when you were younger?" or "What's something you wish you'd known before you started your job?"
2. Share one funny, small regret using "I really should've…" (for example the camping-trip kind).
3. Share one bigger career or life wish using "Looking back, I wish I'd…".
4. React to their answers with phrases from [Week 1 reactions](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md) ("That's rough", "I bet") and ask a follow-up with "What would you have done?".
5. End on a positive note: "No regrets, though, right?" or "Better late than never."
