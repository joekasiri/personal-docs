## Learning objectives

- Ask what-if questions in 5 natural ways: **What if…? / What would happen if…? / Say you… / Let's say… / Imagine…**
- Play along with a strange question instead of freezing: "Ooh, OK. So…"
- Build a **chain of consequences** ("…and then…, which means…") to keep a crazy answer going for 1–2 minutes.
- Switch between "what if" in the present (unreal: *What if you could fly?*) and the past (*What if you'd never moved?*).

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: "what if", "what would you", "what'd you".**

These question starts are very fast and very linked:

- **What if** → the T becomes a flap D and joins "if": "wuh-DIF". "What if we just left?" → "wuh-DIF we just LEFT?"
- **What would you** → "whuddya" ("WUH-duh-ya"). "What would you do?" → "whuddya DO?"
- **What'd you** (what did you / what would you) → "whaja". "What'd you say?" → "whaja SAY?"
- **Would you** → "wouldja" ("WOOD-ja"). "Would you go?" → "wouldja GO?"
- **Say you** → "SAY-ya". "Say you won…" → "SAY-ya WON…"

Rising-then-falling melody: what-if questions usually go **up** on the key word and then fall. "What if you could FLY?" (up on FLY).

**Practice sentences:**

1. Wuh-DIF you could FLY?
2. Whuddya DO with a million BUCKS?
3. Wouldja ever live on a BOAT?
4. Wuh-DIF we just skipped the MEETING?
5. SAY-ya woke up in TOKYO tomorrow. Whuddya DO first?
6. Wuh-DIF dogs could TALK?
7. Whuddya SAY to your younger SELF?
8. Wouldja TELL him, though?
9. LET'S say you had a TWIN. Wouldja SWAP sometimes?
10. Wuh-DIF it rained FOREVER?
11. Whaja do if your boss QUIT tomorrow?
12. Imagine you could PAUSE time. Wouldja USE it?

**Clip to find:** two or more Americans answering silly hypothetical questions together. Search YouTube for "**answering weird hypothetical questions**", "**what if questions with friends podcast**" or "**deep and silly would you rather what if**". Speak along with a 2–3 minute section, twice, no pausing. Copy the fast "wuh-DIF" and "whuddya". Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): What-if questions

### Explanation

What-if questions are a big part of casual conversation in the US: at dinner, on road trips, in team-building games, on first dates. They are **not tests**. The other person doesn't want the "correct" answer. They want to see you **play**. So the worst answer is silence, and the best answer is a fun chain of ideas.

**Five ways to ask:**

| Form | Grammar after it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What if…? | past simple (unreal) | What if you **woke up** as a cat? |
| What would happen if…? | past simple | What would happen if everyone **worked** four days a week? |
| Say you… / Let's say you… | past simple, then "would" question | Say you **won** the lottery. Would you quit? |
| Imagine… | past simple or "could" | Imagine you **could** read minds. |
| What if you'd…? (past) | had + past participle | What if you'd **never moved** here? |

Small note: "What if" can also be **real** and **present**: "What if it rains tomorrow?" (a real possibility). Then you use the present: "What if it **rains**? — Then we'**ll** move it inside." Tonight we mostly use the unreal, crazy kind.

**How to answer without freezing — the 3P method:**

1. **Play along** (1 second): react to the question. "Ooh, OK." / "Oh man, that's a good one." / "Wait, like *any* animal?"
2. **Pick** (straight away): give a clear first answer. "OK, I'd go for a cat. Easy."
3. **Push** the idea (the chain): "…because I'd sleep 16 hours a day, and nobody would expect me to work, which means… honestly, it's my dream life. The only problem is I'd have to eat cat food."

The **chain words** that push the idea forward:

- …and then…
- …which means…
- …so I'd probably…
- …but the problem is…
- …the only downside would be…
- …and the best part? …

**Asking back** is half the skill. When someone asks you a what-if, answer it, then **give it back with a twist**: "What about you? Same question, but you can only pick a bird." This keeps the game going.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ooh, OK. So… | you play along with a strange question | "Ooh, OK. So, first, I'd panic." |
| Wait, like…? | you check the rules (and buy a second) | "Wait, like forever, or just a day?" |
| Easy. I'd… | you have an instant answer | "Easy. I'd pick teleporting." |
| …which means… | you push the idea to a consequence | "…which means no more commuting." |
| The only downside would be… | adding a problem | "The only downside would be the jet lag." |
| What if you…? | asking a crazy question | "What if you could only speak in questions?" |
| Say you… Would you…? | setting up a scenario | "Say you found a wallet with $10,000. Would you keep it?" |
| Let's say… | setting up a scenario (very common at work too) | "Let's say users hate it. What's plan B?" |
| Imagine if… | starting a fun idea | "Imagine if meetings had a time limit of 10 minutes." |
| OK, plot twist:… | adding a new rule to someone's question | "OK, plot twist: you can't tell anyone." |
| I've never thought about that. | you need a second, honestly | "Huh. I've never thought about that. Probably…" |
| Same question, but… | giving the question back with a twist | "Same question, but you only get one day." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: road-trip game**

> A: OK, what if you could have any superpower, but only for one day?
> B: Ooh, OK. Wait, like, one day and then it's gone forever?
> A: Yep.
> B: Then easy, I'd pick teleporting. I'd have breakfast in Paris, lunch in Tokyo, dinner in Mexico City, which means I'd basically eat my way around the world in 24 hours. The only downside would be I'd probably feel super sick by the end.
> A: (laughs) Worth it.
> B: Totally. OK, same question, but it has to be a useless superpower.

**Dialogue 2: at work (a "real" what-if)**

> A: Let's say users don't find the new button. What's our plan?
> B: Good question. If they don't find it, I'd move it up into the header, which means we'd need to rethink the menu a bit.
> A: And what if that doesn't work either?
> B: Then honestly, I'd run a couple of quick user tests before we change anything else. Imagine if we just kept guessing for a month.

**Model answer (1 minute): "What if you woke up tomorrow and you could speak every language in the world?"**

> Oh man, that's a good one. OK, so first, I'd probably test it, like, I'd put on random TV channels from different countries just to check. And then I'd quit my job. No, I'm kidding — but I'd definitely change jobs, because I could work as a translator anywhere, which means I could live anywhere. I'd probably go to Southeast Asia first, because I'd love to actually talk to people in Vietnam and Thailand, not just point at menus. The only downside would be… honestly, I'd hear everything people say about me on the train. That could be kinda awkward. What about you? Same question, but you only get one extra language.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What if you can fly? (as a fantasy) | What if you **could** fly? | Unreal = past form ("could"). |
| What you would do? | What **would you** do? | Question order: would + you. |
| I don't know. (and stop) | Ooh, I've never thought about that. Probably… | Never end on "I don't know" — play along. |
| Say you win the lottery, would you quit? (fantasy) | Say you **won** the lottery. Would you quit? | Unreal scenario = past simple. |
| What if you have never moved here? | What if you'**d** never moved here? | Unreal past = had + past participle. |
| Long silence, then a perfect sentence | "Wait, like…?" + a quick first answer | Speed matters more than a perfect answer in games. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is **voice mode with Claude**: 12 crazy what-if questions, then you ask Claude yours. Setup: [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Record if you can.

**Step 1 — Paste this prompt (or read it out) to start:**

```text
Let's play a fast "what if" game in voice mode, in casual American English. I'm a B2 English learner and I want to answer without preparing. Rules:
1. Ask me the 12 questions below, one at a time, in this order. After I answer, ask ONE quick follow-up or add a "plot twist" rule, then move on.
2. If I'm silent for more than 2 seconds, say "Go, go, go!" and I'll start talking.
3. Don't correct me during the game. After question 6 and after question 12, give me: my 3 biggest mistakes (especially with would / could / would've / if + past), the correct versions, and one more natural phrase I could have used.
4. After question 12, it's my turn: I'll ask YOU 5 what-if questions, and you answer like a friend would, then ask me a follow-up each time.
Speak at natural speed with contractions.

The 12 questions:
1. What if you woke up tomorrow and you were 10 years old again, but you kept all your memories?
2. What if every job paid exactly the same salary? What would you do for work?
3. What if you could press a button and skip all the boring parts of your day? What would you skip?
4. What if you had to live in your office for a whole month?
5. What if animals could vote? Which animal would win an election?
6. What if you could see a number above everyone's head showing how much they like you?
7. What if the internet disappeared for one year, starting tomorrow?
8. What if you'd been born 200 years ago? What would your life be like?
9. What if you could add one new rule that everyone in the world had to follow?
10. What if your phone could talk and it told everyone what you search for?
11. What if you could only use 100 words for the rest of your life? Which words would you keep?
12. What if you could redesign the human body? What would you change?
Start with question 1 now.
```

**Step 2 — Play the game (40 min).** Rules for you:

- Every answer starts with a play-along phrase ("Ooh, OK…", "Wait, like…?", "Easy.").
- Every answer has at least two chain words (…which means…, …the only downside would be…).
- Every answer is 45–90 seconds. Don't rush to finish.
- When Claude says "plot twist", don't restart. Say "OK, so in that case, I'd…" and keep going.

**Step 3 — Your turn to ask (10 min).** Ask Claude 5 of your own what-ifs. Use a different question form each time: What if…? / What would happen if…? / Say you… / Let's say… / Imagine… Then react to Claude's answers and ask one follow-up each time.

**Step 4 — Notes (10 min).** Write down the corrections from questions 6 and 12. Say each correct version out loud 3 times. Note which question made you freeze longest, and why (no idea? no words? grammar?).

**What to focus on tonight:** speed of the **first second**. You don't need a smart answer. You need a fast one. If you only remember one thing, remember "Ooh, OK, so…".
