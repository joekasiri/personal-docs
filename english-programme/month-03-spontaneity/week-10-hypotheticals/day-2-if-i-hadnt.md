## Learning objectives

- Use the third conditional (**If I hadn't + past participle, I would've + past participle**) to talk about a past that didn't happen.
- Use mixed conditionals to connect the past and the present: "If I hadn't moved, I wouldn't **be** here now."
- Say **woulda, coulda, wouldn't've** and **if I'da** naturally.
- Tell a short "sliding doors" story: one small moment, and how life would be different.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: woulda / coulda / if I'da.**

In fast speech, "have" after would / could / might almost disappears. It becomes a tiny "uh":

- would have → **woulda** ("WOOD-uh")
- could have → **coulda** ("KOOD-uh")
- might have → **mighta** ("MY-duh", with a flap T)
- wouldn't have → **wouldn'ta** ("WOOD-n-uh")
- if I had → **if I'd**, and very casually **"if I'da"** ("if EYE-duh")

Important: you **write** "would've" or "would have". "Woulda" is only how it sounds. Never write "would of".

The stress goes on the **past participle** (the important word), and "woulda" is short and quiet:

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| I would have called you. | I woulda CALLED you. |
| We could have won. | We coulda WON. |
| I wouldn't have known. | I wouldn'ta KNOWN. |
| If I had known, I would have come. | If I'da KNOWN, I woulda COME. |
| It might have been better. | It mighta been BETTER. |

**Practice sentences:**

1. If I'da KNOWN, I woulda brought SNACKS.
2. We coulda been there in TEN minutes.
3. Honestly, I wouldn'ta GONE if it was raining.
4. If she hadn't CALLED me, I woulda totally FORGOTTEN.
5. It mighta been the BEST trip ever.
6. You coulda TOLD me!
7. If we'da left EARLIER, we woulda made it.
8. I woulda said YES in a heartbeat.
9. He mighta MISSED the email.
10. If I hadn't taken that JOB, I wouldn'ta met HER.

**Clip to find:** an American storyteller talking about a lucky or unlucky moment. Search YouTube for "**storytime how I almost missed**", "**the day that changed my life storytime**" or "**if I hadn't gone storytime**". Find a 2–3 minute part. Listen once, then speak along with the whole clip twice, no pausing. Every time they say "would've" or "could've", listen for the short "uh". Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): If I hadn't…, I woulda…

### Explanation

**The third conditional** imagines a different **past**. It talks about something that did *not* happen, and its result, which also did *not* happen.

> **If I had / hadn't** + past participle, **I would've / wouldn't have / could've / might've** + past participle

- If I **hadn't missed** the bus, I **would've been** on time. (I missed it. I was late.)
- If we **had tested** it with users, we **would've found** the bug. (We didn't test. We didn't find it.)

People use it for three things:

1. **Lucky moments:** "If I hadn't gone to that party, I would've never met my best friend."
2. **Small regrets and blame (light):** "If you'd told me, I could've helped!"
3. **Work lessons:** "If we'd talked to users first, we wouldn't have built the wrong feature."

**The mixed conditional** connects a past change to the **present**. This is very common in real conversation, maybe even more common than the "pure" third conditional:

> **If I had / hadn't** + past participle (past), **I'd / I wouldn't** + verb (now)

- If I **hadn't moved** to this city, I **wouldn't be** here right now.
- If I'**d studied** design earlier, I'**d probably be** a lead by now.
- If I **hadn't learned** English, I **wouldn't have** this job.

**Short answers save time.** You don't always need the whole sentence:

> A: Would you have gone if you'd known?
> B: No way. / Probably not. / Oh, for sure.
> A: Could you have stopped it?
> B: Maybe. I dunno. Probably not, honestly.

**The "sliding doors" story** (one small moment, a big difference) is the best way to practise this. Keep it simple:

1. **The small moment:** "So, five years ago, I almost didn't apply for my current job."
2. **What really happened:** "My friend basically forced me to send my portfolio."
3. **The other past:** "If she hadn't pushed me, I would've stayed at my old company."
4. **The other present:** "And I'd probably still be doing boring banner ads, honestly."
5. **How you feel:** "So yeah, I owe her one."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| If I hadn't…, I would've never… | a lucky moment changed everything | "If I hadn't taken that class, I would've never met Sam." |
| If I'd known…, I would've… | you didn't have the information | "If I'd known it was your birthday, I would've gotten you something." |
| You could've told me! | friendly complaint | "You were in town? You could've told me!" |
| I would've…, but… | you wanted to, but something stopped you | "I would've come, but I was sick." |
| It could've been way worse. | a bad thing that was not as bad as it might have been | "I fell off the bike, but it could've been way worse." |
| …or I would've… | showing the danger you avoided | "Good thing I checked, or I would've sent it to the whole company." |
| If it wasn't for…, I'd… | someone or something made your present life possible | "If it wasn't for my sister, I'd never be in design." |
| I'd probably still be… | mixed: your present in the other version | "If I hadn't quit, I'd probably still be in that job." |
| Looking back, if we'd… | work lesson | "Looking back, if we'd tested earlier, we would've saved two weeks." |
| Who knows what would've happened? | ending a sliding-doors story | "If I'd said no… who knows what would've happened?" |
| There's no way I would've… | you are sure you wouldn't have | "There's no way I would've finished without your help." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: the lucky meeting**

> A: So how did you two meet?
> B: Honestly, total accident. I was supposed to go to a different party that night, but it got canceled.
> A: No way.
> B: Yeah. If it hadn't been canceled, I would've never gone to Mike's, and I would've never met her.
> A: That's crazy. So you'd be, like, single right now?
> B: Probably! Or I'd be with someone way less fun. (laughs)

**Dialogue 2: a work lesson**

> A: How did the launch go in the end?
> B: Kinda rough, to be honest. We found a huge bug on day one.
> A: Oh no.
> B: Yeah. Looking back, if we'd done one more round of testing, we would've caught it. But we were rushing for the deadline.
> A: Could you have pushed the date?
> B: Maybe. I think if we'd asked earlier, they would've said yes. Lesson learned.

**Model answer (1 minute): "Tell me about a decision that changed your life."**

> OK, so this is a small one, but it changed a lot. About six years ago I was deciding between two courses: one in marketing and one in UX design. Honestly, I almost picked marketing, because it felt safer. But I went to an open day for the UX course, kind of by accident, and the teacher was amazing. If I hadn't gone that day, I would've signed up for marketing for sure. And I think I'd be doing something really different now. I probably wouldn't be in tech at all. I wouldn't have met most of my friends, either, because I met them on that course. So yeah, if I'd stayed home that day… who knows what would've happened? What about you? Was there a moment like that for you?

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| If I would have known, I would have come. | If I **had** known, I would've come. | No "would" in the "if" part. |
| If I didn't miss the bus, I would be on time. (about yesterday) | If I **hadn't missed** the bus, I **would've been** on time. | A finished past needs had + participle. |
| I would of called you. | I **would've** called you. | "Of" is a spelling mistake; it only *sounds* like "of". |
| If I hadn't moved, I wouldn't have been here now. | If I hadn't moved, I **wouldn't be** here now. | "Now" = present result = I'd / wouldn't + verb. |
| If I had went… | If I had **gone**… | Past participle, not past simple. |
| I would have — I would have — I would have… (slow, full) | I woulda… I coulda… | In speech, reduce it. Full forms make you slow. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight's drill is **voice mode with Claude**: about 50 minutes of conversation, then 10 minutes of notes. Setup and tips: [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Record the session if you can.

**Step 1 — Start the session.** Open voice mode and paste this prompt (or read it out):

```text
Let's have a relaxed, casual voice conversation in American English. I'm a B2 English learner practising the third conditional and mixed conditionals ("If I hadn't moved, I would've…", "If I'd known, I'd be…"). Please:
1. Ask me one question at a time about my past: decisions, lucky moments, near misses, things at work that could have gone differently, people I met by chance. Don't let me prepare — ask, and wait for my answer.
2. After each answer, ask 1–2 natural follow-ups, especially "So what would've happened if…?" and "Where do you think you'd be now if…?"
3. Every 5 questions, tell me briefly: (a) any mistakes with "had + past participle" or "would've", with the correct version, and (b) one more natural way to say something I said.
4. Sometimes tell me a short sliding-doors story of your own and let me react and ask you follow-up questions.
5. Speak at natural speed, use contractions like "would've" and "if I'd", and keep your turns short so I talk more than you.
Start with: "So, what's one decision you almost didn't make?"
```

**Step 2 — Talk (50 min).** Rules:

- Answer within 2 seconds. If you need time, use a time-buyer from [Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md), not silence.
- Use at least one mixed conditional in every long answer ("…and I'd probably still be…").
- After about 25 minutes, say: "Can we switch? Ask me about work now — projects, launches, mistakes." Work topics give you natural "looking back, if we'd…" sentences.
- Ask Claude at least 3 questions back with "What would you have done?".

**Step 3 — Notes (10 min).** Write down every correction Claude gave you. For each one, say the correct sentence out loud 3 times, fast, with "woulda" and "coulda".
