# Week 10 exercises: Hypotheticals
[Week 10 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-if-i-could.md) · [Day 2](day-2-if-i-hadnt.md) · [Day 3](day-3-wishes-and-regrets.md) · [Day 4](day-4-what-if-questions.md) · [Day 5](day-5-future-plans-and-predictions.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use this page:** every exercise is done **out loud**. Even "write then say" exercises end with you saying the answer at normal speed. When an exercise says *record*, record it on your phone and listen back once. This month you don't prepare, so read each prompt and start speaking straight away. Use contractions every time: **I'd, would've, should've, I'll, it'll, gonna**. Check yourself with the answer key only after you have said your own answer.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: I'd in 5 seconds
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud, timed

1. Read a question, then answer within 5 seconds.
2. Your answer must start with one of: "Honestly, I'd probably…", "Oh, I'd totally…", "I don't think I'd…", "No way would I…".
3. Give the answer plus **one reason** (one sentence). Then go to the next question.
4. When you reach the end, start again from question 1 and try to answer faster.

1. What would you do if you found $500 on the street?
2. What would you do if your flight was canceled and the next one was in two days?
3. Would you eat a bug for $1,000?
4. If you could have dinner with any living person, who would it be?
5. What would you do if your boss asked you to move to another country?
6. Would you rather live without music or without movies? Why?
7. If you could only keep one app on your phone, which would it be?
8. What would you do if you saw a famous actor at the grocery store?
9. If you were 18 again, what would you study?
10. Would you go to space if you had the chance?
11. What would you do if a friend gave you an ugly gift?
12. If you could retire tomorrow, would you?
13. What would you change about your morning?
14. If you could be any age for a whole year, what age would you pick?
15. Would you live in a tiny house?
16. What would you do if your neighbor played loud music every night?
17. If you could add one room to your apartment, what would it be?
18. Would you ever get a tattoo?
19. What would you do with a free afternoon today?
20. If you could fix one thing in your city, what would it be?

### Exercise 1.2: Fix the if
**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** error correction, then say it

1. Each sentence has one mistake. Say the corrected sentence out loud.
2. Then say it again, faster, with contractions (I'd, wouldn't, it'd).
3. Check the [Day 1 answer key](#day-1-answer-key).

1. If I would have more time, I would learn the guitar.
2. If I have a car, I'd drive to the mountains every weekend. (I don't have a car.)
3. If I were you, I will talk to your manager.
4. I would buy a boat if I would win the lottery.
5. If she knows the answer, she'd tell us. (She doesn't know it.)
6. What you would do if you lost your phone?
7. If I can live anywhere, I'd live in Lisbon. (I can't.)
8. It would be awesome if we would have a four-day week.
9. I wouldn't never do that.
10. If we had more designers, we can finish faster.
11. If my apartment was bigger, I invite more people over.
12. If I were rich, I would to travel all the time.

### Exercise 1.3: The five-step chain
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** record

1. Read the model below out loud once, at normal speed.
2. For each prompt, record a 1-minute answer using the five steps: **Answer (I'd) → Why → Picture it → But → Ask back**.
3. Don't write anything. If you get stuck on a step, skip it and move to the next one.
4. Do at least 5 of the 8 prompts. Listen back to one recording.

**Model — "If you could change jobs tomorrow, what would you do?"**

> **Answer:** Honestly, I'd probably open a little coffee shop.
> **Why:** Because I love the idea of a place where people just hang out and talk.
> **Picture it:** I'd design the whole thing myself, the menu, the logo, the furniture. I'd play really chill music and I'd know all the regulars by name.
> **But:** But I'd hate getting up at 5 a.m. every day, so I'd probably need a business partner who's a morning person.
> **Ask back:** What about you? What would you do?

**Prompts:**

1. If you could live in any country for one year, where would you go?
2. If you had a whole month off work, how would you spend it?
3. If you could design your dream apartment, what would it look like?
4. If you could have any animal as a pet, what would you get?
5. If you could be famous for one thing, what would it be?
6. If you were in charge of your company for a week, what would you change?
7. If you could learn one new language instantly, which one would it be?
8. If you could only travel by one type of transport for the rest of your life, what would it be?

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Past to "woulda"
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** transformation, out loud

1. Read the real past situation.
2. Say the third conditional sentence that imagines the opposite. Start with "If I…" or the subject given.
3. Say it twice: once clearly, once fast with "woulda / coulda / wouldn'ta".
4. Check the [Day 2 answer key](#day-2-answer-key).

1. I missed the train, so I was late for the meeting.
2. I didn't bring an umbrella, so I got soaked.
3. We didn't test the app with users, so we didn't find the problem.
4. She didn't call me, so I didn't know about the party.
5. I stayed up until 3 a.m., so I was exhausted the next day.
6. He didn't check the address, so he went to the wrong restaurant.
7. I didn't save my file, so I lost two hours of work.
8. We left early, so we didn't get stuck in traffic.
9. I took the job in this city, so I met my best friend here.
10. They didn't book a table, so they had to wait an hour.
11. I didn't read the email carefully, so I sent the wrong file.
12. It rained, so we canceled the barbecue.

### Exercise 2.2: Mix it — past change, present result
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** transformation, out loud

1. Each line gives a past fact and a present fact.
2. Make one **mixed conditional** sentence: "If I hadn't / had + past participle, I'd / I wouldn't + verb (now)".
3. Add "probably" in at least half of your answers. Say each sentence twice, the second time faster.
4. Check the [Day 2 answer key](#day-2-answer-key).

1. I learned English at school. → Now I can work for an international company.
2. I didn't learn to drive. → Now I depend on my friends for rides.
3. I moved to this city. → Now I live far from my family.
4. I chose design, not law. → Now I'm happy at work.
5. I didn't save money last year. → Now I can't take a long vacation.
6. I went to that meetup. → Now I have a language exchange partner.
7. I broke my leg skiing. → Now I'm scared of skiing.
8. We didn't hire another developer. → Now the team is overloaded.
9. I said yes to the new project. → Now I'm super busy.
10. I didn't sleep last night. → Now I'm tired.

### Exercise 2.3: Sliding-doors stories
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** record

1. Read the model out loud once.
2. Pick 3 of the prompts. For each one, record a 1-minute story with the five parts: **small moment → what really happened → the other past (If I hadn't…, I would've…) → the other present (I'd probably still be…) → how you feel**.
3. Use your real life when you can. If a prompt doesn't fit your life, invent it. That's fine.
4. Listen back to one story and count how many "woulda / coulda" you said.

**Model — "A trip that almost didn't happen"**

> So, three years ago, I almost didn't go to Mexico with my friends. I was super stressed at work, and I literally had the cancel email ready. But my roommate looked at me and was like, "You're going. End of story." If she hadn't said that, I would've stayed home and worked the whole week. And honestly, that trip is where I decided to change jobs, so if I'd stayed home, I'd probably still be at my old company, burned out. So yeah, I owe her a lot. I bought her tacos for a month.

**Prompts:**

1. A person you met by chance
2. A job or course you almost didn't apply for
3. A time you were almost late for something important
4. A decision at work that changed a project
5. A place you almost didn't move to
6. A hobby you almost gave up
7. A message you almost didn't send
8. A time you almost said no, but said yes

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Wish it back one step
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** transformation, out loud

1. Read the fact. Say an "I wish…" sentence about it. Remember: **now → past simple / could**, **past → I'd + past participle**, **someone's habit → would**.
2. Then add one short detail: "…because…" or "…but it is what it is."
3. Check the [Day 3 answer key](#day-3-answer-key).

1. My apartment is tiny.
2. I can't swim.
3. I didn't go to my friend's wedding.
4. My coworker always interrupts me.
5. It's so cold today.
6. I ate way too much pizza last night.
7. I don't have a car.
8. I didn't take any photos on the trip.
9. My phone battery dies so fast.
10. My neighbor's dog barks all night.
11. I can't take time off this month.
12. I said something rude to my brother.
13. I don't live near the ocean.
14. The meeting is at 8 a.m. tomorrow.

### Exercise 3.2: Shoulda, coulda — light regrets
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then gap-fill dialogue

**Part A (6 min).** For each situation, say a 3-part light regret: **regret ("I really should've…" / "I shouldn't have…") → tiny detail → a laugh or lesson ("Lesson learned." / "Classic me." / "Never again.")**. Three models first:

> - *You forgot sunscreen at the beach.* → "I really should've brought sunscreen. I was in the sun for, like, four hours. I look like a tomato now. Lesson learned."
> - *You bought shoes that hurt.* → "I shouldn't have bought those shoes. They looked amazing in the store, but I literally can't walk in them. Classic me."
> - *You didn't book a hotel early.* → "We should've booked way earlier. Everything was sold out, so we ended up in this weird motel. Never again."

Your situations:

1. You didn't charge your phone before a long day out.
2. You told a joke in a meeting and nobody laughed.
3. You didn't check the weather before a hike.
4. You ordered the spiciest dish on the menu.
5. You waited until the last minute to start a big project.
6. You didn't back up your laptop and it broke.
7. You stayed out late before an early flight.
8. You agreed to help a friend move on your only free day.

**Part B (4 min).** Read the dialogue out loud and fill each gap with should've, shouldn't have, wish, wish I'd, or I'll (contract where you can). Then read it again as both A and B, fast. Check the [Day 3 answer key](#day-3-answer-key).

> A: How was the concert?
> B: Great music, but I (1) ______ worn those boots. My feet are dead.
> A: Ouch. Did you get a good spot?
> B: No! I (2) ______ we'd gotten there earlier. We were, like, way in the back.
> A: You (3) ______ texted me. I was there at six.
> B: No way! I (4) ______ known that. Next time, (5) ______ text you first.
> A: And I (6) ______ eaten that giant hot dog before the show. I felt sick the whole time.
> B: (laughs) Lesson learned.

10. I ______ I (have) more time this week.

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Five ways to ask
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** transformation, out loud

1. Each line is an idea. Turn it into a question using the form in brackets.
2. Say it with the fast sounds: "wuh-DIF", "whuddya", "wouldja", "SAY-ya".
3. Then answer your own question in one sentence.
4. Check the [Day 4 answer key](#day-4-answer-key).

1. you / be invisible for a day (What if…?)
2. everyone / work from home forever (What would happen if…?)
3. you / win a free house anywhere in the world (Say you… Would you…?)
4. our product / suddenly get a million users (Let's say… What would we…?)
5. you / can read your boss's mind (Imagine…)
6. you / never learn English (What if… — past)
7. cars / disappear from cities (What would happen if…?)
8. you / find a time machine in your closet (Say you… What would you…?)
9. people / only work 4 hours a day (Imagine if…)
10. you / be born in another country (What if… — past)

### Exercise 4.2: The 3P method
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** record

1. For each question: **Play along** (1 second) → **Pick** (a clear first answer) → **Push** (a chain with at least two of: …and then…, …which means…, …so I'd probably…, …the only downside would be…, …and the best part?).
2. Talk for 45–60 seconds per question. Record at least 6 of the 10.
3. Use a different play-along starter each time. Starters: "Ooh, OK, so…", "Oh man, that's a good one.", "Wait, like…?", "Easy.", "Huh, I've never thought about that.", "OK, honestly?"

**Model — "What if you could only eat food of one colour?"**

> **Play along:** Wait, like, *any* colour I want?
> **Pick:** OK, then easy, I'd pick green.
> **Push:** Because that gives me salad, avocados, broccoli, green curry, which means I'd actually be super healthy for the first time in my life. And then I'd probably get really creative with matcha desserts. The only downside would be no pizza. And no coffee. Honestly, that's a big downside. Maybe I'd switch to brown.

**Questions:**

1. What if you woke up and everyone in the world spoke only your language?
2. What if you had to wear the same outfit every day for a year?
3. What if cats were the size of horses?
4. What if you could pause time for one hour a day?
5. What if your city got 30 centimetres of snow every day for a month?
6. What if you could only communicate with emojis at work?
7. What if you had a robot that did one boring job for you? Which job?
8. What if every meeting at work had to be done standing on one leg?
9. What if you found out you had a twin living in another country?
10. What if you could live for 300 years?

### Exercise 4.3: Plot twist
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** record, in pairs of turns

1. Answer the question for 30 seconds.
2. Then read the plot twist and **don't restart**. Say "OK, so in that case…" and change your answer for another 30 seconds.
3. Do all 8. If you have a partner, they can read the twist out loud at a random moment.

| Question | Plot twist |
|---|---|
| What if you could live anywhere in the world for free? | You can't speak to anyone in your language there. |
| What if you won $10 million? | You have to spend it all in one month. |
| What if you could have any superpower? | It only works on Tuesdays. |
| What if you could go back in time to one day of your life? | You can't change anything — only watch. |
| What if you could swap jobs with anyone for a week? | Your friends will watch you on live TV. |
| What if you could invite any three people to dinner? | You have to cook for them. |
| What if you never had to sleep again? | You also can't leave your house at night. |
| What if you could design your own island? | You have to share it with your whole team from work. |

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Pick the future
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** gap-fill, out loud

1. Read the situation. Say the sentence with the best future form: **I'm -ing (arranged)**, **gonna (decided / evidence)**, **I'll (deciding now / offer / opinion)**, **might / I'm thinking of (not decided)**.
2. Give yourself 3 seconds per item. Then check the [Day 5 answer key](#day-5-answer-key).

1. Your phone rings. You say: "Don't worry, I ______ (get) it."
2. You booked a dentist appointment for Thursday at 3: "I ______ (see) the dentist on Thursday."
3. You decided last week to paint your bedroom: "This weekend, I ______ (paint) my bedroom."
4. Look at the sky! It ______ (rain).
5. You're considering a pottery class, but you're not sure: "I ______ (take) a pottery class."
6. A friend says "I'm so thirsty." You say: "Oh, I ______ (get) you some water."
7. Your opinion about next week's launch: "I think it ______ (go) fine."
8. Maybe you'll go out tonight, maybe not: "I ______ (go) out tonight, I don't know yet."
9. Your flight leaves at 6 a.m. tomorrow: "I ______ (fly) to Denver tomorrow morning."
10. Your team decided to redesign the homepage next quarter: "We ______ (redesign) the homepage next quarter."
11. A colleague asks for feedback by Friday: "Sure, I ______ (send) it to you by Friday."
12. You're about 80% sure: "I ______ (probably / be) a little late."
13. The score is 5–0 with two minutes left: "They ______ (win) this."
14. You've been considering a move to Austin for months: "I ______ (move) to Austin."
15. "When I ______ (get) home, I'll call you."

### Exercise 5.2: The certainty scale
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

**Part A (5 min).** Say each prediction at **three levels of certainty**: high (definitely / I bet), middle (probably / might), low (I doubt / probably not). One model:

> *It's gonna snow this weekend.* → "I bet it's gonna snow this weekend." / "It might snow this weekend." / "I doubt it's gonna snow this weekend."

1. The new feature is gonna be ready on time.
2. My team is gonna like the new design.
3. Prices are gonna go up next year.
4. I'm gonna finish this book this month.
5. People are gonna use AI for everything soon.
6. My friend is gonna be late.
7. The restaurant is gonna be crowded tonight.
8. I'm gonna get a raise this year.
9. It's gonna be hot tomorrow.
10. My English is gonna be way better by summer.

**Part B (3 min).** Fix the word order of "probably". Say the correct sentence twice. Check the [Day 5 answer key](#day-5-answer-key).

1. I probably will stay home tonight.
2. She will probably not come.
3. We're gonna probably order pizza.
4. It probably will be fine.
5. I won't probably go to the gym.
6. They probably are gonna be late.

2. What are you doing this weekend?
3. Are you going anywhere for the holidays?
4. What are you gonna have for dinner?
5. When are you gonna take a vacation?
