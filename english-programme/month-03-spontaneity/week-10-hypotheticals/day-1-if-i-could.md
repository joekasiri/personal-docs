## Learning objectives

- Use the second conditional (**If I + past, I'd + verb**) to talk about unreal situations now or in the future.
- Say it the spoken way: **I'd**, **I wouldn't**, **if I were you**, **I'd probably**, **I'd totally**.
- Start a hypothetical answer in under 2 seconds with a ready-made opener.
- Keep an "if" answer going for 1–2 minutes by adding a reason, a detail and a "but".

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: "I'd" and "wouldn't" in fast speech.**

Americans almost never say "I would" in full when they talk casually. They say **I'd**, and it is very short: it sounds like "ahd", and the D often joins the next word. "I'd go" sounds like "ah-GO" with a tiny D. "Wouldn't" loses its T and sounds like **"WOOD-n"**. "It would" becomes **"it'd"**, which sounds like "IDD-id" (two short syllables, with a soft flap D).

The stress goes on the **main verb** or on the important new word, not on "would":

| Full form | What you actually hear |
|---|---|
| I would go | ahd GO |
| I wouldn't do that | ah WOOD-n DO that |
| It would be awesome | IDD-id be AWE-some |
| You'd love it | yood LOVE it |
| We'd probably | weed PRAH-bly |
| If I were you | if I wer YOU |

**Practice sentences** (say each one 3 times, then speak along with the clip):

1. If I had more time, **ahd** totally learn to SURF.
2. I **WOOD-n** do that if I were YOU.
3. Honestly, **IDD-id** be kinda AWESOME.
4. If I lived in New York, **ahd** walk EVERYwhere.
5. **Yood** love it — it's SUPER chill.
6. If I could, **ahd** work from a beach for a MONTH.
7. **Weed** probably get LOST, though.
8. I **WOOD-n** wanna be famous. Like, at ALL.
9. If I had a dog, **ahd** name him PICKLE.
10. **IDD-id** be WEIRD, right?
11. If I were my boss, **ahd** cancel half the MEETINGS.

**Clip to find:** a casual American vlogger answering fun questions, talking fast and naturally (not reading). Search YouTube for "**what would I do if Q&A vlog**", "**answering hypothetical questions vlog**" or "**if I won the lottery what I'd buy**". Pick a 2–3 minute section. Play it once just listening, then **speak along** with the whole clip without pausing, twice. Focus only on making "I'd" short and fast. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md) for the speak-along method.

## 2. Main skill (30 min): If I could…, I'd…

### Explanation

The **second conditional** talks about something that is **not true now**, or that probably won't happen. You are imagining.

> **If** + past simple, **I'd / I wouldn't / I could / I might** + verb

- If I **had** a million dollars, I**'d** buy a house by the ocean. (I don't have a million dollars.)
- If I **lived** closer to the office, I**'d** walk to work. (I don't live close.)
- If I **could** speak five languages, I**'d** travel a lot more. (I can't.)

Three things make this "spoken" and fast:

1. **Use "I'd", never "I would" in full**, unless you want strong emphasis ("I WOULD, actually!").
2. **The "if" part often disappears in answers.** If someone asks "What would you do with a million dollars?", you don't repeat the whole "if". You just say: "Oh, I'd travel. For sure." The question already gave the "if".
3. **"If I were you"** is the normal advice phrase. You will also hear "if I was you" in casual American speech. Both are fine when speaking. "If I were" sounds a little more careful.

**When do you use it?** In "what would you do" questions, in giving advice, in dreaming ("If I could live anywhere…"), and at work when you imagine a different option ("If we had more budget, we'd test it with real users").

**How do you keep going for 1–2 minutes?** Use this simple chain:

1. **Answer** with I'd: "Honestly, I'd probably move to Lisbon."
2. **Why**: "Because the weather's amazing and it's not too expensive."
3. **Picture it**: "I'd get a little apartment near the water, I'd work in the mornings…"
4. **But**: "But I'd miss my friends, so I'd probably come back every few months."
5. **Ask back**: "What about you? Where would you go?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Honestly, I'd probably… | you want a fast, relaxed start | "Honestly, I'd probably just sleep for a week." |
| Oh, I'd totally… | you are sure and excited | "Oh, I'd totally say yes." |
| I don't think I'd… | you would *not* do it, said softly | "I don't think I'd move abroad, to be honest." |
| No way would I… | you would definitely not do it (strong) | "No way would I jump out of a plane." |
| If I were you, I'd… | giving advice | "If I were you, I'd ask for a raise." |
| If I had the time / money, I'd… | the only thing stopping you is time or money | "If I had the money, I'd get a really nice camera." |
| It'd be kinda… | describing how it would feel | "It'd be kinda lonely, I think." |
| I'd probably end up… | the result you'd *really* get | "I'd probably end up spending it all on food." |
| The first thing I'd do is… | listing what you'd do | "The first thing I'd do is quit my commute." |
| I could, but I wouldn't. | you are able to but don't want to | "Could I live without my phone? I could, but I wouldn't." |
| Part of me would…, but… | you have mixed feelings | "Part of me would love it, but part of me would hate it." |
| What would you do? | handing the question back | "Anyway, that's me. What would you do?" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a lunch break question**

> A: OK, random question. If you didn't have to work, like ever again, what would you do?
> B: Oh man. Honestly, I'd probably travel for the first year. Like, properly. Not two-week vacations.
> A: Where would you go first?
> B: Japan, for sure. I'd rent a little place in Osaka and just eat my way through the city. But I don't think I'd stop working completely. I'd get bored.
> A: Really?
> B: Yeah, I'd probably end up designing stuff for fun. Like little apps nobody asked for. What about you?

**Dialogue 2: advice at work**

> A: The client wants another round of changes, and it's the fourth time.
> B: Ugh. If I were you, I'd set up a quick call instead of going back and forth on email.
> A: You think?
> B: Yeah. It'd be way faster. And I'd ask them to pick one person who makes the final call. Otherwise you're gonna be stuck in this loop forever.

**Model answer (1 minute): "If you could live in any city, where would you live?"**

> Ooh, good one. Honestly, I'd probably pick Barcelona. The thing is, I love cities where you can walk everywhere, and Barcelona's like that. I'd get a small apartment, nothing fancy, maybe with a balcony, and I'd work from cafés in the morning. In the evening I'd go down to the beach. It'd be kinda perfect. But — and this is a big but — I don't think I'd learn Spanish fast enough, so I'd probably feel like an outsider for a while. And I'd really miss my family. So maybe I'd do six months there and six months at home. What about you? Would you ever move abroad?

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| If I would have money, I would buy a car. | If I **had** money, I'd buy a car. | No "would" in the "if" part. |
| If I have a million dollars, I'd buy a house. | If I **had** a million dollars, I'd buy a house. | Unreal now = past form after "if". |
| I would go… I would visit… I would eat… | I'd go… I'd visit… I'd eat… | Full "I would" every time sounds slow and robotic. |
| If I were you, I will ask. | If I were you, I**'d** ask. | Advice with "if I were you" needs "I'd". |
| What would you do? — I go to Japan. | What would you do? — I**'d** go to Japan. | Keep "I'd" in the answer. It shows you're imagining. |
| No, I would not do it never. | No, I **wouldn't** do it. / No way would I do that. | One negative only; use the contraction. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is a **random-topic drill** with "if" cards, plus one 3/2/1 round. Full rules: [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md). The rule this month: **no preparation**. Read the card, start talking within 2 seconds. Never stop.

**Step 1 — Warm-up (5 min).** Say these three out loud, 30 seconds each, no recording: "If I had a free day tomorrow…", "If I were a famous chef…", "If I could change one thing about my apartment…".

**Step 2 — Random-topic rounds (35 min).** Mix these 15 cards. Pick one without looking, start the recorder, and talk for **1–2 minutes**. Use the five-step chain: answer → why → picture it → but → ask back. Do as many as you can in 35 minutes (aim for 10+).

1. If you could have any job in the world for one year, what would it be?
2. If you could live in any decade, which one would you pick?
3. If you had to give up coffee or your phone for a month, which would you give up?
4. If you were the mayor of your city for a day, what would you change?
5. If you could instantly be an expert at one skill, what would it be?
6. If you won a free vacation tomorrow, where would you go and who would you take?
7. If you could design any app you wanted, with no limits, what would it do?
8. If you could only eat one type of food for a year, what would it be?
9. If you had an extra hour every day, how would you use it?
10. If you could swap lives with a friend for a week, who would it be?
11. If you could talk to animals, which animal would you talk to first?
12. If you didn't need to sleep, what would you do at night?
13. If you could redesign your office, what would it look like?
14. If you were invisible for one day, what would you do (keep it legal)?
15. If you had to move to another country next month, where would you go?

**Step 3 — One 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Pick the card you found **hardest** tonight. Do the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md) with it: 3 minutes, then 2 minutes, then 1 minute, recording each one. Same content, faster each time. In the 1-minute version, make sure every "I would" has become "I'd".

**Step 4 — Listen back and count (5 min).** Listen to your 1-minute version. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms, pauses over 2 seconds, and different connectors. Also count: how many times did you say "I'd"? Aim for 5+.
