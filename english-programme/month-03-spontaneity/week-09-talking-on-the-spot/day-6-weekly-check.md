**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 15 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 15 min

## Before you start

- You finished Days 1–5 of this week: [Talking about objects](day-1-talking-about-objects.md), [Talking about places](day-2-talking-about-places.md), [Talking about people](day-3-talking-about-people.md), [Talking about experiences](day-4-talking-about-experiences.md) and [Would you rather…?](day-5-would-you-rather.md).
- **Live conversation #2 of the week is tonight.** Confirm the time with your partner, or check your meetup's start time now. See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- Have ready: recording app, [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md), the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), and your numbers from the [Month 2 check](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-6-weekly-check.md) so you can compare.
- The test prompt, the backup prompts and the checklists are in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials). **Don't read the backup prompts before the test.**

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the **hardest clip of the week**. For most people that's Day 3 ("in the / at the / with the" joining) or Day 4 (hard clusters like "months" and "both of them"). Speak along with the whole clip twice, without pausing. Then do it a third time and record yourself. Listen to 30 seconds: does your "th" stay light and quick, or do you slow down every time it comes? See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**No preparation.** Read the prompt, start the recorder, and start talking within 2 seconds. Talk for **2 minutes**. Don't stop, don't restart.

> **Prompt:** "Tell me about a place you go to often. Describe what it's like, one person you usually see there, one thing you always bring or use there, and something that happened there once. Then tell me: would you rather keep going there or find somewhere new?"

This prompt uses every skill from this week: a place, a person, an object, an experience and a "would you rather". You don't need to do them in order. If your mind goes blank, jump to the next part.

Then:

1. Listen back once, all the way through.
2. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms/uhhs, pauses over 2 seconds, different connectors.
3. Use the **Week 9 checklist** in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials) to tick which shapes and chunks you used.
4. Write the numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), next to your Month 2 check numbers.

If you can't talk for the full 2 minutes, that's useful information, not a failure. Do one backup prompt from the Day 6 materials straight away, and note how far you got.

## 3. Skill check (15 min)

Rate yourself honestly: **1** = I can't do it yet, **2** = I can do it, but slowly, **3** = it comes out without thinking. Before you rate each line, **say one example out loud** on a topic you haven't used this week.

| Day | Topic | I can… | Rating (1–3) | If you scored 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talking about objects | talk for 1 minute about any object using what it is → my one → a moment → what I think | | Redo [Exercise 1.4](exercises.md#day-1-exercises) with 5 different objects from your home |
| 2 | Talking about places | describe a place by its feeling and vibe, not just facts, and pick one small part of a big place | | Redo [Exercise 2.3](exercises.md#day-2-exercises), then do 10 minutes of the Day 2 voice-mode prompt |
| 3 | Talking about people | describe someone with an adjective + proof, their habit and a quick story | | Redo [Exercise 3.2](exercises.md#day-3-exercises) and say each answer twice, faster |
| 4 | Talking about experiences | answer "Have you ever…?" with a fast first line and switch from "I've…" to "I went…" | | Redo [Exercise 4.2](exercises.md#day-4-exercises), then [Exercise 4.1](exercises.md#day-4-exercises) with a 5-second limit |
| 5 | Would you rather…? | pick in 2 seconds, give a reason, say the problem with the other option, ask back | | Redo [Exercise 5.2](exercises.md#day-5-exercises) with a 5-second limit per question |

Any topic you rate **1**: do the "If you scored 1" task on Day 7 (it can replace the listening) or add 10 minutes to Week 10 Day 1. Any topic you rate **2**: use it at least 3 times in tonight's live conversation.

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

This is **live conversation #2** of the week. For tips on nerves and how to start, see [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: "Talk about anything."** Tonight you don't steer the conversation to one topic. You follow wherever it goes, and you prove that you can talk about whatever comes up. Bring 3 "would you rather" questions from the [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials) for when the chat slows down.

Things to try (tick them off afterwards with the mission checklist in the [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials)):

1. When the other person mentions an object, a place or a person, ask about it ("Wait, what's that place like?") and then talk about your own version for at least 30 seconds.
2. Ask one "Have you ever…?" question and answer it yourself with a fast first line and a short story.
3. Describe one person in your life with an adjective + proof ("She's super calm. Like…").
4. Ask one "would you rather" when there's a pause. Pick your own answer in 2 seconds and defend it.
5. Never answer with just one word. Use [answer, add, ask back](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md) every time.

**Backup plan:** if the live conversation falls through, do 45 minutes of voice mode with Claude and 15 minutes of notes. Paste this:

```text
Let's have a 45-minute casual voice conversation in American English, like two people who just met at a language exchange meetup. I'm a B2 learner. This week I practised talking on the spot about objects, places, people, experiences and "would you rather" questions.

Please:
- Chat naturally and let the topic jump around, the way real conversations do.
- Every few minutes, bring up something random: an object ("What's the oldest thing you own?"), a place, a person in my life, a "Have you ever…?" question, or a "Would you rather…?" question.
- Ask one question at a time and keep your turns short so I talk more than you.
- Don't correct me during the chat.
When I say "stop", tell me: which topics I handled best, where I hesitated most, and my 5 most important mistakes with corrections.
Start by asking how my week was.
```

After the conversation, write down: the topic you handled best, the moment you froze, and what you wish you'd said.
