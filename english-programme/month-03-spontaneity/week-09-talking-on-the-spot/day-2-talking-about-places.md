## Learning objectives

- Talk for 1 minute about any place, real or general, with no preparation.
- Use a 5-step shape for places: where → what it's like → the vibe → a moment there → would I go back.
- Use 10+ chunks for places: "It's one of those places where…", "It's always packed", "It's kind of a hidden gem", "You walk in and…".
- Say the voiced "th" (/ð/) lightly and quickly in fast speech: the, this, that, there, they.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: the voiced "th" — the, this, that, there, they, other.**

Yesterday's "th" had no voice. Tonight's is the same tongue position (tip of the tongue touching the back of your top teeth or just between your teeth), but your throat **buzzes**. Put your hand on your throat and say "thhhis". You should feel it vibrate.

Here's the secret: in fast American speech, this sound is **very short and very light**. In "the", "this", "that" and "there", it often takes almost no time. The common mistake is to replace it with a **d** ("dis", "dat", "dere") or a **z** ("zis"). A light, quick touch of the tongue is enough. Don't push it far out, or you'll slow down.

| Word | ❌ Not like | ✅ Like |
|---|---|---|
| this | dis / zis | thhis (quick, buzzing) |
| there | dere / zere | thhere |
| they | day / zay | thhey |
| other | udder / ozzer | uh-thher |
| the | duh | thuh (before a consonant), thee (before a vowel, sometimes) |

**Practice sentences** (CAPITALS = stress). Say each one 3 times, then faster:

1. THERE'S this little PLACE right by my apartment.
2. They've got the BEST tacos in the city.
3. It's one of those places where EVERYbody knows each other.
4. The vibe there is SUPER chill.
5. I go there, like, every OTHER weekend.
6. That place is ALWAYS packed on Fridays.
7. They redid the whole THING last year.
8. You walk in, and the SMELL hits you right away.
9. I'd rather go to the OTHER one, though.
10. That's the thing about BIG cities, right?
11. Is THIS the place you were talking about?

**Clip to find:** an American travel or city vlog where the creator walks around and talks casually, not a professional narrated documentary. Search YouTube for "**walking tour vlog talking**", "**exploring my neighborhood vlog**" or "**best coffee shops in my city vlog**". Pick a 2–3 minute section. Listen once, then **speak along** with the whole clip, without pausing, twice. Second time, focus on keeping "the", "this", "that" and "there" short and light. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Talking about places

### Explanation

Places come up constantly. "Where are you from?" "Have you been to that new place on Main Street?" "What's your neighborhood like?" "Where'd you go on vacation?" At work: "How's the new office?" These questions sound simple, but many learners answer with a list of facts: "It is a city. It is big. It has many restaurants." That's correct English, but it's boring for the listener and it ends the conversation fast.

When native speakers talk about a place, they don't give facts. They give a **feeling**: what it's like to be there. Tonight's shape helps you do that on the spot:

| Step | Question in your head | Example: "a café you like" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Where** | Where is it, roughly? | "There's this little café like five minutes from my place." |
| 2. **What it's like** | What do you see, hear, smell? | "It's tiny, like six tables, and it's got these huge windows and plants everywhere." |
| 3. **The vibe** | How does it feel? Who goes there? | "The vibe is super chill. It's mostly people on laptops and old guys reading the paper." |
| 4. **A moment** | One time there, or what you usually do there. | "I actually did a whole design sprint there once because our office Wi-Fi was down." |
| 5. **Back or not** | Would you recommend it? Go back? | "Honestly, it's kind of a hidden gem. If you ever go, you've gotta try the banana bread." |

The trick is step 2 and step 3. Use your **senses** (see, hear, smell) and **the people** there. That's what makes the listener picture it. And if you get a big place like "a country you've visited" or "your hometown", don't try to describe the whole thing. Pick **one small part** of it, like one street, one beach or one restaurant, and describe that.

You already learned vivid describing words ("super crowded", "kinda sketchy") in [Week 1 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md). Tonight you use them live, with no time to plan.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| There's this little… (+ place) | you introduce a place casually | "There's this little bookstore near the station." |
| It's like (time) from… / It's right by… | you say where it is | "It's right by the river, like a ten-minute walk from work." |
| It's one of those places where… | you describe what usually happens there | "It's one of those places where you go for one drink and stay till midnight." |
| It's always packed / It's totally dead | you talk about how busy it is | "It's always packed on weekends, but on Tuesdays it's totally dead." |
| The vibe is… | you describe the feeling | "The vibe is kinda fancy, but not in an annoying way." |
| You walk in and… | you describe the first impression | "You walk in and it smells like fresh bread." |
| It's kind of a hidden gem | it's good and not many people know it | "It's kind of a hidden gem. Nobody from out of town knows about it." |
| It's a bit of a tourist trap | it's popular but overpriced or fake | "The main square's pretty, but it's a bit of a tourist trap." |
| It's not much to look at, but… | it looks plain but it's good | "It's not much to look at, but the food is amazing." |
| I used to go there all the time | you talk about the past | "I used to go there all the time when I was in college." |
| It's the kind of place you… | you describe who it's for | "It's the kind of place you take your parents when they visit." |
| If you ever go, you've gotta… | you recommend something | "If you ever go, you've gotta see the sunset from the roof." |

### Examples

**1. "Where are you from?" — going beyond the facts**

> **A:** So where are you from originally?
> **B:** I grew up in a pretty small town, like two hours from the capital. It's one of those places where everybody knows everybody, so you can't, like, do anything without your mom finding out by dinner. There's this one main street with a bakery, a pharmacy and like three cafés, and that's kind of it. I used to go to the same café every day after school. Honestly, it's not much to look at, but I miss how quiet it is. Have you ever lived somewhere small?

**2. A colleague asks about the new office**

> **A:** How's the new office? I haven't been yet.
> **B:** Oh, it's really nice actually. It's right by the train station, so the commute's way easier. You walk in and there's this huge open space with plants everywhere, kinda like a greenhouse. The vibe is way more relaxed than the old one. The only thing is, it's always packed on Tuesdays and Thursdays, because that's when everyone comes in. So if you want a desk by the window, you've gotta get there before nine.

**3. A vacation place — pick one small part**

> **A:** You went to Portugal, right? How was it?
> **B:** Amazing! OK so, the whole trip was great, but there's this one little beach we found by accident. It was like a twenty-minute walk down a cliff, and when you get there, there's literally nobody. Just rocks, super blue water and one guy selling cold drinks out of a cooler. We spent like four hours there. It's totally a hidden gem. If you ever go, you've gotta skip the big beaches and find one of those.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "It is a big city with many buildings and many people." | "It's huge, and it's always packed, like, everywhere you go." | Facts are boring. Describe the feeling of being there. |
| "There is many restaurants." | "There are tons of restaurants." / "There's a ton of restaurants." | *There are* + plural (in speech, "there's a ton of…" is fine). |
| "It's near to my house." | "It's near my house." / "It's close to my house." | *Near* has no *to*. *Close* needs *to*. |
| "The atmosphere is very nice." | "The vibe is super chill." / "It's got a really nice feel to it." | "Atmosphere is very nice" sounds like a review. "Vibe" is natural. |
| "I went there in last summer." | "I went there last summer." | No *in* before *last / next / this*. |
| Describing a whole country for 1 minute | Picking one street, one beach, one café | Small places are easier to picture and easier to talk about. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **voice mode with Claude**, about places. Claude names places at random and you answer straight away. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) for how to set it up.

**Steps:**

1. **Start voice mode and paste this prompt first** (type or paste it, then switch to voice):

```text
Let's do a spontaneous speaking practice in voice mode. I'm a B2 English learner and my goal is to talk without thinking, in casual American English.

Tonight's topic: PLACES. Here's how it works:
1. Name one place at random (examples: a café you like, your hometown, the worst hotel you've stayed in, a park near you, your office, an airport, a place you'd never go back to, your childhood bedroom, a supermarket, a city you want to visit). Don't tell me the list in advance. Pick a different kind of place each time.
2. I'll talk about it for about 1 minute with no preparation.
3. Then ask me 2 quick follow-up questions about it, like a curious friend would, and react naturally ("No way!", "Oh, that sounds nice").
4. Then move straight to the next place. Keep the pace fast.

Rules: Don't correct me during the chat. Keep your own turns short (1–2 sentences) so I do most of the talking. If I go silent for more than 3 seconds, say "Keep going!" or ask a simple question to help me.

After about 40 minutes, when I say "stop", give me feedback: my 5 most important grammar or vocabulary mistakes with the corrected version, 3 places where I sounded unnatural and what a native speaker would say, and the place descriptions where I sounded best.
```

2. **Talk for 40 minutes.** That's roughly 12–15 places. Your goals:
   - Start each answer within 2 seconds. Use a starter if you need one ("Oh, that's a good one. So…").
   - Use the 5-step shape: where → what it's like → the vibe → a moment → back or not.
   - Use at least 2 phrases from tonight's key phrases table in each answer.
   - When Claude asks a follow-up, use [answer, add, ask back](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md) at least once every few places, so it feels like a real chat.
3. **Say "stop" and get feedback (5 min).** Listen to Claude's feedback.
4. **Write notes (10 min).** On your paper, write:
   - the 5 corrected sentences (say each correct version out loud 3 times),
   - 2 phrases Claude used that you want to steal,
   - the place where you froze the most, and what you could have said.
5. If you recorded yourself, count the [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) for one 1-minute answer and write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
