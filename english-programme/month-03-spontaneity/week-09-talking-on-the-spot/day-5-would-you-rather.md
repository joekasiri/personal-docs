## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight: a full vlog speak-along, with all the "th" sounds from this week.**

This week you practised the unvoiced "th" (think), the voiced "th" (this), "th" after another word (in the) and hard clusters (months). Tonight you put them together in one long clip, with no pausing. One bonus: tonight's key phrase, "I'd rather", has a "th" in the middle. It sounds like **"ahd RA-thur"**, with a light, buzzing "th". Not "ahd RA-der".

**Practice sentences** (CAPITALS = stress). Say each 3 times, then at full speed:

1. Honestly? I'd RATHER stay home.
2. I THINK I'd go with the OTHER one.
3. NEITHER, can I pick neither?
4. I'd rather do THAT than sit in traffic for three HOURS.
5. BOTH of them sound kinda TERRIBLE, to be honest.
6. Part of me wants to say the BEACH, but…
7. At least with the MOUNTAINS, there's less people.
8. The THING with being famous is, you never get a BREAK.
9. I'd rather have three MONTHS off than a raise.
10. There's NO way I'd do that. Like, at ALL.
11. OK, that's a tough one. Give me a SECOND.

**Clip to find:** a casual "would you rather" video with two American friends or a couple answering questions together. Search YouTube for "**would you rather challenge couple**", "**would you rather questions with friends**" or "**this or that questions vlog**". These are perfect because they *are* tonight's topic. Pick 2–3 minutes. Listen once, then **speak along** with the whole clip, twice, without pausing. Copy the laughing, the "ohhh, that's hard" and the speed. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Would you rather…?

### Explanation

"Would you rather…?" questions are everywhere in casual English: icebreakers at work, first dates, road trips, parties, team-building games. "Would you rather work from home forever or in an office forever?" They're fun, but they're also a perfect test of talking without thinking, because:

- There's **no right answer**, so you can't "prepare" one.
- You have to **choose fast**. Taking ten seconds to decide feels awkward.
- A one-word answer ("Beach.") is boring. People expect you to *defend* your choice a little.

**The grammar is simple.** You only need these:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| I'd rather + verb | "I'd rather stay home." |
| I'd rather + verb + than + verb | "I'd rather walk than take the bus." |
| I'd go with / I'd pick / I'd choose + noun | "I'd go with the beach." |
| I wouldn't wanna + verb | "I wouldn't wanna be famous." |

Notice: "I'd rather **stay**", not "I'd rather to stay" or "I'd rather staying". The full hypothetical grammar ("If I could…, I'd…") is next week's topic, in [Week 10 Day 1](../week-10-hypotheticals/day-1-if-i-could.md). Tonight, keep it simple.

**The answer shape:**

| Step | What you say | Example: "Big city or countryside?" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Pick — fast** | Choose in the first 2 seconds. You can change your mind later. | "Oh, city. Easy." |
| 2. **Reason** | One main reason | "I get bored really fast, and in a city there's always something going on." |
| 3. **The problem with the other one** | Why not the other option? | "The problem with the countryside is you need a car for everything. Like, want milk? Twenty-minute drive." |
| 4. **Imagine it** | Picture yourself in it for a moment | "Like, imagine me on a farm. I'd last about a week." |
| 5. **Ask back** | "What about you?" | "What about you? You seem like a countryside person." |

**The golden rule: pick first, think later.** Say an answer in the first two seconds, even if you're not sure. If you change your mind while talking, that's natural and fun: "Actually, wait, no. I take it back. Countryside." People do this all the time.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Oh, easy. (+ choice) | the choice is obvious to you | "Oh, easy. Dogs." |
| Ooh, that's a tough one. | you need a second, and it's hard | "Ooh, that's a tough one. OK… I'd say mountains." |
| I'd go with… / I'd pick… | you make a choice | "I'd go with the personal chef, for sure." |
| I'd rather… than… | you compare two actions | "I'd rather be early than late." |
| Hands down, … | it's not even close | "Hands down, the beach." |
| If I had to choose, … | you don't like either option | "If I had to choose, I guess the whisper one." |
| Part of me wants to say…, but… | you're torn between both | "Part of me wants to say rich, but famous sounds fun." |
| The problem with… is… | you say why not the other one | "The problem with being invisible is nobody notices you." |
| At least with…, you… | you find the good side of your choice | "At least with a slow internet, you'd still have the internet." |
| There's no way I'd… | you strongly refuse an option | "There's no way I'd give up coffee." |
| I'm gonna be boring and say… | you choose the safe option | "I'm gonna be boring and say the office." |
| Can I pick both? / Can I cheat? | you want to play with the rules | "Can I cheat? Beach in the morning, mountains at night." |
| Actually, wait, I take it back. | you change your mind mid-answer | "Actually, wait, I take it back. Future, not past." |

### Examples

**1. Team icebreaker**

> **A:** OK, would you rather always be ten minutes late or always be twenty minutes early?
> **B:** Ooh, that's a tough one. I'd go with early. I hate rushing, like, it makes me super anxious. The problem with being late is everyone's waiting for you, and you walk in all sweaty. At least with being early, you can grab a coffee and check your email. Although… twenty minutes is a lot. Like, imagine every single meeting, just sitting there. But yeah, still early. What about you?
> **A:** Late. Definitely late.
> **B:** No way! Really?

**2. Road trip with a friend**

> **A:** Would you rather give up coffee or give up dessert?
> **B:** Oh, easy. There's no way I'd give up coffee. Dessert, bye. I mean, I like dessert, but I could live without it. Coffee is literally how I function. Like, imagine me in a 9 a.m. design review without coffee. Nobody wants that. Can I ask you one?

**3. Changing your mind mid-answer**

> **A:** Would you rather travel to the past or the future?
> **B:** Past, for sure. I'd love to see, like, my city a hundred years ago. Actually, wait, I take it back. The future. Because the problem with the past is there's no dentists, and no internet, and honestly I'd probably get sick in like a day. At least with the future, it's probably more comfortable. And I'd get to see how everything turns out. Yeah, future. Final answer.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I would rather to live in a city." | "I'd rather live in a city." | *Would rather* + base verb, no *to*. |
| "I prefer the beach than the mountains." | "I'd rather go to the beach than the mountains." / "I prefer the beach to the mountains." | *Prefer… to…*, or *rather… than…*. |
| "Mmm… mmm… I don't know… both are good…" (10 seconds) | "Ooh, tough one. I'd go with… the beach." | Pick in 2 seconds. You can change your mind later. |
| "Beach." (end) | "Beach. Hands down. The problem with mountains is…" | Defend your choice. A one-word answer ends the game. |
| "I would rather that I am rich." | "I'd rather be rich." | Keep it simple: *I'd rather be…* |
| "I would rather live in city because is more interesting." | "I'd rather live in a city, because it's way more interesting." | Don't drop *a* and *it's* when speaking fast. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **random-topic drill with "would you rather" cards**, then **one 3/2/1 round**. Follow the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) rules: no looking, start within 2 seconds, keep going for the full minute.

**Tonight's 20 "would you rather" cards.** Would you rather…

1. …live in a big city or in the countryside?
2. …never use social media again, or never watch a movie or series again?
3. …always be ten minutes late, or always be twenty minutes early?
4. …have a job you love with low pay, or a boring job with great pay?
5. …be able to speak every language, or play every musical instrument?
6. …work from home forever, or in an office forever?
7. …give up coffee, or give up dessert?
8. …travel to the past, or to the future?
9. …have a personal chef, or a personal driver?
10. …only be able to whisper, or only be able to shout?
11. …live without music, or without TV?
12. …have a huge house far from everything, or a tiny apartment right downtown?
13. …be famous, or be rich but unknown?
14. …go on vacation to the beach, or to the mountains?
15. …never have to sleep, or never have to eat?
16. …have unlimited free flights, or unlimited free food at any restaurant?
17. …be able to read minds, or be invisible whenever you want?
18. …present to 500 people, or have a one-hour call in a language you don't speak?
19. …have your phone battery always at 10%, or have super slow internet forever?
20. …relive one year of your life, or skip ahead five years?

**Steps:**

1. **Warm-up (5 min).** Say these 5 times each, fast: "Ooh, that's a tough one", "I'd go with…", "The problem with… is…", "At least with…", "What about you?"
2. **Random-topic rounds (35 min).** Start recording. Pick a card. **Choose within 2 seconds**, then talk for **1 minute** using the shape: pick → reason → the problem with the other one → imagine it → ask back (ask the empty room, that's fine). Next card straight away. Aim for 15–20 cards. Extra challenge for every fifth card: change your mind halfway ("Actually, wait, I take it back…") and defend the new choice.
3. **One 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Pick the card that was hardest. Do a [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md) on it. In the 3-minute version, add a short real story that supports your choice (an [experience](day-4-talking-about-experiences.md) from yesterday's lesson works well), and use your [opinion phrases](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-1-giving-opinions.md).
4. **Listen back and count (5 min).** Listen to the 1-minute version. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) and write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). Compare with Day 1's numbers.
