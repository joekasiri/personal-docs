## Learning objectives

- Talk for 1 minute about anyone you know, with no preparation.
- Use a 4-step shape for people: who they are to me → what they're like (with proof) → their thing → a quick story.
- Use 10+ chunks about people: "She's the kind of person who…", "We go way back", "He's got this habit of…", "She cracks me up".
- Link "th" to the word before it in fast speech: in the, at the, with the, on the.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: "th" after another word — in the, at the, with the, on the, and the.**

On Days 1 and 2 you practised "th" on its own. In real speech, "the", "this" and "that" almost always come **after** another word, and the two words join together. This is where most learners slow down. Native speakers don't stop between the words. They slide:

| Written | What it sounds like | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| in the office | in-nthuh OFFice | The N moves forward to your teeth and flows straight into "th". |
| at the party | a-thuh PARty | The T almost disappears. Your tongue goes straight to "th". |
| with the team | wi-thuh TEAM | One "th" sound, not two. |
| on the weekend | on-nthuh WEEKend | Like "in the". |
| and the… | 'n-thuh | "And" becomes a tiny "'n". |
| all the time | all-thuh TIME | Smooth, no gap. |

**Practice sentences** (CAPITALS = stress). Say each one 3 times, slowly first, then at full speed:

1. She's the kind of person who's ALWAYS at the CENTER of things.
2. I met him at the GYM, like, five years ago.
3. He's the funniest guy in the OFFICE.
4. We hang out on the WEEKENDS, mostly.
5. She's always on the PHONE with her MOM.
6. He gets along with EVERYbody on the team.
7. She texts me all the TIME, it's kinda funny.
8. We sat at the back of the CLASS and talked the whole time.
9. He's the one who ALWAYS remembers birthdays.
10. I ran into her at the STATION last week.

**Clip to find:** an American YouTuber or podcaster talking about a friend, partner or family member in a casual, warm way. Search for "**storytime about my best friend**", "**my roommate story vlog**" or "**talking about my family podcast casual**". Pick 2–3 minutes. Listen once, then **speak along** with the whole clip, twice, without pausing. Second time, don't let any "in the / at the / with the" break the flow. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Talking about people

### Explanation

People are the most common topic of casual conversation. "How do you know Sam?" "What's your boss like?" "Do you have siblings?" "Who's the funniest person you know?" At a party, you'll talk about the host, your friends and your colleagues. At work, you'll talk about clients and teammates.

The usual problem is that learners answer with a list of adjectives: "He is nice. He is funny. He is kind." That says almost nothing, because everyone is "nice". Native speakers give **one or two adjectives and then prove them** with a habit or a short story. Here's the shape:

| Step | Question in your head | Example: "your manager" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Who they are to me** | How do I know them? How long? | "So my manager, Lena, I've worked with her for like two years now." |
| 2. **What they're like + proof** | One or two words, and *why* I say that | "She's super calm. Like, nothing stresses her out. Even when a launch goes wrong, she's just like, 'OK, what do we know?'" |
| 3. **Their thing** | A habit, a phrase they always say, something only they do | "She's got this habit of drawing everything. You ask her a question, and she grabs a marker and starts sketching boxes on the whiteboard." |
| 4. **A quick story** | One moment that shows who they are | "One time she literally drew a user flow on a napkin at lunch, and we ended up shipping it." |

Step 2 is the important one: **adjective + proof**. If you say "she's calm", the next words should be "like…" and an example. That one word "like" is your bridge.

You also don't need to describe what people look like. In casual chat, people rarely care about hair colour. They care about personality, your relationship, and funny or surprising details. If you need to describe appearance or quick details, you already have the language from [Week 1 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md). And when you quote what someone says, use the casual reported speech from [Week 5 Day 3](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-3-casual-reported-speech.md) ("she's like…", "he goes…").

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| He's / She's the kind of person who… | you describe personality through behaviour | "He's the kind of person who replies to emails at 2 a.m." |
| She's one of those people who… | same idea, a bit more casual | "She's one of those people who can talk to anyone." |
| We go way back | you've known someone a long time | "Oh, Tom and I go way back. Like, kindergarten." |
| We hit it off right away | you liked each other immediately | "We met at a workshop and hit it off right away." |
| I get along really well with… | you have a good relationship | "I get along really well with my sister now." |
| We're not super close, but… | the relationship is distant | "We're not super close, but we grab lunch sometimes." |
| He's got this habit of… | you describe a typical thing they do | "He's got this habit of humming when he's thinking." |
| She's always the one who… | they always do the same role | "She's always the one who plans the trips." |
| He's a bit of a… (perfectionist / night owl / neat freak) | you label a type of person, lightly | "He's a bit of a neat freak. His desk is spotless." |
| She cracks me up | they make you laugh a lot | "My grandma cracks me up. She's so blunt." |
| He's really easy to talk to | conversation with them is easy | "He's really easy to talk to, even if you just met him." |
| You'd like her / You'd get along | you connect the person to the listener | "You'd totally get along. You're both obsessed with running." |

### Examples

**1. At a party: "How do you know Sam?"**

> **A:** So how do you know Sam?
> **B:** Oh, we go way back! We were roommates in college, actually. He's the kind of person who knows everyone, like, you can't walk down a street with him without him saying hi to three people. And he's always the one who organizes stuff. Like this party, he planned it in like two days. Honestly, he cracks me up. What about you, how'd you guys meet?

**2. A friend asks about your new teammate**

> **A:** How's the new designer on your team?
> **B:** She's great, actually. We hit it off right away. She's a bit of a perfectionist, like, she'll spend an hour on one icon. But in a good way, you know? And she's got this habit of asking "but why?" in every meeting, which kinda drove our PM crazy at first, but now everyone does it. She's really easy to talk to, too. You'd like her.

**3. "Do you have any siblings?" — when there's not much to say**

> **A:** Do you have any siblings?
> **B:** Yeah, one older brother. We're not super close, honestly, he lives in another city and we're pretty different. He's one of those people who's super into cars and sports, and I'm, like, not at all. But the funny part is, every time we talk on the phone, we end up talking for like two hours. So I guess we get along better than I think.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "He is nice and kind and friendly." | "He's super easy-going. Like, nothing bothers him." | Adjective lists say nothing. One adjective + proof. |
| "She is a person who always helps." | "She's the kind of person who always helps." | "The kind of person who…" is the natural chunk. |
| "I know him since 10 years." | "I've known him for ten years." | Present perfect + *for* with a period of time. |
| "We are going way back." | "We go way back." | Fixed phrase, present simple. |
| "He has a habit to hum." | "He's got this habit of humming." | *Habit of + -ing.* |
| "She makes me laugh very much." | "She cracks me up." / "She's hilarious." | More natural and shorter. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is split: **30 minutes of random-topic drill** with people cards, then **30 minutes of live conversation #1**.

### Part A: People-card drill (30 min)

Follow the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) rules. Pick without looking, start within 2 seconds, talk for **1 minute**, record everything.

**Tonight's 12 people cards:**

1. Your best friend from school
2. A neighbour you know (or don't know)
3. The funniest person you know
4. A teacher you remember
5. Someone at work you get along with
6. A relative you see only at big family events
7. The most organised person you know
8. Someone you lost touch with
9. A boss or manager you've had
10. A friend who is very different from you
11. Someone who taught you something important
12. The last new person you met

**Steps:**

1. **Warm-up (3 min):** say "She's the kind of person who…", "We go way back" and "He's got this habit of…" 5 times each, with a real person in mind.
2. **Rounds (22 min):** pick, talk 1 minute, next. Aim for 10–12 cards. Every answer must include at least one **adjective + proof** ("She's super calm. Like…").
3. **Quick check (5 min):** listen to one recording. Did you prove your adjectives? Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) and write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (30 min)

Use [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md) for tips on nerves and how to start.

**Your mission: "People in our lives."** Have a normal, relaxed chat, but steer it towards people at least twice.

1. Ask your partner about someone in their life: "Who's the funniest person you know?", "Do you have siblings?" or "What's your boss like?" Then ask **2 follow-up questions** about that person (see [follow-up questions](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md)).
2. When they ask back, talk about one of **your** people for at least a minute, using the 4-step shape.
3. Use at least **3 phrases** from tonight's key phrases. Tick them off on paper after the call.
4. Tell one short story about a person using "I was like… / she goes…".
5. Don't plan your answers before the call. Just go.

**Backup plan (voice mode):** if your partner cancels, open voice mode with Claude and paste this:

```text
Let's have a 30-minute casual conversation in voice mode, like two new friends getting coffee. I'm a B2 English learner practising talking without preparation, in casual American English.

Steer the conversation towards people in our lives at least 3 times: ask me about my friends, family, colleagues or someone I've lost touch with, and tell me about "your" people too (invent a realistic life). Ask natural follow-up questions. Keep your turns short so I talk more than you.

Don't correct me during the chat. When I say "stop", give me my 5 most important mistakes with corrections, and tell me which of these phrases I used: "the kind of person who", "we go way back", "hit it off", "got this habit of", "cracks me up", "easy to talk to".
```

After the conversation (live or voice), write down: 1 thing that went well, 1 moment you froze, and what you'll try on Day 6.
