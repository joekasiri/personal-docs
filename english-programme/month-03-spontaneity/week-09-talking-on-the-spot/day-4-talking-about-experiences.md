## Learning objectives

- Answer "Have you ever…?" and "What's the best/worst… you've ever…?" straight away, with a natural first line.
- Switch smoothly from present perfect ("I've been there twice") to past simple ("The first time, I went with…").
- Turn a yes, a no or a "not really" into a 1-minute answer.
- Say "th" in hard clusters (months, clothes, both of them) without slowing down.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: hard "th" clusters — months, clothes, fifth, both of them, with them.**

Some words put "th" right next to other consonants: "months" (n-th-s), "clothes" (th-z), "fifth" (f-th). Even native speakers find these hard, so they **simplify** them. Here's the good news: you're allowed to do the same.

| Written | Careful | What many Americans actually say |
|---|---|---|
| months | mun-th-s | **munts** or **muns** (the "th" almost disappears) |
| clothes | cloth-z | **cloze** (sounds like "close" the verb) |
| both of them | boath of them | **BOATH-uv-'em** |
| with them | with them | **WITH-'em** ("them" → "'em") |
| three things | three things | **three-THINGS** (one smooth line) |
| sixth | sicks-th | **sicks** (the "th" is tiny) |

The rule: when "th" is easy (start of a word, "think", "the"), say it clearly. When it's stuck between other consonants, keep the rhythm and let it be small. Rhythm matters more than one perfect sound.

**Practice sentences** (CAPITALS = stress). Say each 3 times:

1. I lived there for like SIX munts.
2. I packed WAY too many cloze.
3. I've been there TWICE, BOATH-uv-'em in the summer.
4. I went WITH-'em to the beach, and it was a DISASTER.
5. It was the BEST three weeks of my LIFE.
6. I've done it a couple of TIMES, actually.
7. Have you EVER been to a music festival?
8. It was my sicks time on a plane, and I was STILL scared.
9. I THOUGHT it was gonna be EASY. It was NOT.
10. That was hands DOWN the worst trip I've ever TAKEN.

**Clip to find:** a "travel fails" or "worst experience" storytime from a chatty American creator. Search YouTube for "**worst travel experience storytime**", "**things I've done once and never again**" or "**my craziest experience storytime**". Pick 2–3 minutes. Listen once, then **speak along** twice without pausing. Second time, focus on keeping the rhythm through words like "months", "clothes" and "both of them". See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Talking about experiences

### Explanation

"Have you ever…?" is one of the most common conversation questions in English. "Have you ever been to Mexico?" "Have you ever broken a bone?" "What's the best concert you've ever been to?" "What's the weirdest food you've ever tried?" They come up at parties, first dates, team lunches and icebreakers.

Two things make them hard to answer on the spot:

1. **You have to search your memory fast.** If you can't find anything, you freeze.
2. **The grammar changes in the middle.** The question uses the present perfect ("have you ever…"), but your story uses the past simple ("I went…"). Learners often get stuck between the two.

**The tense switch.** Here's how it works. The first line of your answer talks about your experience *in general*, with no time: present perfect. As soon as you say *when* or start the story, switch to past simple.

> "Have you ever been to New York?"
> "Yeah, **I've been** twice, actually. *(general, no time → present perfect)* The first time **I went** with my parents, when I **was** like twelve, and **I hated** it. *(specific time → past simple)*"

**The answer shape:**

| Step | What you say | Example: "Have you ever gotten lost?" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Quick first line** | Yes / no / sort of, in a natural way | "Oh, so many times. I've got the worst sense of direction." |
| 2. **When and where** (switch tense) | One time, the specific one | "The worst one was in Tokyo, like three years ago." |
| 3. **What happened** | 3–4 quick sentences | "My phone died, I couldn't read any signs, and I ended up walking for like two hours in the wrong direction." |
| 4. **How it felt / looking back** | Your feeling now | "At the time I was freaking out, but looking back, it was kind of the best night of the trip." |
| 5. **Ask back** | Give them a turn | "What about you? Are you good with directions?" |

**When the answer is "no".** Don't stop at "No, never." Say the closest thing you *have* done, or say what you think about it:

- "No, never. But I've done something kinda similar…"
- "Not that I can remember. I'd love to, though, because…"
- "No, and honestly, I never will. I'm way too scared of…"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Actually, yeah, once. | you have one example | "Actually, yeah, once. It was a total disaster." |
| Oh, so many times. | you have lots of examples | "Missed a flight? Oh, so many times." |
| Not that I can remember. | you're not sure, probably no | "Not that I can remember. Maybe as a kid?" |
| I've never…, but I've… | you give the closest experience | "I've never been skiing, but I've been snowboarding once." |
| Oh man, the worst one was probably… | you choose one example from many | "Oh man, the worst one was probably my sister's wedding." |
| That was hands down the best… | something was clearly the best | "That was hands down the best meal I've ever had." |
| I'll never forget… | the memory is very strong | "I'll never forget the look on his face." |
| It was way harder than I expected | it surprised you | "The hike was way harder than I expected." |
| Looking back, … | you say how you feel about it now | "Looking back, I'm glad it happened." |
| I'd do it again in a heartbeat | you'd happily do it again | "Skydiving? I'd do it again in a heartbeat." |
| Never again. / Once was enough. | you'd never do it again | "A 14-hour bus ride? Once was enough." |
| It was a once-in-a-lifetime thing | it was rare and special | "Seeing the northern lights was a once-in-a-lifetime thing." |

### Examples

**1. Team lunch: a "no" that keeps going**

> **A:** Have you ever done one of those escape rooms?
> **B:** No, never, actually. But I've done something kinda similar. We did this murder-mystery dinner for my friend's birthday, where everyone gets a character. I was the "evil butler", which was hilarious, because I'm terrible at lying. I'd love to try an escape room, though. Is it hard?

**2. Icebreaker: best concert**

> **A:** What's the best concert you've ever been to?
> **B:** Oh, that's easy. It was this small jazz show in a basement bar, like four years ago. I went by myself because my friend canceled last minute, and I almost didn't go. There were like thirty people there, and the band was right in front of us, like, two meters away. I'll never forget it. That was hands down the best night of that whole year. Do you go to concerts much?

**3. Work chat: a hard experience**

> **A:** Have you ever had to present to, like, the whole company?
> **B:** Yeah, once. Last year I presented our redesign at the all-hands. It was way harder than I expected. My slides froze halfway through, so I just had to talk without them for like five minutes. At the time I wanted to disappear, but looking back, people said it was the best part, because it felt more real. Would I do it again? Maybe. Ask me in a year.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Yes, I have been in Paris last year." | "Yeah, I've been to Paris. I went last year." | With a specific time (*last year*), use past simple. |
| "Have you ever went there?" | "Have you ever been there?" / "Have you ever gone there?" | *Have* + past participle. |
| "I have been to there." | "I've been there." | No *to* before *there* or *here*. |
| "No." (silence) | "No, never. But I've…" / "Not that I can remember. Have you?" | A bare "no" kills the conversation. Give the closest thing. |
| "It was the best concert I ever saw it." | "It was the best concert I've ever been to." | Don't repeat the object (*it*). Natural verb: *been to*. |
| "I never forget it." | "I'll never forget it." | Use *will* for a promise about the future. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **voice mode with Claude.** Claude picks 10 random experience topics, and you answer each one straight away. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Steps:**

1. **Start voice mode and paste this prompt:**

```text
Let's do a spontaneous speaking session in voice mode. I'm a B2 English learner and I want to talk without thinking, in casual American English.

Part 1 (about 35 minutes): Pick 10 random EXPERIENCE topics, one at a time. Don't tell me the list in advance, and mix easy and harder ones. Ask each one as a natural question, like "Have you ever...?", "What's the best/worst/weirdest... you've ever...?" or "Tell me about a time you...". Possible areas: travel, food, sports, work, school, accidents, celebrations, being scared, being embarrassed, meeting someone famous, getting lost, learning something hard. I'll answer straight away for 1–2 minutes. Then ask me 1 or 2 short follow-up questions, react naturally, and sometimes share a short experience of "your own" so it feels like a real chat.

Part 2 (about 10 minutes): Fast round. Ask me 15 quick "Have you ever...?" questions in a row. I'll answer each one with a natural first line plus one detail, in under 10 seconds. Keep the pace very fast.

Rules: Don't correct me during the chat. If I stay silent for more than 3 seconds, say "Just say the first thing that comes to mind!" Keep your turns short.

When I say "stop", give me feedback: my 5 most important mistakes with the corrected sentence (especially present perfect vs past simple), 3 more natural ways to start an answer, and which answer was my best.
```

2. **Part 1 — 10 experience topics (35 min).** For each one:
   - First line within 2 seconds ("Actually, yeah, once." / "Oh, so many times." / "No, never, but…").
   - Switch to the past simple as soon as you say when.
   - Finish with how you feel now ("Looking back…") and ask back at least every second answer.
3. **Part 2 — fast round (10 min).** Answer each "Have you ever…?" in under 10 seconds: first line + one detail. No long stories.
4. **Feedback (5 min).** Say "stop" and listen.
5. **Notes (10 min).** Write down your 5 corrections and say each correct version 3 times. Write the topic that was hardest. If you recorded yourself, count the [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) for one answer and fill in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

**What to focus on tonight:** speed of the *first line*, and the tense switch. Don't worry about perfect stories.
