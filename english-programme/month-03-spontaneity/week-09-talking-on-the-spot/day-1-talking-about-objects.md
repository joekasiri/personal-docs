## Before you start

- Read the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) once. Tonight each topic is **1 minute**.
- Have your [sentence-starter bank](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-3-sentence-starter-bank.md) nearby. You'll use those openings to start each topic.
- Prepare tonight's topic cards: write the 15 objects from section 3 on small pieces of paper, fold them and put them in a cup or bowl. **Don't read them again after writing them.** Or number them 1–15 and use a random number app.
- Have ready: recording app, timer, [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).

## Learning objectives

- Talk for 1 minute about any everyday object, with no preparation.
- Use a simple 4-step shape (what it is → my one → a moment → what I think) so your mind never goes empty.
- Use 10+ natural chunks for describing things: "It's one of those…", "It's kind of a…", "I've had it for ages", "It's seen better days".
- Say the unvoiced "th" (/θ/) clearly in fast speech: think, thing, three, both.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: the unvoiced "th" — think, thing, three, month, both.**

This "th" has **no voice**. Your throat doesn't buzz. You put the tip of your tongue lightly between your teeth (or just behind your top teeth) and blow air out, like a soft hiss. That's it. It's a quiet sound, and in fast speech Americans say it quickly and lightly. You don't need to push your tongue far out.

The common mistakes are replacing it with **t** ("tink"), **s** ("sink") or **f** ("fink"). "I tink" sounds like a strong accent, and "I sink" means something else entirely.

| Word | ❌ Not like | ✅ Like |
|---|---|---|
| think | tink / sink | th-ink (air between tongue and teeth) |
| thing | ting / sing | th-ing |
| three | tree / free | th-ree |
| both | bot / bos | bo-th |
| month | mont | mun-th |

**Practice sentences** (CAPITALS = stress). Say each one 3 times at normal speed, then faster:

1. I THINK it's the THIRD one I've BOUGHT.
2. It's one of those THINGS you don't NEED, but you WANT.
3. I've had it for like THREE YEARS.
4. The THING is, it's kinda BROKEN.
5. I use it, like, every single MONTH.
6. BOTH of them are SUPER old.
7. I THOUGHT it was GONNA be useless.
8. It's thin, it's light, and it's PRETTY cheap.
9. Honestly, I'd say it's worth like a THOUSAND bucks to me.
10. I never really THOUGHT about it beFORE.

**Clip to find:** a chatty American vlogger showing and talking about their stuff, not reading a script. Search YouTube for "**what's in my bag vlog**", "**things I use every day vlog**" or "**favorite things I own talking**". Choose a 2–3 minute part where they talk a lot. Listen once. Then **speak along** with the whole clip, without pausing, twice. The first time, just keep up. The second time, listen for every "th" word and make sure your tongue goes to your teeth. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md) for the speak-along method.

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Talking about objects

### Explanation

Objects come up in conversation all the time, and usually without warning. "Oh, cool water bottle, where'd you get it?" "What's that thing on your keyring?" "Wait, you still use a paper notebook?" They also come up in icebreaker questions at meetups and work events: "What's one thing you couldn't live without?" These are easy questions, but under pressure your mind can go empty. You say "It's… a bottle. For water. It's good." And then silence.

The problem isn't vocabulary. You know enough words to describe a bottle. The problem is that you don't have a **shape** for the answer, so you don't know what comes next. Tonight you get a shape that works for any object:

| Step | Question in your head | Example: "your water bottle" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **What it is** | What kind of thing is it? | "Oh, it's one of those big metal ones that keeps stuff cold all day." |
| 2. **My one** | Where's mine from? How long have I had it? What's it like? | "I've had mine for like three years. It's got stickers all over it and a huge dent in the side." |
| 3. **A moment** | One time it mattered, or a normal day with it. | "I actually dropped it down the stairs at work once. It made the loudest noise ever, the whole office turned around." |
| 4. **What I think** | Do I love it? Is it overrated? Would I recommend it? | "Honestly, I'd be lost without it. I drink way more water when I have it with me." |

You don't have to use all four steps every time, and you don't have to go in order. But when your mind goes blank, **jump to the next step**. Blank after "what it is"? Talk about your one. Blank after that? Tell a moment. This is a casual version of the shape in the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md), made for objects.

Two more tips:

- **Describe, don't define.** A dictionary says "a container for liquid". A person says "it's one of those big metal ones". Talk about *your* version of the thing, not the thing in general.
- **If you don't own it, say so and keep going.** "I don't actually have one, but my roommate does, and…" or "I used to have one when I was a kid…". Every object connects to something in your life.

You learned how to make descriptions vivid in [Week 1 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md) and how to use softeners like "kinda" and "pretty" in [Week 4 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md). Tonight you use them, but with no time to plan.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's one of those… that… | you name the type of thing | "It's one of those little speakers that clips onto your bag." |
| It's kind of a… / It's basically a… | the thing is hard to name exactly | "It's basically a notebook, but the pages are erasable." |
| I've had it for ages / for like (time) | you say how long it's been yours | "I've had this jacket for like eight years." |
| It's got a… / It's got these… | you describe a feature | "It's got these tiny buttons on the side that I always press by accident." |
| I got it from… / Someone gave it to me… | you say where it came from | "My sister gave it to me when I moved out." |
| I use it for… / I use it to… | you say what you do with it | "I use it to block out noise on the train." |
| The thing about it is… | you're about to say the important part | "The thing about it is, it's really heavy." |
| It's seen better days / It's pretty beat-up | it's old or damaged | "My laptop's seen better days. The screen's kinda cracked." |
| I couldn't live without it / I could totally live without it | you give your opinion of it | "I could totally live without my smartwatch, honestly." |
| It's way better than… / It's nothing special, but… | you compare or play it down | "It's nothing special, but it does the job." |
| It reminds me of… | the object connects to a memory | "It reminds me of my grandpa's old kitchen." |
| Don't ask me why, but… | you admit something a bit silly | "Don't ask me why, but I still keep the box." |

### Examples

**1. At a meetup icebreaker**

> **A:** OK, quick one. What's one thing you always have with you?
> **B:** Oh, easy. My headphones. They're one of those big over-ear ones, like, noise-canceling. I've had them for like two years, and they've seen better days, the padding's kinda falling apart. But the thing about them is, I work in an open office, so without them I literally can't focus. Like, one time I forgot them at home and I got nothing done all day. So yeah, I couldn't live without them. What about you?

**2. A colleague notices something on your desk**

> **A:** Wait, what is that thing?
> **B:** Ha, it's kind of a… it's basically a timer, but it's a cube. You flip it to the side you want, like 15 minutes, 25 minutes, and it starts. I got it from a design conference, they were giving them out. Don't ask me why, but it actually works. I use it when I'm doing wireframes, so I don't spend three hours on one screen.
> **A:** That's genius.
> **B:** Right? It's nothing special, but it does the job.

**3. When you don't own the object**

> **A:** Do you have a car?
> **B:** No, I don't actually. But my dad had this really old red one when I was a kid, and it reminds me of every summer vacation. It was super loud, the AC never worked, and we'd drive like six hours to the coast with the windows down. Honestly, I kinda miss it. These days I just take the train, which is way less stressful.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "It's a bottle. It is for water. It is good." | "It's one of those big metal ones. I've had mine for years." | Short flat facts stop the conversation. Talk about *your* one. |
| "I have it since three years." | "I've had it for three years." | Use present perfect + *for* with a time period. |
| "It's a thing for to block noise." | "It's a thing for blocking noise." / "It's a thing to block noise." | Use *for + -ing* or *to + verb*, not *for to*. |
| "It has many functionalities." | "It does a bunch of stuff." / "It's got a lot of features." | "Functionalities" sounds like a product spec. Keep it casual. |
| (silence for 5 seconds) "…I don't know what to say." | "Honestly, I've never really thought about it, but…" | Never stop. Use a starter and jump to the next step of the shape. |
| "It's very very very useful." | "It's super useful." / "It's honestly a lifesaver." | One strong word sounds more natural than repeating "very". |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **random-topic drill with object cards**, then **one 3/2/1 round**. Follow the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) rules: pick without looking, start talking within 2 seconds, keep going for the full minute, no stopping.

**Tonight's 15 object cards** (write them on folded papers before you start):

1. Your phone case
2. Something in your fridge right now
3. Your favourite mug or cup
4. A gadget you bought and never use
5. Your keys and what's on your keyring
6. The bag you take to work
7. Your bed or your pillow
8. An old toy you remember from childhood
9. Your laptop
10. A piece of clothing you wear too much
11. Something you lost and never found
12. Your toothbrush
13. An umbrella
14. A gift you didn't like
15. The chair you're sitting on right now

**Steps:**

1. **Warm-up (5 min).** Say your three favourite starters from the [sentence-starter bank](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-3-sentence-starter-bank.md) out loud, five times each. Then say the 4-step shape out loud once: "What it is, my one, a moment, what I think."
2. **Random-topic rounds (35 min).** Start recording. Pick a card, read it, and **start talking within 2 seconds**. Talk for **1 minute** (use a timer, but don't stare at it). When the timer goes, put the card aside and pick the next one straight away. Aim for 12–15 cards. If you finish all 15, mix them up and do your 3 weakest again. Rules:
   - No restarting. If a sentence goes wrong, keep going.
   - If your mind goes blank, jump to the next step of the shape.
   - Try to use at least two chunks from tonight's key phrases in every minute.
3. **One 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Pick the card you found **hardest**. Do it as a [3/2/1](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md): 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each one. In the 3-minute version, add a longer "moment" story and connect it with your [storytelling connectors](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) ("So basically…", "The funny part is…").
4. **Listen back and count (5 min).** Listen to the **1-minute** version only. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms, pauses longer than 2 seconds, different connectors. Write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

## 4. Review (15 min)

Spaced review, **out loud**, using your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md):

1. **Yesterday's phrases (4 min):** say each phrase from your last practice night in a new sentence about an object.
2. **Last week's phrases (4 min):** pick 5 phrases from Week 8. Say each one twice in a fresh sentence.
3. **Last month's phrases (3 min):** pick 3 phrases from Month 1 (for example your [reaction phrases](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md)) and say them out loud.
4. **Log tonight's 5 phrases (4 min).** Suggested: "It's one of those… that…", "I've had it for ages", "It's seen better days", "The thing about it is…", "I could totally live without it". Write an example sentence for each about one of **your** things. Fill in tonight's row of the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] Start talking about a random object within 2 seconds.
- [ ] Fill a full minute using the 4-step shape (what it is → my one → a moment → what I think).
- [ ] Use at least 5 object chunks without looking at the table.
- [ ] Say "think", "thing", "three" and "both" with your tongue on your teeth, at normal speed.
- [ ] Write tonight's 3 numbers in your nightly log.
