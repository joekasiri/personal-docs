## Learning objectives

- Order food and drinks naturally, including changes ("without onions", "on the side").
- Complain about a problem politely but clearly, using a simple 4-step shape.
- Hear and copy the difference between a **polite** tone and an **annoyed** tone.
- Stay calm and keep talking when the other person says no.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: polite vs annoyed tone.** The same words can sound friendly or angry. The difference is not the grammar. It's the music.

- **Polite** = your voice goes a bit higher, the ends of sentences go up or stay soft, and you speak at a relaxed speed. You often add a small laugh or a "sorry" sound.
- **Annoyed** = your voice drops lower and goes flat. You stress words you normally wouldn't ("I ordered it TWENTY minutes ago"). Words get cut short, or stretched in a heavy way ("Seriously?" → "SEE-riously").

In real life you want to sound **polite but firm**: polite music, clear words. You practise the annoyed version so you can *hear* when you drift into it.

Say each sentence twice: first polite (soft, going up), then annoyed (low, flat, heavy stress).

| # | Sentence | Polite | Annoyed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Excuse me, could we get some water? | ex-KYOOZ me, c'd we get some WADDer? ↗ | exCUSE me. Could we GET some water. ↘ |
| 2 | I think there might be a mistake. | I THINK there might be a mis-TAKE ↗ | I THINK there's a MIStake. ↘ |
| 3 | I ordered this like twenty minutes ago. | I ORdered this like twenny MINutes ago? | I ORDERED this TWENNY minutes ago. |
| 4 | It's a little cold, actually. | it's a LIDDle COLD, AC-tually ↗ | it's COLD. ↘ |
| 5 | Sorry, is it possible to change it? | SORry, izit POSSible to CHANGE it? ↗ | is it POSSIBLE to change it. ↘ |
| 6 | That's not what I asked for. | that's NOT what I asked for ↗ (with a small laugh) | that's NOT. what I. ASKED for. |
| 7 | No worries, take your time. | no WORries, TAKE yer TIME ↗ | (annoyed version sounds sarcastic: no WORRies. TAKE your TIME.) |
| 8 | Could I get that without onions? | c'd I GED that without ONions? | |
| 9 | We've been waiting a while. | we've been WAIDing a WHILE ↗ | we've been waiting a WHILE. ↘ |
| 10 | Thanks so much, I appreciate it. | THANKS so MUCH, I a-PREE-shee-ay-dit ↗ | (annoyed: thanks. ↘) |

**Clip to find:** a vlog or reality-style video where someone orders food, or a comedy sketch with a customer and a waiter. Search YouTube for **"ordering food vlog USA"**, **"restaurant complaint comedy sketch"** or **"customer service roleplay American English"**. Pick a 2–3 minute clip with clear voices.

**How:** play the whole clip and **speak along** with one person, without pausing. Copy their tone exactly, including the polite rise and any annoyed drops. Do it three times. The third time, close your eyes and only follow the music. Full method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Ordering and complaining

### Explanation

**Ordering.** In American English, ordering is short and friendly. Americans almost never say "I want…" to a server. That sounds like a child or a boss. Instead they say **"Can I get…?"** or **"I'll have…"**. Changes to an order are normal and not rude: "Can I get that without cheese?", "Can I get the dressing on the side?". The server will often ask quick questions back ("For here or to go?", "Anything to drink?"). Answer short and fast: "To go, please." "Just water, thanks."

**Complaining.** Many learners avoid complaining because they're scared of sounding rude, so they just eat the cold food. Others sound too strong because they translate directly ("This is bad, I want a new one"). The American way is **soft start, clear problem, clear ask, thank you**. Use this 4-step shape:

1. **Soft start** – get attention kindly: "Hey, sorry to bother you…"
2. **The problem, as a fact** – no blame: "I think this is the chicken, but I ordered the fish."
3. **What you want** – a clear request: "Would it be possible to swap it?"
4. **Thanks** – end warm: "Thanks so much, no rush."

The secret is: **polite music, clear words.** Your voice stays friendly, but you don't hide the problem. If the person says no, you don't get angry and you don't give up. You stay calm and ask one more time with a different option: "OK, what *can* you do?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Can I get…? | Ordering anything (the most common way) | "Can I get a large iced coffee?" |
| I'll have… / I'll go with… | Choosing from a menu | "I think I'll go with the burger." |
| What would you recommend? | You don't know what to order | "It's my first time here. What would you recommend?" |
| Could I get that without… / with… on the side? | Changing your order | "Could I get that without onions, and the sauce on the side?" |
| For here, please. / To go, please. | Answering "For here or to go?" | "To go, please. Thanks." |
| Sorry to bother you, but… | Starting a complaint softly | "Sorry to bother you, but I think there's a mistake." |
| I think there might be a mistake. | Saying something is wrong without blaming | "I think there might be a mistake on the check." |
| I ordered…, but this is… | Explaining the problem as a fact | "I ordered the veggie one, but this has chicken." |
| It's been a little while… | Your food is very late | "It's been a little while. Is our order still coming?" |
| Would it be possible to…? | Asking for a fix | "Would it be possible to heat it up a little?" |
| I'm not trying to be difficult, but… | Pushing a second time, still polite | "I'm not trying to be difficult, but this is the second time." |
| What can you do? / Is there anything you can do? | They said no and you want another option | "OK, I get it. Is there anything you can do?" |
| Could we get the check? | Asking for the bill (US: check, not bill) | "Could we get the check when you get a chance?" |
| No worries. / I appreciate it. | Ending warmly after the problem is solved | "No worries at all. I appreciate it." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: ordering at a café**

> **Barista:** Hi! What can I get you?
> **You:** Hey! Can I get a medium oat latte?
> **Barista:** Sure. Hot or iced?
> **You:** Iced, please. And could I get it with an extra shot?
> **Barista:** You got it. Anything else?
> **You:** Um, what would you recommend to eat? Something small.
> **Barista:** The banana bread's really good today.
> **You:** Sold. I'll have one of those.
> **Barista:** For here or to go?
> **You:** To go, please. Thanks!

**Dialogue 2: the wrong dish (polite but firm)**

> **You:** Hey, sorry to bother you. I think there might be a mistake. I ordered the salmon, but this is the chicken.
> **Server:** Oh no, I'm so sorry. Let me check with the kitchen.
> **You:** No worries. Would it be possible to get the salmon? I'm not in a huge rush.
> **Server:** Of course. It'll be about ten minutes.
> **You:** That's totally fine. Thanks so much.

**Dialogue 3: the shop says no (staying calm)**

> **You:** Hi, I bought these headphones last week, and the left side stopped working.
> **Assistant:** Do you have the receipt?
> **You:** I have it on my phone. Here.
> **Assistant:** OK… so, we can't do refunds after five days, sorry.
> **You:** I get that. I'm not trying to be difficult, but they broke after six days. Is there anything you can do? Like, could I swap them for a new pair?
> **Assistant:** Let me ask my manager. An exchange might be OK.
> **You:** That'd be great. I appreciate it.

**Model answer: a short complaint at work (product example)**

> "Hey, sorry to bother you. So, we ordered lunch for the design workshop, twelve sandwiches, but only eight showed up. Would it be possible to send the other four? The session starts at twelve thirty, so if they could get here by then, that'd be amazing. Thanks so much."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I want a cappuccino. | Can I get a cappuccino? | "I want" sounds demanding to American ears. |
| Give me the bill, please. | Could we get the check? | "Give me" is too direct, and Americans say "check". |
| This food is bad. | This is a little cold, actually. | Describe the exact problem as a fact, softly. |
| Excuse me, I have a complain. | Sorry, I have a quick problem with my order. | "Complain" is the verb; the noun is "complaint". "Quick problem" sounds lighter. |
| I am waiting since forty minutes. | We've been waiting for like forty minutes. | Use present perfect continuous + "for" for time up to now. |
| Can you change it? It's not good. | Would it be possible to swap it for the fish? | Say exactly what you want, so the fix is easy. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is a **random-topic drill** with restaurant and shop cards, plus one 3/2/1 round. Full method: [Random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md). The rule: read the card, and start talking **within 2 seconds**. No planning. Record every round.

**Step 1 – Warm-up (5 min).** Say each phrase from the key-phrase table once, in a polite tone, as fast as you can. Then say three of them in an annoyed tone and hear the difference.

**Step 2 – Random-topic rounds (35 min).** Write these 14 cards on paper or in a notes app, shuffle them, and pick one without looking. Talk for **1–2 minutes** per card. "Scene" cards: you play the customer and imagine the other person's answers out loud ("…and she's like, sorry, we're out of that…") and keep reacting. "Talk" cards: speak about the topic.

1. **Scene:** You're at a taco truck. Order for yourself and two friends who can't eat meat.
2. **Talk:** The best meal you've ever had in a restaurant. Where, who with, why it was so good.
3. **Scene:** Your pizza arrives 50 minutes late and cold. Call the restaurant.
4. **Talk:** Do you think tipping 20% is fair? Why or why not?
5. **Scene:** The barista gives you a hot drink, but you asked for iced. The line behind you is long.
6. **Talk:** A food from your country that you'd recommend to an American, and how you'd order it.
7. **Scene:** You find a charge on the check for a dessert you never ordered.
8. **Talk:** Are you the kind of person who complains, or who stays quiet? Give an example.
9. **Scene:** A hotel gave you a room next to the elevator, and it's noisy all night. Talk to reception.
10. **Talk:** The worst customer service you've ever had (in any shop, airline or app).
11. **Scene:** You want to return a jacket without the receipt. The assistant isn't sure.
12. **Talk:** What makes a café a good place to work on a laptop?
13. **Scene:** Your online food order is missing a drink. Talk to the delivery app's support chat out loud.
14. **Talk:** Describe how ordering food works in your country compared with the US.

If you finish all 14, shuffle and go again. Second time, play each scene with a **different twist**: the other person is rude, or very friendly, or doesn't understand you.

**Step 3 – One 3/2/1 round (15 min).** Topic: **"The time I had to complain about something."** Tell it in 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each. Use the 4-step complaint shape inside the story, and some reported speech ("so I was like, sorry to bother you…", see [casual reported speech](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-3-casual-reported-speech.md)). Method: [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Step 4 – Listen back and count (5 min).** Listen to the 1-minute version and one random-topic round. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms, pauses longer than 2 seconds, different connectors. Also note: did you ever sound annoyed when you meant to sound polite?
