# Week 12 exercises: Role-plays

[Week 12 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-ordering-and-complaining.md) · [Day 2](day-2-making-plans.md) · [Day 3](day-3-meeting-new-people.md) · [Day 4](day-4-asking-for-help-and-favours.md) · [Day 5](day-5-arguing-the-other-side.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use this page.** Everything here is done **out loud**. Never just read silently. When an exercise says "record", record it and listen back once. For role-plays, you'll find two kinds of material:

- **Full scripts**: read both parts out loud with the right tone, like an actor. This puts the phrases in your mouth.
- **Character cards**: no script. Each card gives a **name**, a **situation**, **what they want** and a **twist**. You play your role and speak the other character's lines out loud too (or imagine them and react). Start within 2 seconds. Bring the twist in halfway through, and react to it without stopping.

Tip: copy the character cards onto paper so you can pick one without looking.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Order it three ways

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each order below, say it three different ways: (a) with "Can I get…?", (b) with "I'll have…" or "I'll go with…", (c) with a change: "…without…" or "…on the side" or "…with extra…".
2. Use a friendly, polite tone, with a small rise at the end.
3. Then do the whole list again, faster, choosing just one way each time.

1. A medium iced coffee with oat milk.
2. The chicken burrito, but no sour cream.
3. A Caesar salad, dressing separate.
4. Two slices of pepperoni pizza, to go.
5. A cheeseburger, medium-rare, with fries.
6. A green tea, very hot.
7. The pancakes, with the syrup separate.
8. A large fries for the table.
9. The veggie wrap, without onions, add avocado.
10. A sparkling water and the check.

### Exercise 1.2: Fix the rude complaint

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each complaint below is too direct or too rude. Rewrite it using the 4 steps: **soft start → problem as a fact → what you want → thanks**.
2. Say your version out loud twice in a polite-but-firm tone.
3. Check the model answers in the [Day 1 answer key](#day-1-answer-key).

1. "This soup is cold. Bring another one."
2. "I am waiting forty minutes. Where is my food?"
3. "This is wrong. I didn't order this."
4. "Your bill is wrong. Fix it."
5. "The room is very noisy. I want another room now."
6. "These shoes are broken. Give me my money back."
7. "My delivery has no drink. You forgot."
8. "This table is too close to the door. We don't like it."

### Exercise 1.3: Character-card role-plays

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, record

**Part A – Full script (3 min).** Read both parts out loud. Make the server a little defensive and keep your voice polite but firm.

> **Server:** Here you go, the spicy noodles!
> **You:** Oh, thanks. Sorry, I think there might be a mistake. I asked for the mild ones.
> **Server:** Hmm, the ticket says spicy.
> **You:** Oh, really? I'm pretty sure I said mild, but no worries, it happens. Would it be possible to swap them?
> **Server:** I mean, the kitchen's really busy right now.
> **You:** I totally get it. I'm not trying to be difficult, but I honestly can't eat spicy food. Is there anything you can do?
> **Server:** OK, let me see if they can make a mild one quickly.
> **You:** That'd be great. Thanks so much, I appreciate it.
> **Server:** Should be about ten minutes.
> **You:** Perfect. Could I get some water while I wait?

**Part B – Character cards (7 min).** Pick 2 cards. Improvise each for 2–3 minutes. You are the customer. Speak the other character's lines too, or imagine them.

**Card 1 – The burger place**
- **Name:** Tyler, cashier, 19, on his first day.
- **Situation:** You ordered a veggie burger and fries. You get a beef burger and onion rings.
- **What Tyler wants:** to fix it without calling his manager.
- **Twist:** they've run out of veggie burgers.

**Card 2 – The hotel**
- **Name:** Ms. Alvarez, hotel receptionist.
- **Situation:** You booked a room with a view. Your room faces a brick wall, and the AC is loud.
- **What she wants:** to keep you happy without giving a refund.
- **Twist:** the only free room with a view is smaller and on the top floor, with no elevator.

**Card 3 – The phone shop**
- **Name:** Jason, phone store assistant.
- **Situation:** Your new phone case cracked after three days. You have no receipt, only the box.
- **What Jason wants:** to follow the rules: no receipt, no return.
- **Twist:** he says he can do an exchange if you can show the payment on your banking app.

**Card 4 – The coffee order for the team**
- **Name:** Priya, café owner near your office.
- **Situation:** You pre-ordered 10 coffees for a design workshop at 9:00. At 9:05 only 6 are ready.
- **What Priya wants:** to keep your company as a regular customer.
- **Twist:** she offers the other 4 for free, but they'll take 15 more minutes, and your workshop is starting now.

### Exercise 2.3: Plan cards

**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, record

**Part A – Full script (3 min).** Read both parts. Jordan is vague; you pin him down.

> **You:** Hey, so are we still doing something this weekend?
> **Jordan:** Yeah, totally, we should hang out.
> **You:** Cool. Are you free Saturday?
> **Jordan:** Maybe. I might have a thing. We'll see.
> **You:** OK, what about Sunday, then? Does Sunday afternoon work for you?
> **Jordan:** Sunday could work, yeah.
> **You:** Awesome. Let's say two o'clock at the park, and we can grab food after?
> **Jordan:** Two… yeah, OK. Two works.
> **You:** Sounds like a plan. I'll text you tomorrow to confirm. And if something comes up, just tell me, no worries.
> **Jordan:** Deal. See you Sunday.

**Part B – Character cards (9 min).** Pick 3 cards. Improvise each for 2–3 minutes. Use all four moves: suggest, answer, lock it in, change or cancel.

**Card 1 – The birthday dinner**
- **Name:** Kayla, your close friend.
- **Situation:** It's your birthday next Friday. You want 6 friends at a restaurant.
- **What Kayla wants:** to help you organise, but she's on a tight budget.
- **Twist:** the restaurant you chose is expensive. She suggests a picnic instead.

**Card 2 – The road trip**
- **Name:** Omar, a friend from your design course.
- **Situation:** You both want a weekend road trip to the coast next month.
- **What Omar wants:** to leave Friday morning.
- **Twist:** you can't leave until Friday at 6 p.m. because of a client presentation.

**Card 3 – The coworker lunch**
- **Name:** Grace, a new engineer on your team.
- **Situation:** You want to welcome her with a lunch this week.
- **What Grace wants:** to say yes, but she's shy and keeps saying "Whatever works for you".
- **Twist:** she mentions she only has 30 minutes for lunch every day.

**Card 4 – The last-minute cancel**
- **Name:** Ben, your old roommate.
- **Situation:** You agreed to see a basketball game together tonight. You have the tickets.
- **What Ben wants:** he calls one hour before to cancel — his kid is sick.
- **Twist:** you can't get a refund. You need to be kind to Ben *and* find someone else to use his ticket.

### Exercise 3.1: Upgrade the intro

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each intro below is boring: it gives the other person nothing to ask about. Add **one hook** (a funny detail, a connection to the host, something you just did).

4. "I just moved here."

### Exercise 3.2: Party chain script

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, then improvise

**Part A – Full script (4 min).** Read both parts with a warm, smiling voice. Notice the chain: intro → connection → name → common ground → introduce someone → exit.

> **You:** Hey, I don't think we've met. I'm Sam — I'm the one who brought the very sad-looking cake.
> **Nora:** Haha, I saw it! It looks great. I'm Nora.
> **You:** Nora, nice to meet you. So how do you know Mike?
> **Nora:** We play in the same soccer league. Every Tuesday.
> **You:** Wait, Mike plays soccer? He told me he hates running.
> **Nora:** He mostly stands in goal and complains.
> **You:** That sounds right. So what do you do, when you're not playing soccer?
> **Nora:** I'm a teacher. Fifth grade.
> **You:** Oh, no way. My mom was a teacher for like thirty years.
> **Nora:** Really? Then you know how crazy it is.
> **You:** I've heard all the stories. Oh — Jake! Have you two met? Jake, this is Nora. Nora teaches fifth grade, and Jake's thinking about becoming a teacher.
> **Jake:** Wait, really? I have so many questions.
> **You:** See, you guys should talk. I'm gonna grab some food before the cake disappears. Great meeting you, Nora!
> **Nora:** You too, Sam!

**Part B – Improvise (6 min).** Do the chain again, but this time say only **your** lines and invent everything. The other person's lines below are fixed; react to each one within 2 seconds. Record it.

> **Guest:** Hi! I don't think I know anyone here.
> **You:** …
> **Guest:** I'm Hana. I'm actually Mike's dentist. Is that weird?
> **You:** …
> **Guest:** What about you, how do you know him?
> **You:** …
> **Guest:** Oh cool. I've always wanted to learn about design. I use like ten apps a day.
> **You:** …
> **Guest:** (A friend of yours walks by.)
> **You:** (Introduce them, with a hook for each.) …
> **You:** (Leave warmly.) …

6. You want to borrow a friend's car for the weekend.
7. You need your roommate to pass you the remote.
8. You want a former colleague to write you a reference for a new job.
9. You need a neighbor to take in a package while you're at work.
10. You want a friend to lend you $500 until next month.
11. You want a stranger to take a photo of you and your friends.

3. Check the [Day 4 answer key](#day-4-answer-key).

1. "Would you mind to send me the file?"
2. A: "Would you mind closing the window?" B: "Yes, of course!" (B means: OK, I'll do it.)

### Exercise 4.3: Say yes, say no

**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, record

**Part A – Answer the request (6 min).** Answer each request the way the brackets tell you, within 2 seconds. "Not now" = not at that time, but offer another time.

1. "Could you take a look at my CV tonight?" [yes]
2. "Would you mind if I sat here?" [yes — careful with "mind"!]
3. "Any chance you could help me paint my apartment this weekend?" [no]
4. "Can you cover my shift on Saturday?" [not now]
5. "Do you happen to know a good dentist?" [yes]
6. "I hate to ask, but could I borrow $200?" [no]
7. "Could you join a quick call with the client at 4?" [not now]
8. "Would you mind watering my plants next week?" [yes]

**Part B – Character cards (6 min).** Pick 2 cards. Improvise for 3 minutes each. This time **you** are the person being asked, and you want to say no or "not now" — kindly.

**Card 1 – The moving friend**
- **Name:** Tasha, a friend from your gym.
- **Situation:** She asks you to help her move on Sunday — 3rd floor, no elevator.
- **What Tasha wants:** a yes, she's desperate.
- **Twist:** you really can't on Sunday, but you could help pack on Saturday evening.

**Card 2 – The extra project**
- **Name:** Mark, a product manager.
- **Situation:** He asks you to design a quick landing page "by Friday, it's tiny".
- **What Mark wants:** it done without going through your manager.
- **Twist:** it's not tiny — it's 6 pages — and you're already at full capacity.

**Card 3 – The airport ride**
- **Name:** Uncle Reza, visiting from abroad.
- **Situation:** He asks you to pick him up from the airport at 2 a.m. on a work night.
- **What he wants:** family to welcome him.

**4.2 – corrections:**

1. "Would you mind **sending** me the file?" (mind + -ing)
2. B: "**No, not at all!**" or "**Sure, no problem.**" ("Yes" means "Yes, I mind".)
3. "Do you happen **to have** a charger?"
4. "**Could I borrow** your jacket? I'm freezing." (polite request; you borrow, they lend)
5. "**I was wondering if you could** help me move on Saturday." (past continuous is softer; "help me move", no "to")
6. "Can I borrow your bike?" (no "me")
7. "**You're a lifesaver. I owe you one!**" (casual thanks for friends)
8. "I hate to ask, but **could you** help me with the slides? **No pressure if not.**" ("you must" is an order)

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: The 3-question trick

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. For each statement, argue the side you **don't** agree with. Spend **20 seconds** writing three notes: **Who benefits? · Hidden cost of the other side? · One real example?**
2. Then talk for **45 seconds** using your notes and at least two key phrases. Start with a devil's advocate phrase.
3. Compare your notes with the [Day 5 answer key](#day-5-answer-key) (they show notes for both sides, so you'll find yours).

1. Remote work is better than office work.
2. Everyone should go to university.
3. Paper books are better than e-books.
4. Tipping should be banned.
5. People should work in the same job for many years.
6. Public transport should be free.
7. Design is more important than features.
8. It's better to travel alone than with friends.

### Exercise 5.2: Push-back ping-pong

### Exercise 5.3: Flip the opinion

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, record

**Part A – Flip (5 min).**
1. Say your **real** opinion on each topic in one sentence.
2. Immediately flip it: argue the opposite for 1 minute. Start with "OK, let me play devil's advocate…". End with "OK, I've gotta be honest, I don't actually believe that."

Topics: (1) pineapple on pizza · (2) working out in the morning · (3) living abroad · (4) using AI to write emails · (5) owning a car in a big city.

**Part B – Debate cards (5 min).** Pick 1 card and do the debate for 5 minutes. You play both characters, switching voices. Each character must keep their position and answer every push-back.
