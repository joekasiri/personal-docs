# Week 12: Role-plays

[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 3: Spontaneity](../README.md) · [← Week 11: Never stopping](../week-11-never-stopping/README.md) · [Week 13: Group conversations →](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you step into real situations and improvise them: ordering food and complaining when it goes wrong, making plans with a friend, meeting a stranger at a party, asking someone for a favour, and arguing for an opinion you don't actually hold. In every role-play the other person does something you can't predict. You don't get a script for your own lines, only a character card that tells you who you are and what you want. Your job is to react straight away, in character, with the right tone of voice. By Day 6 you should be able to walk into any of these five situations and keep talking without planning your next sentence. Day 6 is also your **Month 3 check**.

## How this builds on last week

Last week ([Week 11: Never stopping](../week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) gave you the rescue tools: paraphrasing when a word is missing, correcting yourself smoothly, talking when you're unsure, getting back on track and summarising. Role-plays are where you need them most, because the other person keeps surprising you. This week you won't learn new rescue tools. You'll use last week's tools inside realistic scenes, together with the on-the-spot talking from [Week 9](../week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md) and the hypotheticals from [Week 10](../week-10-hypotheticals/README.md).

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes, no studying.** Find a short comedy sketch or a "customer service" style video where people get annoyed, excited or awkward (search YouTube for "funny restaurant complaint sketch", "awkward first meeting comedy sketch" or "improv scene two actors"). Watch it once just to enjoy it. Then go for a walk and, in your head, replay one scene from the other character's point of view: what would *you* have said? Say one or two lines out loud if nobody is around. That's it. Then rest. You've just finished three months, and your brain needs the break to lock in what you practised.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] order food, ask for changes and complain politely but firmly when something goes wrong
- [ ] make, change and cancel plans with a friend without sounding rude or stiff
- [ ] start a conversation with a stranger at a party, keep it going and leave it naturally
- [ ] ask for help or a favour in a way that makes it easy for the other person to say yes (or no)
- [ ] argue convincingly for an opinion you don't hold, for 1–2 minutes, with reasons and examples
- [ ] switch your tone of voice on purpose: polite, annoyed, excited, warm, apologetic, firm
- [ ] talk for 2 minutes on a random topic with zero preparation (your Month 3 target)
