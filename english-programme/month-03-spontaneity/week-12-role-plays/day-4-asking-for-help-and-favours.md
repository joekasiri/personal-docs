Tonight uses that grammar but focuses on something new: **the social side of asking** — how to make a request easy to accept, easy to refuse, and how to say thanks or answer someone else's request.

## Learning objectives

- Ask for small and big favours with the right level of softness.
- Give the other person an easy way to say no ("No pressure if not").
- Say yes, say no and say thank you to a favour in natural ways.
- Use an apologetic, careful tone when the favour is big.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: apologetic and careful.** When you ask for something big, Americans slow down a little, soften their voice and often start with a small, stretched sound: "Sooo…", "Hey, um…", a little nervous laugh. The pitch goes up at the end, like a question even when it isn't. This tells the listener: "I know this is a lot."

Compare:
- Normal: "Can you send me the file?" (quick, flat)
- Careful: "Hey, sooo… I hate to ask, but could you maybe send me the file again?" ↗ (slower, softer, rising)

| # | Sentence | Stress and sound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hey, could you do me a favor? | hey, c'jew DO me a FAvor? ↗ |
| 2 | Sooo… I hate to ask, but… | SOOO… I HATE ta ASK, but… |
| 3 | Would you mind taking a look? | wouldja MIND TAKing a LOOK? ↗ |
| 4 | Any chance you could cover for me Friday? | ANy CHANCE you could COVer fer me FRIday? |
| 5 | No pressure if not, seriously. | no PRESSure if NOT, SEriously ↘ |
| 6 | I was wondering if you could help me move. | I was WONdering if you could HELP me MOVE ↗ |
| 7 | Do you happen to have a charger? | d'you HAPpen ta have a CHARGer? |
| 8 | You're a lifesaver. | you're a LIFE-saver! (warm, high) |
| 9 | I owe you one. | I OWE ya ONE |
| 10 | It's totally fine if you can't. | it's TOdally FINE if you CAN'T ↘ |

**Clip to find:** a vlog where someone asks friends or neighbors for help (moving day, a road trip with a broken car), or an advice video about "how to ask for a favor". Search YouTube for **"moving day vlog friends help"**, **"asking my neighbor for help vlog"** or **"how to ask for a favor politely"**.

**How:** speak along with the whole clip, no pausing, three times. Exaggerate the soft, careful parts. Method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Asking for help and favours

### Explanation

The bigger the favour, the more "padding" you put around it. Think of three sizes:

| Size | Example | How to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Small** (takes 10 seconds) | pass the salt, hold a door, tell you the time | "Could you…?", "Do you happen to…?" |
| **Medium** (takes some time) | review a file, lend you a charger for an hour | "Would you mind…?", "Any chance you could…?" |
| **Big** (costs time, money or effort) | help you move, cover a shift, lend money | Warm-up + "I hate to ask, but…" + reason + "No pressure if not" |

**The big-favour shape:**
1. **Warm-up:** "Hey, so… can I ask you something?"
2. **Soften:** "I hate to ask, but…" / "I was wondering if…"
3. **The request + a short reason:** "…could you help me move on Saturday? My friend with the truck just bailed."
4. **The exit door:** "No pressure if you can't, seriously."
5. **The thanks / offer back:** "I'll buy pizza. I owe you one."

**The "exit door" is important.** It shows respect, and — strangely — it makes people more likely to say yes.

**Answering a favour.**
- Yes: "Sure, no problem!", "Of course, happy to help."
- Not now: "I can't today, but I could do tomorrow?"
- No: "I'd love to, but I really can't this weekend. Sorry!" (always soft, always a reason, often an alternative)

**Grammar watch:** "Would you mind…?" is followed by **-ing**, and the "yes" answer is "**No, not at all**" (because the question means "Is it a problem for you?").

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Could you do me a favor? | Opening any favour | "Hey, could you do me a quick favor?" |
| Do you happen to have / know…? | Small things, from strangers too | "Do you happen to have a pen?" |
| Would you mind + -ing? | Medium favours | "Would you mind watching my bag for a sec?" |
| Any chance you could…? | Medium favours, casual | "Any chance you could look at my slides before 3?" |
| I was wondering if you could… | Polite, a bit more formal (work) | "I was wondering if you could join the user interview Thursday." |
| I hate to ask, but… | Big favours | "I hate to ask, but could you cover my shift?" |
| No pressure if not. / Totally fine if you can't. | Giving an exit door | "No pressure if not, I know you're busy." |
| Could you give me a hand with…? | Physical help | "Could you give me a hand with this table?" |
| Is it OK if I…? | Asking permission | "Is it OK if I leave a bit early today?" |
| Sure, no problem. / Happy to help. | Saying yes | "Sure, no problem. Send it over." |
| I'd love to, but… | Saying no kindly | "I'd love to, but I'm out of town that weekend." |
| You're a lifesaver. / I owe you one. | Thanking for a favour | "You fixed it? You're a lifesaver. I owe you one." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a medium favour at work**

> **You:** Hey Dev, any chance you could take a look at my checkout flow before the review? Just like ten minutes.
> **Dev:** Today? I'm kinda slammed.
> **You:** Totally get it. Tomorrow morning works too, the review's at noon. No pressure if not.
> **Dev:** Tomorrow at nine I can do.
> **You:** Amazing. I'll send you the link tonight. Thanks, I owe you one.

**Dialogue 2: a big favour from a friend**

> **You:** Hey, so… can I ask you something kind of big?
> **Chris:** Uh-oh. Sure.
> **You:** I hate to ask, but are you free Saturday morning? I'm moving, and the guy with the truck just bailed on me. Could you give me a hand? It's only like twenty boxes.
> **Chris:** Twenty boxes and a couch, I bet.
> **You:** …OK, and a couch. Totally fine if you can't, seriously. But I'll buy pizza and beer.
> **Chris:** Haha, fine. Nine a.m.?
> **You:** You're a lifesaver.

**Dialogue 3: saying no kindly**

> **Neighbor:** Would you mind feeding my cat while I'm away next week?
> **You:** Oh, I'd love to, but I'm actually traveling for work Monday to Thursday. I could do Friday and the weekend, though?
> **Neighbor:** That'd still help a lot!
> **You:** Great, just leave me the key and the instructions.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Would you mind to help me? | Would you mind helping me? | "Mind" is followed by -ing. |
| (to "Would you mind…?") Yes, sure! | No, not at all! / Sure, no problem. | "Yes" literally means "Yes, I mind". |
| Help me move on Saturday, please. | I hate to ask, but could you help me move on Saturday? | "Please" doesn't make a command polite. A big favour needs padding. |
| Can you lend me your car? I need it. | Any chance I could borrow your car Sunday? Totally fine if not. | Give an exit door and use "borrow" for what *you* take. |
| Thank you very much for your help. (to a friend) | You're a lifesaver! I owe you one. | Very formal thanks sounds distant with friends. |
| I can't. | I'd love to, but I can't this week. Sorry! | A bare "I can't" sounds cold. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

**Voice mode with Claude: three characters, you improvise.** About 50 minutes of conversation + 10 minutes of notes. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 – Paste this prompt:**

```text
Let's do an improv role-play. I'm a B2 English learner practising asking for help and favours in natural, casual American English. You'll play 3 characters, one at a time, about 12 minutes each. I don't get a script — I improvise. Here are the character cards:

CHARACTER 1 — Mrs. Carter, my 70-year-old neighbor.
Situation: I'm going on vacation for 10 days and need someone to water my plants and take in my mail.
What she wants: she's lonely and wants to chat about her grandkids first.
Twist: halfway through, she mentions she's going to visit her sister for 3 of those days.

CHARACTER 2 — Dev, a colleague on my product team.
Situation: I need him to review my design file by tomorrow morning for a big client review.
What he wants: he's overloaded and stressed.
Twist: he agrees only if I help him with his presentation slides in return — and his deadline is the same as mine.

CHARACTER 3 — Rick, the building manager.
Situation: my heating broke and it's freezing; I want it fixed before the weekend.
What he wants: to go home; he says the repair person can only come Monday.
Twist: if I'm polite but persistent, he admits there's an emergency repair number — but only if I ask the right way.

Rules:
- Speak naturally and casually, short turns, so I do most of the talking.
- Make me work for the yes. Don't agree too fast.
- Don't correct me during a scene. After each character, give me: 3 corrections, 1 phrase I could have used, and a score 1–5 for how polite-but-clear I sounded.
- If I'm silent for more than 2 seconds, react in character ("...Hello?").
Start with Mrs. Carter knocking on my door.
```

**Step 2 – Play the three scenes (about 40 min).** Focus on:
- **Size the favour.** Mrs. Carter = medium, Dev = big (he's stressed), Rick = it's your right, but polite persistence works better.
- **Use the exit door** at least once in every scene.
- **React to the twist straight away.** Use a reaction ("Oh, OK…"), then a new option within 2 seconds. If you lose a word, paraphrase ([Week 11 Day 1](../week-11-never-stopping/day-1-paraphrasing.md)).
- **Tone:** careful and apologetic with Dev, warm with Mrs. Carter, polite but firm with Rick (from [Day 1](day-1-ordering-and-complaining.md)).

**Step 3 – Swap roles (10 min).** Ask Claude: *"Now you ask ME for 3 favours, one small, one medium, one big. I'll say yes to one, no to one and 'not now' to one."*

**Step 4 – Notes (10 min).** Write down Claude's corrections and scores. Which character was hardest? Why?
