## Learning objectives

- Suggest a plan casually and answer other people's suggestions fast ("I'm down", "I can't, but…").
- Agree on a time and a place without long back-and-forth.
- Change or cancel a plan politely and offer another option.
- Sound **excited** when you mean yes and **unsure** when you mean maybe, so people read you correctly.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: excited vs unsure.** When Americans make plans, the tone tells you the real answer before the words do.

- **Excited** = big pitch jumps, the stressed word goes high and long, faster speed. "Oh my gosh, YES! I'm SO down!"
- **Unsure** = flat, low, slow, with a stretched first word and a falling end. "Yeeeah… maybe? I'll… see." An unsure "yeah" usually means *no*.

If you sound flat when you mean yes, people think you don't want to come. Practise both so you control the signal.

| # | Sentence | Excited (high, fast) | Unsure (flat, slow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yeah, I'm down! | YEAH, I'm SO DOWN! ↗ | yeeeah… I mean, I'm down… ↘ |
| 2 | That sounds awesome. | that sounds AWE-some! | that sounds… awesome. ↘ |
| 3 | Wanna grab dinner Friday? | WANna grab DINner FRIday? ↗ | |
| 4 | Let me check and get back to you. | | lemme CHECK 'n get BACK to ya ↘ |
| 5 | I might be able to make it. | | I MIGHT be able ta MAKE it… |
| 6 | Oh, I've been wanting to go there! | oh, I've been WANNing ta GO there! | |
| 7 | Saturday works for me. | SATurday WORKS for me! ↗ | SATurday… works for me, I guess. |
| 8 | Can we push it to next week? | | c'n we PUSH it ta NEXT week? |
| 9 | Count me in! | COUNT me IN! ↗ | |
| 10 | Hmm, I'll have to see. | | hmmm, I'll hafta SEE. ↘ |

**Clip to find:** two friends chatting about weekend plans in a vlog or podcast, or a "day in my life" vlog where the person calls a friend. Search YouTube for **"friends planning a trip vlog"**, **"calling my best friend vlog"** or **"weekend plans chat podcast"**.

**How:** speak along with the whole clip, no pausing, three times. Each time, focus on one thing: first speed, then the high excited jumps, then the flat unsure moments. Method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Making plans

### Explanation

Making plans in English has four moves. You'll need all four tonight.

1. **Suggest.** Keep it light and easy to refuse: "Wanna grab lunch sometime this week?" or "Are you free Saturday?" Americans rarely say "Shall we…?" in casual talk. It sounds British or old-fashioned.
2. **Answer.** Yes: "I'm down!", "Count me in", "Sounds good." Maybe: "Let me check and get back to you." No: never just "no". Say **no + reason + another option**: "I can't Saturday, I've got a thing. But Sunday works?"
3. **Lock it in.** Agree on time and place in one or two lines: "Let's say seven at that Thai place?" "Perfect, see you there." Then confirm: "I'll text you the address."
4. **Change or cancel.** Life happens. The polite way is: **sorry + short reason + new option**. "Hey, I'm so sorry, something came up at work. Can we push it to Thursday?" A "rain check" means "not today, but let's do it another time".

The big point: **plans in English are soft.** People leave room for the other person to say no. Pressure ("You must come!") sounds strange. Warm and flexible sounds normal.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What are you up to this weekend? | Opening, before you suggest | "What are you up to this weekend? Anything fun?" |
| Wanna grab…? | Suggesting food, coffee or a drink | "Wanna grab coffee after the standup?" |
| Are you free on…? | Asking about a specific day | "Are you free on Thursday night?" |
| I'm down. / Count me in. | Saying yes, happily | "Hiking Sunday? I'm down." |
| How about…? / What about…? | Offering a time, place or idea | "How about that new ramen place on 5th?" |
| Does… work for you? | Checking a time | "Does seven work for you?" |
| Let's say… | Proposing the final detail | "Let's say 7:30, outside the theater." |
| Let me check and get back to you. | You're not sure yet | "Let me check with my partner and get back to you." |
| I can't, but… | Saying no with another option | "I can't Friday, but I'm free all Saturday." |
| Something came up. | Cancelling, short reason | "Ugh, something came up at work tonight." |
| Can we push it to…? | Moving a plan later | "Can we push it to next week?" |
| Can I take a rain check? | Saying no for now, yes for later | "I'm wiped out. Can I take a rain check?" |
| Sounds like a plan. | Closing the deal | "Saturday at noon? Sounds like a plan." |
| Text me when you're there. | Final detail before you meet | "Cool, text me when you're there." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a fast yes**

> **Maya:** Hey, what are you up to Saturday?
> **You:** Nothing yet. Why, what's up?
> **Maya:** A bunch of us are going to that food market downtown. Wanna come?
> **You:** Oh, I'm so down! What time?
> **Maya:** Like eleven-ish?
> **You:** Perfect. Let's say eleven at the main entrance?
> **Maya:** Sounds like a plan. I'll text you when I'm there.

**Dialogue 2: no + another option**

> **Jordan:** Wanna catch a movie Friday night?
> **You:** Ah, I can't Friday, I've got a work dinner. But I'm free Sunday afternoon. Does that work for you?
> **Jordan:** Sunday's good. The 4 p.m. show?
> **You:** Works for me. I'll grab the tickets.

**Dialogue 3: cancelling politely**

> **You:** Hey, I'm so sorry, I hate doing this, but something came up. My manager moved a deadline to tomorrow morning. Can I take a rain check on tonight?
> **Sam:** Oh no, no worries! Is everything OK?
> **You:** Yeah, just work stuff. Can we push it to next Tuesday? Dinner's on me.
> **Sam:** Deal. Good luck with the deadline!

**Model answer: planning a team thing at work**

> "Hey, so we finally shipped the new onboarding flow, and I feel like we should celebrate. How about team lunch next week? I was thinking Wednesday or Thursday, around twelve. Does either of those work for you guys? If not, just let me know and we'll find another day. I'll book the place once we decide."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shall we go to the cinema? | Wanna catch a movie? | "Shall we" sounds formal or British; Americans say "movie", not "cinema". |
| No, I can't. | I can't, but how about Sunday? | A bare "no" sounds cold. Always add another option. |
| I will come at 7. | I'll be there at 7. / See you at 7. | "I will come" sounds stiff. Use "I'll be there". |
| Are you free in Saturday? | Are you free on Saturday? / Are you free Saturday? | Use "on" with days (or no preposition at all). |
| Sorry, I can't come because I have a problem. | Sorry, something came up. Can we push it to Thursday? | "A problem" sounds serious. "Something came up" is the normal short reason. |
| We meet at 8? | Wanna meet at 8? / Let's say 8? | Statements as questions sound unclear. Use a real suggestion shape. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is **voice mode with Claude**: about 50 minutes of conversation + 10 minutes of notes. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Step 1 – Start the session.** Open voice mode and paste (or read out) this prompt:

```text
Let's do a role-play practice for making plans. I'm a B2 English learner and I want to sound natural and casual, American-style.

You'll play 4 different friends, one at a time. For each friend, you text-call me (we're just talking) and we try to make a weekend plan together. Each friend has a hidden problem that you reveal in the middle of the chat, so I have to adjust:
1. Alex: wants to go hiking Saturday morning, but halfway through says they hurt their ankle.
2. Priya: wants to try a new restaurant, but she's vegan and the place I pick has nothing for her.
3. Marcus: says yes to everything but keeps being vague ("maybe", "we'll see"). I have to pin him down to a real time and place.
4. Lena: we've already agreed on Friday dinner, and then she calls to cancel. I have to react kindly and reschedule.

Rules:
- Speak casually and fast, like a real friend. Use phrases like "I'm down", "rain check", "something came up".
- Keep your turns short (1–3 sentences) so I do most of the talking.
- Don't correct me during the role-plays. After each friend, give me 3 quick corrections or more natural ways to say what I said, then start the next friend.
- If I'm silent for more than 2 seconds, just say "So?" to push me.
Start with Alex.
```

**Step 2 – Run the four friends (about 40 min, 10 min each).** For every friend:
- Use all four moves: suggest, answer, lock it in, change or cancel.
- Make at least one suggestion yourself. Don't just answer.
- When the hidden problem comes, react first ("Oh no!", "Wait, seriously?") and then offer a new option within 2 seconds.
- Listen to your tone: does your "yes" sound excited? Does your "maybe" sound like a maybe?

**Step 3 – Extra round (10 min).** Ask Claude: *"Now play a work colleague. We need to find a time for a 30-minute design review this week, but both our calendars are packed. Make it hard."* Practise "Does… work for you?", "Can we push it to…?" and "Let's say…" in a work setting.

**Step 4 – Notes (10 min).** Write down the corrections Claude gave you. Mark the 3 that came up more than once. Those go into your phrase log tonight.
