## Learning objectives

- Introduce yourself at a party in a way that gives the other person something to talk about.
- Remember and use names, and ask again when you forget, without embarrassment.
- Find common ground quickly and build on it.
- Introduce two people to each other, and leave a conversation naturally.
- Sound warm and friendly — the "smiling voice".

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's sound: warm and friendly.** When you smile while you speak, your voice really changes: it gets a little higher and brighter, and vowels sound more open. Americans use this "smiling voice" a lot when meeting people. Without it, a nervous learner can sound cold or bored, even with perfect words.

Tips:
- **Actually smile** while you shadow. You'll hear it in the recording.
- Stretch the stressed word warmly: "It's SO nice to meet you."
- End introductions with a small rise, as if inviting the other person in.

| # | Sentence | Stress and sound |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hey, I don't think we've met. I'm Sam. | HEY, I don't THINK we've MET. I'm SAM ↗ |
| 2 | So nice to meet you! | so NICE ta MEET ya! |
| 3 | How do you know Dana? | how d'ya KNOW DAna? ↗ |
| 4 | Oh, we used to work together. | oh, we YOOS-ta work ta-GETH-er |
| 5 | Wait, no way, I'm from there too! | WAIT, no WAY, I'm FROM there TOO! |
| 6 | Sorry, what was your name again? | SORry, whad was yer NAME a-GAIN? (smile, soft) |
| 7 | Have you two met? This is Leo. | have you TWO MET? THIS is LEO ↗ |
| 8 | That's so cool. How'd you get into that? | that's SO COOL. HOWdja get INto that? |
| 9 | I'm gonna grab another drink. | I'm gonna GRAB a-NOTH-er DRINK |
| 10 | It was really great meeting you. | it was REALLY GREAT MEEding you |

**Clip to find:** a vlog where the creator goes to a party, a networking event or a new city and meets people. Search YouTube for **"meeting new people vlog"**, **"going to a party alone vlog"** or **"networking event vlog small talk"**.

**How:** speak along with the whole clip, no pausing, three times — smiling each time. Method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Meeting new people

### Explanation

At a party, you usually meet people in a chain: **intro → connection → common ground → (introduce someone) → exit**.

**1. A better intro.** "Hi, I'm Yousef" is fine, but it gives nothing back. Add **one hook** — something the other person can ask about: "Hi, I'm Sam. I'm Dana's coworker — I'm the one who keeps making her redo the slides." Now they can laugh and ask.

**2. The connection question.** At parties, the easiest first question is always about the host or the event: "So how do you know Dana?" Everybody has an answer, and it often leads to a story.

**3. Names.** Say the name back straight away: "Leo — nice to meet you, Leo." That helps you remember it. If you forget, ask early and lightly: "Sorry, what was your name again? I'm terrible with names." Americans do this all the time. It's only awkward if you wait an hour.

**4. Common ground.** Listen for anything you share (a city, a job, a show, a hobby) and jump on it: "Wait, you climb? Where do you go?" Shared things make strangers feel like friends.

**5. Introducing people.** If someone joins, include them: "Oh, have you two met? Leo, this is Mia. Mia's a product designer too." Give each person a hook about the other.

**6. Leaving.** At a party, people move around, so leaving a group is normal. Use a light reason and a warm close: "I'm gonna grab another drink. It was really great meeting you!" Or take them with you: "I'm gonna get some food — wanna come?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I don't think we've met. I'm… | Starting with a stranger | "Hey, I don't think we've met. I'm Sam." |
| So how do you know [host]? | The first question at any party | "So how do you know Dana?" |
| [Name] — nice to meet you, [Name]. | Repeating a name so you remember it | "Leo — nice to meet you, Leo." |
| Sorry, what was your name again? | You forgot a name | "Sorry, what was your name again? I'm terrible with names." |
| Wait, you too? / No way, me too! | Finding common ground | "Wait, you're from Toronto too?" |
| How'd you get into that? | They mention a job or hobby | "You do pottery? How'd you get into that?" |
| Have you two met? | Introducing two people | "Have you two met? Mia, this is Leo." |
| You guys should talk, you both… | Giving people a reason to connect | "You guys should talk, you're both into rock climbing." |
| I'm gonna grab another drink / some food. | Leaving a conversation | "I'm gonna grab some food. Great meeting you!" |
| It was really great meeting you. | Warm ending | "It was really great meeting you. Enjoy the party!" |
| Are you on Instagram / LinkedIn? | Staying in touch | "We should stay in touch. Are you on LinkedIn?" |
| I'll let you two catch up. | Leaving two people who know each other | "Oh, I'll let you two catch up." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: from intro to common ground**

> **You:** Hey, I don't think we've met. I'm Sam — I work with Dana.
> **Rachel:** Oh, hi! I'm Rachel. I'm her neighbor.
> **You:** Rachel, nice to meet you. So, neighbor — are you the one with the super cute dog?
> **Rachel:** Haha, yes, that's Biscuit. You've heard about him?
> **You:** Dana shows us pictures in meetings. Like, all the time.
> **Rachel:** Oh no. That's amazing. So what do you guys do?
> **You:** We're on the same product team. I'm a designer. What about you?
> **Rachel:** I'm a nurse, actually. Night shifts, so this is like my morning.
> **You:** Wait, seriously? So you're having breakfast right now?
> **Rachel:** Basically, yeah. Breakfast chips.

**Dialogue 2: forgetting a name and introducing someone**

> **You:** Sorry, this is so bad — what was your name again? I'm terrible with names.
> **Leo:** Haha, Leo. Don't worry.
> **You:** Leo. OK, locked in. Oh — Mia! Have you two met? Mia, this is Leo. Leo just moved here from Austin.
> **Mia:** Oh, no way, I lived in Austin for three years!
> **Leo:** Really? Which part?
> **You:** See, I told you guys you should talk. I'm gonna grab a drink — anyone want anything?

**Dialogue 3: leaving warmly**

> **You:** OK, I've gotta go say hi to Dana before she thinks I didn't come. It was really great meeting you, Rachel.
> **Rachel:** You too! Find me later if you want more Biscuit stories.
> **You:** Oh, I definitely will.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nice to meet you too. (said first) | Nice to meet you! / Nice to meet you too. (only as a reply) | "Too" only works after the other person says it first. |
| From where do you know Dana? | How do you know Dana? | "How do you know…?" is the fixed question. |
| I forgot your name, sorry. Say again. | Sorry, what was your name again? | The "again" question sounds light and normal. |
| I present you Leo. | This is Leo. / Have you met Leo? | "I present you" is a direct translation; Americans just say "This is…". |
| OK, bye. (walking away) | I'm gonna grab some food. It was great meeting you! | Give a light reason and a warm close. |
| What is your job? | So what do you do? | "What's your job?" sounds like a form. "What do you do?" is the natural question. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight: **30 minutes of party drill** + **30 minutes of live conversation #1**.

### Part A: Party drill (30 min)

Method: like the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md), but each card is a **person** you meet. Shuffle the 12 cards, pick one, and start talking **within 2 seconds**. Speak your side out loud, imagine their answers, react to them, and keep it going for 2 minutes. Each round must have: an intro with a hook, the connection question, their name said back, one piece of common ground and a warm exit. Record every round.

1. A woman by the snack table who is the host's sister and has just flown in from Chicago.
2. A guy who says he works "in crypto" and won't explain what that means.
3. Someone who looks just as nervous as you and is standing alone near the door.
4. A guest who used to live in your home country.
5. A UX researcher who has heard of your company's app — and has complaints about it.
6. An older man who is the host's dad and loves talking about baseball.
7. Someone wearing a T-shirt from a band you also like.
8. A woman who just told you her name, and you instantly forgot it.
9. Two people who don't know each other — introduce them, with a hook for each.
10. A guest who only gives one-word answers.
11. Someone who is clearly about to leave, but you want to get their contact.
12. A person who talks nonstop, and you need to leave politely after 2 minutes.

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (30 min)

**Mission: meet someone new — a real stranger.** Best options: a language-exchange app partner you've never spoken to, a meetup, a coworking space, or a colleague from another team you've never chatted with. See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

Try these 5 things:

1. Open with an intro that has a hook (not just your name).
2. Say their name back within the first 10 seconds, and use it once more later.
3. Find one piece of common ground and ask two follow-up questions about it ([follow-up questions](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md)).
4. Smile while you talk. Listen later: can you hear the warm voice?
5. End warmly and suggest staying in touch.

**Backup (if your partner cancels): voice mode with Claude.** Paste this:


Write down 3 moments from the conversation where you didn't know what to say. Those are tonight's review material.
