- Reread [Week 8 Day 4: Contractions in your own speech](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-4-contractions-in-your-own-speech.md). Fast speech makes more slips, and contractions keep corrections fast.

## Learning objectives

- Fix a wrong word, a wrong fact or a badly built sentence in the middle of speaking, without starting again.
- Use the right correction phrase for the right kind of slip (small word, bigger meaning, softer version).
- Correct yourself in one breath, so the listener barely notices.
- Stop correcting things that don't need correcting.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: "I mean" and the restart.**

When native speakers fix something, the correction has a clear sound pattern:

1. They **cut** the wrong word short, sometimes in the middle: "on Tues- Wednesday".
2. They say **"I mean"** fast and low: "ah-meen".
3. They say the **right word louder and higher** than the wrong one, so it "wins": "on TUESday, I mean WEDNESday".

The restart is quick. There is no long pause, no "sorry", no sigh. The right word just jumps over the wrong one.

Practise these out loud. Stressed words are in CAPITALS.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | It's on Tuesday, I mean WEDNESday. | its-on-TOOZ-day, ah-meen WENZ-day |
| 2 | She's my cousin, I mean my SISTER-in-law. | shees-my-CUZ-in, ah-meen my SIS-ter-in-law |
| 3 | We had like fifty, or rather FIF-teen people. | we-had-lk-FIF-tee, er-RAH-ther fif-TEEN PEE-pul |
| 4 | It was good, well, OK-ish. | it-wuz-GOOD, wel, oh-KAY-ish |
| 5 | I went there in the sum- in the SPRING. | I-wen-there-in-the-SUM, in-the SPRING |
| 6 | He's the CEO, sorry, the C-T-O. | hees-the-see-ee-OH, sorry, the SEE-tee-OH |
| 7 | What I MEANT was, I didn't LIKE it. | whad-I-MENT-wuz, I-DID-n LIKE it |
| 8 | Let me put that a different WAY. | lemme-PUD-that-a-DIF-rent WAY |
| 9 | It's cheap, well, not CHEAP, but not crazy. | its-CHEEP, wel, not CHEEP, bud not CRAY-zee |
| 10 | I hate it. Well, "hate" is a strong WORD. | I-HAYD-it. wel, HATE-is-a-strong WORD |

**Speak-along (10 min):** find a long, chatty conversation where people speak quickly and fix themselves. Search YouTube for **"unscripted podcast catch up"**, **"two friends podcast chat"** or **"vlog talking to camera rambling"**. Speak along with 1–2 minutes three times, no pausing. Every time you hear a correction, copy the cut and the jump in pitch. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Self-correction

### Explanation

When you speak without preparing, you make slips: the wrong day, the wrong number, a word that's too strong, a sentence that starts in a way you can't finish. Many learners react in one of two bad ways. They **stop and start the whole sentence again** ("Sorry, sorry. Let me start again. Yesterday I…"), or they **don't fix it at all**, so the listener gets the wrong information.

Native speakers fix slips **on the move**. They cut the wrong bit, add a short correction phrase, and say the right bit. The whole thing takes less than a second, and the sentence continues.

There are three kinds of slip, and each has its own phrases:

| Kind of slip | What happened | Best phrases |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Wrong word or fact** | You said Tuesday, you meant Wednesday | "I mean…", "sorry, …", "no, wait, …" |
| **2. Wrong strength** | "Hate" is too strong, "fine" is too weak | "well, …", "or rather…", "actually, more like…", "OK, maybe not X, but…" |
| **3. Wrong meaning** | Your sentence came out unclear or rude | "What I meant was…", "Let me put that another way…", "What I'm trying to say is…" |

Two rules make self-correction sound natural:

1. **Don't repeat the whole sentence.** Repeat only from the wrong word: "We launched in May, I mean in June, and…". Not: "We launched in May. Sorry. We launched in June. And…"
2. **Only fix what matters.** If you said "he go" instead of "he goes", the listener understood you. Keep going. Fix facts, numbers, names and meaning. Let small grammar slips go in conversation. You can work on them in the review.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| …I mean… | you said the wrong word or fact | "It's on the third floor, I mean the fourth." |
| Sorry, … | you want a very quick fix | "Sarah, sorry, Sophie, said she'd join." |
| No, wait, … | you realise mid-sentence the fact is wrong | "It was 2019. No, wait, 2020, 'cause it was right before the lockdown." |
| …or rather… | you want a more exact word | "It's a big project, or rather, a long one." |
| Well, … | you're making something softer or more honest | "I loved it. Well, I liked most of it." |
| Actually, more like… | you're fixing a number or amount | "It took an hour. Actually, more like two." |
| OK, maybe not X, but… | your word was too strong | "OK, maybe not a disaster, but it wasn't great." |
| What I meant was… | someone understood you the wrong way | "What I meant was, the idea's good, the timing isn't." |
| Let me put that another way. | your sentence got messy | "Let me put that another way: we need fewer screens." |
| What I'm trying to say is… | you're circling and want to land | "What I'm trying to say is, I need more time." |
| That came out wrong. | you sounded rude by mistake | "That came out wrong. I mean it's different, not bad." |

### Examples

**Mini dialogue 1: wrong fact, fixed on the move**

> **A:** When did you start at the new company?
> **B:** Back in March, I mean April, 'cause my first week was right after Easter. So, like, seven months now.
> **A:** Oh, nice. And how's it going?

**Mini dialogue 2: wrong strength**

> **A:** Did you like the restaurant?
> **B:** It was terrible. Well, OK, maybe not terrible, but the food was super salty and we waited forever. I wouldn't go back.

**Mini dialogue 3: wrong meaning, someone reacts**

> **A:** So what do you think of my design?
> **B:** It's very… busy.
> **A:** Oh. You don't like it?
> **B:** No, no, that came out wrong. What I meant was, there's a lot of good stuff on one screen. I think if we split it into two screens, people will actually see all of it.
> **A:** Ah, OK, that's fair.

**Model answer: a 30-second answer with three natural corrections**

> "So last weekend I went hiking with, like, five friends, or rather four, one of them bailed at the last minute. We drove up on Saturday, I mean Friday night, and stayed in this cabin. The hike was brutal. Well, not brutal, but I'm really out of shape, so it felt brutal. What I'm trying to say is, I need to get to the gym."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "We met in 2018. Sorry, sorry, I'm wrong. We met in 2018… no. Let me start again." | ✅ "We met in 2018, I mean 2019, at a conference." | One short phrase is enough. Long apologies make the listener nervous too. |
| ❌ "It was Monday… [pause] …no… [pause] …it was Tuesday." | ✅ "It was Monday, no, wait, Tuesday." | Pauses make a correction sound like a crisis. Keep it in one breath. |
| ❌ "I mean, I mean, I mean…" | ✅ "I mean, the main problem was the budget." | Repeating it is a filler. Say it once and then the correction. |
| ❌ "He go, sorry, he goes, sorry, he went…" | ✅ "He went to the meeting and…" (just carry on) | Don't correct small grammar in a chat. Fix facts and meaning only. |
| ❌ "What I want to say it is…" | ✅ "What I'm trying to say is…" / "What I meant was…" | Learn the phrase as one chunk. No extra "it". |
| ❌ "Or rather" for a totally different fact: "It was Paris, or rather Tokyo." | ✅ "It was Paris, I mean Tokyo." | "Or rather" makes a word more exact. For a plain mistake, use "I mean". |

### Practise

Do these in order, out loud:

1. **Exercise 2.1: Pick the phrase** (8 min). Choose the best correction phrase for 12 slips, then say each one.
2. **Exercise 2.2: Fix it on the move** (12 min). Say 12 sentences with the slip and the fix in one breath.
3. **Exercise 2.3: Slip story** (10 min). Read a story with 6 slips, retell it fixing each one as you go, and record it.

All in [Day 2 exercises](exercises.md#day-2-exercises).

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight's drill is **voice mode with Claude**, a "spot the slip" chat. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) for setup.

**Session (50 min).** Start voice mode and paste this prompt:

```text
Let's do a 45-minute casual voice chat to practise self-correction. I'm a B2 English learner working on speaking without stopping.

Rules for you:
- Ask me casual questions about my week, my job (I work in design/product), travel, food and my plans. One question at a time, keep your turns short.
- About every third question, ask me something with lots of details: dates, numbers, names, places. That's where I'll make slips.
- Sometimes, on purpose, repeat back a detail I said slightly wrong (for example a wrong day or number) so I have to correct you or myself naturally.
- Twice during the chat, say "Hmm, what do you mean?" after one of my answers, even if it was clear, so I practise "What I meant was…".
- Don't correct my grammar during the chat. Only at the end.

At the end, give me:
1. How many times I corrected myself and which phrases I used (I mean / or rather / well / what I meant was / let me put that another way).
2. Any time I restarted a whole sentence instead of fixing it on the move.
3. Any time I said something wrong and didn't fix it.
4. My 5 most common grammar mistakes, with the corrected version.
Start with your first question.
```

**What to focus on:**

- Fix facts on the move, in one breath. No "sorry, sorry, let me start again".
- Use at least four different correction phrases during the session. Keep a tally on paper.
- When Claude says a detail wrong, correct it like a friend would: "No, it was Thursday, actually."
- When Claude asks "What do you mean?", answer with "What I meant was…" or "Let me put that another way…".
- If you're missing a word in the correction, paraphrase it like [last night](day-1-paraphrasing.md).

**Notes (10 min):** write down Claude's feedback. Copy the 5 grammar mistakes into your phrase log in their corrected form, and say each correct version out loud 3 times.

Fill in tonight's row in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). Add the number of self-corrections Claude counted.
