# Week 11 exercises: Never stopping

[Week 11 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-paraphrasing.md) · [Day 2](day-2-self-correction.md) · [Day 3](day-3-talking-when-youre-unsure.md) · [Day 4](day-4-staying-on-track.md) · [Day 5](day-5-summarising.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** do everything **out loud**, never silently in your head. Use a timer. When an exercise says "record", record it and listen back once. The goal this week is not perfect English, it's **never stopping**: if you get stuck in an exercise, use the rescue move you're practising and keep going. Try each exercise first, and only then look at the answer key. The model answers are examples, not the only right answers.

### Exercise 1.3: Rescue the sentence

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each sentence has a **[missing word]**. Pretend you can't remember it.
2. Say the sentence, and when you reach the gap, paraphrase the word with one of tonight's phrases. Then **finish the sentence**.
3. Don't stop. The listener should understand the full sentence.

### Exercise 2.1: Pick the phrase

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Read each slip. Choose the best correction phrase from the box.
2. Say the full sentence with the slip and the correction, in one breath.
3. Some have more than one good answer.

**Box:** I mean · sorry · no, wait · or rather · well · actually, more like · OK, maybe not X, but · what I meant was · that came out wrong

| # | What you said | What's true |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "My flight's at 6 p.m." | It's at 6 a.m. |
| 2 | "The movie was perfect." | It was good, but not perfect. |
| 3 | "It took ten minutes." | It took about thirty. |
| 4 | "Talk to Jake about it." | You meant Josh. |
| 5 | "Your presentation was long." | Your friend looks hurt. You meant it had lots of useful detail. |
| 6 | "I live in the city centre." | You live near the centre, about ten minutes away. |
| 7 | "Everyone hated the new logo." | Most people didn't like it. Two people loved it. |
| 8 | "It's a big company." | It's not big in people, it's big in money. You want a more exact word. |

| 11 | "I'll send it today." | Your friend thinks you mean right now. You mean before tonight. |
| 12 | "There were like a thousand people." | There were maybe three hundred. |

### Exercise 2.2: Fix it on the move

**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, twice (second time faster)

1. Each line has a slip in **bold** and the fix in brackets.
2. Say the sentence, make the slip, fix it, and **finish the sentence** without stopping. One breath for the slip and the fix.
3. Cut the wrong word short and stress the right word, like in [Day 2 shadowing](day-2-self-correction.md#1-shadowing-15-min).

1. "I'm going to **Boston** (→ Baltimore) next weekend for my friend's birthday."
2. "The meeting's on the **fifth** (→ fifteenth) floor, just take the elevator."
3. "We had **twenty** (→ about twelve) people at the workshop, which was perfect."
4. "My boss is **angry** (→ a little annoyed) about the delay."
5. "I've been here **five years** (→ four and a half) and I still get lost."
6. "The app is **free** (→ free for a month, then ten bucks) so just try it."
7. "My **uncle** (→ my dad's cousin) runs a restaurant downtown."
8. "I **always** (→ usually) cook on Sundays."
9. "It's **next to** (→ across from) the train station, you can't miss it."
10. "We finished the project **early** (→ on time, just) and the client was happy."
11. "I **hate** (→ I'm not a big fan of) running, so I swim instead."
12. "She said **yes** (→ she said probably) so let's plan for her too."

### Exercise 2.3: Slip story

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** read, then record the retelling

1. Read the story once. It has **6 slips** (in bold). The notes tell you what's true.
2. Close the page. Retell the story out loud in your own words, **making each slip and fixing it on the move** with a different correction phrase each time.
3. Record it. Target: under 90 seconds, no restarts.

> **The story:** Last **Friday** my team had a big presentation for a new client. There were **ten** people from their side in the room. I was super nervous because it was my **first** presentation in English. My manager **hated** my slides. The client asked a really hard question about **the budget**. In the end, they **signed** the contract.

**Notes (what's true):**

- Friday → it was Thursday
- ten people → more like six
- first presentation → not the first, the first one *that big*
- hated → she thought they were too long
- the budget → it was about the timeline
- signed → they said they'd sign next week

### Exercise 3.3: Fix the over-hedger

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each answer has a problem: too much hedging, a bare "I don't know", hedging a fact the speaker knows, or a wrong phrase.
2. Say what the problem is, then say a better version out loud.

1. *Q: Where are you from?* — "I think maybe I'm probably from Lyon, I'm not sure."
2. *Q: How far is the airport?* — "I don't know."
3. *Q: Is the new café any good?* — "Apparently, I think, from what I've heard, maybe it's good."
4. *Q: How many people live in Tokyo?* — "Sorry, my English is not good enough for this."
5. *Q: When's the deadline?* (You know it's Friday.) — "I'm not sure, but I think it could be maybe Friday?"
6. *Q: Why did the project get cancelled?* — "Don't quote me on this." [stops]
7. *Q: How does the stock market work?* — "I'm no expert, but it is a system where the shares of the companies are bought and sold by the investors in a regulated environment."
8. *Q: What year did you finish university?* — "You've got me there." (You know exactly: 2016.)

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Back on track

**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each answer goes off on a tangent. Read it out loud.
2. At the **⟶** sign, add a "back on track" phrase (Sorry, I'm going off on a tangent / But that's another story / Anyway, back to… / Long story short / The point is…) and say the return sentence given.
3. Use a different phrase each time.

1. *Q: How was your weekend?* — "Really nice. We went to this farmers' market, and there was a guy selling honey from his own bees, and he told us bees can recognise human faces, which is kinda creepy if you think about it ⟶ *(return: we bought way too much food and cooked all Sunday)*."
2. *Q: Why did you choose design?* — "When I was a kid, I used to draw all the time. My mom kept all my drawings in a box, which she still has, and last year she showed them to my girlfriend, which was so embarrassing ⟶ *(return: I always liked making things look better)*."
3. *Q: What's your new manager like?* — "She's really organised. She came from a big tech company, actually the same one my cousin works at, and my cousin says the free food there is unbelievable, like sushi every day ⟶ *(return: she's organised and she actually listens)*."
4. *Q: How did you get to the airport?* — "We took a taxi, and the driver was from Morocco, and we started talking about Moroccan food, and he gave me his mom's recipe for this chicken thing, which I still haven't tried ⟶ *(return: we got there with like five minutes to spare)*."
5. *Q: Do you like your apartment?* — "Yeah, it's nice. It's on the top floor, so there's a view, and you can see the stadium, and last month there was this huge concert and the whole building was shaking ⟶ *(return: I like it, but it's too small)*."
6. *Q: How did the user testing go?* — "Good! We tested with six people. One guy brought his dog, which was super cute, it was a corgi, and I've always wanted a corgi, but my building doesn't allow dogs ⟶ *(return: most people couldn't find the search button)*."
