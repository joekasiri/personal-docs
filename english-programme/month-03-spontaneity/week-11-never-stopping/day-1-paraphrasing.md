## Learning objectives

- Describe a thing you can't name using five simple "frames" (category, use, look, place, comparison).
- Keep talking through a missing word instead of going silent or switching language.
- Use the "missing word" signal phrases so the listener knows to help you.
- Describe 20+ everyday words in under 15 seconds each.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: "like" as a filler in fast speech.**

Americans use "like" all the time in casual speech, and not to mean "similar to". It fills a small gap, softens a number or a description, or introduces what someone said or thought. When you shadow it, notice two things:

1. "Like" used as a filler is **unstressed and fast**: it sounds like "lk" or "lai'". It never gets its own beat.
2. It usually comes **just before the important word**, so the important word gets the stress: "it was like, HUGE".

You will use this tonight: when you paraphrase, "it's like a…" is your first rescue. Say it fast and light, then stress the description.

Practise these out loud. Stressed words are in CAPITALS.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | It was like, SO crowded. | it-wuz-lk SO CROWD-id |
| 2 | It's like a little BOX for your keys. | its-lk-a-liddle BOX fer-yer KEYS |
| 3 | We waited like twenty MINUTES. | we-WAY-did-lk TWEN-ee MIN-its |
| 4 | She's like, "No WAY." | shees-lk NO WAY |
| 5 | It's kind of like a SPOON, but FLAT. | its-kinda-lk-a SPOON, bud FLAT |
| 6 | He was like, totally LOST. | he-wuz-lk TOH-dlee LOST |
| 7 | It's like the thing you use to OPEN wine. | its-lk-the-thing-ya-YOOZ-ta OH-pen WINE |
| 8 | I was there for like an HOUR. | I-wuz-there-fer-lk-an HOUR |
| 9 | It's this, like, ORANGE fruit, kinda SOUR. | its-this-lk OR-inj FROOT, kinda SOUR |
| 10 | The app is like Uber, but for DOG walkers. | the-APP-is-lk OO-ber, but-fer DOG WAW-kers |

**Speak-along (10 min):** find a casual vlog where someone describes things or tells a story to the camera. Search YouTube for **"casual vlog storytime"**, **"day in my life vlog talking"** or **"get ready with me chat"**. Choose a speaker who says "like" a lot. Play 1–2 minutes and speak **at the same time** as them, without pausing. Copy how quickly they say "like" and how the next word jumps out. Do it three times. See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md) for the speak-along method.

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Paraphrasing

### Explanation

You will forget words. Everybody does, in every language. The problem is not the missing word. The problem is what you do next. Most learners do one of three things: go silent, say the word in their own language, or give up the whole sentence ("…never mind"). All three stop the conversation.

**Paraphrasing** means you go *around* the word. You describe it until the listener understands, or until they say the word for you. Native speakers do this every day ("you know, the thing on the wall that… the thermostat!"). It sounds natural, not like a mistake.

The trick is to have **five frames** ready, so you never have to think about *how* to describe something:

| Frame | Question it answers | Example (for "stapler") |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Category** | What kind of thing is it? | "It's a kind of office tool…" |
| **2. Use** | What do you do with it? | "…you use it to join pieces of paper together…" |
| **3. Look** | What does it look like? | "…it's small, usually black, you push down on the top…" |
| **4. Place** | Where do you find it? | "…every office has one on the desk…" |
| **5. Comparison** | What is it similar to or the opposite of? | "…it's like a paper clip, but with metal you push through." |

You don't need all five. Usually two are enough. Start with **category + use**, because they give the most information fastest.

There are also three kinds of missing word, and each has its own starter:

- **A thing** (object, food, tool): "It's like a…", "It's the thing you use to…"
- **A place**: "You know, the place where…"
- **A person or job**: "The person who…", "The guy who…"
- **An action**: "When you… (describe it)", "It's what you do when…"
- **A feeling or quality**: "It's that feeling when…", "It's kind of the opposite of…"

Finally, **signal the gap.** A short phrase like "what's the word…" tells the listener you know what you mean but can't find the word. Often they help you. If they don't, you just carry on: "anyway, it's basically…". The conversation keeps moving either way.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's like a… kind of a… | you need a category and you're still thinking | "It's like a… kind of a big pan with a lid." |
| It's the thing you use to… | you're missing the name of an object | "It's the thing you use to open cans." |
| You know, the place where… | you're missing the name of a place | "You know, the place where you get your car fixed." |
| The person who… | you're missing a job or role | "The person who checks your teeth… the hygienist!" |
| It's what you do when… | you're missing a verb | "It's what you do when you sleep for twenty minutes in the afternoon." |
| It's that feeling when… | you're missing a feeling word | "It's that feeling when you see someone else succeed and you want it too." |
| What's the word… / What's it called… | you want to signal the gap and invite help | "We need a, what's it called, a… backup plan." |
| It's kind of the opposite of… | an opposite is easier to say | "It's kind of the opposite of shy." |
| It's similar to X, but… | you know a related word | "It's similar to a bakery, but they only sell cakes." |
| Anyway, it's basically… | nobody helped and you want to move on | "Anyway, it's basically a tiny screwdriver." |
| I can't think of the word, but… | you want to be honest and keep going | "I can't think of the word, but it's when the bank says no to your card." |

### Examples

**Mini dialogue 1: a missing object word**

> **A:** So how was the move? Did everything fit in the new apartment?
> **B:** Mostly, yeah. But I forgot to buy a… what's it called… it's the thing you put on the floor next to the door so people can wipe their shoes?
> **A:** Oh, a doormat?
> **B:** Yes! A doormat. So now everyone's just walking mud straight into the living room.

**Mini dialogue 2: a missing work word, nobody helps**

> **A:** What slowed the project down?
> **B:** Honestly, it was the, um, the step where the other team has to say yes before we can launch. It's kind of like an approval thing, but more formal. Anyway, it's basically a checkpoint, and it took three weeks.
> **A:** Oh, the sign-off process. Yeah, that's always slow.

**Mini dialogue 3: a missing feeling word**

> **A:** How did you feel when they picked your design?
> **B:** It was that feeling when you've been worried for days and then suddenly it's all OK, like your whole body goes soft. I don't know the word for it.
> **A:** Relief?
> **B:** Relief, yeah. Huge relief.

**Model answer: describing a word alone (target "umbrella", 12 seconds)**

> "OK, it's a kind of… it's something you carry when the weather's bad. You hold it over your head so you don't get wet. It folds up, it's usually black, and it always breaks when it's windy. You know, the thing everyone forgets on the train."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "It's… [5 seconds of silence] …sorry, I forgot." | ✅ "It's the thing you use to… cut paper, like small scissors." | Silence stops the conversation. Start the frame first, the words will follow. |
| ❌ "It's a [word in your language]." | ✅ "It's like a kind of soup, kinda spicy, with noodles." | The listener can't help you. A description they can understand. |
| ❌ "It's a thing for clean the floor." | ✅ "It's a thing for cleaning the floor." / "…to clean the floor." | After "for", use -ing. After "use it", use "to + verb". |
| ❌ "It's the place where you can buying medicine." | ✅ "It's the place where you can buy medicine." | After "can", the base verb. Simple grammar keeps you fast. |
| ❌ "Never mind." (gives up) | ✅ "Anyway, it's basically a small shop that sells everything." | Giving up kills the story. "Anyway, it's basically…" moves you on. |
| ❌ "It is an object which is utilised for…" | ✅ "It's the thing you use to…" | Formal language is slow. Keep paraphrases casual and short. |

3. **Exercise 1.3: Rescue the sentence** (8 min). Finish 10 sentences where the key word is "missing".

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) with a twist: **forbidden words**. For each topic card, there are two words you are not allowed to say. This forces you to paraphrase in real speech, not just in an exercise. The normal random-topic rules still apply: no preparation, start talking within 2 seconds of reading the card, keep going for 1 minute, record every round.

**Warm-up (5 min):** describe five things in the room you're sitting in, without saying their names. 10 seconds each.

**Random-topic rounds (35 min):** shuffle these 14 cards (write them on paper or put them in a notes app and pick without looking). Talk for **1 minute** each. If you say a forbidden word, don't stop: paraphrase it and keep going, then make a tick on your paper. Do as many as you can in 35 minutes (aim for 12 or more).

| # | Topic | Forbidden words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your kitchen | fridge, oven |
| 2 | Your morning routine | coffee, shower |
| 3 | A gym you've been to | weights, treadmill |
| 4 | Your phone and what's on it | app, screen |
| 5 | A wedding you went to | bride, cake |
| 6 | Going to the dentist | teeth, drill |
| 7 | An airport you know well | plane, security |
| 8 | Your desk at work | laptop, monitor |
| 9 | A rainy day | umbrella, wet |
| 10 | A supermarket | cart, checkout |
| 11 | A design tool you use | Figma, prototype |
| 12 | Your favourite meal | (the name of the dish), plate |
| 13 | Moving to a new apartment | boxes, landlord |
| 14 | A car journey | seatbelt, traffic |

**One 3/2/1 round (15 min):** pick the topic where you got the most ticks. Do the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md) on it: 3 minutes, then 2 minutes, then 1 minute, same content, recorded. Keep the forbidden words forbidden. By the 1-minute version your paraphrases should be short and automatic.

**Listen back and count (5 min):** listen to the 1-minute version. Count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms, pauses over 2 seconds, different connectors. Also count your **ticks** (forbidden words said). Write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
