## Learning objectives

- Answer a question about something you don't really know, instead of saying "I don't know" and stopping.
- Show how sure you are with the right phrase (guessing, half-remembering, heard it somewhere).
- Turn "I don't know" into a short, useful answer: what you *do* know + a guess + a question back.
- Use stretched thinking sounds ("sooo", "weeell") instead of "umm".

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: stretched words.**

When Americans need half a second to think, they often don't say "umm". They **stretch the first word** of the answer: "Sooo…", "Weeell…", "I meeean…", "Hmmm, I thiiink…". This sounds relaxed and natural, and it keeps your voice going, so the listener knows you haven't finished.

How to do it:

1. Stretch the **vowel**, not the consonant: "sooo", not "sssso".
2. Let your voice go **slightly down and then level**, as if you're walking slowly into the sentence.
3. Don't stretch more than about one second. After that, start the real sentence.

Practise these out loud. The stretched part is written long, stressed words in CAPITALS.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sooo, I'm not totally SURE, but… | sooo, I'm-NOT-toh-dlee SHUR, but |
| 2 | Weeell, from what I've HEARD… | weeell, frum-whad-I've HURD |
| 3 | Hmmm, I thiiink it's in MAY? | hmmm, I-thiiink-its-in MAY |
| 4 | I meeean, I could be WRONG… | I-meeean, I-c'd-be WRONG |
| 5 | It's like, I dunno, fifty BUCKS? | its-lk, I-DUNNO, FIF-tee BUCKS |
| 6 | Ooh, that's a hard ONE. | ooh, thats-a-HARD WUN |
| 7 | Sooo the way I underSTAND it… | sooo, the-WAY-I-un-der-STAN-dit |
| 8 | Weeell, don't QUOTE me on this… | weeell, DOHNT-KWOHT-me-on-THIS |
| 9 | Hmmm, if I reMEMber right… | hmmm, if-I-ree-MEM-ber RIGHT |
| 10 | Yeeeah, no, I have NO idea. | yeeeah, NO, I-have NO-ai-DEE-a |

**Speak-along (10 min):** find a podcast or street interview where people are asked questions they didn't expect. Search YouTube for **"street interview random questions"**, **"podcast guests guess the answer"** or **"trivia game podcast friends"**. Speak along with 1–2 minutes three times, no pausing. Copy the stretched starts and the rising "question" tone people use when they guess ("in MAY?"). Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Talking when you're unsure

### Explanation

Someone asks: "How long has your company been around?" or "Is it expensive to live in Denver?" or "What's the population of your city?" You don't know. Most learners say "I don't know" and stop. The conversation dies, and you feel like you failed.

Native speakers almost never stop at "I don't know". They say **how sure they are**, then **give what they've got**: a guess, a memory, something they heard. Being unsure is normal. Being silent is what feels awkward.

**How is this different from Week 6 Day 5?**

| | [Week 6 Day 5: Hedging](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md) | Tonight: Talking when you're unsure |
|---|---|---|
| The question is about… | what you **think** (opinion) | what is **true** (facts, knowledge) |
| Example question | "Is remote work better?" | "When did remote work become normal?" |
| Typical phrase | "I guess it depends…" | "I'm not sure, but I think it was around 2020." |
| What you're softening | how strongly you believe something | how much you actually know |

There are four "sureness levels". Pick the phrase that matches how much you know:

| Level | You… | Phrases |
|---|---|---|
| **Pretty sure** | know it but not 100% | "I'm pretty sure…", "If I remember right…" |
| **Half sure** | have a guess | "I'm not sure, but I think…", "I'd say maybe…", "Probably around…" |
| **Heard it somewhere** | have second-hand info | "From what I've heard…", "Apparently…", "I read somewhere that…" |
| **No idea** | really don't know | "I have no idea, to be honest.", "You've got me there.", "No clue, but…" |

Then use the **unsure answer pattern**, so even "no idea" becomes a full answer:

1. **Sureness phrase:** "I'm not sure, but…"
2. **What you know or guess:** "…I think it's pretty expensive, 'cause my friend there pays a lot for rent."
3. **Hand it back:** "Do you know?" / "Why, are you thinking of moving?" / "I should look that up."

Notice: in step 3 you are recycling follow-up questions from [Week 2 Day 2](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md) and answer-add-ask from [Week 3 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md).

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I'm pretty sure… | you're about 80% sure | "I'm pretty sure it closes at ten." |
| If I remember right… | it's a memory, and memories can be wrong | "If I remember right, we launched that in the spring." |
| I'm not sure, but I think… | you have a guess | "I'm not sure, but I think it's about two hours by train." |
| I'd say maybe… / Probably around… | you're guessing a number | "I'd say maybe thirty people? Probably around that." |
| From what I've heard… | your info is from other people | "From what I've heard, the new manager is really nice." |
| Apparently… | you're repeating something you were told or read | "Apparently, they're closing the office on Fridays." |
| Don't quote me on this, but… | you want to share a guess but not promise it | "Don't quote me on this, but I think the price is going up." |
| I'm no expert, but… | the topic is technical or outside your field | "I'm no expert, but I think it's a battery problem." |
| You've got me there. | you really don't know and want to admit it with a smile | "How old is the building? Ooh, you've got me there." |
| I have no idea, but I can find out. | it's work, and you should follow up | "I have no idea, but I can find out and message you." |
| It's either X or Y. | you have two possible answers | "It's either Tuesday or Wednesday, I'll check." |

### Examples

**Mini dialogue 1: half sure, with a hand-back**

> **A:** How long does it take to get to the beach from your place?
> **B:** Hmmm, I'm not sure, I've only been once. I'd say maybe forty minutes by car? Probably more on a weekend. Why, are you thinking of going?
> **A:** Yeah, maybe Saturday.

**Mini dialogue 2: heard it somewhere, at work**

> **A:** Do you know why they're changing the design system?
> **B:** Sooo, from what I've heard, the old one was getting really hard to maintain. Apparently there were like three different button styles in the product. But don't quote me on this, I'm not on that team. Maybe ask Priya, she'd know.

**Mini dialogue 3: no idea, but still a real answer**

> **A:** What's the population of your hometown?
> **B:** Oh, you've got me there. I have no idea, to be honest. It's not tiny, there's a university and a big hospital, so… I'd say maybe two hundred thousand? I should look that up, it's kind of embarrassing that I don't know.
> **A:** Haha, I don't know mine either.

**Model answer: a 30-second answer to "How does a credit score work?"**

> "Weeell, I'm no expert, but from what I understand, it's basically a number that shows if you pay your bills on time. If you pay on time, it goes up, and if you miss payments, it goes down. I think there's other stuff too, like how much you borrow, but I'm not sure about the details. Do you know how it works exactly? I've always been kinda confused about it."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "I don't know." [stops] | ✅ "I don't know exactly, but I think it's about an hour. Have you been?" | A bare "I don't know" ends the conversation. Add a guess and a question. |
| ❌ "I am not sure but I think that it is maybe probably around ten." | ✅ "I'm not sure, but I think it's around ten." | One sureness word is enough. Too many makes you sound lost. |
| ❌ "I think it's around ten." (when you're 100% sure) | ✅ "It's ten." / "It's definitely ten." | Don't soften facts you know. It makes people doubt you. |
| ❌ "Apparently, I think it's closed." | ✅ "Apparently, it's closed." / "I think it's closed." | "Apparently" already means "I heard". Don't stack it with "I think". |
| ❌ "Sorry, my English is not good, I don't know." | ✅ "Hmmm, you've got me there." | Not knowing a fact has nothing to do with your English. Don't apologise for it. |
| ❌ "Don't quote me." [then gives no answer] | ✅ "Don't quote me on this, but I think it's next month." | The phrase introduces a guess. You still need the guess. |

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

### Part 1: "You don't know this" drill (30 min)

This is a random-topic drill with **knowledge questions you probably can't answer**. The goal is not to be right. The goal is to talk for **45 seconds** on each one, using the unsure answer pattern. No preparation: read the card, start talking within 2 seconds, record every answer. See the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) for the basic method.

Shuffle these 14 cards and do as many as you can in 25 minutes:

1. How do airplanes stay in the air?
2. How many people live in New York City?
3. Why is the sky blue?
4. How does your phone know where you are?
5. When was the first website made?
6. How do they decide the price of a coffee?
7. Why do cats purr?
8. How long does it take to become a doctor in the US?
9. How does a dishwasher get things clean?
10. How do apps make money if they're free?
11. Why do people get jet lag?
12. How do they choose the Oscars winners?
13. What's the most spoken language in the world?
14. How does a company decide your salary?

For each one: sureness phrase → what you know or guess → hand it back ("Do you know?", "I should look that up", "What do you think?"). Use at least three different sureness phrases across the session.

**Listen and count (5 min):** play back two answers. Count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) and how many times you said a bare "I don't know" with nothing after it. Target: zero.

### Part 2: Live conversation #1 (30 min)

Have your first live conversation of the week. See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md) for where to find partners and how to handle nerves.

**Your mission: ask questions that make *both* of you unsure, and keep talking anyway.**

Try these 4 things:

1. Ask your partner at least two "how does it work" or "how many" questions about their city, job or country ("How does the subway system work in your city?", "How many people work at your company?"). When they're unsure, react and help them guess.
2. When *you* get a question you can't fully answer, use the unsure answer pattern at least three times.
3. Use "From what I've heard…" or "Apparently…" at least once to share something second-hand.
4. If you're missing a word, paraphrase it like on [Day 1](day-1-paraphrasing.md), and fix any slip on the move like on [Day 2](day-2-self-correction.md).

Afterwards, write down one thing you said "I don't know" to and what you could have said instead.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, do 30 minutes in voice mode with Claude ([voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)). Paste this:

```text
Let's have a 25-minute casual voice chat. Play a friendly American who's curious about everything. Ask me lots of questions I probably can't fully answer: how things work, numbers, history of places, facts about my country and my job in design/product. Also share a few "facts" yourself and ask me if I know whether they're true.

My goal is to never stop at "I don't know". I should say how sure I am, guess, and hand the question back. Don't correct my grammar during the chat. At the end, tell me:
1. Every time I said "I don't know" and then stopped.
2. Which sureness phrases I used (pretty sure / not sure but I think / from what I've heard / apparently / no idea but).
3. Whether I sounded too unsure about things I actually knew.
Start now.
```
