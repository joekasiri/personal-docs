## Learning objectives

- Notice when you've gone off on a tangent, and get back to your main point in one sentence.
- Recover smoothly when you lose your train of thought.
- Return to your point after someone interrupts you.
- Answer hard questions in voice mode with no silence longer than 2 seconds.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: "you know" and trailing off.**

"You know" is the glue of casual American speech. It has two jobs:

1. **Checking the listener is with you**, said fast and low in the middle of a sentence: "it was one of those, ya-know, really long meetings".
2. **Trailing off**, at the end of an unfinished sentence, when the listener can guess the rest: "and then it just kind of… you know." The voice goes down and gets quieter.

When people lose their thread, they often trail off, pause very briefly, and then **restart higher and louder** with a "back on track" phrase: "…you know. ANYWAY, the point is…".

Practise these out loud. Stressed words are in CAPITALS.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | It was one of those, you know, really LONG days. | it-wuz-WUN-uv-those, ya-know, really LONG DAYZ |
| 2 | And he just kind of… you know. | an-he-JUST-kinda… ya-know |
| 3 | ANYWAY, where was I? | EN-ee-way, WHERE-wuz-I |
| 4 | Oh RIGHT, the apartment. | oh RIGHT, the a-PART-ment |
| 5 | Sorry, I'm going off on a TANGENT. | sorry, I'm-gonna-OFF-on-a TAN-jent |
| 6 | What was I SAYING? | WHAD-wuz-I SAY-in |
| 7 | I totally lost my train of THOUGHT. | I-TOH-dlee-LOST-my-TRAIN-uv THOT |
| 8 | Back to the TRIP, though. | BACK-ta-the TRIP, tho |
| 9 | You know what I MEAN? Like, it's just… a LOT. | ya-know-whad-I MEEN? lk, its-just… a LOT |
| 10 | So yeah, the POINT is, I need a vacation. | so-YEAH, the POINT-is, I-need-a vay-KAY-shun |

**Speak-along (10 min):** find a rambling storyteller, someone who goes off topic and comes back. Search YouTube for **"storytime rant vlog"**, **"comedian podcast long story"** or **"friends podcast tangent"**. Speak along with 1–2 minutes three times, no pausing. Listen for the moment they come back to the point, and copy the jump in volume. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Staying on track

### Explanation

When you speak without preparing, your thoughts don't come out in a straight line. You start telling someone about your trip, then you mention the hotel, then the hotel reminds you of a funny thing your sister said, and suddenly you've forgotten what you were talking about. Or someone asks a question in the middle of your story, and you can't find your way back.

This is completely normal. Native speakers do it all the time. The difference is that they have **"back on track" phrases** ready, so getting lost doesn't become a silence.

There are three situations:

| Situation | What it feels like | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The tangent** | You realise you're talking about something else | Name it ("Sorry, I'm going off on a tangent") and go back ("Anyway, back to…") |
| **2. The blank** | Your mind goes empty mid-sentence | Admit it lightly ("Where was I?", "I totally lost my train of thought") and grab the last thing you remember |
| **3. The interruption** | Someone asks a question or changes the subject | Answer briefly, then return ("But yeah, so, what I was saying was…") |

The secret for "the blank" is: **say the last thing you remember out loud.** "Where was I? Oh right, the hotel. So the hotel…". Saying it out loud gives your brain the thread again. Asking the listener also works and sounds friendly: "What was I saying?". They usually tell you.

**"Anyway" here is not the same as in Week 3.** In [Week 3 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md) "anyway" moved you to a *new* topic or ended a chat. Tonight "anyway, back to…" takes you back to an *old* topic, your main point.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Where was I? | your mind went blank | "Where was I? Oh right, the interview." |
| What was I saying? | you want the listener to help you find your thread | "Sorry, what was I saying?" "The landlord." "Yes! So the landlord…" |
| I totally lost my train of thought. | you can't remember your point at all | "I totally lost my train of thought. Anyway, it was a good trip." |
| Anyway, back to… | you want to return to the main topic | "Anyway, back to the wedding." |
| Sorry, I'm going off on a tangent. | you notice you're talking about something else | "Sorry, I'm going off on a tangent. The point is, we were late." |
| But that's another story. | you started a side story you don't want to finish | "My cousin got lost too, but that's another story." |
| Oh right, so… | you just remembered your thread | "Oh right, so after the meeting…" |
| But yeah, so… / But yeah, anyway… | you're coming back after answering an interruption | "Yeah, it's in Brooklyn. But yeah, so, the apartment…" |
| Going back to what I said before… | you want to link back to an earlier point | "Going back to what I said before, the timeline is the real issue." |
| Long story short… | your tangent got long and you want to jump to the end | "Long story short, we missed the flight." |
| The point is… / My point is… | you want to state your main idea clearly | "The point is, it was worth it." |

"Long story short" is one of the [12 connectors from Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md). Tonight you use it as an exit from a tangent.

### Examples

**Mini dialogue 1: the tangent**

> **A:** So how was your trip to Chicago?
> **B:** It was great. We stayed in this hotel near the river, which my friend found on some app, and actually that app is pretty cool, it shows you the real photos from guests, not the fake ones, and… sorry, I'm going off on a tangent. Anyway, back to Chicago. The food was amazing.

**Mini dialogue 2: the blank**

> **A:** And what did your manager say?
> **B:** So she said that the, um… the… oh man, I totally lost my train of thought. What was I saying?
> **A:** The deadline.
> **B:** Oh right, so she said the deadline can move a week, but only if we cut the onboarding screens.

**Mini dialogue 3: the interruption**

> **B:** …so we get to the airport, and the line for security is just insane, like a hundred people—
> **A:** Wait, which airport?
> **B:** JFK. Terminal four. But yeah, so, the line is insane, and our flight is in forty minutes…

**Model answer: a 40-second answer with a tangent and a return**

> "The best job I ever had was at a small design agency. There were only eight of us, and the office was above a bakery, so it always smelled amazing. Actually, I got kinda addicted to their cinnamon rolls, I put on like five pounds that year, but that's another story. Anyway, back to the job. The reason I loved it was that I got to do everything: research, design, even talking to clients. The point is, I learned more there in one year than in three years anywhere else."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "…and… [silence for 6 seconds]…" | ✅ "…and, where was I? Oh right, the bakery." | Say the last thing you remember. It brings the thread back. |
| ❌ "Sorry, sorry, I forgot what I want to say, sorry." | ✅ "I totally lost my train of thought. Anyway…" | One light phrase is enough. Don't make it a big deal. |
| ❌ "I lost my train of thinking." | ✅ "I lost my train of thought." | It's a fixed phrase: "train of thought". |
| ❌ "Anyway, back to the topic." | ✅ "Anyway, back to the trip." | Name the actual topic. "The topic" sounds like a meeting. |
| ❌ "Where I was?" | ✅ "Where was I?" | Question word order: was + I. |
| ❌ After an interruption, starting the whole story again | ✅ "But yeah, so, the line was insane…" | Jump back to exactly where you stopped. |

### Practise

Do these in order, out loud:

1. **Exercise 4.1: Back on track** (10 min). Read 6 messy answers with tangents and say them with a "back on track" phrase in the right place.
2. **Exercise 4.2: Interrupt yourself** (10 min). Tell 5 short stories, interrupting each one with a given side story, then returning.
3. **Exercise 4.3: Blank recovery** (10 min). Practise 10 "blank" moments with the last-word trick.

All in [Day 4 exercises](exercises.md#day-4-exercises).

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is **voice mode with Claude**: hard questions with a strict rule. **You are never silent for more than 2 seconds.** If you don't know, use [Day 3](day-3-talking-when-youre-unsure.md). If you're missing a word, paraphrase ([Day 1](day-1-paraphrasing.md)). If you need a start, use a [time-buyer from Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md). If you lose the thread, use tonight's phrases. See [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) for setup.

**Session (45 min).** Record the session on your phone if you can. Start voice mode and paste this:

```text
Let's do a 40-minute voice session called "Never silent". I'm a B2 English learner. I work in design/product. My rule: I must never be silent for more than 2 seconds.

Please:
- Ask me hard, open questions one at a time: questions about my life, values, work, the future, how things work, and a few strange or philosophical ones. Make them hard to answer quickly.
- Let me talk for 1-2 minutes per answer. Sometimes interrupt me in the middle with a short side question (like "Wait, where was that?" or "Who's that?"), so I have to answer and then get back to my point.
- Twice, ask me a follow-up about something I mentioned only as a side detail, to pull me onto a tangent. See if I can get back to my main point.
- If I go silent for more than 2 seconds, say "Beep!" and I'll keep going.
- Don't correct grammar during the session.

At the end, give me:
1. How many times you said "Beep!".
2. Which "back on track" phrases I used (Where was I? / Anyway, back to… / I lost my train of thought / But yeah, so… / The point is…).
3. Any answer where I never got back to my main point.
4. My 5 most common grammar mistakes, corrected.
Ask your first question now.
```

**What to focus on:**

- Keep your voice going. A stretched "sooo" or "weeell" ([Day 3 shadowing](day-3-talking-when-youre-unsure.md#1-shadowing-15-min)) is better than silence.
- After every interruption, go back with "But yeah, so…" and the exact point where you stopped.
- When Claude pulls you onto a tangent, answer it briefly, then say "Anyway, back to…" by yourself.
- End at least half your answers with "The point is…" or "So yeah, my point is…". This prepares you for tomorrow's lesson on summarising.

**Listen and notes (15 min):** listen to 5 minutes of your recording. Count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) for those 5 minutes. Write down Claude's beep count and feedback, and copy the 5 corrected mistakes into your phrase log.

Fill in tonight's row in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), and add your beep count.
