## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the clip you found hardest this week. For most people it's the Day 4 rambling storyteller, because the speed changes all the time. Speak along with it three times without pausing. On the third time, record yourself and listen: do your "you know", "I mean" and stretched "sooo" sound light and fast, like the speaker's, or slow and heavy? See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

This week's test checks if you keep going when things go wrong. The prompt is designed to make you miss words and not know things.

**Tonight's prompt (don't read it until you're ready to record):**

> **"Explain to a friend how something in your home works, for example your washing machine, your coffee maker or your Wi-Fi router, and what you'd do if it broke tomorrow."**

1. Read the prompt, start recording, and start talking **within 2 seconds**. No notes, no preparation.
2. Talk for **2 minutes**. You will probably be missing words (parts of the machine) and be unsure about how it really works. Good. Paraphrase, guess, correct yourself, and come back if you go off on a tangent.
3. End with a **summary sentence**.
4. Listen back and count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md):
   - umms and uhhs
   - pauses longer than 2 seconds
   - different connectors
5. Also tick the **rescue moves checklist** in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials): did you paraphrase, self-correct, use a sureness phrase, use a back-on-track phrase, and finish with a summary?
6. Write everything in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) and compare with last week.

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

This is live conversation #2 of the week. A meetup, a language exchange, a colleague, or an online tutor. See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md) for where to go and how to deal with nerves.

**Your mission: have a conversation where something "goes wrong" at least five times, and you never stop.**

Try these 5 things:

1. **Ask about something technical in their life** ("How does your job actually work, day to day?", "How did you learn to cook that?"). When they explain, summarise it back at least twice: "So you're saying…?"
2. **Paraphrase at least twice.** If you don't miss any words naturally, do it on purpose: describe a dish, a tool or a place instead of naming it, and see if they guess.
3. **Answer at least two questions you're unsure about** with a sureness phrase and a guess, never a bare "I don't know".
4. **Tell one story with a tangent,** and bring it back with "Anyway, back to…" or "But that's another story".
5. **Correct yourself on the move** at least once, in one breath.

After the conversation, write down: one moment you would have frozen a month ago, and what you did instead.

**Backup plan:** if the live conversation falls through, do 40 minutes in voice mode with Claude ([voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)) with this prompt:

```text
Let's have a 40-minute casual voice chat. Play a friendly American I just met at a meetup. Ask about my life, my job in design/product and my hobbies, and share your own stories too. During the chat:
- Ask me at least 3 questions I probably can't fully answer (facts, numbers, how things work).
- Interrupt me twice with a side question, then let me get back to my point.
- Talk about something long and detailed at least twice so I can summarise it back to you.
- If I can't find a word, wait and let me paraphrase it. Don't give me the word.
At the end, tell me which of these I did and how well: paraphrasing, self-correction, answering when unsure, getting back on track, summarising. Then list my 5 most common grammar mistakes, corrected.
```
