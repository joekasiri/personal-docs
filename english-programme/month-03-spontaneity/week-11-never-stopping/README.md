# Week 11: Never stopping

[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 3: Spontaneity](../README.md) · [← Week 10: Hypotheticals](../week-10-hypotheticals/README.md) · [Week 12: Role-plays →](../week-12-role-plays/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you learn to **keep talking when something goes wrong**. A missing word, a wrong word, a fact you don't know, a lost thought, a long messy answer: each of these is a moment where you usually freeze. Native speakers hit the same problems all the time. The difference is that they have a ready-made move for each one, so the conversation never stops. By Day 6 you will have five "rescue moves" you can use without thinking: go around the word, fix the sentence while you speak, answer even when you're not sure, find your way back after a tangent, and wrap everything up in one clean sentence.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 10](../week-10-hypotheticals/README.md) you talked about imaginary situations, which pushed you to say things you had never planned. That made the gaps in your English very easy to notice: words you didn't have, sentences that went the wrong way. This week is about those gaps. In Week 9 and Week 10 the rule was "start talking immediately". This week the rule is **"never stop once you've started"**. In [Week 3 Day 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) you learned time-buyers ("Let me think…", "Good question…") to *start* an answer. This week's phrases are for the *middle* of an answer, when you're already talking and something breaks.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Go for a walk and listen to one episode of a casual podcast where two or three people chat without a script (search "two friends podcast chat" or "unscripted podcast catch up"). Your only task: **count the rescue moves.** Every time a speaker loses a word ("what's it called…"), corrects themselves ("I mean…"), admits they don't know ("don't quote me on that") or gets back on track ("anyway, where was I"), make a tally on your phone. Most people are surprised to find 20 or more in half an hour. That is the lesson of this week: fluent speakers are not perfect, they just never stop. Then rest. No recording, no notes.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] describe any object, place or idea you can't name, in under 15 seconds, without stopping
- [ ] fix a wrong word or a bad sentence while you are speaking, without starting from the beginning
- [ ] give a useful answer to a question you don't really know the answer to, and be honest about how sure you are
- [ ] notice when you have drifted off topic and get back to your point in one sentence
- [ ] answer hard questions in voice mode with no silence longer than 2 seconds
- [ ] finish a long, messy answer with a short, clear summary sentence
