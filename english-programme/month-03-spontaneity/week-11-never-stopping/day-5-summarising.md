## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: the falling tone at the end of a summary.**

When people summarise, their voice does something special:

1. The summary phrase ("so basically", "long story short") is **fast and a bit lower**.
2. The main idea gets **one big stress**: "the HOTEL was the problem".
3. The very end **falls clearly down** and stops. This fall tells the listener "I'm done, your turn". A rising end sounds like you're still going or not sure.

Often people say **"So yeah."** after the summary, with a long falling "yeah". It's a very American way to hand over the turn.

Practise these out loud. Stressed words are in CAPITALS, ↘ means the voice falls.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So basically, it was a waste of TIME. ↘ | so-BAY-sik-lee, it-wuz-a-WAYST-uv TIME ↘ |
| 2 | Long story short, we MISSED it. ↘ | long-story-SHORT, we MISST-it ↘ |
| 3 | So yeah. ↘ | so YEAAAH ↘ |
| 4 | Bottom line, we need more PEOPLE. ↘ | BOD-um-line, we-need-more PEE-pul ↘ |
| 5 | So what I'm saying is, I'm not GOING. ↘ | so-whad-I'm-SAY-in-is, I'm-not GO-in ↘ |
| 6 | In a nutshell, it's Airbnb for BOATS. ↘ | in-a-NUT-shell, its-AIR-bee-en-bee fer BOATS ↘ |
| 7 | So you're saying it's too EXPENSIVE? ↗ | so-yer-SAY-in its-too ex-PEN-siv ↗ |
| 8 | The main thing is, nobody got HURT. ↘ | the-MAIN-thing-is, NO-buddy got HURT ↘ |
| 9 | So, all in all, a pretty good WEEKEND. ↘ | so, all-in-ALL, a-priddy-good WEEK-end ↘ |
| 10 | At the end of the day, it's your CALL. ↘ | at-the-END-uv-the-day, its-yer CALL ↘ |

Notice number 7 goes **up**: when you summarise someone *else* to check, it's a question.

**Speak-along (10 min):** find a podcast or vlog where the speaker tells a long story and then wraps up. Search YouTube for **"storytime vlog long story short"**, **"podcast recap what happened"** or **"vlogger explains the drama"**. Speak along with the last 1–2 minutes of the story three times, no pausing. Copy the falling tone on the wrap-up. Method: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (30 min): Summarising

### Explanation

When you speak without preparing, your answers get long. You add details, go on tangents, correct yourself, guess. All of that is good: it means you're not stopping. But it can leave the listener thinking, "OK… so what's your point?"

A **summary sentence** fixes that. At the end of a long answer, you give the main idea in one short sentence. It does three jobs:

1. It makes a messy answer sound organised.
2. It tells the listener you've finished, so they can speak.
3. It gives *you* a clear way to end, instead of trailing off with "…and yeah… so… that's it".

There are two kinds of summary:

| Kind | What it is | Phrases |
|---|---|---|
| **Summarising yourself** | your own long answer in one sentence | "So basically what I'm saying is…", "Long story short…", "The bottom line is…", "So yeah, in a nutshell…" |
| **Summarising someone else** | their long answer, back to them, to show you understood | "So you're saying…?", "So basically…?", "So the main thing is…?", "Wait, so…?" |

**How to build a summary in 2 seconds:** ask yourself, "If they only remember one thing, what is it?" Say that thing with a summary phrase. Don't add new information. Don't repeat the details. Keep it under 15 words.

**Summarising someone else** is a powerful conversation tool. It shows you were really listening, and it gives you a "free" turn: you don't need a new idea, you just reflect theirs. It's close to [checking understanding from Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/day-5-checking-understanding.md), but there *you* checked that the listener understood you. Here you check that you understood *them*.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So basically what I'm saying is… | you want to wrap up your own long answer | "So basically what I'm saying is, I like the job, but not the commute." |
| So basically… | short, casual version of the above | "So basically, it was a disaster." |
| Long story short… | you want to skip to the end of a story | "Long story short, we got the apartment." |
| The bottom line is… | you want to state the most important fact | "The bottom line is, we can't launch in May." |
| In a nutshell… | you're summarising an idea or a product | "In a nutshell, it helps teams share designs faster." |
| The main thing is… | there were many points but one matters most | "The main thing is, everyone's OK." |
| All in all… / Overall… | you're giving a final judgement | "All in all, a pretty good trip." |
| So yeah. | you've finished and want to hand over the turn | "…and that's why I quit. So yeah." |
| So you're saying…? | you're checking you understood someone | "So you're saying the design's fine, it's the copy?" |
| So, if I get this right…? | the other person's explanation was long or complex | "So, if I get this right, you'd move only if the salary's higher?" |
| Wait, so…? | you're surprised and summarising at the same time | "Wait, so you've never been to the beach here?" |

### Examples

**Mini dialogue 1: summarising yourself after a messy answer**

> **A:** So how's the new apartment?
> **B:** Ugh, it's… OK, so the location's amazing, it's right by the park, and the kitchen's way bigger than my old one. But the walls are super thin, I can hear my neighbor's TV, like, every night, and the landlord takes forever to fix anything. We've had a broken window for two weeks. So basically what I'm saying is, great place, terrible building. So yeah.
> **A:** Haha, that's a pretty common combo.

**Mini dialogue 2: summarising someone else**

> **A:** So we're thinking about Lisbon, 'cause flights are cheap, but Mark's never been to Spain, and Barcelona is kind of on the way, and my sister lives in Madrid, so we could stay with her for free…
> **B:** So, if I get this right, Spain wins 'cause it's free and Mark gets a new country?
> **A:** Exactly. Lisbon's next year.

**Mini dialogue 3: at work**

> **A:** The engineers said the new filters are possible, but they'd need to rebuild the search, which is like six weeks, and they've got the payments thing first, so maybe Q2?
> **B:** So you're saying, not this quarter.
> **A:** Yeah, pretty much.
> **B:** OK. The bottom line is, we need a simpler version for now. Let's sketch one.

**Model answer: a 45-second answer to "What's your job like?" with a summary**

> "So I work in product design, at a company that makes software for hospitals. My day is kind of half meetings, half actual design. In the mornings it's usually calls with product managers and engineers, and in the afternoons I try to block time to work in Figma, but honestly that doesn't always happen. The best part is the research, when we talk to nurses and doctors and see how they actually use our stuff. The worst part is how slow everything moves, 'cause hospitals are super careful. So basically, it's a meaningful job, but you need a lot of patience. So yeah."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ "…and yeah… so… I don't know… that's it." | ✅ "…So basically, it was worth it. So yeah." | Trailing off sounds unsure. A summary gives you a clean end. |
| ❌ "Long story short" + the long story again | ✅ "Long story short, we got there at midnight." | A summary is short. Don't repeat the details. |
| ❌ "So basically what I'm saying is that, in summary, to conclude…" | ✅ "So basically, …" | One summary phrase is enough. "In summary" and "to conclude" are for essays. |
| ❌ "In a nutshell, I mean basically, the bottom line…" | ✅ "The bottom line is, we're late." | Don't stack phrases. Choose one. |
| ❌ "So you are saying that the design is not good." (flat, like an accusation) | ✅ "So you're saying the design needs work?" ↗ | When you summarise someone else, use rising tone and softer words. |
| ❌ A rising end on your own summary: "So basically it was good? ↗" | ✅ "So basically it was good. ↘" | Rising sounds unsure. Let your own summary fall. |

### Practise

Do these in order, out loud:

1. **Exercise 5.1: One-line summaries** (10 min). Read 6 long answers and summarise each in one sentence of under 15 words.
2. **Exercise 5.2: Summarise them back** (10 min). Listen to (read) 6 things a friend says and reply with "So you're saying…?".
3. **Exercise 5.3: The 10-second squeeze** (10 min). Squeeze 8 stories into 10 seconds each. Record them.

All in [Day 5 exercises](exercises.md#day-5-exercises).

## 3. Speaking drill (60 min)

Tonight is the [random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md) with one new rule: **every answer ends with a summary sentence**, with a falling tone. And this week's other rescue moves are all allowed, in fact encouraged: paraphrase, correct yourself, guess, go on a tangent and come back. No preparation, start within 2 seconds of reading the card, record every round.

**Warm-up (5 min):** summarise your day so far in 10 seconds. Then your week in 10 seconds. Then your last job in 10 seconds. Then your favourite film's plot in 10 seconds.

**Random-topic rounds (35 min):** shuffle these 14 cards. Talk for **90 seconds** on each, and finish with a summary phrase. Use a different summary phrase each time if you can. Aim for 12 or more cards.

1. The best decision you made this year
2. Something about your country that foreigners don't understand
3. A design or product you think is badly made
4. What you'd change about your city
5. A time you were completely lost (literally or not)
6. The perfect weekend
7. Why people love or hate social media
8. Something you used to believe but don't anymore
9. The hardest part of learning English
10. A meeting that went badly
11. A friend who changed your life
12. Whether working from home is good for people
13. A place you'd never go back to
14. What makes a good manager

**One 3/2/1 round (15 min):** take the card you found hardest and do the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md): 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute, recorded. Each version ends with the **same summary sentence**. As the time gets shorter, cut details but keep the summary.

**Listen back and count (5 min):** listen to the 1-minute version. Count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md). Also check: did every answer end with a clear summary and a falling tone? Write the numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
