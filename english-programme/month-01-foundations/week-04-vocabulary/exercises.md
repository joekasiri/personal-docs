# Week 4 exercises: Vocabulary for everyday talk
[Week 4 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-phrasal-verbs.md) · [Day 2](day-2-collocations.md) · [Day 3](day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md) · [Day 4](day-4-everyday-idioms.md) · [Day 5](day-5-talking-about-your-job.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** vocabulary only becomes *active* when you say it, so every exercise is **out loud**. Always make sentences about **your own life** when you can — that's what makes words stick. Where it says **record**, record and listen back once. Check the key *after* you've said your answer.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Say it 5 times
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Say each model sentence **5 times**, faster each time. Stress the small word (UP, OUT, INto).
2. Then say **your own sentence** with the same phrasal verb, about your life, 3 times.
3. About 1 minute per phrasal verb.

| # | Phrasal verb | Model sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | end up | We ended up eating at a gas station at midnight. |
| 2 | figure (it) out | The new software's confusing, but I'll figure it out. |
| 3 | run into | I ran into my dentist at the gym. Super awkward. |
| 4 | catch up | We should grab a drink and catch up. |
| 5 | come up | Something came up, so I can't make it tonight. |
| 6 | hang out | We usually hang out at my place on Fridays. |

| 8 | find out | I just found out my landlord's selling the building. |
| 9 | pick (someone) up | Can you pick me up from the airport? |

| 11 | get along | My two roommates don't really get along. |
| 12 | check (it) out | There's a new bakery downstairs. You should check it out. |

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: The scale
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

For each thing, choose a level on the scale and say one sentence about it. Then say a **second** sentence at a **different** level ("Actually, it's not *that* bad…"). Use a different intensifier each time.

**Scale:** not that · a little / kinda · pretty · really / so / super · totally / absolutely + extreme word · way better / way too

1. Your neighborhood
2. The last meal you ate
3. Your commute to work
4. Monday mornings
5. Your favorite app
6. The weather this week
7. Your last vacation
8. Your company's coffee
9. Learning English
10. Your bed

### Exercise 3.2: Soften it
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence is blunt. Say a **softer** version using *not that…, a little bit, kinda, not a big fan of, isn't really my thing, not my favorite, honestly*. Then check the models.

1. The food was bad.
2. I don't like football.
3. Your idea is wrong.
4. The hotel was dirty.
5. This app is ugly.
6. The movie was boring.
7. It's too expensive.
8. I hate karaoke.
9. The meeting was useless.
10. Your music is too loud.
11. The new logo is terrible.
12. I don't want to go to the party.

### Exercise 3.3: Fix the mistakes
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence has one mistake. Say the correct version twice.

1. The concert was very amazing.
2. It's way expensive.
3. The food was very very delicious.
4. I'm not a big fan to sushi.
5. It was absolutely good.
6. The weather is pretty. (meaning: quite good)
7. I was very exhausted.
8. This version is way good.
9. It's a little bit amazing.
10. Hiking isn't really my things.

### Exercise 3.4: 20-second reviews
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, record

Give a **20-second review** of each thing. Include one positive point turned **up**, and one negative point turned **down** (softened). Record numbers 1–5, listen to one, then do 6–10.

1. The last restaurant you went to
2. Your phone
3. The city you live in
4. A movie or show you watched recently
5. Your favorite coffee place
6. The app you use most
7. Your gym (or the last sport you tried)
8. The last hotel or Airbnb you stayed in
9. Your laptop
10. Your company's office

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.1 — two models:**
> *1.* My neighborhood's super quiet. Actually, it's kinda *too* quiet on weekends.
> *3.* My commute is pretty easy — like twenty minutes. But the train is way too crowded in the morning.

**Exercise 3.2 — model answers:**
1. Honestly, the food wasn't that great. 2. I'm not a big fan of football. 3. I'm not sure that's the best idea. / Hmm, I see it a little differently. 4. The hotel was a little bit dirty. / It wasn't the cleanest. 5. The app's not my favorite, design-wise. 6. The movie was kinda slow. / It wasn't that exciting. 7. It's a little bit pricey. / It's kinda expensive. 8. Karaoke isn't really my thing. 9. Honestly, the meeting wasn't super useful. 10. Could you turn it down a little bit? 11. The new logo isn't my favorite. / It's a bit much. 12. I'm not really feeling the party tonight. / I think I'll skip it, honestly.

**Exercise 3.3:**
1. The concert was **totally / absolutely / so** amazing. 2. It's **way too** expensive. / It's **super** expensive. 3. The food was **so / really** delicious. 4. I'm not a big fan **of** sushi. 5. This version is **way better**. 9. It's **totally / absolutely** amazing. *(don't soften an extreme word)* 10. Hiking isn't really my **thing**.

**Exercise 3.4 — model review (1):**

### Exercise 4.1: Match the idiom to the meaning
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

| Idiom | | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. a piece of cake | | a. not decided yet |
| 2. under the weather | | b. stop working for today |
| 3. call it a day | | c. suddenly, with no warning |
| 4. hit the road | | d. very easy |
| 5. on the same page | | e. very late, just before the deadline |

### Exercise 4.2: Gap-fill dialogues
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** write then say

Fill each gap with one of the 12 idioms (drop "at" from "at the last minute" only if the grammar needs it). Then perform each dialogue out loud, both roles.

**Dialogue 1: at work**
> **A:** Before the client call, let's make sure we're all (1) ________.
> **B:** Sure. The budget part is still (2) ________, though.
> **A:** OK, we won't mention it. And the demo?
> **B:** Oh, the demo's (3) ________. We've practised it ten times.
> **A:** Great. It's six already — I think we can (4) ________.

**Dialogue 2: friends**
> **A:** Are you OK? You look tired.
> **B:** I'm a little (5) ________. I think I'm getting a cold.
> **A:** Oh no. Do you still wanna come to the party?
> **B:** I don't know. Can I (6) ________ and tell you tomorrow?
> **A:** Of course. If you can't come, (7) ________.
> **B:** Thanks. Honestly, I always decide (8) ________.

**Dialogue 3: a story**
> **A:** So how did you meet your partner?
> **B:** Totally (9) ________. I was at a wedding and didn't know anyone.
> **A:** Ha! How did you start talking?
> **B:** She told this terrible joke to (10) ________, and I was the only one who laughed.
> **A:** That's so cute. When did you leave?
> **B:** At like 2 a.m. Then we had to (11) ________ — it was a four-hour drive home.
> **A:** Ugh. And now you have a baby! How's that going?
> **B:** Exhausting. **A:** (12) ________. It gets easier!

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Your job in 3 sentences
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Fill in the template below about **your** job. Keep it simple enough for someone outside your field.
2. Say it out loud 5 times, until you don't need to read it.
3. Then say it in **15 seconds** (fast version) and in **45 seconds** (with one extra detail).

**Template:**
1. **Title + where:** "I'm a ________ at ________." / "I work in ________."
2. **Simple explanation:** "Basically, I ________." / "It's kind of like ________."
3. **Something real + ask back:** "Right now I'm working on ________. What about you?"

**Model A (product designer):**
> I'm a product designer at a fintech company. Basically, I figure out how our app should work, so it's super easy to use — it's kind of like being an architect, but for apps. Right now I'm redesigning how people send money. What do you do?

**Model B (same job, for a kid):**
> You know the apps on your mom's phone? I help decide where all the buttons go, so people don't get confused. It's kind of like drawing, but for phones.

### Exercise 5.2: A normal day
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say, record

1. Write a quick timeline of a normal workday — **just keywords**, 6–8 lines (for example: *9:00 team call · 10–12 meetings · lunch at desk · 2–5 design · 3:00 coffee break · 6:00 done*).
2. Turn it into a **1-minute** description out loud, using: *I usually… · The first thing I do is… · Most of my morning is… · I spend a lot of time… · Around [time], I… · I try to take a break… · I usually call it a day around…*
3. Record it. Then do it again without looking at your timeline.

**Model:**
> So I usually start around nine. The first thing I do is check Slack and my email, which honestly takes a while. Then most of my morning is meetings — team calls, chats with developers, that kind of thing. I usually have lunch at my desk, which isn't great, I know. In the afternoon I actually get to design, which is the best part. I try to take a break around three and grab a coffee. And I usually call it a day around six, unless something comes up.

### Exercise 5.3: The recent-problem story
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say, record

1. Fill in the 4 boxes for a **real** recent problem at work — keywords only.
2. Tell it out loud in **1 minute**, using at least one phrasal verb and one collocation in each box.
3. Record it. Ask yourself: would someone outside your field understand it? If not, simplify one part and tell it again.

| Box | Your keywords | Useful chunks |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting** | | "Recently we were working on…" / "So last month…" |
| **Problem** | | "We had this problem where…" / "The thing is…" |
| **Turning point** | | "We found out that…" / "Turns out…" / "So we decided to…" |
| **Ending** | | "In the end, we figured it out…" / "Now it's way better." / "Lesson learned:…" |

**Model:**
> So last month we were working on a new sign-up screen. We had this problem where a ton of people started signing up and then just… left, halfway through. The thing is, we had no idea why. So we had a few calls with real users, and we found out that one question — "What's your company size?" — was confusing everyone. Turns out half of them were freelancers. In the end, we made the question optional, and way more people finished signing up. Lesson learned: always talk to users first.
