# Week 4: Vocabulary for everyday talk
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 1: Foundations](../README.md) · [← Week 3](../week-03-small-talk/README.md) · [Week 5 →](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/README.md)

## This week's goal

You know a lot of English words. But in casual American conversation, people don't say "I discovered", "I encountered", "I postponed" or "I am feeling unwell". They say **"I found out"**, **"I ran into"**, **"I put it off"**, **"I'm a little under the weather"**. This week you build the **everyday vocabulary layer** that makes you sound natural instead of like a textbook: the most common **phrasal verbs**, the **make/do/take/have/get collocations** that natives never get wrong, **intensifiers and softeners** ("pretty good", "kinda", "not that great"), a set of **everyday idioms**, and the simple language for **talking about your job**. Shadowing all week focuses on the many ways Americans "lose" the T sound: **button → "bu'n"**, **can't → "can'"**, **internet → "inner-net"**, **twenty → "twenny"**. On Day 6 you do your weekly check **and your first monthly check** against your baseline.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 3](../week-03-small-talk/README.md) you learned the structure of small talk: [openers](../week-03-small-talk/day-1-conversation-openers.md), [echoes](../week-03-small-talk/day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md), [answer-add-ask](../week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md), [time-buyers](../week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) and [endings](../week-03-small-talk/day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md). This week fills that structure with natural words. Every "add" in your answer-add-ask, every story from [Week 1](../week-01-storytelling/README.md), every answer to a [Week 2 question](../week-02-questions/README.md) gets better with the chunks you learn this week. Every 4/3/2 story still ends with a question to the listener.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Go for a walk and listen to a casual podcast or vlog where someone talks about their **work life** — how they got their job, a bad day, a funny colleague (search "talking about my job podcast", "work stories podcast", "day in the life chatty vlog").

Your listening task:
1. Catch **5 phrasal verbs** (end up, figure out, come up…). Say each sentence quietly after the speaker.
2. Catch **2 idioms** or fixed expressions you didn't know. Log them tomorrow.
3. Notice the T: when does the speaker say a clear T, a soft "d", or no T at all?

Then stop and rest. You've finished Month 1. Next week, [Month 2](../../month-02-speed/README.md) makes everything faster.
