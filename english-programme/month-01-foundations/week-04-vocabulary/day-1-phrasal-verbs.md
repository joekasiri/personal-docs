## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: the glottal T before N

When a T comes **right before an "n" sound** (often spelled -tten, -tton, -tain, -tn't), Americans don't say a T at all. They **stop the air in the throat** for a split second — like the tiny stop in the middle of "uh-oh" — and then say the N through the nose. This is called a **glottal stop**. We'll write it as an apostrophe: **'**.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| button | "BUH'n" |
| written | "RIH'n" |
| certain | "SER'n" |
| important | "im-POR'nt" |
| gotten | "GAH'n" |
| mountain | "MOWN'n" |
| didn't | "DIH'n" |
| Manhattan | "man-HA'n" |

Don't say "BUT-TON" with a clear T, and don't say "buddon" with a flap D ([Week 1 Day 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md#1-shadowing-20-min) — the flap is for T between vowels, like "water"). Before N, it's the throat stop.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. Press the BUTton. → "Press the BUH'n."
2. I'm not CERtain. → "I'm not SER'n."
3. It's really imPORtant. → "It's really im-POR'nt."
4. I've GOTten so much better. → "I've GAH'n so much better."
5. She didn't CALL. → "She DIH'n CALL."
6. It's WRITten on the board. → "It's RIH'n on the board."
7. We hiked up the MOUNtain. → "We hiked up the MOWN'n."
8. I've FORgotten his name. → "I've fer-GAH'n his name."
9. The kitten is so CUTE. → "The KIH'n is so CUTE."
10. I wouldn't do THAT. → "I WOO'n do THAT."
11. Can you close the CURtains? → "C'n ya close the CUR'ns?"
12. It's a CERtain kind of PERson. → "It's a SER'n kinda PERson."

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Phrasal verbs

### Explanation

A **phrasal verb** is a verb + a small word (up, out, into, off, along…) that together have a new meaning. "Run" means run. But **"run into"** someone means *meet them by chance*. "Figure" is a number. But **"figure out"** means *understand or solve*.

**Why they matter:** in casual American English, phrasal verbs are **everywhere**. Formal verbs (*discover, encounter, postpone, tolerate*) sound like a report. Phrasal verbs sound like a person.

| Formal (sounds like writing) | Casual (sounds like a person) |
|---|---|
| I discovered that… | I **found out** that… |
| I encountered my old boss. | I **ran into** my old boss. |
| We postponed the meeting. | We **put off** the meeting. |
| I finally understood it. | I finally **figured it out**. |
| A problem arose. | Something **came up**. |

**Separable or not?** Some phrasal verbs can be split by the object; some can't. You don't need to learn rules — learn each one as a chunk with **"it"** or **"someone"** in the right place:

- **Separable** — the pronoun goes **in the middle**: *figure **it** out · pick **you** up · put **it** off · check **it** out*. (Not "figure out it".)
- **Not separable** — the pronoun goes **after**: *run into **her** · get along with **him** · catch up with **them**.*
- **No object:** *end up · show up · come up · hang out.*

**Stress:** the small word is usually **stressed**: *figure it OUT · I ran INto him · she showed UP late*.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| end up (+ -ing / + place) | something happens that you didn't plan | "We ended up staying till 2 a.m." |
| figure (it) out | you understand or solve something after thinking | "I couldn't figure out the new software, so I asked Sam." |
| run into (someone) | you meet someone by chance | "I ran into my old roommate at the airport." |
| catch up (with someone) | you talk to someone about what's new after some time | "Let's grab lunch and catch up." |
| come up | something unexpected happens, or a topic appears | "Sorry, something came up — can we do Thursday?" |
| hang out | you spend relaxed time with friends | "We just hung out at her place and watched movies." |
| show up | you arrive (often late or unexpectedly) | "He showed up forty minutes late." |
| find out | you discover information | "I just found out she's leaving the company." |
| pick (someone/something) up | you collect someone/something; or learn something casually | "Can you pick me up at eight?" / "I picked up some Spanish in Mexico." |
| put (it) off | you delay something | "I keep putting off my dentist appointment." |
| get along (with someone) | you have a good relationship | "I get along really well with my team." |
| check (it) out | you look at something interesting, or visit a place | "You gotta check out this new café." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: catching up**

> **A:** Hey! We never got to catch up last week. What happened?
> **B:** Ugh, sorry. Something came up at work and I ended up staying till nine.
> **A:** That's rough. Did you figure it out?
> **B:** Kind of. Turns out the whole problem was one setting.
> **A:** Ha, classic. Oh, guess who I ran into yesterday? Maya!
> **B:** No way! How's she doing?
> **A:** Good! She's moving to Austin. I just found out.

**Dialogue 2: weekend plans**

> **A:** What are you up to this weekend?
> **B:** Not much. Probably just hanging out at home. I keep putting off cleaning my apartment.
> **A:** Ha, same. Hey, wanna check out that new food market on Saturday?
> **B:** Sure! Can you pick me up?
> **A:** Yeah, I'll pick you up at eleven.
> **B:** Perfect. My roommate might come too — you guys get along, right?
> **A:** Totally.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "I figured out it." | ✅ "I **figured it out**." — *with a pronoun, it goes in the middle.* |
| ❌ "I ran him into at the store." | ✅ "I **ran into him** at the store." — *"run into" doesn't split.* |
| ❌ "We ended up to stay home." | ✅ "We **ended up staying** home." — *"end up" + -ing.* |
| ❌ "I discovered that the store was closed." (in casual talk) | ✅ "I **found out** the store was closed." — *more natural in conversation.* |
| ❌ "He appeared at the party." | ✅ "He **showed up** at the party." — *"appear" sounds like a magic trick or a film.* |
| ❌ "I get along good with my boss." | ✅ "I get along **well** with my boss." / "We get along **great**." |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — you ended up somewhere unplanned (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a time you ended up somewhere you didn't plan to be."**
> You missed your stop, took the wrong train, followed a friend to a random party, got stuck at an airport, said yes to a last-minute trip. How did it start? What came up? Who did you run into? How did you get home in the end?

- Use at least **5 phrasal verbs** from tonight. Good ones for this story: *end up, come up, run into, figure out, show up, find out*.
- Use the [4 boxes](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md) and 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- End every round with **a question to the listener**: *"Have you ever ended up somewhere totally random?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3, **and** count your phrasal verbs.

### Part B: Upgrade an old story (15 min)

Take your Week 1 **work story** ([Day 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md)). Tell it again in **3 minutes**, replacing every formal verb you can with a phrasal verb: *found out*, *figured it out*, *came up*, *ended up*, *put it off*. Record it. Then tell it in **2 minutes**.

### Part C: Phrasal verb questions (10 min)

Ask yourself these questions out loud, and answer each for about 1 minute. End each answer with an ask-back ([Week 3 Day 3](../week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md)).

1. Who do you get along with best at work?
2. What's something you keep putting off?
3. Where do you usually hang out with friends?
4. What's something you found out recently that surprised you?
5. Who's the last person you ran into by chance?
