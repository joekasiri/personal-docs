## Learning objectives

- Drop the T in **-st, -ft, -ct** clusters before a consonant: "just now" → "jus' now", "last night" → "las' night".
- Understand and use 12 everyday idioms that Americans really use in casual talk.
- Use idioms in the right situation — and not too many at once.
- Keep idioms in their **fixed form** (you can't change the words).
- Use all 12 in a 30-minute voice chat with Claude.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: the dropped T in -st, -ft, -ct

When a T is **squeezed between two consonants** — usually at the end of a word ending in **-st, -ft, -ct** before another consonant — Americans just **skip it**.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| just now | "JUS' now" |
| last night | "LAS' night" |
| first time | "FIRS' time" |
| next week | "NEX' week" |
| must be | "MUS' be" |
| left side | "LEF' side" |
| exactly | "eg-ZAC-ly" |
| perfect timing | "PER-fec' TIMing" |

Before a **vowel**, the T often comes back, linked: "just a minute" → "jus-ta minute", "first of all" → "firs-ta-vall".

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. I just GOT here. → "I jus' GOT here."
2. Last night was CRAzy. → "Las' night was CRAzy."
3. It was my first TIME. → "It was my firs' TIME."
4. See you next WEEK. → "See ya nex' WEEK."
5. You must be TIRED. → "You mus' be TIRED."
6. It's on the left SIDE. → "It's on the lef' SIDE."
7. That's exactly what I MEANT. → "That's eg-ZAC-ly what I MEANT."
8. Perfect TIMing! → "PER-fec' TIMing!"
9. Let's call it a DAY. → "Le's call ida DAY." (flap T + linking)
10. It was the last MINute. → "It was the las' MINute."
11. Just a SEC. → "Jus-ta SEC." (vowel after → T comes back)
12. It's not rocket SCIence. → "It's no' rocke' SCIence." (held T from [Day 2](day-2-collocations.md#1-shadowing-20-min))

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"chatty vlog my week"**, **"Sunday reset vlog talking"**, **"two friends podcast chat"**. Casual vloggers say "just", "last", "next", "first" all the time.
- Every time you hear a missing T in *just / last / next / first / must*, pause and repeat.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Everyday idioms

### Explanation

An **idiom** is a fixed phrase whose meaning you can't guess from the words. "It's a piece of cake" has nothing to do with cake — it means *it's very easy*.

Tonight's 12 idioms are chosen because Americans use them **in normal, casual conversation**, at work and with friends. They're not old-fashioned, and they're not slang that will sound strange from you.

**Three rules for using idioms well:**

1. **Don't change the words.** It's "a piece of cake", not "a part of cake" or "a piece of a cake". Idioms are fixed chunks.
2. **Don't overdo it.** One idiom in a few minutes sounds natural. Five in one sentence sounds like a textbook — or a joke.
3. **Know the situation.** "Under the weather" is for being a little sick, not seriously ill. "Hang in there" is for someone having a hard time, not for a tragedy.

### Key phrases

| Idiom | Meaning / use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a piece of cake | something is very easy | "The test? Piece of cake." |
| under the weather | you're a little sick | "I'm feeling kinda under the weather, so I'm staying home." |
| call it a day | you stop working for today | "We've been at this for ten hours. Let's call it a day." |
| hit the road | you leave (start a journey or go home) | "It's getting late, I should hit the road." |
| on the same page | people agree / understand the same thing | "Before we start, let's make sure we're all on the same page." |
| out of the blue | suddenly, unexpectedly | "Out of the blue, my old boss called me with a job offer." |
| up in the air | not decided yet | "Our vacation plans are still up in the air." |
| sleep on it | you wait until tomorrow to decide | "I don't know. Let me sleep on it." |
| no big deal | it's not important, don't worry | "You forgot? No big deal." |
| (at) the last minute | very late, just before the deadline | "He always finishes his slides at the last minute." |
| break the ice | you make people feel relaxed at the start | "I told a stupid joke to break the ice." |
| hang in there | encouragement for someone having a hard time | "Ugh, that week sounds awful. Hang in there." |

**Note:** "out of the blue" is close to the Week 1 connector "and then out of nowhere" ([Day 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md)). Both mean "suddenly". Use "out of nowhere" for sudden events in stories, and "out of the blue" especially for a surprise message, call or idea.

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: end of a long day at work**

> **A:** Are we on the same page about the new flow?
> **B:** I think so. The login stuff is still up in the air, though.
> **A:** Yeah, let's sleep on it and talk tomorrow.
> **B:** Good idea. Honestly, I'm kinda under the weather.
> **A:** Oh no. Then let's call it a day. Go home and rest.
> **B:** Thanks. I'm gonna hit the road. Hang in there with the client!

**Dialogue 2: catching up with a friend**

> **A:** So how did you end up at the new company?
> **B:** Totally out of the blue. A recruiter messaged me on a Friday night.
> **A:** No way! Was the interview hard?
> **B:** Honestly? Piece of cake. They just wanted to chat.
> **A:** Nice. And the first day?
> **B:** Kinda awkward. But my manager told a really bad joke to break the ice, and everyone laughed.
> **A:** Ha! And I'm sorry I canceled dinner at the last minute last week.
> **B:** Oh, no big deal!

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "It's a piece of a cake." | ✅ "It's **a piece of cake**." — *fixed words — don't add or change anything.* |
| ❌ "I'm under the weather" (about a serious illness) | ✅ "I'm **under the weather**" = a cold, feeling a bit sick — *for something serious, just say it plainly.* |
| ❌ "Let's call it a night" at 10 a.m. | ✅ "Let's **call it a day**." — *"call it a night" is for the evening.* |
| ❌ "We are on the same page?" (as a question) | ✅ "**Are we on the same page?**" — *normal [question word order](../week-02-questions/day-1-direct-questions.md).* |
| ❌ Five idioms in one minute | ✅ One or two idioms in a conversation — *too many sounds unnatural.* |
| ❌ "Out of the blue sky, he called." | ✅ "**Out of the blue**, he called." |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): Voice mode with Claude

Setup is in [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

### Steps

1. **Setup (3 min):** new chat, paste the prompt below, switch to voice mode, start recording on your phone.
2. **Chat (30 min):** a casual catch-up with Claude playing your friend Sam. Use **all 12 idioms** — tick each one off on paper when you use it. Also use phrasal verbs, collocations and softeners from this week.
3. **Feedback (7 min):** say "feedback". Write down every correction, and which idioms Claude thought you used wrongly or unnaturally.
4. **Repeat (5 min):** say every corrected sentence 3 times.

### Prompt to paste

```
I'm practising casual spoken American English. My level is B2 and my goal is to speak fast and relaxed without thinking.

Please:
- Talk to me like a friendly American friend, casually, using contractions and everyday phrases.
- Keep your turns short (1–3 sentences) so I do most of the talking.
- Ask me follow-up questions so I have to keep going.
- Don't correct me during the conversation. Just remember my mistakes.
- When I say "feedback", stop and give me: my 5 most important mistakes with the correct version, 3 phrases I could have used to sound more natural, and one thing I did well.

Tonight's topic and focus:
Everyday idioms. You're my friend Sam, and we're catching up over coffee after a few weeks. Ask me about work, my week, my plans and any news. Create situations where I can naturally use these 12 idioms: a piece of cake, under the weather, call it a day, hit the road, on the same page, out of the blue, up in the air, sleep on it, no big deal, at the last minute, break the ice, hang in there. Use one or two of them yourself too, naturally. Also, this week I'm practising phrasal verbs (end up, figure out, run into, catch up), make/do/take/have/get collocations, and softeners (kinda, pretty, not that great). In your feedback, tell me which idioms I used naturally, which I used in the wrong situation, and which I didn't use at all.
```

### What to focus on

- **Use all 12 idioms**, but spread them out over the 30 minutes — about one every 2–3 minutes.
- Keep using [answer, add, ask back](../week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md) and [time-buyers](../week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md).
- When Claude tells you news, use an [echo question](../week-03-small-talk/day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md).
- End the chat yourself with a [3-step ending](../week-03-small-talk/day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md) — and maybe "I should hit the road".
