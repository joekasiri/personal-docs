## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: the "held" T before a consonant

When a word **ends in T** and the **next word starts with a consonant**, Americans usually don't release the T. The tongue goes to the T position (or the throat stops, like yesterday) — but **no puff of air** comes out. It sounds like the T is missing, or "swallowed".

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| can't go | "CAN' go" |
| that guy | "THA' guy" |
| get back | "GE' back" |
| what time | "WHA' time" |
| right now | "RI' now" |
| not bad | "NO' bad" |
| it's not that big | "it's NO' tha' big" |

Compare with [Week 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md#1-shadowing-20-min): before a **vowel**, the T becomes a flap ("ge**d** it"). Before a **consonant**, it's held ("ge**'** back").

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. I CAN'T go. → "I CAN' go."
2. Who's that GUY? → "Who's tha' GUY?"
3. I'll get BACK to you. → "I'll ge' BACK ta ya."
4. What TIME is it? → "Wha' TIME is it?"
5. I'm busy right NOW. → "I'm busy ri' NOW."
6. Not BAD! → "No' BAD!"
7. It's not that BIG a deal. → "It's no' tha' BIG a deal."
8. Let me take a BREAK. → "Le' me take a BREAK."
9. I made a great deCIsion. → "I made a grea' deCIsion."
10. That's not FAIR! → "Tha's no' FAIR!"
11. Can you get me a COFfee? → "C'n ya ge' me a COFfee?"
12. That was a lot of FUN. → "Tha' was a lodda FUN."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"chatty vlog talking about my week"**, **"what I did this weekend vlog"**, **"grwm chatty storytime"**.
- Listen for *can't, that, get, what, right, not, it* before consonants. Do you hear the T? (Usually not!)
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Collocations — make, do, take, have, get

### Explanation

A **collocation** is a pair of words that natives always put together. There's often no logic — it's just how English is. You **make** a decision (not *do*), **take** a picture (not *make*), **have** a chat. If you use the "wrong" verb, people understand you, but you sound translated. If you use the right one, you sound natural, and — more important for you — you don't have to **think** about which verb to use.

So learn them as **chunks**: not "decision", but "**make a decision**".

There are some loose patterns that help:

| Verb | Rough idea | Common chunks |
|---|---|---|
| **make** | you create or produce something | make a decision · make a mistake · make plans · make money · make sense · make friends · make a reservation · make dinner · make a call · make it (= arrive / succeed) |
| **do** | work, tasks, activities (often not creating something new) | do a favor · do the dishes · do laundry · do your best · do research · do a project · do business · do well/badly · do something fun |
| **take** | time, transport, actions you "accept" or "grab" | take a break · take a picture · take a while · take your time · take a shower · take the bus · take notes · take a risk · take a look · take it easy |
| **have** | experiences, meals, conversations | have a chat · have fun · have a good time · have lunch · have a meeting · have a problem · have a look · have a baby · have an idea |
| **get** | receive, become, arrive (very common in American English) | get ready · get lost · get home · get better · get the job · get a coffee · get tired · get married · get it (= understand) · get going (= leave) |

**American note:** Americans say **take a shower**, **take a look** and **take a break** a lot more than **have** a shower/look/break (more British). Use "take" in your American English.

**Three that change meaning:**
- **make it** = arrive in time / succeed: *"Did you make it to the airport?"*
- **get it** = understand: *"Oh, I get it now."*
- **take it easy** = relax: *"Take it easy this weekend."*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| make a decision | you choose between options | "I need to make a decision by Friday." |
| make a mistake | you do something wrong | "I made a huge mistake in the email." |
| make plans | you arrange something with people | "Let's make plans for next weekend." |
| make sense | something is logical | "That totally makes sense." |
| do (someone) a favor | you help someone, or ask for help | "Can you do me a favor and send me the file?" |
| do your best | you try as hard as you can | "Don't worry, just do your best." |
| take a break | you stop for a short rest | "Let's take a ten-minute break." |
| take a while | something needs a lot of time | "It took a while, but we fixed it." |
| take a risk | you do something that might go wrong | "Moving cities was a big risk, but I took it." |
| have a chat | you talk informally | "Can we have a quick chat after lunch?" |
| have fun / have a good time | you enjoy yourself | "We had so much fun at the wedding." |
| get ready | you prepare (to go out) | "Give me five minutes to get ready." |
| get lost | you don't know where you are | "We got lost on the way to the hotel." |
| make it | you arrive in time / succeed | "We made it just before the doors closed." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: making plans**

> **A:** Hey, are you making plans for the long weekend?
> **B:** Not yet. I need to make a decision — beach or mountains.
> **A:** Take your time. Honestly, either way you'll have fun.
> **B:** True. Can you do me a favor and send me that cabin link?
> **A:** Sure. Oh, it takes a while to get there, though. Like four hours.
> **B:** That's fine. As long as we make it before dark.

**Dialogue 2: a day at work**

> **A:** You look exhausted.
> **B:** I am. I made a mistake in the client deck and had to redo half of it.
> **A:** Oh no. Did you get it done?
> **B:** Yeah, finally. It took a while, though.
> **A:** You should take a break. Wanna get a coffee?
> **B:** Yes, please. Let's have a quick chat about Friday too.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "I did a decision." | ✅ "I **made** a decision." |
| ❌ "I made a photo of the sunset." | ✅ "I **took** a picture of the sunset." |
| ❌ "We made a party for her birthday." | ✅ "We **threw** a party / **had** a party for her birthday." |
| ❌ "I did a mistake." | ✅ "I **made** a mistake." |
| ❌ "Can you make me a favor?" | ✅ "Can you **do** me a favor?" |
| ❌ "I make sport every morning." | ✅ "I **work out** / **play sports** every morning." — *"make sport" is a translation from other languages.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a big decision (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a big decision you made — and what made you take the risk."**
> A new job, a move, buying something expensive, ending something, starting something. How long did it take you to make the decision? Who did you have a chat with about it? Did you make any mistakes? Did it make sense in the end?

- Use at least **6 collocations** from tonight (*make a decision, take a risk, take a while, have a chat, make a mistake, make sense, get used to…*).
- Use at least **2 phrasal verbs** from [Day 1](day-1-phrasal-verbs.md) (*figure out, end up…*).
- End every round with **a question to the listener**: *"What's the biggest risk you've ever taken?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Make / do / take / have / get — one minute each (15 min)

Talk for **1 minute** on each topic below, using **as many chunks with that verb** as you can. Record it. Count the chunks after each one.

1. **make** — "Your perfect Sunday" (make breakfast, make plans, make a call…)
2. **do** — "Your chores and weekly routine" (do laundry, do the dishes, do the shopping…)
3. **take** — "Your last vacation" (take a flight, take pictures, take it easy…)
4. **have** — "Your last birthday" (have a party, have fun, have dinner…)
5. **get** — "Your morning routine" (get up, get ready, get a coffee, get to work…)

Then do one more minute mixing **all five**: "Your last week at work."

### Part C: Quick fix (10 min)

Listen to Part B. Write down any collocation you weren't sure about. Check it in tonight's table or the key in [Exercise 2.1](exercises.md#day-2-exercises), and say the correct chunk 5 times.
