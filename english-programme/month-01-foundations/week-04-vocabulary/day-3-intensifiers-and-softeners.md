# Week 4 · Day 3: Intensifiers and softeners

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- In [Week 1 Day 5](../week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md) you used a few intensifiers to describe places ("super crowded", "kinda sketchy"). Tonight you learn the full scale — how to turn any adjective **up** or **down** — and how to soften negative opinions so you sound friendly, not rude.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, a review-style or opinion vlog clip (see section 1).
- Think of a restaurant, café, app or product you have strong feelings about (for the drill).

## Learning objectives

- Say **NT → N** like Americans: internet → "inner-net", twenty → "twenny".
- Use the intensity scale: *not that good → kinda good → pretty good → really good → super good → amazing*.
- Use **totally / absolutely** with extreme words ("totally amazing") — never "very amazing".
- Soften negative opinions: "It's not that great", "I'm not a big fan of…", "It's a little bit expensive".
- Review a place or product in a 4/3/2 with 8+ intensifiers and softeners.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: NT → N

When **NT** comes **between two vowel sounds** (often in an unstressed syllable), many Americans drop the T completely. Only the N is left.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| internet | "IN-er-net" |
| twenty | "TWEN-ee" |
| wanted | "WAN-ed" |
| center | "SEN-er" |
| interview | "IN-er-view" |
| plenty | "PLEN-ee" |
| printer | "PRIN-er" |
| advantage | "ad-VAN-ij" |

The T stays when the syllable after it is **stressed**: con**TAIN**, in**TEN**se. And it's never dropped at the very start of a word.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. The INternet is so SLOW. → "The IN-er-net is so SLOW."
2. It was like TWENty dollars. → "It was like TWEN-ee dollars."
3. I WANted to GO. → "I WAN-ed ta GO."
4. It's in the CENter of town. → "It's in the SEN-er a town."
5. I have an INterview toMORrow. → "I have an IN-er-view t'MORrow."
6. There's PLENty of FOOD. → "There's PLEN-ee a FOOD."
7. The PRINter's BROken again. → "The PRIN-er's BROken again."
8. That's a big adVANtage. → "That's a big ad-VAN-ij."
9. It's pretty INteresting. → "It's priddy IN-er-esting." (NT → N, and the flap T in "pretty"!)
10. I'm TOtally into it. → "I'm TOdally IN-to it."
11. She's like TWENty-five. → "She's like TWEN-ee-five."
12. It was kinda inTENSE. → (T stays: stressed syllable) "It was kinda in-TENSE."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"honest restaurant review vlog"**, **"things I'm loving right now"**, **"rating fast food chains honest"**. Reviewers use tons of intensifiers and softeners — and words like "twenty", "plenty", "internet".
- Shadow the **opinion sentences** — copy how they stretch "SOOO good" and soften "it's… not great".
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Intensifiers and softeners

### Explanation

Opinions are a huge part of casual talk: "How was the movie?", "Do you like your new apartment?", "What did you think of the restaurant?". Americans almost never answer with a bare adjective ("It was good."). They **turn it up or down**:

**The intensity scale** (from weakest to strongest):

| Level | Words | Example: "good" |
|---|---|---|
| very weak / negative | not that · not very · not super | It was **not that** good. |
| weak | a little · a bit · kinda · sort of | It was **kinda** good. |
| medium | pretty · fairly | It was **pretty** good. |
| strong | really · so · super | It was **really** good. / It was **so** good. / It was **super** good. |
| very strong | way (+ comparative) · totally · absolutely + extreme word | It was **way** better than I expected. / It was **totally amazing**. |

**Important — "pretty" doesn't mean "beautiful" here.** "Pretty good" = quite good, better than OK. It's one of the most common phrases in American English.

**Extreme adjectives** already mean "very": *amazing, awesome, terrible, huge, tiny, exhausted, starving, freezing, perfect, impossible*. Don't use "very" with them. Use **totally, absolutely, really** or **so**:
> ❌ very amazing · ✅ **totally** amazing · ✅ **absolutely** perfect · ✅ **so** exhausted

**"Way"** is very American and very casual: *way better · way too expensive · way more fun*. It only goes with comparatives (better, more…) or "too".

**Softeners: being negative without being rude.** Americans soften negative opinions a lot. Saying "It's bad" or "I don't like it" can sound harsh. Instead:

| Harsh | Soft |
|---|---|
| It's bad. | It's **not that great**. / It's **not the best**. |
| I don't like jazz. | I'm **not a big fan of** jazz. / Jazz **isn't really my thing**. |
| It's expensive. | It's **a little bit** expensive. / It's **kinda pricey**. |
| He's boring. | He's **not the most exciting** guy. |
| The design is ugly. | The design is **not my favorite**. / It's **a bit much**. |

**Honestly** is also useful here — it softens and prepares the listener: *"Honestly? It was kinda disappointing."*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pretty (+ adjective) | it's quite good/bad, better than OK | "The food was pretty good, actually." |
| super (+ adjective) | very, casual | "The staff were super friendly." |
| so (+ adjective) | very, often with feeling | "It was so good. I'm going back tomorrow." |
| kinda / kind of | a little, not completely | "It was kinda weird, but fun." |
| a little (bit) | softens something negative | "It's a little bit loud in there." |
| totally / absolutely (+ extreme word) | very strong | "The view was totally amazing." |
| way better / way too… | much better / much too | "The new version is way better." "It's way too sweet." |
| not that (+ adjective) | softens a negative | "The movie wasn't that good." |
| I'm not a big fan of… | you don't like something, politely | "I'm not a big fan of horror movies." |
| …isn't really my thing | you don't enjoy it | "Clubbing isn't really my thing." |
| kinda pricey | a bit expensive | "It's great, but kinda pricey." |
| It's not bad! | it's OK or better (often positive!) | "Hey, this coffee's not bad!" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a new restaurant**

> **A:** So how was that new Thai place?
> **B:** Honestly? Pretty good. The curry was so good.
> **A:** Nice! What about the service?
> **B:** Super friendly. But it was a little bit slow.
> **A:** Was it expensive?
> **B:** Kinda pricey. Like twenty bucks for noodles. But way better than the place on Main Street.

**Dialogue 2: giving feedback at work (softly)**

> **A:** What do you think of the new homepage?
> **B:** I mean, the colors are really nice. The layout's not bad.
> **A:** But…?
> **B:** Honestly, the font isn't my favorite. It's a bit hard to read on mobile.
> **A:** Yeah, that makes sense.
> **B:** But overall it's way cleaner than the old one. Totally.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "The trip was very amazing." | ✅ "The trip was **totally amazing**." / "**so** amazing." — *no "very" with extreme words.* |
| ❌ "It was very very good." | ✅ "It was **so good**." / "**super good**." — *one strong word is enough.* |
| ❌ "The food is bad." (to the person who cooked) | ✅ "It's **not really my thing**, but…" — *soften negative opinions.* |
| ❌ "It's way expensive." | ✅ "It's **way too** expensive." / "It's **super** expensive." — *"way" needs "too" or a comparative.* |
| ❌ "I'm not a big fan to jazz." | ✅ "I'm not a big fan **of** jazz." |
| ❌ "It's pretty." (meaning "pretty good") | ✅ "It's **pretty good**." — *"pretty" alone = beautiful.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a review (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Review a restaurant, café, app or product you have strong feelings about — love it or hate it."**
> What is it? How did you find out about it? What's great about it? What's not so great? Would you recommend it? Tell a short story about one time you used it or went there.

- Use at least **8 intensifiers and softeners** (pretty, super, so, kinda, totally, way better, not that…, not a big fan of…).
- Include **both** positive and negative points — and soften the negative ones.
- Use 2+ [collocations](day-2-collocations.md) and 2+ [phrasal verbs](day-1-phrasal-verbs.md) (*check it out, find out, have a good time, take a while…*).
- End every round with **a question to the listener**: *"Have you been? / Have you tried it?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Turn it up, turn it down (15 min)

Describe each of these **twice** for 30 seconds: first as if you **love** it (turn it up: so, super, totally, way better), then as if you **don't** like it but you're being polite (turn it down: not that…, a little bit, not my thing). Record it.

1. Your city's public transport.
2. The last movie or show you watched.
3. Working from home.
4. Your phone.
5. Spicy food.
6. Your job.

### Part C: Intensity check (10 min)

Listen to round 3 from Part A. Write down every intensifier and softener you used. Did you use the **same one** too many times (for example "really, really, really")? Choose 2 different ones and tell round 3 again in 2 minutes using them.
