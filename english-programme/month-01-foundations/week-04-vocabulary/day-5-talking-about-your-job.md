**How to find the clip:**
- One American speaker talking casually about their **work day** — about 2 minutes, or a 2-minute part of a longer video.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Talking about your job

### Explanation

When people ask about your job in small talk, they don't want your job description. They want a **simple picture**: what you do, maybe who for, and something interesting. Three common mistakes make this hard:

- **Too technical**: "I do UX for a B2B SaaS platform with a focus on design systems." Most people get lost.
- **Too short**: "I'm a designer." — and silence.
- **Too long**: five minutes of detail before they've asked for it.

Use this **3-part answer**, about 20–30 seconds:

| Part | What you say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Title + where** | "I'm a…" / "I work as a… at…" / "I work in…" | "I'm a product designer at a fintech company." |
| **2. Simple explanation** | What that means, for someone outside your field. "Basically, I…" | "Basically, I figure out how the app should work, so it's easy for people to use." |
| **3. Something real** | What you're working on, or what you like. Then ask back. | "Right now I'm redesigning how people send money. What about you?" |

**For outsiders, compare to something everyday:** *"It's kind of like being an architect, but for apps."*

**A normal day** — use time phrases and the present simple for habits:
> *"I usually start at nine. The first thing I do is check messages. Most of the morning is meetings, and in the afternoon I actually get to design. I try to take a break around three. And I usually call it a day around six."*

**A recent problem** — use the [4 boxes](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md), past tenses ([Week 1 Day 4](../week-01-storytelling/day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md)) and this week's vocabulary:
> *"Recently we had this problem where users couldn't find the 'pay' button. Turns out it was hidden on smaller phones. So we ran some quick tests, figured out the issue, and moved it. Now payments are up like twenty percent."*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I'm a… at… / I work as a… | you give your job title | "I'm a product designer at a small startup." |
| I work in… | you give your field | "I work in tech, on the design side." |
| Basically, I… | you explain simply | "Basically, I make sure the app's easy to use." |
| It's kind of like… | you compare to something everyday | "It's kind of like being an architect, but for websites." |
| My job is mostly about… | you give the main part | "My job's mostly about talking to users and sketching ideas." |
| On a normal day, I… / I usually… | you describe your routine | "On a normal day, I start with a team call." |
| Most of my day is… | you say how you spend your time | "Most of my day is meetings, honestly." |
| I spend a lot of time… | you describe a big part of the job | "I spend a lot of time testing ideas with users." |
| The best part is… / The hardest part is… | you give your feelings about it | "The best part is seeing people actually use what I made." |
| Right now I'm working on… | you say your current project | "Right now I'm working on a new sign-up flow." |
| Recently we had this problem where… | you start a problem story | "Recently we had this problem where the app kept crashing." |
| So we ended up… / We figured out… | you explain the solution | "So we ended up rebuilding the whole screen." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: at a party**

> **A:** So what do you do?
> **B:** I'm a product designer at a fintech company.
> **A:** Oh, cool. What does that mean, exactly?
> **B:** Basically, I figure out how the app should work, so it's super easy to use. It's kind of like being an architect, but for apps.
> **A:** Oh, I get it. Do you like it?
> **B:** Yeah, I do! The best part is watching people use something I designed. What about you, what do you do?

**Dialogue 2: a colleague from another team**

> **A:** What's a normal day like for you?
> **B:** Honestly? Pretty busy. I usually start with a team call at nine. Most of the morning is meetings, and then in the afternoon I actually get to design.
> **A:** What are you working on right now?
> **B:** A new payment screen. Recently we had this problem where people couldn't find the "pay" button.
> **A:** Seriously? How come?
> **B:** Turns out it was hidden on smaller phones. Took a while to figure it out, but we moved it, and now it's way better.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "I work like a designer." | ✅ "I work **as** a designer." — *"as" = your role; "like" = similar to.* |
| ❌ "I'm working in a startup since 2021." | ✅ "I've **been working at** a startup **since** 2021." — *still true now, started in the past.* |
| ❌ "I'm product designer." | ✅ "I'm **a** product designer." — *jobs need "a/an".* |
| ❌ "I do UX for a B2B SaaS platform…" (to a stranger) | ✅ "**Basically**, I make apps easy to use." — *start simple; add detail if they ask.* |
| ❌ "In my work, I am responsible for…" | ✅ "**My job's mostly about**…" — *"responsible for" sounds like a CV, not a chat.* |
| ❌ "I'm designer." → silence | ✅ Title + simple explanation + **ask back** ([Week 3](../week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md)). |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a recent problem you solved at work (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a recent problem you solved at work."**
> What's your job, in one sentence? What was the problem? How did you find out about it? Who helped? How did you figure it out? What happened in the end — and what did you learn?

- Start with your **one-sentence job explanation** (from Exercise 5.1), then the [4 boxes](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md).
- Use at least **3 phrasal verbs** (*find out, figure out, end up, come up*), **3 collocations** (*make a decision, take a while, have a meeting*), **2 softeners/intensifiers** and **1 idiom** from this week.
- Keep it understandable for **someone outside your field**. No jargon without a simple explanation.
- End every round with **a question to the listener**: *"Have you ever had a problem like that at work?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Three listeners (15 min)

Explain **what you do** (1 minute each) to three imaginary people. Change your words for each one. Record it.

1. A 10-year-old kid at a family party.
2. Someone from your field at a design meetup.
3. Your grandmother.

### Part C: Month 1 warm-up (10 min)

Tomorrow is your **monthly check**. Tell one story that uses **everything from Month 1** in 3 minutes: connectors (Week 1), at least one question to the listener (Week 2), an echo or reaction to your own story (Week 3), and this week's vocabulary. Topic: *"The best or worst week you've had at work."* Don't count your numbers — just enjoy how much you can do now.
