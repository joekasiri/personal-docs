## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: question melody

Questions have two main melodies in American English:

- **Wh- questions** (what, where, when, who, why, how) usually **fall** at the end. The voice goes up on the main stressed word, then down. ↘
  > *Where are you FROM? ↘* · *What do you DO? ↘*
- **Yes/no questions** (do you…?, are you…?, have you…?) usually **rise** at the end. ↗
  > *Are you HUNgry? ↗* · *Have you been to MEXico? ↗*

If you make a wh- question rise, it can sound like you didn't hear ("Where are you from? ↗" = "Sorry, where did you say?"). If you make a yes/no question fall, it can sound cold or like an interrogation.

Also notice that the **small words at the start are squashed** ([Week 1 Day 4 rhythm](../week-01-storytelling/day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md#1-shadowing-20-min)): "What do you do?" sounds like **"Whaddya DO?"**. You'll work on these reduced starts all week.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed. Follow the arrow.

1. Where are you FROM? ↘ → "Where're ya FROM?"
2. What do you DO? ↘ → "Whaddya DO?"
3. Are you HUNgry? ↗ → "Are ya HUNgry?"
4. Have you been to MEXico? ↗ → "Have ya been ta MEXico?"
5. How was your WEEKend? ↘ → "How was yer WEEKend?"
6. Did you SEE it? ↗ → "Didja SEE it?"
7. Who are you going WITH? ↘ → "Who're ya goin' WITH?"
8. Is it FAR? ↗ → "Izit FAR?"
9. Why did you LEAVE? ↘ → "Why'dja LEAVE?"
10. Do you like your JOB? ↗ → "D'ya like yer JOB?"
11. When does it START? ↘ → "When does it START?"
12. Can you HELP me? ↗ → "C'n ya HELP me?"

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Direct questions

### Explanation

In many languages you make a question just by changing your voice: "You live here?". English **sometimes** does this in casual speech, but most of the time it needs a special order. Here's the pattern for almost every question:

| Question word | Helper verb | Subject | Main verb… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | do | you | work? |
| What | did | you | do last weekend? |
| How long | have | you | lived here? |
| What | are | you | doing tonight? |
| When | are | you | gonna move? |
| — | Do | you | like it? |

The **helper verb** depends on the tense:

| Tense | Helper | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple (habits, facts) | do / does | What **do** you usually do on Sundays? |
| Present continuous (now, plans) | am / is / are | What **are** you working on right now? |
| Past simple (finished past) | did | Where **did** you go on vacation? |
| Present perfect (life experience, up to now) | have / has | **Have** you ever been to Japan? How long **have** you worked there? |
| Future (plans) | are … gonna / will | What **are** you gonna do this weekend? Where **will** you stay? |
| Can / would / should | can / would / should | **Would** you ever live abroad? |

Three special cases that natives use all the time:

1. **"Be" questions have no helper.** *Where **are** you from? What **was** it like? **Is** it far?*
2. **Subject questions** — when the question word *is* the subject — have **no do/did**. *Who **called** you? What **happened**? Who **made** this?* (Not "Who did call you?")
3. **Prepositions go at the end.** *Who did you go **with**? What are you talking **about**? Where are you **from**? What's it **for**?* In fast speech: *"Who'd you go with?"*

And one very useful chunk: **"What's … like?"** asks for a description. *What's your new job like? What was the hotel like? What are your coworkers like?*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What do you…? | you ask about habits or facts | "What do you usually do after work?" |
| What are you…-ing? | you ask about now or near plans | "What are you up to this weekend?" |
| What did you…? | you ask about a finished past event | "What did you have for lunch?" |
| Have you ever…? | you ask about life experience | "Have you ever tried surfing?" |
| How long have you…? | you ask about something that started in the past and continues | "How long have you been in the city?" |
| What are you gonna…? | you ask about future plans | "What are you gonna do for your birthday?" |
| What's … like? | you want a description | "What's your new apartment like?" |
| Who … with? / What … about? | the preposition goes at the end | "Who'd you go with?" "What's the movie about?" |
| What happened? / Who called? | the question word is the subject — no "did" | "What happened at the meeting?" |
| How often do you…? | you ask about frequency | "How often do you go to the gym?" |
| Would you ever…? | you ask about something imaginary or possible | "Would you ever quit your job to travel?" |
| Which one…? / Whose…? | you ask about a choice / an owner | "Which one did you pick?" "Whose idea was that?" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: meeting someone new**

> **A:** So where are you from?
> **B:** Originally? Lisbon. But I've been here like five years.
> **A:** Oh, nice! What brought you here?
> **B:** Work, mostly. I'm a product designer.
> **A:** Cool. What are you working on right now?
> **B:** A banking app. Kinda boring, honestly.
> **A:** Ha. Have you ever worked on something fun?
> **B:** Yeah, a travel app once. That was awesome.

**Dialogue 2: Monday morning**

> **A:** Hey! How was your weekend?
> **B:** Really good, thanks. I went to a wedding.
> **A:** Oh, fun! Whose wedding was it?
> **B:** My college roommate's.
> **A:** Where was it?
> **B:** At this vineyard outside the city.
> **A:** Nice. What was it like?
> **B:** Gorgeous. But super hot.
> **A:** Who'd you go with?
> **B:** Just my partner. What did you do?

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "Where you live?" | ✅ "Where **do** you live?" — *present simple needs "do".* |
| ❌ "What did you did?" | ✅ "What did you **do**?" — *after "did", use the base verb.* |
| ❌ "Who did call you?" | ✅ "Who **called** you?" — *"who" is the subject, so no "did".* |
| ❌ "How long you are here?" | ✅ "How long **have you been** here?" — *still true now = present perfect, and the helper goes before "you".* |
| ❌ "With who you went?" | ✅ "**Who** did you go **with**?" — *preposition at the end.* |
| ❌ "What means this word?" | ✅ "What **does** this word **mean**?" — *"mean" needs "does".* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — the last time you met someone new (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about the last time you met someone new."**
> At work, at a party, on a trip, online. Where were you? Who was it — describe them ([Week 1 Day 5](../week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md))? What did you talk about? What questions did they ask you? Did you see them again?

- Use your 4 "must use" connectors from [Week 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- **End every round by asking the listener 3 questions** in 3 different tenses. For example:
  - *"Have you ever met someone like that?"* (present perfect)
  - *"Where did you meet your best friend?"* (past simple)
  - *"Do you find it easy to talk to new people?"* (present simple)
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Answer your own questions (15 min)

Play the 3 questions at the end of round 3. Pause after each one and **answer it yourself** for 1 minute, as if a friend had asked you. Then ask **2 more questions** of your own from the 50 in [Exercise 1.1](exercises.md#day-1-exercises) and answer those too. Record it.

### Part C: Question speed round (10 min)

Set a timer for **3 minutes**. Imagine you've just met someone at a party. Ask them as many **different** questions as you can, without stopping. Record it. Count how many you asked. Do it twice more and try to beat your number. Tonight's target: **25 in 3 minutes**.
