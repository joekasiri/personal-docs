**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- You can build [direct questions](day-1-direct-questions.md) and [follow-ups](day-2-follow-up-questions.md). Tonight you learn the softer, more polite version — and the word-order rule that trips up almost every learner.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, an interview or "asking for directions" clip (see section 1).
- Think of a time you got lost or couldn't find something (for the drill).

## Learning objectives

- Hear and say **"whaddya"**, **"whatcha"** and **"whaddidja"** in fast questions.
- Turn a direct question into an indirect one: "Where's the station?" → "Do you know where the station is?"
- Use **normal word order** (subject + verb) after the opening — not question order.
- Use **if / whether** for yes/no questions: "Do you know if it's open?"
- Use indirect questions in a story: "I asked this guy if he knew where…"

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: "whaddya", "whatcha", "whaddidja"

"What" + a helper + "you" turns into one quick chunk. The T becomes a flap D ([Week 1 Day 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md#1-shadowing-20-min)), and "you" becomes "ya" or "cha".

| Full | Reduced | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| What do you…? | whaddya | "WUH-dyə" |
| What are you…? | whatcha / whaddaya | "WUH-chə" |
| What did you…? | whaddidja / whadja | "WUH-dih-jə" / "WUH-jə" |
| What have you…? | whaddaya / what've ya | "WUH-də-və" |

**Bonus — indirect question melody.** "Do you know where the station is?" is a yes/no question on the outside, so it usually **rises** at the end ↗. "Can you tell me what time it starts?" also rises ↗. "I was wondering what time it starts." is a statement, so it **falls** ↘.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. What do you THINK? → "Whaddya THINK?"
2. What are you DOing? → "Whatcha DOin'?"
3. What did you SAY? → "Whaddidja SAY?"
4. What do you wanna EAT? → "Whaddya wanna EAT?"
5. What are you gonna DO? → "Whatcha gonna DO?"
6. What did you BUY? → "Whadja BUY?"
7. What have you been UP to? → "Whaddaya been UP to?"
8. Do you know where it IS? ↗ → "D'ya know where it IS?"
9. Can you tell me what time it STARTS? ↗ → "C'n ya tell me what time it STARTS?"
10. I was wondering if you're FREE. ↘ → "I was wonderin' if you're FREE."
11. Any idea how much it COSTS? ↗ → "Any idea how much it COSTS?"
12. Do you remember what she SAID? ↗ → "D'ya remember what she SAID?"

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"what are you eating today street interview"**, **"what do you do for a living street interview"**, **"asking strangers what they think"**.
- Street-interview hosts say "whaddya" and "whatcha" again and again. Repeat each question 3 times, then say a short answer.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Indirect questions

### Explanation

An **indirect question** is a question inside a polite "wrapper". Instead of asking straight away, you start with a phrase like *Do you know…?* or *Can you tell me…?*

> Direct: *Where's the bathroom?*
> Indirect: *Do you know where the bathroom is?*

**When to use them:**
- With **strangers** or people you don't know well (a receptionist, someone on the street).
- When you want to sound **softer** — for example at work, or when you ask something a bit personal.
- When you're **not sure** they know the answer ("Do you happen to know…?").
- **In stories**, when you report a question: *"I asked him where the station was."*

### The word-order rule

After the wrapper, the question **stops being a question**. It goes back to **normal sentence order: subject + verb**. And **do / does / did disappear**.

| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| Where **is the office**? | Do you know where **the office is**? |
| What time **does it start**? | Can you tell me what time **it starts**? |
| Where **did she go**? | Do you know where **she went**? |
| How much **is it**? | Any idea how much **it is**? |
| Why **are they** late? | I wonder why **they're** late. |

Look at row 2 and 3: "does it start" → "it start**s**", "did she go" → "she **went**". The tense moves into the main verb.

**Yes/no questions** use **if** (or **whether**, which is a bit more formal):

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| **Is it** open today? | Do you know **if it's** open today? |
| **Did he** call? | Do you know **if he called**? |
| **Can I** park here? | Do you know **if I can** park here? |

**The same rule works in normal sentences:** *I don't know where the office is.* · *I'm not sure what time it starts.* · *What I need is more practice.* (not "What do I need is…")

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Do you know…? | the most common, neutral wrapper | "Do you know where the nearest pharmacy is?" |
| Can you tell me…? | you ask for information, politely | "Can you tell me what time the last train leaves?" |
| Do you happen to know…? | you're not sure they know — extra polite | "Do you happen to know if this place takes cards?" |
| Any idea…? | very casual, with friends | "Any idea what time Sam's getting here?" |
| Do you remember…? | you ask about something in the past | "Do you remember what the password was?" |
| I was wondering… | you ask softly, often at work (it's a statement, not a question) | "I was wondering if you had time to look at my designs." |
| Would you mind telling me…? | formal, or a slightly personal question | "Would you mind telling me how much you paid for it?" |
| I don't know / I'm not sure… | you say you don't know | "I'm not sure what she meant." |
| I wonder… | you think out loud | "I wonder why they changed the logo." |
| I asked him if / where… | you report a question in a story | "So I asked this guy if he knew where the hostel was." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: at a hotel**

> **A:** Hi, sorry — do you know what time breakfast starts?
> **B:** Seven o'clock, in the restaurant downstairs.
> **A:** Great. And can you tell me where I can get a taxi?
> **B:** Sure, just outside the main door.
> **A:** Perfect. Oh, and do you happen to know if the pool's open tonight?
> **B:** Until ten.

**Dialogue 2: at work**

> **A:** Hey, I was wondering if you had five minutes today.
> **B:** Sure, what's up?
> **A:** Do you remember who made the original icons? I need the files.
> **B:** Hmm, I'm not sure who made them. Any idea when they were made?
> **A:** Like 2021, I think.
> **B:** Then ask Priya. I bet she knows where they are.

**In a story:**

> So I was totally lost in Tokyo. I asked this guy if he spoke English, and he goes, "A little." So I asked him where the station was, and he just… walked me there. Like, ten minutes. Turns out he was going the other way.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "Do you know where is the office?" | ✅ "Do you know where **the office is**?" — *subject before verb after the wrapper.* |
| ❌ "Can you tell me what time does it start?" | ✅ "Can you tell me what time **it starts**?" — *no "does"; the verb takes the -s.* |
| ❌ "I don't know where did she go." | ✅ "I don't know where **she went**." — *no "did"; past goes into the verb.* |
| ❌ "Do you know is it open?" | ✅ "Do you know **if it's** open?" — *yes/no questions need "if".* |
| ❌ "What do I need is more practice." | ✅ "**What I need** is more practice." — *inside a sentence = normal order.* |
| ❌ "I asked him where was the station." | ✅ "I asked him where **the station was**." — *same rule in stories.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a time you got lost (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a time you got lost, or couldn't find something important."**
> In a new city, a huge building, an airport, a festival; or your keys, passport, a file at work. Where were you? Who did you ask for help? What did you ask them? How did it end?

- Include **at least 2 indirect questions** you asked people in the story: *"I asked this woman if she knew where…"*, *"I was like, 'Do you know where Gate 40 is?'"*
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and at least one [past continuous "I was … when …"](../week-01-storytelling/day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md).
- End each round with one question to the listener: *"Do you remember the last time you got lost?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Polite stranger challenge (15 min)

Imagine you've just arrived in a new city. For each place below, ask **3 indirect questions** out loud, as if to a person there. Then answer each one yourself as the local. Record it.

1. At the hotel reception.
2. At a train station information desk.
3. To a person on the street.
4. At a new office on your first day.
5. At a restaurant, to the server.

### Part C: "I don't know…" answers (10 min)

Answer each question out loud with a full "I'm not sure…" / "I have no idea…" sentence, using **normal word order**, then add a guess. Example: *"Where's the nearest ATM?" → "I'm not sure where the nearest ATM is, but there's probably one at the station."*

1. What time does the store close?
2. Where did Alex put the keys?
3. How much does a ticket cost?
4. Why is the train late?
5. Who designed this logo?
6. When's the next meeting?
7. Is the café open on Sundays?
8. Did the client approve the design?
