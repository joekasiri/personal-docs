## Before you start

- Yesterday you built [direct questions](day-1-direct-questions.md). Tonight you learn the most useful kind: the **second** question, the one that shows you're really listening.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, and a **podcast or interview clip** where someone talks about their life for at least 10 minutes (you'll pause it a lot in Exercise 2.2). Search "casual podcast interview" or "life story podcast".
- Have your [reaction phrases](../week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md) from Week 1 fresh in your mind — tonight you combine them with follow-ups.

## Learning objectives

- Hear and say **"d'you"** (do you) and **"didja"** (did you) in fast questions.
- Find the "hook" in what someone says — the detail that's worth asking about.
- Ask follow-ups for **details, reasons, feelings and results**.
- Combine a reaction and a follow-up: "No way! How'd that happen?"
- Ask 2 follow-ups after anything a speaker says, without planning.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: "d'you" and "didja"

In fast questions, **do you** and **did you** almost disappear:

| Full | Reduced | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Do you…? | d'you / d'ya | "jə" or "dyə" |
| Did you…? | didja | "DID-jə" |
| Did you…? (very fast) | 'dja | "jə" |
| Where did you…? | where'dja | "WAIR-jə" |
| Why did you…? | why'dja | "WHY-jə" |

The D + Y sound together becomes a **"j"** sound, like the start of "jam". That's why "did you" sounds like "didja". Listen for it everywhere this week.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed. Remember: wh- falls ↘, yes/no rises ↗.

1. Do you LIKE it? ↗ → "D'ya LIKE it?"
2. Did you GO? ↗ → "Didja GO?"
3. Do you know HIM? ↗ → "D'ya know HIM?"
4. Did you have FUN? ↗ → "Didja have FUN?"
5. Where did you STAY? ↘ → "Where'dja STAY?"
6. Why did you QUIT? ↘ → "Why'dja QUIT?"
7. Do you want SOME? ↗ → "D'ya want SOME?"
8. Did you end up GOING? ↗ → "Didja end up GOin'?"
9. What did you THINK? ↘ → "Whad'ja THINK?"
10. Do you still TALK to her? ↗ → "D'ya still TALK to 'er?"
11. Did you TELL him? ↗ → "Didja TELL 'im?"
12. Who did you MEET there? ↘ → "Who'dja MEET there?"

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"podcast interview funny questions"**, **"catching up with a friend vlog"**, **"roommate Q&A"**.
- Listen for **"didja"** and **"d'ya"**. Each time you hear one, pause and repeat the question 3 times.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Follow-up questions

### Explanation

A first question starts a conversation. A **follow-up** question keeps it alive. It tells the other person: *I heard you, I'm interested, tell me more.* People love talking to someone who asks good follow-ups — and while they talk, **you** get a break from speaking.

**Step 1: find the hook.** In almost everything people say, there's one interesting detail — a "hook". Ask about that.

> *"I went to a wedding this weekend. It was at this vineyard and it rained the whole time."*
> Hooks: **vineyard**, **rained the whole time**.
> Follow-ups: *"Wait, the whole time? What did you do?"* · *"Whose wedding was it?"*

**Step 2: choose a type.** Follow-ups usually ask for one of four things:

| Type | Questions |
|---|---|
| **More details** | Like what? · Where was this? · Who else was there? · What was that like? · And then what? |
| **The reason** | How come? · Why's that? · What made you…? · What do you mean? |
| **Feelings** | How did you feel about it? · Were you nervous? · Was it worth it? · Did you like it? |
| **The result** | How'd that go? · What happened? · Did you end up…? · So what are you gonna do? |

**Step 3: react first (optional, but natural).** Put a [reaction](../week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md) before the follow-up: *"No way! How come?"* · *"Oh, nice! How'd it go?"* · *"Ugh, that's rough. What happened?"*

**Short is better.** The best follow-ups are often only 2–3 words: *How come? Like what? And then? Wait, what?* They're fast, and they work for almost anything.

**"How come?"** means "why?" but it's softer and more casual. Note the word order: *How come you left?* (no "did").

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How come? | you want the reason, casually | A: "I'm not drinking coffee anymore." B: "How come?" |
| How'd that go? | you want to know the result | A: "I had my interview yesterday." B: "Oh! How'd that go?" |
| Wait, what happened? | something surprising or unclear happened | A: "We had to leave the restaurant." B: "Wait, what happened?" |
| What was that like? | you want them to describe an experience | A: "I lived in Seoul for a year." B: "Whoa. What was that like?" |
| Like what? | they said something general and you want an example | A: "There's so much to do there." B: "Like what?" |
| What do you mean? | you want them to explain | A: "My job's kinda weird." B: "Weird how? What do you mean?" |
| And then what? | you want the story to continue | A: "So the police showed up." B: "And then what?" |
| Did you end up…? | you ask about the final result | "Did you end up taking the job?" |
| What made you…? | you ask about the reason for a decision | "What made you move to Berlin?" |
| Was it worth it? | you ask if something hard or expensive was good in the end | "Twelve hours on a bus? Was it worth it?" |
| How did you feel about it? | you ask about emotions | "Your boss said that? How did you feel about it?" |
| Who else was there? | you want more people in the story | "Who else was at the dinner?" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: one statement, many follow-ups**

> **A:** I quit my job last month.
> **B:** Wait, really? How come?
> **A:** I was just burned out. Like, totally.
> **B:** That's rough. What are you doing now?
> **A:** Honestly? Nothing. Traveling a bit.
> **B:** Nice! Where'd you go?
> **A:** Mexico, for three weeks.
> **B:** Oh, cool. What was that like?
> **A:** Amazing. I didn't check my email once.
> **B:** Ha! Was it hard not to?

**Dialogue 2: at work**

> **A:** The client meeting was… interesting.
> **B:** Interesting how?
> **A:** They want to change the whole color scheme.
> **B:** Seriously? Like, everything?
> **A:** Pretty much.
> **B:** What made them change their mind?
> **A:** Their CEO's wife didn't like the blue.
> **B:** No way. So what are you gonna do?

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ A: "I went to Peru." B: "OK." (silence) | ✅ "**Oh, nice! What was that like?**" — *silence after news can sound like you're not interested.* |
| ❌ "How come did you leave?" | ✅ "How come **you left**?" — *"how come" uses normal word order, no "did".* |
| ❌ A: "I went to Peru." B: "I went to Chile last year! It was…" | ✅ Ask **one** follow-up first, *then* share your story — *don't jump straight to yourself.* |
| ❌ "Why? Why? Why?" | ✅ Mix types: details, reason, feelings, result — *"why" every time feels like an interrogation.* |
| ❌ "What was it like the hotel?" | ✅ "What was **the hotel** like?" — *the thing goes between "was" and "like".* |
| ❌ "Did you ended up going?" | ✅ "Did you **end up** going?" — *base verb after "did".* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a first time (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a time you tried something for the first time."**
> A sport, a food, a new job, a new city, an instrument, a dance class, public speaking. Why did you try it? What happened? How did you feel? Would you do it again?

- Use the [4 boxes](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md): setting → problem → turning point → ending.
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- End each round with **one direct question** to the listener: *"Have you ever tried it?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Be your own listener (15 min)

1. Play round 1. Every time you hear a **hook**, pause and ask a follow-up out loud: *"Wait, why were you so nervous?"*, *"Who else was there?"*
2. Write down your **5 best follow-ups**.
3. Now answer each of them out loud, for about 1 minute each. This is what a real conversation would do to your story — make it longer and deeper.

### Part C: The question you'd hate to be asked (10 min)

Think of **2 follow-up questions about your first-time story** that would be hard to answer (for example: *"Why didn't you just give up?"*, *"What would you do differently?"*). Record yourself answering each one for 1 minute without stopping. If you get stuck, use a connector: *"The thing is…"*, *"Anyway…"*.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Reread yesterday's 5 phrases** (Day 1 direct questions). Ask each question out loud, then answer it in one sentence.
2. **Log 5 new phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): your 5 favorite follow-ups from tonight. For each, write a mini-dialogue: *A: "I moved to Denver." B: "Oh, nice! What made you move?"*
3. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
