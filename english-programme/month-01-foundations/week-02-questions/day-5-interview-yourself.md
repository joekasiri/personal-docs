## Learning objectives

- **Chain** questions: take a word from the answer and turn it into the next question.
- Switch between interviewer and guest smoothly, with no long pauses.
- Keep a self-interview going for **10 minutes**.
- Use all four question types from this week in one conversation.

### The full interview clip (12 min)

- Find a casual interview where a host asks a guest about their life. 2–3 minutes is enough. Search YouTube for **"casual podcast interview"**, **"how did you get into your career interview"**, **"life story interview podcast"**.
- Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)) — but this time shadow **both** people: the host's questions *and* the guest's first sentence of each answer.
- Notice how the host builds each question from the guest's last answer. That's tonight's skill.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Interview yourself

### Explanation

When you practise alone, it's easy to only practise *answering*. But in a real conversation you're constantly switching: answer, ask, listen, ask again. Interviewing yourself trains that switch. You play **the interviewer** (asking) and **the guest** (answering), out loud, and you don't stop.

**The secret is chaining.** You don't need a list of 50 questions. You need **one** opening question. After that, every new question comes from the last answer:

> **Q:** Where did you grow up?
> **A:** In a small town by the sea. It was super quiet.
> **Q (chain on "quiet"):** Quiet how? Was it boring?
> **A:** Kind of, yeah. There was nothing to do on weekends, so I drew a lot.
> **Q (chain on "drew"):** Wait, is that how you got into design?
> **A:** Yeah, I think so…

**How to chain:**
1. Listen to your own answer.
2. Pick **one word** — usually a noun, a feeling or a surprise ("quiet", "drew").
3. Make a question about it — a follow-up ("Quiet how?"), a tag ("You loved it, didn't you?"), a direct question ("What did you draw?") or an indirect question ("Do you remember when you started?").

**When the chain dies**, use a topic switch: *"OK, let's talk about…" · "So, next question…" · "Totally different question —…"* — then start a new chain.

**How to sound like two people:** change your position or your voice slightly. Some learners turn their head left to ask and right to answer. It sounds silly. It works.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So tell me about… | you open a topic as the interviewer | "So tell me about your job." |
| You said…, right? Why…? | you chain from an earlier answer | "You said it was boring, right? Why'd you stay?" |
| … how? | you ask for detail about an adjective | "Weird how?" "Hard how?" |
| What was that like? | you ask for an experience | "You moved alone at 18? What was that like?" |
| How did you get into…? | you ask how something started | "How did you get into design?" |
| Looking back, …? | you ask for a reflection | "Looking back, would you change anything?" |
| OK, totally different question — | you switch topics | "OK, totally different question — what's your guilty pleasure?" |
| Let's talk about… | you start a new chain | "Let's talk about travel." |
| That's a good question. / Honestly? | you start an answer and buy a second | "Honestly? I have no idea." |
| Last question: … | you close the interview | "Last question: where do you see yourself in five years?" |

### Examples

**A 1-minute model self-interview (Q = interviewer, A = you):**

> **Q:** So tell me about your weekend.
> **A:** Pretty chill. I went to a pottery class with a friend.
> **Q:** Pottery? How'd that happen?
> **A:** So basically, my friend got two tickets for her birthday and her boyfriend bailed.
> **Q:** Ha! Was it fun?
> **A:** Yeah, it was. Way harder than it looks, though.
> **Q:** Hard how?
> **A:** Like, the clay just keeps falling apart. I made a "bowl" that looks like a pancake.
> **Q:** You're gonna go back, aren't you?
> **A:** I think so, yeah. Next month.
> **Q:** OK, totally different question — do you remember the last time you tried something totally new?

**Dialogue: chaining with a real person**

> **A:** What do you do for fun?
> **B:** I run a lot. I'm training for a half marathon.
> **A:** Oh, nice! When's the race?
> **B:** In October, in Chicago.
> **A:** Chicago? How come there?
> **B:** My brother lives there, so it's kind of an excuse to visit.
> **A:** That makes sense. Is he running too?
> **B:** No way. He hates running.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Reading questions from a list, one after another, with no link | ✅ **Chain**: build each question from a word in the last answer. |
| ❌ Answering only "Yes." then asking the next question | ✅ Short answer **+ a detail** ([Day 4](day-4-question-tags-and-short-answers.md)) — *the detail gives you the next hook.* |
| ❌ Stopping for 5 seconds to think of a question | ✅ Use a quick follow-up: "How come?", "Like what?", "And then?" — *they work after almost anything.* |
| ❌ "How did you got into design?" | ✅ "How did you **get** into design?" — *base verb after "did".* |
| ❌ "Can you tell me what do you do on weekends?" | ✅ "Can you tell me what **you do** on weekends?" — *[indirect word order](day-3-indirect-questions.md).* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a decision that changed your life (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a decision that changed your life."**
> Moving to a new city, choosing what to study, taking (or leaving) a job, starting a hobby, a relationship, saying yes to a trip. What were your options? Why did you choose what you chose? What happened after? Are you happy with it now?

- Use the [4 boxes](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md) and 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- **End every round with 3 questions to the listener**, one of each kind:
  - direct: *"What's the biggest decision you've ever made?"*
  - follow-up style (about the answer you imagine they gave): *"Oh, really? How'd that work out?"*
  - indirect: *"Do you remember how old you were when you decided what to study?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: The listener interviews you (15 min)

Imagine a curious friend just heard your round 3. Interview yourself **about the story** for 10 minutes, recording. The friend asks follow-ups, tags and indirect questions; you answer. Start with: *"Wait, so how did your parents feel about it?"* and chain from there.

### Part C: Quick numbers check (10 min)

Count your 3 numbers on the **first 2 minutes** of Part B. Compare with your round 3 numbers. Is talking in an interview easier or harder for you than telling a story? Write one line about it in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
