## Learning objectives

- Hear and say **"wouldja"**, **"couldja"**, **"dontcha"** and **"wontcha"**.

- Answer yes/no questions with natural **short answers** ("Yeah, I did." "Not really.").
- Answer Claude's rapid-fire questions with short answers plus one extra detail.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: tag melody + "wouldja", "couldja", "dontcha"

**Tags have two melodies, with two meanings:**

| Melody | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Falling** ↘ | "I'm pretty sure. Just agree with me." Used for chatting. | It's freezing today, ISN'T it? ↘ |
| **Rising** ↗ | "I'm not sure. Please tell me." A real question. | You're coming tonight, AREN'T you? ↗ |

Most small-talk tags **fall**. Use a rising tag only when you really don't know.

**Reductions:** the D or T + "you" becomes a "j" or "ch" sound again:

| Full | Reduced | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Would you…? | wouldja | "WUH-jə" |
| Could you…? | couldja | "KUH-jə" |
| Don't you…? / …don't you? | dontcha | "DOHN-chə" |
| Won't you…? | wontcha | "WOHN-chə" |
| Didn't you…? | didncha | "DIDN-chə" |

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. Follow the arrows on the tags.

1. It's so HOT today, ISN'T it? ↘
2. You WORK here, RIGHT? ↗ (you really don't know)
3. You know Sarah, DONTCHA? ↘ → "You know Sarah, DOHN-chə?"
4. Couldja PASS me that? → "KUH-jə PASS me that?"
5. Wouldja like some COFfee? → "WUH-jə like some COFfee?"
6. That was AMAZing, WASN'T it? ↘
7. You didn't TELL him, DID you? ↗
8. You'll be there, WONTCHA? ↘ → "You'll be there, WOHN-chə?"
9. You went to the PARty, DIDNCHA? ↘ → "…DIDN-chə?"
10. We've MET before, HAVEN'T we? ↗
11. It's not THAT bad, IS it? ↘
12. Wouldja MIND if I sat here? → "WUH-jə MIND if I sat here?"

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"couples Q&A vlog"**, **"best friends answer questions about each other"**, **"roommates guess answers"**. These have lots of "right?", "didn't you?" and quick "Yeah, I did."
- Shadow the tags and the short answers. Mark each tag ↗ or ↘ before you copy it.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Question tags and short answers

### Explanation

**Question tags** are mini-questions at the end of a statement. Americans use them to make a statement friendly, to check something, or to invite the other person to talk.

**1. Casual tags: "right?" and "yeah?"** — the easiest, and very common in American English. They work after any sentence.
> *You're from Chicago, **right**?* · *That was the plan, **yeah**?* · *So we meet at six, **right**?*

**2. Grammar tags** — copy the helper verb, flip positive/negative, and add the pronoun:

| Statement | Tag | Full sentence |
|---|---|---|
| positive, "be" | negative "be" | It's late, **isn't it**? |
| positive, present simple | don't / doesn't | You like sushi, **don't you**? |
| positive, past simple | didn't | She called you, **didn't she**? |
| positive, present perfect | haven't / hasn't | You've been there, **haven't you**? |
| positive, will / can | won't / can't | You'll come, **won't you**? |
| negative statement | positive tag | You're not tired, **are you**? · He didn't go, **did he**? |

Special one: **I'm … , aren't I?** (*I'm next, aren't I?*). And **Let's …, shall we?** is British; Americans just say *Let's go, OK?*

**Short answers** — natives rarely answer yes/no questions with just "yes" or "no". They add the helper verb, or use a soft casual answer:

| Question | Short answer (yes) | Short answer (no) |
|---|---|---|
| Do you like it? | Yeah, I do. | No, not really. |
| Did you go? | Yeah, I did. | Nope, I didn't. |
| Are you busy? | Yeah, kinda. | No, I'm good. |
| Have you been to Rome? | Yeah, I have! | No, I haven't. Never. |
| Can you swim? | Yeah, I can. | Not very well, no. |
| Is it far? | Yeah, it is. | No, not at all. |

**Soft answers** (when it's not a clear yes or no): *Kind of. · Sort of. · I think so. · I don't think so. · I guess. · I hope so. · Not really. · Not yet. · Sometimes. · It depends.*

A short answer is **not** the end. In real conversation, you give the short answer and then a little bit more — *"Yeah, I did. It was awesome."* You'll practise this properly in [Week 3](../week-03-small-talk/README.md).

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| …, right? | you check something casually | "You're the new designer, right?" |
| …, isn't it? ↘ | you invite agreement about "is" | "It's crazy busy today, isn't it?" |
| …, don't you? | you check a habit or fact | "You live near the park, don't you?" |
| …, didn't you? | you check a past event | "You went to that concert, didn't you?" |
| …, haven't you? | you check experience | "You've worked with Dana before, haven't you?" |
| You're not…, are you? | you're surprised or a bit worried | "You're not leaving already, are you?" |
| Yeah, I did. / Nope, I didn't. | you answer a past question | A: "Did you see the game?" B: "Yeah, I did. Crazy ending." |
| Yeah, I have. / No, never. | you answer an experience question | A: "Have you been to Austin?" B: "No, never. Is it nice?" |
| Kind of. / Sort of. | the answer is "partly" | A: "Are you nervous?" B: "Kind of, yeah." |
| I think so. / I don't think so. | you're not 100% sure | A: "Is the meeting still on?" B: "I think so." |
| Not really. | a soft "no" | A: "Do you like horror movies?" B: "Not really." |
| Not yet. | no, but you plan to | A: "Have you seen the new office?" B: "Not yet!" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: at a party**

> **A:** You're Ben's friend, right?
> **B:** Yeah, I am! We worked together.
> **A:** Oh, cool. You're a designer too, aren't you?
> **B:** Kind of. More product than visual.
> **A:** Gotcha. You've been to one of these before, haven't you?
> **B:** No, never. First time.
> **A:** It's pretty fun, isn't it?
> **B:** Yeah, it is. Way better than I expected.

**Dialogue 2: at work**

> **A:** You sent the files to the client, didn't you?
> **B:** Yeah, I did. This morning.
> **A:** And they haven't replied yet, have they?
> **B:** Not yet. Should I follow up?
> **A:** I don't think so. Let's give them till tomorrow, OK?
> **B:** Sounds good.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "You like coffee, isn't it?" | ✅ "You like coffee, **don't you**?" — *the tag copies the helper: like → don't.* Or just say "**right?**" |
| ❌ "She went home, isn't she?" | ✅ "She went home, **didn't she**?" — *past simple → didn't.* |
| ❌ "You're not tired, aren't you?" | ✅ "You're not tired, **are you**?" — *negative statement → positive tag.* |
| ❌ A: "Did you go?" B: "Yes, I went." | ✅ "**Yeah, I did.**" — *short answers use the helper, not the main verb.* |
| ❌ A: "Have you been to Rome?" B: "Yes." (silence) | ✅ "**Yeah, I have!** Twice, actually." — *a bare "yes" sounds cold; add something.* |
| ❌ A: "Are you busy?" B: "Yes, I do." | ✅ "**Yeah, I am.**" — *match the helper in the question.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): Voice mode with Claude

Setup is in [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

### Steps

1. **Setup (3 min):** new chat, paste the prompt below, switch to voice mode, start recording on your phone.
2. **Rapid-fire round (10 min):** Claude asks quick yes/no questions. Answer each with a **short answer + one detail**.
3. **Guessing game (10 min):** you guess facts about Claude's "character" using **tags**: *"You live in a big city, don't you?"* Claude says if you're right.
4. **Story round (12 min):** tell Claude one story from Week 1. Claude interrupts with tags and follow-ups. You answer, then ask Claude 3 questions back (one direct, one follow-up, one indirect).
5. **Feedback (10 min):** say "feedback", write down every correction, and repeat each one 3 times.

### Prompt to paste

```
I'm practising casual spoken American English. My level is B2 and my goal is to speak fast and relaxed without thinking.

Please:
- Talk to me like a friendly American friend, casually, using contractions and everyday phrases.
- Keep your turns short (1–3 sentences) so I do most of the talking.
- Ask me follow-up questions so I have to keep going.
- Don't correct me during the conversation. Just remember my mistakes.
- When I say "feedback", stop and give me: my 5 most important mistakes with the correct version, 3 phrases I could have used to sound more natural, and one thing I did well.

Tonight's topic and focus:
Question tags and short answers. We'll do 3 rounds.
Round 1: ask me 20 fast yes/no questions about my life ("Do you cook?", "Did you go out last weekend?", "Have you ever been to Canada?"). I'll answer with a short answer plus one detail, like "Yeah, I did. It was awesome."
Round 2: pretend you're a character called Jamie with a made-up life (job, city, hobbies). I'll guess facts about you using question tags, like "You live in a big city, don't you?" Tell me if I'm right.
Round 3: I'll tell you a story. Use tags and follow-ups while I talk, like "Wait, that was last year, right?"
I'll tell you when to switch rounds. In feedback, focus on my tags (correct helper verb, positive/negative), my short answers, and my question word order.
```

### What to focus on

- **Never answer with just "yes" or "no".** Always short answer + one detail.
- Use at least **10 tags** in round 2. Mix "right?" with grammar tags (isn't it / don't you / didn't you / haven't you).
- Make most of your tags **fall** ↘ unless you really don't know.
- In round 3, use [follow-ups](day-2-follow-up-questions.md) and one [indirect question](day-3-indirect-questions.md) when you ask Claude questions.
