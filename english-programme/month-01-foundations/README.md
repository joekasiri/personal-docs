# Month 1: Foundations
[Programme home](../README.md) · [Month 2: Speed →](../month-02-speed/README.md)

## What this month is about

This month you build the basic pieces that every casual conversation is made of: **stories, questions, small talk and everyday vocabulary**. You already know most of the words. The problem is that under pressure they don't come out. So this month is not about learning lots of new things. It's about saying a small number of very useful chunks out loud, again and again, until they start to come out by themselves.

Everything this month is **practised and rehearsed**. You get a prompt, you have a few seconds to remember the story, and then you tell it three times with the [4/3/2 drill](../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md). That might feel like "cheating" because real conversation isn't rehearsed. But it's the right first step. Rehearsing the same story, with the same connectors and the same reactions, is how chunks move from your passive memory (you recognise them) to your active memory (you say them without thinking). In Month 3 the preparation stops. By then the chunks will already be there.

Each week adds one layer. **Week 1** gives you stories: connectors, reactions, a simple four-part structure, past tenses and vivid description. **Week 2** gives you questions, so a conversation goes both ways. **Week 3** gives you small talk: how to open a chat, keep it going, buy time without "umm", and end it politely. **Week 4** fills in the everyday vocabulary that makes you sound natural: phrasal verbs, collocations, softeners, idioms and talking about your job.

At the same time, your ears and mouth get trained with **shadowing**: one American sound feature at a time (the flap T in "wadder", reductions like "gonna", linking, rhythm, question intonation, weak forms, and the many ways Americans "lose" their T). You use the pause-and-repeat method all month.

At the start of Week 1 you record a **2-minute baseline**, before you learn anything. Every number from now on is compared with it. At the end of the month you record again and see how far you've come.

## Goals for the month

By the end of Month 1 you can…

- tell a practised 2-minute story using **at least 4 different connectors**, with a clear setting → problem → turning point → ending.
- react naturally while someone tells you a story ("No way!", "That's rough", "Wait, really?") instead of staying silent.
- ask direct, follow-up and indirect questions **with the right word order**, and use question tags like "You work here, right?".
- open a small-talk chat, answer with more than "fine", keep it going with **answer, add, ask back**, and end it politely.
- replace most of your "umm"s with natural time-buyers like "Let me think…" or "Good question…".
- use 10+ everyday phrasal verbs, the most common make/do/take/have/get collocations, and a handful of idioms in your own stories.
- explain in simple words what you do at work, what a normal day looks like, and one recent problem you solved.

## How your nights change this month

This is your first month, so this is the starting pattern. Every night is 2 hours.

| Block | Time | What you do this month |
|---|---|---|
| Shadowing | 20 min | **Pause-and-repeat**: play 1–2 sentences, pause, copy them exactly. One sound feature per night. |
| Main skill | 40 min | One lesson per night (Days 1–5): explanation, key phrases, examples, then the exercises out loud. |
| Speaking drill | 45 min | **4/3/2 drill** on Days 1, 2, 3 and 5, with a specific story prompt in each lesson. **Day 4 is always voice mode with Claude.** |
| Review | 15 min | Log 5 phrases in your phrase log, and reread yesterday's phrases out loud in a sentence. |
| Live conversations | 1 per week | On **Day 6**, after your re-record test. Voice mode with Claude is the backup. |

- **Day 6** every week: free shadowing, a 2-minute unprepared re-record, your 3 numbers, a skill check and a live conversation.
- **Day 7** every week: light day, 30 minutes of listening while you walk, then rest.

## The 4 weeks

| Week | Focus | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| [Week 1: Storytelling](week-01-storytelling/README.md) | Connectors, reactions, story structure, past tenses, description · shadow: flap T, reductions, linking, rhythm | tell a short, clear story with real connectors and react to other people's stories |
| [Week 2: Questions](week-02-questions/README.md) | Direct, follow-up and indirect questions, question tags, self-interviews · shadow: question intonation, "d'you / didja / whaddya" | ask questions quickly and correctly, and keep someone talking with follow-ups |
| [Week 3: Small talk](week-03-small-talk/README.md) | Openers, echo questions, answer-add-ask, time-buyers, changing topic and ending · shadow: weak forms ("ta", "fer", "'n") | start, keep going and end a casual chat without awkward silences |
| [Week 4: Vocabulary for everyday talk](week-04-vocabulary/README.md) | Phrasal verbs, collocations, intensifiers and softeners, idioms, talking about your job · shadow: T glottal stop and dropped T | sound more natural with everyday chunks and talk simply about your work |

## End-of-month check

On [Week 4 Day 6](week-04-vocabulary/day-6-weekly-check.md) you do your normal weekly check **plus the monthly check**. You record a new 2-minute unprepared story of the same kind as your baseline ("a day that didn't go as planned"), count your 3 numbers, and compare them with your Week 1 Day 1 baseline. Follow the full procedure in [Monthly check](../toolkit/monthly-check.md) and fill in the results table there.

**Targets for Month 1 (compared with your baseline):**

| Number | Target |
|---|---|
| Fillers ("umm", "uhh") + long pauses (over 2 seconds) | about **15% fewer** than your baseline total |
| Different connectors in 2 minutes | **4 or more** |

Example: baseline 20 umms + 6 long pauses = 26. Target: about 22 or fewer.

If you don't hit the target, don't just push on. Send Claude your numbers (the message is on the Day 6 page) and adjust before Month 2.
