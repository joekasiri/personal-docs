## Before you start

- This week you've built the skeleton of a story: [connectors](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md), [structure](day-3-story-structure.md) and [past tenses](day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md). Tonight you add the colour: what people and places **looked, sounded and felt like**.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, and one **2-minute storytime clip** you like (find it before you start — see section 1).
- Have yesterday's corrections from Claude open for your review.

## Learning objectives

- Shadow a full 2-minute clip, copying speed, rhythm, linking and reductions together.
- Describe places with casual, vivid words: *super crowded, kinda sketchy, tiny, cozy, packed*.
- Describe people quickly: age, look, personality, how they acted.
- Describe your own feelings in a story: *freaking out, starving, totally lost*.
- Use "the kind of place where…" and "he looked like…" to paint a picture fast.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: everything together

Tonight there's no single sound. You put this week's sounds together on **one full 2-minute clip**: the flap T (Day 1), reductions (Day 2), linking (Day 3) and rhythm (Day 4).

**How to find the clip:**
- One American speaker, talking casually, telling a story about a place or a person. About 2 minutes long (or a 2-minute part of a longer video).
- Search YouTube for **"worst Airbnb storytime"**, **"weirdest hotel I stayed in storytime"**, **"my crazy roommate storytime"**, **"travel horror story vlog"**.
- If it has subtitles, turn them on for the first listen only.

**How to use it (pause-and-repeat, see [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)):**
1. Listen to the whole 2 minutes once, just for the story (2 min).
2. Go sentence by sentence: play, pause, repeat. Copy everything — the speed, the laughs, the "like"s, the stretched words (12 min).
3. Record yourself doing the whole clip with pauses, then listen to 30 seconds of the original and 30 seconds of you (6 min).

### Warm-up sentences (before the clip)

These mix all of this week's sounds. Say each twice.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Describing people and places

### Explanation

A story without description is like a movie with no picture. "I stayed at a hotel. It was bad." The listener can't see anything. Compare: *"It was this tiny hotel above a nightclub, super loud, and the guy at the desk looked like he hadn't slept in a week."* Now they can picture it — and they want to know what happened next.

You don't need long, beautiful sentences. Casual description uses **short, strong chunks**:

**1. Intensifier + adjective.** In casual American speech, people rarely say "very". They say **super, really, so, kinda, pretty** (more on these in [Week 4](../week-04-vocabulary/day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md)).
> *super crowded · really cozy · so loud · kinda sketchy · pretty fancy*

**2. Strong single words.** Some adjectives already mean "very":
> *packed* (very crowded) · *tiny* (very small) · *huge* · *gorgeous* (very beautiful) · *starving* (very hungry) · *exhausted* (very tired) · *freezing* (very cold)

**3. Picture phrases.** These paint a scene in one go:
> *It was one of those places where… · It was the kind of place where… · He was the kind of guy who… · She looked like… · It smelled like… · You could hear…*

**4. Feelings.** In a story, the listener wants to know how **you** felt:
> *I was freaking out · I was totally lost · I felt so awkward · I was starving · I was kinda nervous · I was like, so relieved*

**People — a quick formula:** *age-ish* + *one look detail* + *one personality detail* + *what they did*.
> *"This guy in his fifties, tall, big gray beard, super friendly — he just started talking to us like we were old friends."*

**Talking about age casually:** *in his twenties, in her forties, like thirty, maybe sixty-ish, early thirties, late forties*.

**A word you'll hear a lot: "sketchy".** It means *it looks a bit dangerous or not trustworthy*: a sketchy street, a sketchy website, a sketchy deal.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| super crowded / packed | there are too many people | "The bar was packed. Like, you couldn't move." |
| kinda sketchy | a place or thing looks unsafe or untrustworthy | "The street was kinda sketchy, so we walked fast." |
| tiny / huge | very small / very big | "Our room was tiny — the bed touched three walls." |
| cozy | small, warm and comfortable (positive) | "It was this really cozy little café with old sofas." |
| It was one of those places where… | you describe the typical feeling of a place | "It was one of those places where everyone knows everyone." |
| He was the kind of guy who… | you describe someone's personality through behaviour | "He was the kind of guy who talks to everyone in the elevator." |
| She looked like… | you describe appearance or state | "She looked like she'd just run a marathon." |
| in his/her twenties (thirties…) | you give age casually | "The owner was this woman in her sixties, super funny." |
| super friendly / kinda awkward / really chill | quick personality | "My new roommate's really chill. Kinda quiet, but nice." |
| I was freaking out | you were very worried or excited | "I couldn't find my passport and I was freaking out." |
| I was totally lost | you didn't know where you were or what was happening | "Everything was in Japanese and I was totally lost." |
| You could hear… / It smelled like… | sounds and smells make it real | "You could hear the trains every five minutes." |

### Examples

**Model story — a place:**

> So this one time I booked this super cheap Airbnb in New Orleans. The pictures looked really cozy, right? Turns out it was above a bar. Like, directly above. You could hear the music until 3 a.m. And the street was kinda sketchy — there was this guy outside who just stood there all night. The room itself was tiny, and it smelled like old pizza. I was freaking out a little the first night. But the host was this woman in her sixties, super friendly, the kind of person who gives you a hug when you arrive. She made us breakfast every morning. In the end, it was one of the best trips I've ever had.

**Dialogue — describing a person:**

> **A:** So what's the new manager like?
> **B:** Honestly? Kinda intense.
> **A:** Intense how?
> **B:** He's the kind of guy who sends you messages at 11 p.m. Like, "Quick question."
> **A:** Oh no.
> **B:** But he's super smart. And he looks like he's like, twenty-five.
> **A:** Wait, really?

**Dialogue — describing a place:**

> **A:** How was the conference?
> **B:** It was so packed. Like, there were lines for everything.
> **A:** Ugh. What was the venue like?
> **B:** It was this huge old building, gorgeous on the outside, but inside it was freezing.
> **A:** That's rough.
> **B:** Yeah, everyone was sitting there in their coats.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "The bar was very very crowded." | ✅ "The bar was **super crowded**." / "**packed**." — *use one strong word instead of "very very".* |
| ❌ "He was very sympathetic." (meaning nice) | ✅ "He was **really nice** / **super friendly**." — *"sympathetic" means he understood your problem, not that he was nice.* |
| ❌ "She has thirty years." | ✅ "She's **like thirty**." / "She's **in her thirties**." — *age uses "be", not "have".* |
| ❌ "The trip was very boring for me, I was boring." | ✅ "The trip was **boring**. I was **bored**." — *-ing = the thing; -ed = your feeling.* |
| ❌ "It looked like dangerous." | ✅ "It **looked dangerous**." / "It looked **kinda sketchy**." — *"look like" + noun; "look" + adjective.* |
| ❌ "I was freaking." | ✅ "I was **freaking out**." — *you need "out".* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — the strangest place you've stayed (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about the strangest or most memorable place you've ever stayed."**
> A hotel, an Airbnb, a hostel, a tent, a friend's couch, your grandparents' house. What was the place like? Who was there — describe one person. What happened while you were there?

- Use the [4 boxes](day-3-story-structure.md). Put your description in the **setting** box, and at least one feeling in the **problem** or **turning point** box.
- Use at least **6 description chunks** from tonight (for example: *tiny, kinda sketchy, the kind of guy who…, I was freaking out, you could hear…, in her sixties*).
- Use at least **4 connectors** from [Day 1](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: An old story, new colour (15 min)

Take **one story from earlier this week** — your work story (Day 1), trip story (Day 2) or embarrassing story (Day 3). Do a quick **3 min / 2 min** version, adding:

- one description of a **place** (2–3 chunks),
- one description of a **person** (age-ish + look + personality),
- one **feeling** ("I was freaking out", "I felt so awkward").

### Part C: Listen and compare (10 min)

Listen to your Day 1 work story round 3, then tonight's Part B. Write down 2 things that are better tonight. Write down 1 thing you still want to fix. This is what you'll carry into Day 6.

1. **Reread yesterday's 5 phrases** (Day 4 — including Claude's corrections). Say each corrected sentence again, then make one new sentence with it.
2. **Log 5 new phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md) from tonight's key phrases. Make each sentence about a real person or place in your life ("My office kitchen is always packed at noon.").
3. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
4. Look at the [Week 1 Day 6 check](day-6-weekly-check.md) so you know what's coming tomorrow, and confirm your live conversation.
