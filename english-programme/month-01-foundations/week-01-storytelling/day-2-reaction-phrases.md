# Week 1 · Day 2: Reaction phrases

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Yesterday you learned the [12 storytelling connectors](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and recorded your baseline. Have your phrase log open on yesterday's 5 phrases — you'll review them at the end.
- Have ready: phone for recording, headphones, timer, one storytime clip.
- Think of a trip or a weekend from the last year that you remember well (for the drill).

## Learning objectives

- Say the four most common American reductions: gonna, wanna, kinda, gotta.
- React to good news, bad news, surprises and explanations with the right phrase.
- Use a reaction **with the right tone** — your voice matters as much as the words.
- Tell a trip or weekend story with 4/3/2, then practise reacting to it as a listener.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: reductions

In fast, casual speech, Americans squash some very common word pairs into one short word. You don't have to *write* them like this, but you need to **hear** them and **say** them, or you'll sound slow and stiff.

| Full form | Reduced | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| going to (+ verb) | gonna | "GUH-nuh" |
| want to | wanna | "WAH-nuh" |
| kind of | kinda | "KYNE-duh" |
| got to / have got to | gotta | "GAH-duh" (with a flap T) |
| what do you | whaddya | "WUH-dyuh" |

**Rule:** "gonna" only works for the *future*: "I'm gonna call her." It does **not** work for movement to a place: "I'm going to Chicago" stays "going to" (you can say "goin' to", but not "gonna Chicago").

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. I'm gonna GRAB a COFfee. → "I'm GUH-nuh GRAB a COFfee."
2. Do you wanna COME? → "D'you WAH-nuh COME?"
3. It was kinda WEIRD. → "It was KYNE-duh WEIRD."
4. I gotta GO. → "I GAH-duh GO."
5. Whaddya wanna DO tonight? → "WUH-dyuh WAH-nuh DO tonight?"
6. We're gonna be LATE. → "We're GUH-nuh be LATE."
7. I don't wanna TALK about it. → "I don't WAH-nuh TALK about it."
8. It's kinda FAR from here. → "It's KYNE-duh FAR from here."
9. You gotta SEE this. → "You GAH-duh SEE this."
10. Whaddya THINK? → "WUH-dyuh THINK?"
11. I was gonna CALL you! → "I was GUH-nuh CALL you!"
12. She's kinda SHY, but she's NICE. → "She's KYNE-duh SHY, but she's NICE."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"weekend vlog talking"**, **"get ready with me storytime"**, **"sit down chat vlog"**. People talking about their plans use "gonna" and "wanna" a lot.
- Every time you hear gonna, wanna, kinda or gotta, pause and repeat the full sentence 3 times.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 minutes on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Reaction phrases

### Explanation

When someone tells you a story, staying silent feels cold to an American listener. It can sound like you're bored or not following. Native listeners give **small reactions** all the time: "No way!", "Oh no!", "That's awesome!". These reactions:

- show you're listening and you care,
- encourage the other person to keep going,
- give **you** a turn to speak without having to build a full sentence.

That last point is important for "talking without thinking". A reaction is the easiest possible thing to say. If you own 15 reactions, you'll never be stuck in silence while someone talks to you.

Choose the reaction by **what kind of news** you just heard:

| Kind of news | Reactions |
|---|---|
| Surprising | No way! · Wait, really? · Seriously? · You're kidding. |
| Good | Oh, nice! · That's awesome! · Good for you! · Congrats! |
| Bad (small) | Ugh, that's annoying. · Oh no! · That sucks. |
| Bad (bigger) | That's rough. · Oh man, I'm sorry. · That must've been hard. |
| An explanation or a reason | That makes sense. · Fair enough. · Oh, gotcha. |
| Something you can imagine | I bet. · I can imagine. · Oh, totally. |

**Tone matters.** "Really?" in a flat, low voice sounds bored or even rude. "Really?!" with your voice going **up** sounds interested. Exaggerate your tone more than feels normal to you. To an American ear, it will sound just right.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No way! | you're surprised (good or bad) | A: "I ran into my old boss in Tokyo." B: "No way!" |
| Wait, really? | you're surprised and want more | A: "They cancelled the whole project." B: "Wait, really? Why?" |
| That's rough. | something hard happened to them | A: "I had to work every weekend this month." B: "Ugh, that's rough." |
| Fair enough. | you accept their reason, even if you'd do it differently | A: "I left early 'cause I was tired." B: "Fair enough." |
| That makes sense. | their explanation is logical | A: "We moved the launch so we could test more." B: "That makes sense." |
| Oh, nice! | good news, small or medium | A: "I finally finished the redesign." B: "Oh, nice!" |
| I bet. | you can easily imagine their feeling | A: "I was so tired after that flight." B: "I bet." |
| That's awesome! | really good news | A: "I got the job!" B: "That's awesome! Congrats!" |
| Ugh, that's so annoying. | small, everyday bad luck | A: "My train was 40 minutes late." B: "Ugh, that's so annoying." |
| Seriously? | surprise, sometimes a bit shocked | A: "He didn't even say thank you." B: "Seriously?" |
| Oh no! | something bad is happening in the story | A: "And then I realized my passport was at home." B: "Oh no!" |
| Good for you! | they did something brave or positive | A: "I finally told my manager I need help." B: "Good for you!" |
| You're kidding. | it's almost too surprising to believe | A: "The flight was free." B: "You're kidding." |
| Oh, gotcha. | you now understand | A: "It's on the second floor, not the first." B: "Oh, gotcha." |
| That must've been hard. | something serious or emotional | A: "My grandma was in the hospital all week." B: "Oh, that must've been hard." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a friend's bad trip**

> **A:** So we get to the hotel, and they've lost our booking.
> **B:** Oh no!
> **A:** Yeah, and it's like midnight.
> **B:** Ugh, that's rough.
> **A:** But then the guy at the desk goes, "We'll give you the suite for the same price."
> **B:** No way! That's awesome.
> **A:** I know! Turns out it was the best room in the whole hotel.
> **B:** Ha, I bet.

**Dialogue 2: work news**

> **A:** We're pushing the release to next month.
> **B:** Wait, really? How come?
> **A:** The payment stuff isn't ready, and we don't wanna launch half of it.
> **B:** Oh, that makes sense.
> **A:** Yeah. Kinda annoying, but…
> **B:** Fair enough. Better late than broken.

**Dialogue 3: small talk at the coffee machine**

> **A:** I finally bought a bike.
> **B:** Oh, nice! For getting to work?
> **A:** Yeah. I'm gonna try riding in every day.
> **B:** Good for you! I could never do that in winter.
> **A:** Honestly, me neither. We'll see.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ A: "My train was late." B: "Oh, I'm so sorry for you." | ✅ "**Ugh, that's annoying.**" — *"I'm sorry for you" sounds too heavy, almost like pity. Save "I'm sorry" for serious things.* |
| ❌ "Congratulation!" | ✅ "**Congrats!**" or "**Congratulations!**" — *always with an S.* |
| ❌ "Really." (flat, falling voice) | ✅ "**Really?!**" (voice goes up) — *a flat "really" sounds bored or sarcastic.* |
| ❌ A: "I got the job!" B: "Fair enough." | ✅ "**That's awesome!**" — *"fair enough" means "OK, I accept your reason". It's not for good news.* |
| ❌ A: "I lost my wallet." B: "Oh, nice!" | ✅ "**Oh no!**" — *listen to the news before you react. Don't use the same reaction for everything.* |
| ❌ Silence, then a long answer about yourself | ✅ React first ("No way!"), *then* add your comment — *the reaction tells them you heard.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 with a trip or weekend story (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about the best or the worst weekend trip you've had in the last year or two."**
> Where did you go? Who with? What went right or wrong? Was there a moment when everything changed? How did you feel at the end?

- Before you start, pick **4 connectors** from [Day 1](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) to use on purpose.
- Put in at least **one thing someone said**, with "and she goes…" or "I was like…", and react to it *inside* your story: "and I was like, 'No way!'"
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. All recorded.
- Count your 3 numbers on round 3 and write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

### Part B: React to yourself (15 min)

Now you're the listener.

1. Play your **round 1** recording.
2. Every 15–20 seconds, **pause** and react out loud, as if your best friend just told you that part: "No way!", "Oh no!", "Wait, really? Then what?", "That's rough."
3. Use **at least 10 different reactions** from tonight's table. Tick them off on paper as you use them.
4. If a reaction comes out flat, say it again with bigger tone.

### Part C: Quick round with a new story (10 min)

Do one more fast 4/3/2, but this time **2 min / 1.5 min / 1 min**, with this prompt:
> **"Tell me about a time you had to change your plans at the last minute."**

Use 3 connectors and include one reaction inside the story ("I was like, 'Seriously?'").

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Reread yesterday's 5 phrases** (Day 1 connectors). Cover your own sentence, then say a **new** sentence with each phrase, out loud.
2. **Log 5 new phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): pick the 5 reactions you're least used to saying. Write a mini-situation for each, like *"My flight got cancelled." → "Ugh, that's rough."*
3. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] say gonna, wanna, kinda and gotta naturally in a sentence
- [ ] choose the right reaction for good news, bad news, surprises and explanations
- [ ] say "Really?!" and "No way!" with interested, rising tone
- [ ] tell your weekend trip story in 2 minutes with at least 4 connectors
