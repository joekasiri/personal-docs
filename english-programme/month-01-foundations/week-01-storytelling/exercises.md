# Week 1 exercises: Storytelling
[Week 1 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) · [Day 2](day-2-reaction-phrases.md) · [Day 3](day-3-story-structure.md) · [Day 4](day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md) · [Day 5](day-5-describing-people-and-places.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** do every exercise **out loud**, even the "write" ones — write, then say. Speak at a normal, relaxed speed, not slowly and carefully. If an exercise says **record**, record it on your phone and listen back once. Answer keys are at the end of each day. Check them *after* you've said your own answer.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Say each connector 5 times in a sentence
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Read the model sentence for each connector once.
2. Say it **5 times**, a little faster each time. By the 5th time, don't look at the page.
3. Then make **your own sentence** with the same connector, about your life, and say that one 3 times.
4. Move on. About 1 minute per connector.

| # | Connector | Model sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So basically… | So basically, I spent the whole weekend fixing my bike. |
| 2 | The thing is… | The thing is, I'd never fixed anything before. |
| 3 | Anyway… | Anyway, so I watched a bunch of videos. |
| 4 | And then out of nowhere… | And then out of nowhere, the chain just snapped. |
| 5 | Turns out… | Turns out I'd bought the wrong size. |
| 6 | Which was weird, because… | The shop was closed, which was weird, because it's usually open on Sundays. |
| 7 | I was like… | And I was like, "OK, I give up." |
| 8 | And he goes… / And she goes… | So I call my neighbor, and he goes, "Bring it over, I've got tools." |
| 9 | Long story short… | Long story short, he fixed it in ten minutes. |
| 10 | The funny part is… | The funny part is, he's never even ridden a bike. |
| 11 | At that point… | At that point, I'd already spent like forty bucks on parts. |
| 12 | In the end… | In the end, I bought him a beer and we became friends. |

1. Read the story. Choose the best connector for each gap from the box. Use each one once.
2. Check the answer key.
3. Read the full story out loud **twice**: once reading, once looking up as much as you can.

**Box:** so basically · the thing is · anyway · and then out of nowhere · turns out · which was weird, because · I was like · and she goes · long story short · in the end

### Exercise 1.3: Connector chains
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last one

For each story skeleton below, tell a **1-minute story** using the 4 connectors given, in any order. Invent the details. Don't write anything — just talk. Do all six, then record your favourite one again.

1. **A delayed flight.** Connectors: *so basically · and then out of nowhere · at that point · in the end*
2. **A dinner that went wrong.** Connectors: *the thing is · turns out · the funny part is · long story short*
3. **A strange email from your boss.** Connectors: *so basically · which was weird, because · I was like · anyway*
4. **Meeting a celebrity (or someone who looked like one).** Connectors: *and she goes · I was like · turns out · in the end*
5. **A day when your phone died.** Connectors: *the thing is · at that point · long story short · the funny part is*
6. **A surprise party.** Connectors: *so basically · and then out of nowhere · and he goes · in the end*

### Answer key

**Exercise 1.2:** (1) So basically (2) The thing is (3) And then out of nowhere (4) I was like (5) Turns out (6) which was weird, because (7) Anyway (8) and she goes (9) Long story short (10) In the end

*Other answers are sometimes possible: for example "Anyway" could also work in gap 9. If yours makes sense when you say it out loud, it's fine.*

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: React in 2 seconds
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, timed

1. Read each piece of news. **React within 2 seconds**, with big, natural tone. Don't think — just react.
2. After the reaction, add **one short comment or question** ("No way! Where?").
3. Go through all 20 once. Then check the key and do all 20 again, faster, trying a *different* reaction each time.

1. "I just got a promotion."
2. "My flight got cancelled. I'm stuck here till tomorrow."
3. "I saw a guy walking a cat on a leash this morning."
4. "I left early because I had a headache."
5. "We finally finished the app redesign."
6. "My laptop died in the middle of my presentation."
7. "I'm moving to Canada next year."
8. "I didn't go to the party because I was too tired."
9. "My grandma's been in the hospital all week."
10. "The tickets were completely free."
11. "I had to work every weekend this month."
12. "I ran a half marathon on Sunday."
13. "The meeting was moved to 8 a.m."
14. "They changed the office Wi-Fi password again and nobody told us."
15. "We're having a baby!"
16. "I was so tired after the night shift."
17. "We use Figma instead of Sketch because the whole team can work on one file."
18. "I found 50 dollars in my old jacket."
19. "My landlord's raising the rent by 20%."
20. "I finally asked my manager for a raise."

### Exercise 2.2: Same words, different tone
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud, record

Your voice changes the meaning. Say each phrase in the **3 ways** shown. Record yourself, then listen: can you hear a clear difference? If not, exaggerate more.

| Phrase | Way 1 | Way 2 | Way 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Really? | interested (voice goes up high) | shocked (louder, longer: "Reeeally?") | bored (flat, low — **don't** use this one) |
| No way! | happy surprise (high, bright) | shocked by bad news (lower, slower) | "I don't believe you" (laughing) |
| Oh, nice! | small good news (short, bright) | very good news (longer: "Oh, NIIICE!") | polite but not excited (short, flat) |
| That's rough. | small bad news (light, quick) | serious bad news (slow, soft) | — |
| Seriously? | amazed (up, smiling) | annoyed ("Seriously?!" sharp) | — |
| Fair enough. | relaxed, accepting | slightly disappointed (slower) | — |

### Exercise 2.4: React to a story, chunk by chunk
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Read chunk 1 out loud as the storyteller.
2. Then **switch roles**: react out loud as the listener, in under 2 seconds. Add a short question if it fits.
3. Continue chunk by chunk. Use a **different** reaction every time.
4. Then do it again without looking at the key. Aim for 8 different reactions.

1. "So last Saturday I was supposed to go to my friend's wedding in the mountains."
2. "And I wake up, and my car won't start."
3. "So I call a taxi, and they say it'll cost like two hundred bucks."
4. "I didn't wanna miss the wedding, so I just said yes."
5. "And then halfway there, the taxi driver goes, 'Wait — are you going to Emma's wedding?'"
6. "Turns out he was Emma's cousin. He was going to the wedding too!"
7. "So he didn't charge me anything."
8. "And in the end, we sat at the same table and he gave a speech about how he met me."

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Put the story parts in order
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud

Each story has 4 parts, but they're mixed up. Decide the order (setting → problem → turning point → ending), then **tell the story out loud** in the right order, adding connectors between the parts.

**Story A**
- a) In the end, I gave my talk in socks, and honestly, nobody noticed.
- b) So this one time, I was speaking at a design conference in Austin.
- c) That's when a woman in the front row offered me her spare shoes — but they were two sizes too small.
- d) Ten minutes before my talk, I realized I'd left my shoes at the hotel. I was wearing flip-flops.

**Story B**
- a) Turns out the "dog" was a raccoon.
- b) This was back when I lived in a tiny apartment in Chicago.
- c) Every night I heard something in the kitchen, and I thought the neighbor's dog was getting in.
- d) Long story short, I moved out a month later. So yeah, lesson learned: close the window.

**Story C**
- a) So I was at the airport, waiting for my flight to Lisbon.
- b) The guy next to me goes, "Excuse me, is this the gate for Lisbon? Because the board says Berlin."
- c) I got so into my book that I didn't hear any announcements.
- d) In the end, we both ran to the right gate and made it with like two minutes left.

### Exercise 3.2: Cut it down
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, record

Each story below is too long and messy. Retell it **out loud in 4–6 sentences**, one or two per box. Record your version. Then compare with the model answer, and tell it once more.

**Long story 1:**
> OK so, it was, I think, a Thursday, maybe Wednesday, in the summer, July or August, and I was working from home, which I do on Thursdays, or Wednesdays, depends, and I had my coffee, the one I always get, and my cat was there, she's orange, her name is Mango, and I had this call at ten, no, ten thirty, with the whole product team, maybe fifteen people, and I was presenting the new onboarding flow, which I'd worked on for like three weeks, and Mango jumped on the keyboard and somehow ended the call for everyone, and I had to send a link again, and everyone was laughing when they came back, and my boss said Mango should join the team, so now she's kind of famous.

**Long story 2:**
> So my friend Jake, you don't know him, he's tall, he works in finance, he's from Ohio, he invited me to go fishing, and I've never been fishing, I don't really like fish actually, and we drove for two hours, his car is really old, it doesn't have AC, and it was super hot, and we got to the lake and he realized he'd forgotten all the fishing stuff at home, all of it, so we just sat there, and then we decided to rent a boat, and we just floated around and talked for like four hours, and it was honestly one of the best days of the summer.

### Exercise 3.3: Four-box speed stories
**Time:** 20 min · **Type:** out loud, record 2 of them

For each prompt: take **20 seconds** to think of one word per box (setting · problem · turning point · ending). Then tell the story in **1 minute**. Invent details if you need to — this is practice, not a confession. Do all 8 (about 12 minutes), then choose your best 2 and **record** them again.

1. A time you got lost.
2. A time you said the wrong thing to the wrong person.
3. A time technology let you down (a phone, a laptop, an app).
4. A time a stranger helped you.
5. The worst meal you've ever had.
6. A time you were really nervous before something.
7. A time something good happened by accident.
8. A time you were the last one to find out about something.

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.1:** Story A: b → d → c → a · Story B: b → c → a → d · Story C: a → c → b → d

**Exercise 3.2 — model answers:**

*Model 1:* So this one time, I was presenting a new onboarding flow on a call with the whole product team. I'd worked on it for three weeks, and I was super nervous. And then out of nowhere, my cat jumps on the keyboard and ends the call — for everyone. When they all came back, they were laughing so hard. In the end, my boss said the cat should join the team. So now she's kind of famous.

*Model 2:* So my friend took me fishing for the first time last summer. We drove two hours in his old car with no AC. Turns out he'd forgotten all the fishing stuff at home. So we just rented a boat and floated around talking for four hours. Honestly, it was one of the best days of the summer.

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Choose the tense
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Put the verb in brackets in the right form: past simple, past continuous (was/were + -ing) or past perfect (had + past participle). Use contractions where natural (I'd, we'd).
2. Check the key.
3. Read the whole story out loud twice, at normal speed.

> Last month I (1) ________ (work) on a big presentation for a client. It was late, and everyone else (2) ________ (already / go) home. I (3) ________ (sit) at my desk, eating cold pizza, when the lights suddenly (4) ________ (go) off. I (5) ________ (not / save) my slides in over an hour, so I (6) ________ (freak) out. While I (7) ________ (look) for my phone, I (8) ________ (hear) footsteps in the hallway. Turns out it (9) ________ (be) the security guard. He (10) ________ (just / turn) off the lights because he thought the office was empty. In the end, my laptop (11) ________ (save) everything automatically. I (12) ________ (be gonna) cry, but I laughed instead.

### Exercise 5.3: Describe from memory
**Time:** 20 min · **Type:** out loud, record 2

For each prompt, close your eyes for 10 seconds and picture it. Then describe it for **1 minute**, without notes. Use this checklist each time:

- 2 chunks for **what it looked like** (tiny, packed, gorgeous, run-down…)
- 1 chunk for a **sound or smell** (you could hear…, it smelled like…)
- 1 **"the kind of… who/where…"** sentence
- 1 **feeling** (how did you feel there or with them?)

Do all 8. Record prompt 4 and prompt 8.

1. The first apartment or house you remember living in.
2. Your favorite café or bar.
3. A teacher you'll never forget.
4. The busiest place you've ever been (a market, a station, a festival).
5. A colleague who makes work more fun.
6. A hotel or Airbnb you didn't like.
7. A family member who is "a character".
8. The office or building where you work now.
