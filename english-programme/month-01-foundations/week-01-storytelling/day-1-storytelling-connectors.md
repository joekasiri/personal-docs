- You've finished [Getting started](../../toolkit/getting-started.md): recording app, a folder for recordings, phrase log, and 3–4 saved clips.

## Learning objectives

- Record your 2-minute baseline and count your 3 numbers.
- Pronounce the American flap T ("wadder", "ged it") in common phrases.
- Know what each of the 12 storytelling connectors does and where it goes in a story.
- Say each connector 5 times in a sentence until it comes out smoothly.
- Use at least 5 connectors in a 4/3/2 work story.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: the flap T

In American English, a **T between two vowel sounds** usually becomes a very quick, soft **D**. Your tongue just taps the top of your mouth once, quickly. "Water" sounds like **"wadder"**. "Better" sounds like **"bedder"**.

It also happens **between words**: "get it" → **"ged it"**, "a lot of" → **"a lodda"**. And after an R: "party" → **"pardy"**.

It does **not** happen when the T starts a stressed syllable: "Tuesday", "atTACK", "hoTEL" keep a strong T.

| Written | Say it like | |
|---|---|---|
| water | WAH-der | "wadder" |
| better | BEH-der | "bedder" |
| a lot of | a LAH-da | "a lodda" |
| get it | GEH-dit | "ged it" |
| what about | wa-da-BOUT | "wadabout" |
| party | PAR-dee | "pardy" |

### Practice sentences

Say each sentence 3 times. Stressed words are in CAPITALS. Tap the flap T lightly; don't make it a hard D.

1. Can I get a bottle of WAter? → "Can I geddə BOD-dle of WAH-der?"
2. It's a little BETter now. → "It's a LID-dle BED-der now."
3. What about SATurday? → "Wadabout SAD-er-day?"
4. I'll get it LATer. → "I'll ged it LAY-der."
5. We got out at EIGHT. → "We godout at EIGHT."
6. Put it on the TAble. → "Pud it on the TAble."
7. It was a pretty CIty. → "It was a priddy CID-dee."
8. Let it GO, it doesn't MATter. → "Led it GO, it doesn't MAD-der."
9. I totally forGOT about it. → "I todally for-GODabout it."
10. Wait a MINute — what is it? → "Waida MIN-it — wadis it?"
11. I've got a LOT of work. → "I've godda LODDA work."
12. That was the best PARty ever. → "Thad was the best PAR-dee ever."

### Find a clip

Use the **pause-and-repeat** method from [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md): play one or two sentences, pause, repeat them exactly, copying speed and melody.

- Find a casual American speaker telling a story to camera or to a friend. Not a presentation, not a news reader.
- Search YouTube for: **"storytime vlog"**, **"casual vlog storytime"**, **"what happened at work storytime"**.
- Listen for words like *water, better, got it, a lot of, little, party, whatever*. Every time you hear a flap T, pause and repeat that sentence 3 times.
- Spend about 8 minutes on the sentences above and 12 minutes on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): The 12 storytelling connectors

### Explanation

Native speakers don't tell stories with "and… and… and then… and then…". They use **connectors**: short, ready-made chunks that move the story forward and tell the listener what's coming. "Turns out…" means *a surprise is coming*. "Long story short…" means *I'm skipping to the end*. "The funny part is…" means *get ready to laugh*.

Connectors help you in two ways:

1. **They make you sound natural.** A story full of connectors sounds like a real American telling a story to a friend.
2. **They buy you time.** While you say "So basically…", your brain is already finding the next sentence. A connector is a "free" chunk you don't have to build.

Here are the 12, grouped by where they usually go in a story:

- **To start or sum up:** So basically… · The thing is…
- **To move forward or jump:** And then out of nowhere… · At that point… · Anyway…
- **For surprises and weird things:** Turns out… · Which was weird, because… · The funny part is…
- **For what people said or thought:** I was like… · And he goes… / And she goes…
- **To finish:** Long story short… · In the end…

Two important notes:

- **"I was like…"** and **"he goes…"** introduce what someone said *or thought*, in a casual way. Follow them with the actual words: *I was like, "Are you serious?"*. They're informal, so use them with friends and colleagues, not in a job interview.
- **"Turns out…"** is short for "It turned out that…". In casual speech you just say "Turns out…" and then a past-tense sentence: *Turns out he was the new manager.*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So basically… | you start a story or give the short version | "So basically, our client changed the whole design two days before launch." |
| The thing is… | you explain the important background or the real problem | "The thing is, nobody had tested it on mobile." |
| Anyway… | you go back to the main story after a side detail, or move on | "Anyway, so I get to the office and…" |
| And then out of nowhere… | something sudden and unexpected happens | "And then out of nowhere, the whole site goes down." |
| Turns out… | you reveal a surprise or the real reason | "Turns out someone had deleted the main file." |
| Which was weird, because… | something didn't make sense | "He didn't reply, which was weird, because he always replies in like five minutes." |
| I was like… | you say what you said or thought, casually | "And I was like, 'OK, this is not happening.'" |
| And he goes… / And she goes… | you report what someone else said, casually | "And she goes, 'Did you even read my email?'" |
| Long story short… | you skip the boring middle and jump to the result | "Long story short, we worked all weekend and launched on Monday." |
| The funny part is… | you point to the funny or ironic detail | "The funny part is, the bug was in my own code." |
| At that point… | you describe the moment when things changed | "At that point, I just gave up and called IT." |
| In the end… | you tell the final result | "In the end, the client loved it." |

### Examples

**Model story (about 1 minute), a work story:**

> So basically, last month we were launching a new sign-up screen for our app. The thing is, we'd tested it a lot, so we were all pretty relaxed. And then out of nowhere, on launch morning, people start messaging us saying the button doesn't work. I was like, "What? It worked yesterday!" So I call our developer, and he goes, "Yeah, I see it. Give me ten minutes." Turns out someone had changed one tiny setting the night before. Which was weird, because nobody was supposed to touch anything. At that point my manager was literally standing behind me. Long story short, he fixed it in like fifteen minutes. The funny part is, the person who changed the setting was my manager. Anyway, in the end, it was fine, and now we have a rule: nobody touches anything the night before a launch.

**Mini-dialogue:**

> **A:** How was your weekend?
> **B:** Oh man. So basically, I was supposed to go hiking with my sister.
> **A:** Nice!
> **B:** Yeah, but the thing is, she forgot to book the car. So we get to the rental place, and the guy goes, "Sorry, we've got nothing."
> **A:** No!
> **B:** Yeah. Long story short, we took the bus. In the end, it was actually kinda fun.

**Mini-dialogue:**

> **A:** Did you ever get your package?
> **B:** Yeah, finally. Turns out it was at my neighbor's place the whole time. Which was weird, because the app said "delivered to front door".
> **A:** Ha! Classic.
> **B:** I know. The funny part is, my neighbor had been using the box as a table.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "At the end, we launched it." | ✅ "**In the end**, we launched it." — *"In the end" = finally, the result. "At the end" needs "of": at the end of the meeting.* |
| ❌ "It turned out, he is the manager." | ✅ "**Turns out he was** the manager." — *keep the story in the past.* |
| ❌ "And he said me, 'Give me ten minutes.'" | ✅ "And **he goes**, 'Give me ten minutes.'" or "And he **told** me…" — *"say" doesn't take a person directly.* |
| ❌ "I was like that I was angry." | ✅ "**I was like, 'Are you kidding me?'**" — *"I was like" is followed by the actual words or a feeling, not "that".* |
| ❌ "And then… and then… and then…" | ✅ "And then… **at that point**… **turns out**… **in the end**…" — *mix your connectors.* |
| ❌ "Long story short…" (then 3 more minutes) | ✅ "Long story short, **we missed the flight**." — *after "long story short", give the result fast.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: Baseline recording (12 min) — do this before you study

This recording is your starting point for the whole programme. **Don't prepare. Don't read anything first.** It's supposed to be messy.

1. Start recording. Name the file `W01-D1-baseline`.
2. Answer this prompt for **2 minutes** (use a timer):
   > **"Tell me about a day that didn't go as planned."**
   It can be any day: a trip, a work day, a weekend. Just start talking and keep going until the timer stops.
3. Stop. Don't listen yet. Take a 1-minute break.
4. Listen and count your 3 numbers ([how](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)): fillers (umm/uhh), long pauses (over 2 seconds), different connectors.
5. Write them in the **Baseline** table of your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). **Never delete this recording.**

### Part B: 4/3/2 with a work story (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a time something went wrong on a project at work."**
> A bug before a launch, a client who changed their mind at the last minute, a file that disappeared, a meeting that went badly. What was the project? What went wrong? What did you do? How did it end?

1. 30 seconds to remember the story. Pick **5 connectors** from tonight and say them once quietly: for example *So basically · The thing is · And then out of nowhere · Turns out · In the end*.
2. Round 1: **4 minutes**, recording.
3. Rest 30 seconds.
4. Round 2: **3 minutes**, recording. Add one connector you didn't use in round 1.
5. Rest 30 seconds.
6. Round 3: **2 minutes**, recording.
7. Listen to round 3 and count your 3 numbers. Write them in your nightly log.

### Part C: Connector upgrade (13 min)

1. Listen to round 1 again. Write down every place you said "and then" or stopped for a long time (5 min).
2. For each place, choose a connector that would fit there.
3. Tell the story one more time in **2 minutes**, recording, using **8 or more** connectors on purpose. It will feel forced. That's fine — tonight you're training your mouth, not performing (8 min).

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Choose the 5 connectors that felt hardest tonight. For each one, write **your own sentence** about your life (for example: *"Turns out the café near my office is closed on Mondays."*).
2. Say each of your 5 sentences out loud 3 times.
3. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): baseline numbers, round-3 numbers, the 5 phrases.
4. There's no "yesterday" to review tonight. Instead, read the 12 connectors once more, out loud, from top to bottom.

## Expected outcome

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] say your baseline numbers and know where they're written
- [ ] tap the flap T in "water", "get it" and "a lot of"
- [ ] say all 12 connectors from memory, in order of the story
- [ ] use at least 5 connectors in a 2-minute work story
