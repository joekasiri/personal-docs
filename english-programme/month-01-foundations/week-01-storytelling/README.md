## This week's goal

Stories are the heart of casual conversation. "What did you do this weekend?", "How was the trip?", "Anything new at work?" — every one of these questions is really asking for a short story. This week you learn the tools that make a story easy to tell and easy to follow: the **12 storytelling connectors**, the **reaction phrases** you use when someone else tells a story, a simple **four-part structure**, the **past tenses** that natives use in casual stories, and **vivid, casual description** of people and places. You also record your **baseline** on Day 1: 2 minutes, unprepared, before you learn anything. By Day 6 you should already hear a difference.

## How this builds on last week

This is the first week of the programme, so it builds on your setup from [Getting started](../../toolkit/getting-started.md): your recording app, your recordings folder, your phrase log and your clips. If you haven't done those yet, do them before Day 1. Day 1 needs them.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Go for a walk and listen to one episode (or 30 minutes) of a casual podcast where two or three Americans chat and tell each other stories. Search for "two friends podcast chat", "comedy podcast storytime" or "best friends podcast episode".

Your listening task while you walk:
1. Every time a speaker starts a story, notice **how** they start it ("So this one time…", "OK so last week…").
2. Count how many of this week's **12 connectors** you hear. Say each one quietly to yourself when you hear it.
3. Notice how the *listener* reacts. Catch **3 reactions** you didn't learn this week and log them tomorrow.

Then stop. No drills, no exercises. Your brain locks in what you practised while you rest.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] use at least 4 of the 12 storytelling connectors in a 2-minute story without planning them
- [ ] react to someone else's story with the right phrase for good news, bad news and surprises
- [ ] tell a story in four clear parts: setting, problem, turning point, ending
- [ ] use "I was walking home when…" and "I'd already…" correctly in a casual story
- [ ] describe a person and a place in a lively, casual way ("super crowded", "kinda sketchy")
- [ ] say the flap T, gonna/wanna, and linked words like "pick it up" naturally
- [ ] count your 3 numbers on a recording in under 10 minutes
