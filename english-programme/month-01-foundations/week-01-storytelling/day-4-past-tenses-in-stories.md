## Learning objectives

- Hear and copy English **sentence rhythm**: stressed words long and clear, the rest short and squashed.
- Use **past simple** for the main events of a story.
- Use **past continuous** ("I was walking…") for the background and for an action that got interrupted.
- Use **"had already / had just"** for something that happened *before* another past moment.
- Tell stories to Claude in voice mode and collect your past-tense mistakes.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: sentence stress and rhythm

English has a "big-small-small-big" rhythm. The **important words** (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, negatives like *didn't*) are **STRESSED**: longer, louder, clearer. The **small words** (a, the, to, was, of, and, I, you, it) are **squashed** — short, quick and quiet.

Many learners give every word the same weight: "I. Was. Walk-ing. To. The. Sta-tion." That sounds slow and robotic. Americans say: "i-wuz-WALK-in-tuh-the-STA-tion." Only two words are really strong.

Try tapping the table on each stressed word. The taps should feel **evenly spaced**, even when there are many small words between them.

| Sentence | Rhythm (CAPITALS = stressed) |
|---|---|
| I was walking to the station. | i-wuz-WALK-in-tuh-the-STA-tion |
| I'd already left. | i'd-al-READ-y-LEFT |
| What were you doing? | wut-wer-ya-DO-in? |

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. Tap on the CAPITALS. Squash everything else.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Past tenses in stories

### Explanation

Casual stories mostly use three past forms. You know them all from grammar books. Tonight the goal is to use them **fast and automatically**, as chunks.

**1. Past simple — the main events.** These are the things that *happened*, one after another. Most of your story is past simple.
> *I **got** to the airport, **checked** the board, and **saw** that my flight was cancelled.*

**2. Past continuous (was/were + -ing) — the background, or an action that got interrupted.** It "sets the scene" while the main event happens.
> *I **was walking** home when I **saw** my ex.* (walking = background; saw = main event)
> *Everyone **was dancing**, the music **was** super loud…*

The classic pattern is: **was + -ing … when + past simple.**
> *I was cooking dinner **when** the fire alarm went off.*

**3. "Had already / had just" + past participle — something earlier.** Use it when you need to say "this happened *before* that other past moment". In speech it's usually shortened: **I'd, we'd, she'd**.
> *When I got to the gate, the plane **had already left**.* (it left first, then I arrived)
> *I**'d just** sat down when my phone rang.* (sat down a second before)

Don't use "had" for everything. Use it only when the order would be confusing without it. Most of the time, past simple is enough.

**Bonus chunk: "was gonna".** Native speakers use **"I was gonna…"** for a plan in the past that didn't happen: *I was gonna call you, but my phone died.*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I was (doing) when… | an action in progress got interrupted | "I was walking home when this guy started following me." |
| While I was… | two things happened at the same time | "While I was waiting, I started chatting with the woman next to me." |
| Everyone was… / It was… | you describe the background scene | "It was raining and everyone was in a bad mood." |
| I was about to… when… | something stopped you just before you did it | "I was about to hit send when I saw the typo." |
| I was gonna… but… | a past plan that didn't happen | "I was gonna take the bus, but it was packed." |
| I'd already… | something finished before another past moment | "By the time I replied, they'd already hired someone." |
| I'd just… when… | something had happened a moment before | "I'd just gotten out of the shower when the doorbell rang." |
| By the time… | the second event came too late | "By the time we got there, the kitchen had closed." |
| I hadn't even… | you emphasise something that hadn't happened yet | "I hadn't even opened my laptop when the first bug report came in." |
| We were still… | something hadn't finished yet | "We were still eating when they started cleaning up." |

### Examples

**Model story (past tenses in bold):**

> So last Friday I **was working** late, right? Everyone else **had already gone** home. I **was** about to leave when my laptop **made** this horrible noise and the screen **went** black. I **was** like, "No, no, no." I **hadn't saved** my file in like two hours. So I **called** my friend who works in IT, and she **was** at a party — I could hear everyone **singing** in the background. But she **talked** me through it. Turns out the laptop **had** just **restarted** for an update. In the end, the file **was** fine. I **was gonna** buy her a drink to say thanks, but she **said** pizza **was** better.

**Dialogue: asking about the story**

> **A:** What were you doing when it happened?
> **B:** I was just sitting there, eating lunch.
> **A:** And had you told anyone?
> **B:** No, I hadn't even told my manager yet.
> **A:** Oh no. So what happened?
> **B:** She walked in while I was reading the email.

**Dialogue: quick stories**

> **A:** Why are you so late?
> **B:** Ugh. I was literally walking out the door when my landlord called.
> **A:** Oh no.
> **B:** And by the time I got off the phone, I'd already missed my bus.
> **A:** That's rough.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "I was walk home." | ✅ "I was **walking** home." — *past continuous needs -ing.* |
| ❌ "When I walked home, I was seeing a fox." | ✅ "When I **was walking** home, I **saw** a fox." — *the background gets -ing; the short event is past simple.* |
| ❌ "When I arrived, the train already left." | ✅ "When I arrived, the train **had already left**." — *it left before you arrived.* |
| ❌ "I had gone to the store, I had bought milk, and I had gone home." | ✅ "I **went** to the store, **bought** milk, and **went** home." — *events in order = past simple. Don't overuse "had".* |
| ❌ "I was knowing him from college." | ✅ "I **knew** him from college." — *"know", "like", "want", "need" don't usually take -ing.* |
| ❌ "Yesterday I go to the office and I see my boss…" (when you don't mean to) | ✅ "Yesterday I **went** to the office and I **saw** my boss…" — *stay in the past unless you're doing it on purpose.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): Voice mode with Claude

Tonight there's no 4/3/2. You tell stories to Claude, answer its questions, and get corrections on your past tenses. Setup is in [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

### Steps

1. **Setup (3 min):** open a new chat in the Claude app. Paste the prompt below. Switch to voice mode. Start recording on your phone if you can.
2. **Session (30 min):** tell 3 stories — your work story, your trip story, your embarrassing story. Answer every question Claude asks. Ask Claude at least **3 questions back** ("What would you have done?", "Has that ever happened to you?").
3. **Feedback (7 min):** say "feedback". Write down every correction.
4. **Repeat (5 min):** say each corrected sentence out loud 3 times.

### Prompt to paste

```
I'm practising casual spoken American English. My level is B2 and my goal is to speak fast and relaxed without thinking.

Please:
- Talk to me like a friendly American friend, casually, using contractions and everyday phrases.
- Keep your turns short (1–3 sentences) so I do most of the talking.
- Ask me follow-up questions so I have to keep going.
- Don't correct me during the conversation. Just remember my mistakes.
- When I say "feedback", stop and give me: my 5 most important mistakes with the correct version, 3 phrases I could have used to sound more natural, and one thing I did well.

Tonight's topic and focus:
I'm going to tell you 3 short stories from my life: a work problem, a weekend trip, and an embarrassing moment. Start by asking me "So what happened at work?" After each story, ask me 2–3 questions about it, especially questions like "What were you doing when…?", "Had you already…?" and "What happened next?", so I have to use past simple, past continuous and "had already". In your feedback, focus on my past tense mistakes first, then my use of storytelling connectors like "turns out", "at that point" and "in the end".
```

### What to focus on

- Use **"I was … when …"** at least once in every story.
- Use **"I'd already…"** or **"By the time…"** at least twice in the whole session.
- Use 4+ [connectors](day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and react to Claude's comments with [reaction phrases](day-2-reaction-phrases.md).
- Keep each story to [4 boxes](day-3-story-structure.md). Claude's questions will make it longer — that's good.
