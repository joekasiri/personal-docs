# Week 3 · Day 1: Conversation openers

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Last week you made [questions](../week-02-questions/README.md) automatic. Tonight you use them to **start** a conversation.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, a clip of two people chatting casually (see section 1).
- Think about the last 3 times someone said "How are you?" to you in any language. What did you answer?

## Learning objectives

- Squash **to → "ta"** and **for → "fer"** in the middle of a sentence.
- Use the most common American openers for friends, colleagues and strangers.
- Understand that "How's it going?" is a greeting, not a real question — and answer it the way natives do.
- Answer openers with **more than "fine"**: a quick answer plus a little detail.
- Start a conversation with a stranger using the situation you're both in.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: weak forms "ta" and "fer"

Small words like *to*, *for*, *and*, *of*, *can*, *them* are almost never said "fully" in the middle of a sentence. They become **weak forms**: short, quiet, with a "uh" sound (the sound in "the" or "a"). This is a big reason why American speech sounds so fast to learners.

| Word | Full (strong) | Weak | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| to | "TOO" | **"ta"** / "tə" | I need ta GO. |
| for | "FOR" | **"fer"** / "fə" | Thanks fer COMing. |

**Important:** at the **end** of a sentence, or when you stress them, these words stay strong: *Who's it FOR? · Where are you going TO?* Only the middle ones are squashed.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. I'm going to the STORE. → "I'm goin' ta the STORE."
2. Thanks for COMing. → "Thanks fer COMin'."
3. Nice to MEET you. → "Nice ta MEET ya."
4. What's for DINner? → "What's fer DINner?"
5. I've been waiting for HOURS. → "I've been waitin' fer HOURS."
6. Do you want to GRAB lunch? → "D'ya want ta GRAB lunch?" (or "wanna")
7. Good to SEE you! → "Good ta SEE ya!"
8. It's for my SISter. → "It's fer my SISter."
9. I talked to him for like an HOUR. → "I talked ta him fer like an HOUR."
10. We went to Chicago for the WEEKend. → "We went ta Chicago fer the WEEKend."
11. Who's it FOR? → "Who's it FOR?" (end of sentence = strong)
12. Back to WORK, I guess. → "Back ta WORK, I guess."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"two friends podcast chat"**, **"catching up with my best friend vlog"**, **"coffee chat podcast"**.
- Focus on the **first 2 minutes** of the clip — that's where people greet each other and open the chat.
- Every time you hear "ta" or "fer", pause and repeat the sentence 3 times.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Conversation openers

### Explanation

**Openers are greetings, not real questions.** When an American says "How's it going?" or "How are you?", they usually don't want your health report. They want a quick, friendly answer — and then the chat can begin. But a one-word "Fine." can feel like a closed door. The best answers are **short + a tiny detail**, which gives the other person something to ask about.

| Opener | ❌ Closed answer | ✅ Natural answer |
|---|---|---|
| How's it going? | Fine. | Pretty good! Kinda busy, but good. You? |
| How was your weekend? | Good. | Really nice, actually. I finally went to that new market. |
| What've you been up to? | Nothing. | Not much, honestly. Just work and trying to get to the gym. |

**Three kinds of openers:**

1. **With friends and colleagues you know:** *How's it going? · How are you doing? · What's up? · What've you been up to? · How was your weekend? · How's work? · How've you been?* (for someone you haven't seen in a while) · *Long time no see!*
2. **With strangers** — use the **situation** you're both in. You're at the same party, the same line, the same event, the same bad weather: *So how do you know Sam? · Is this your first time here? · Have you tried the food yet? · This line is crazy, huh? · Are you here for the conference?*
3. **At work:** *Busy day? · How's the project going? · Did you have a good weekend? · Got any plans for the weekend?* (Friday) · *How was your vacation?*

**"What's up?"** is special: the normal answer is **"Not much, you?"** or **"Not much. Just…"**. Don't answer with a long story.

**Mirror the opener.** If someone asks "How's it going?", ask them back — "How about you?", "You?", "How's your week going?". It's rude to forget.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How's it going? | the most common casual greeting | A: "Hey, how's it going?" B: "Pretty good! You?" |
| What've you been up to? | you haven't seen them for a few days or weeks | "Hey stranger! What've you been up to?" |
| How've you been? | you haven't seen them for a long time | "Oh my gosh, hi! How've you been?" |
| How was your weekend? | Monday or Tuesday | "Morning! How was your weekend?" |
| What's up? | very casual, friends and young colleagues | A: "What's up?" B: "Not much, you?" |
| So how do you know [host]? | you meet a stranger at a party or event | "So how do you know Maya?" |
| Is this your first time here? | at an event, class, meetup, gym | "Is this your first time at one of these?" |
| Pretty good! / Not bad! | a positive answer | "Not bad! Better now that it's Friday." |
| Can't complain. | a relaxed "OK" answer | "Can't complain. Work's fine, weather's nice." |
| Honestly? A little tired. | an honest, slightly negative answer | "Honestly? A little tired. Long week." |
| Busy, but good. | the classic work answer | "Busy, but good. We're launching next week." |
| Oh, you know, same old. | nothing new | "Oh, you know, same old. Work, gym, sleep." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: Monday at the office**

> **A:** Morning! How was your weekend?
> **B:** Really nice, actually. I went hiking for the first time in like a year.
> **A:** Oh, nice! Where'd you go?
> **B:** Just up in the hills outside the city. How about you? Do anything fun?
> **A:** Not really. Mostly just cleaned my apartment.
> **B:** Ha, that's kind of fun too. Sort of.

**Dialogue 2: a friend you haven't seen in months**

> **A:** No way! Hi! How've you been?
> **B:** Good, good! Super busy, but good. I changed jobs, actually.
> **A:** Wait, really? Where are you now?
> **B:** A small startup. Way more stressful, but I love it. What about you? What've you been up to?
> **A:** Oh, you know, same old. Same job, same apartment. But I got a cat!

**Dialogue 3: a stranger at a friend's party**

> **A:** Hey, so how do you know Ana?
> **B:** We work together. I'm on her team. You?
> **A:** We went to college together. Is this your first time at her place?
> **B:** Yeah! It's gorgeous. I didn't know she had a balcony.
> **A:** I know, right? Have you tried the dumplings yet? She made them herself.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ A: "How's it going?" B: "Fine." (silence) | ✅ "**Pretty good! Kinda busy, but good. You?**" — *add a detail and ask back.* |
| ❌ A: "How are you?" B: (2 minutes about your health) | ✅ "**Honestly? A little tired.** Long week." — *openers want a short answer first.* |
| ❌ A: "What's up?" B: "The sky!" / "I'm fine, thank you." | ✅ "**Not much, you?**" — *"What's up?" = "What's new?". "Fine" doesn't answer it.* |
| ❌ "How do you do?" | ✅ "**Nice to meet you.**" — *"How do you do?" sounds very old-fashioned in the US.* |
| ❌ "I'm fine, thank you, and you?" (like a textbook) | ✅ "**Good, thanks! How about you?**" — *same idea, but relaxed.* |
| ❌ Opening a stranger with "What's your job?" | ✅ Start with the **situation**: "So how do you know Sam?" — *a job question first can feel like a job interview.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — an interesting stranger (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about the most interesting conversation you've had with a stranger."**
> On a plane, on a train, at a party, in a line, at a conference. Who started it — and how? What was the opener? What did you talk about? Did you ever see them again?

- Include **the opener** in your story: *"And she goes, 'Is this your first time in Denver?'"*
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and [describe the person](../week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md).
- **New rule from this week:** end every round with **a question to the listener**: *"Have you ever had a conversation like that with someone you didn't know?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: 30 openers in 10 minutes (15 min)

Stand up and walk around the room. Imagine **5 people** in front of you, one at a time. For each one, say an opener, answer it yourself as them (short + detail), then answer their "you?" as yourself. 6 exchanges each. Record it.

1. A coworker on Monday morning.
2. A friend you haven't seen in 6 months.
3. A stranger at a friend's birthday party.
4. Someone next to you at a design meetup.
5. Your neighbor in the elevator on Friday evening.

### Part C: The "How are you?" bank (10 min)

Write **5 answers to "How's it going?"** that are true for you this week — one positive, one neutral, one tired, one busy, one excited. Say each 5 times. These are now your ready-made answers for Day 6.

1. **Reread Week 2 Day 6's phrases** — the questions you starred. Ask each one out loud, then answer it.
2. **Log 5 new phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): 2 openers and 3 of your new answers from Part C.
3. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
