# Week 3 · Day 2: Echo questions and showing interest

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- You know how to [open a chat](day-1-conversation-openers.md), and you learned [reaction phrases](../week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md) in Week 1 ("No way!", "That's rough"). Tonight you add two more listening tools: **echo questions** and **back-channels**. We won't re-teach the reactions — you'll combine them with tonight's phrases.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, a clip of two people chatting where one tells a story.

## Learning objectives

- Squash **and → "'n"** in common pairs ("rock 'n roll", "you 'n me").
- Use **echo questions** to show surprise and interest: "You went to Japan?" · "You did?"
- Use **auxiliary echoes**: "I've been to Peru." → "Oh, you have?"
- Use **back-channels** (uh-huh, mm-hm, right, oh yeah?) to show you're listening without interrupting.
- Combine echo + reaction + follow-up: "Japan? No way! When?"

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: and → "'n"

In the middle of a sentence, **and** is usually just **"'n"** — a quick N sound, sometimes with a tiny "uh" before it ("ən"). The D disappears.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| rock and roll | "rock 'n roll" |
| salt and pepper | "salt 'n pepper" |
| you and me | "you 'n me" |
| come and see | "come 'n see" |
| black and white | "black 'n white" |

At the **start** of a sentence, when it's a connector ("And then out of nowhere…"), it's usually a bit stronger: "AN' then…".

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. Me and my SISter went. → "Me 'n my SISter went."
2. Fish and CHIPS, please. → "Fish 'n CHIPS, please."
3. Come and SEE this! → "Come 'n SEE this!"
4. We talked and talked for HOURS. → "We talked 'n talked fer HOURS."
5. It was nice and QUIet. → "It was nice 'n QUIet."
6. Go and GRAB a seat. → "Go 'n GRAB a seat."
7. Bread and BUTter. → "Bread 'n BUTter."
8. I woke up and it was SNOWing. → "I woke up 'n it was SNOWin'."
9. Try and RELAX. → "Try 'n reLAX."
10. Up and DOWN, up and DOWN. → "Up 'n DOWN, up 'n DOWN."
11. You and me should go SOMEtime. → "You 'n me should go SOMEtime."
12. Wait and SEE. → "Wait 'n SEE."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- This time, shadow the **listener** as much as the speaker: their "uh-huh", "wait, what?", "you did?". Copy the tone exactly.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Echo questions and showing interest

### Explanation

When someone talks to you, there are three levels of "I'm listening":

| Level | What it is | When | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Back-channel** | a tiny sound or word, *while* they talk | every few sentences | uh-huh · mm-hm · right · yeah · OK · oh yeah? · wow |
| **2. Echo question** | you repeat part of what they said, with a **rising** voice | when you hear something surprising or interesting | "Japan?" · "You did?" · "Six hours?" |
| **3. Reaction + follow-up** | a full reaction and a question ([Week 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md), [Week 2](../week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md)) | when they finish a point | "No way! What happened?" |

**Back-channels** keep the speaker going. Without them, an American speaker may stop and ask "Are you OK?" or "Does that make sense?". Use them every 2–3 sentences, quietly, without interrupting. **Nodding** helps too.

**Echo questions** come in two types:

**Type 1: key-word echo** — repeat the surprising word or phrase, with your voice going **up** ↗.
> A: "I went to Japan last week." → B: "**Japan?** ↗ Wow!"
> A: "The meeting lasted six hours." → B: "**Six hours?** ↗"
> A: "She quit." → B: "**She quit?** ↗ Like, today?"

**Type 2: auxiliary echo** — just the **subject + helper verb**, rising. This is very natural and very American.

| They say | You echo |
|---|---|
| I've been to Peru. | Oh, **you have?** |
| I don't drink coffee. | **You don't?** Wow. |
| She's moving to London. | **She is?** |
| We went to the beach. | Oh, **you did?** |
| He can speak five languages. | **He can?** |
| It was free. | **It was?** |
| They're getting married. | **They are?** That's awesome! |

The rule: copy the helper verb (have, do, is, did, can, was…). If there's no helper ("We **went**"), use **do / does / did**. Keep it positive if their sentence is positive, negative if it's negative ("I **don't** drink coffee" → "You **don't**?").

**The combo:** echo + reaction + follow-up is the most natural "interested listener" chunk in English:
> *"You did? No way! How was it?"* · *"Six hours? That's rough. What happened?"*

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Uh-huh. / Mm-hm. | they're talking and you want them to continue | A: "So I get to the office…" B: "Uh-huh." |
| Right. / Yeah. | you follow and agree | A: "…and it's always the same people." B: "Right." |
| Oh yeah? | you're interested, "tell me more" | A: "I started a new hobby." B: "Oh yeah? What?" |
| Really? / Wow. | light surprise while they talk | A: "He's only 23." B: "Wow." |
| [Key word]? ↗ | you echo the surprising part | A: "We drove to Canada." B: "Canada?" |
| You did? / You have? / You are? | auxiliary echo | A: "I met the CEO." B: "You did?" |
| You don't? / You didn't? | auxiliary echo of a negative | A: "I've never seen snow." B: "You haven't?" |
| Wait, [repeat]? | the surprise is big | A: "She left at midnight." B: "Wait, midnight?" |
| Like, [detail]? | you check the extreme part | A: "I ate all of it." B: "Like, the whole pizza?" |
| Gotcha. / OK. | you understand, keep going | A: "It's the second door on the left." B: "Gotcha." |
| And then? | you want the story to continue | A: "So the police show up…" B: "And then?" |
| Oh wow, I didn't know that. | you learn something new | A: "This building used to be a factory." B: "Oh wow, I didn't know that." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a colleague's weekend**

> **A:** So on Saturday I went to this cooking class…
> **B:** Oh yeah?
> **A:** Yeah, Korean food. And the teacher was this tiny old lady…
> **B:** Uh-huh.
> **A:** …and she made us make kimchi with our hands. Like, no gloves.
> **B:** No gloves?
> **A:** None! My hands were red for two days.
> **B:** Two days? Ha! That's rough. Was the kimchi good, at least?
> **A:** Honestly? Amazing.

**Dialogue 2: auxiliary echoes**

> **A:** I've never been on a plane.
> **B:** You haven't? Wow. Like, never?
> **A:** Never. I'm going to Spain next month, though.
> **B:** You are? That's awesome! Are you nervous?
> **A:** Kinda. My brother's coming with me.
> **B:** Oh, he is? Nice. Has he flown before?

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ A: "I went to Japan." B: "Japan." ↘ (flat) | ✅ "**Japan?** ↗" — *a falling echo sounds bored or annoyed. Make it rise.* |
| ❌ A: "I've been to Peru." B: "You did?" | ✅ "**You have?**" — *copy the helper verb: have → have.* |
| ❌ A: "We went to the beach." B: "You went?" | ✅ "**You did?**" — *no helper in their sentence → use did.* |
| ❌ A: "I don't eat meat." B: "You do?" | ✅ "**You don't?**" — *negative stays negative.* |
| ❌ Silence for 2 minutes, then "OK." | ✅ "Uh-huh… Right… Oh yeah?" every few sentences — *silence makes the speaker unsure.* |
| ❌ Back-channelling so loudly that you interrupt | ✅ Keep back-channels **short and quiet**, in the speaker's pauses. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — something surprising you found out (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about something surprising you found out recently."**
> About a friend or family member, a place, your job, a fact you read, a secret, something about yourself. How did you find out? What did you say when you heard it? What happened next?

- Include **your own reaction** inside the story with an echo: *"And she goes, 'I'm moving to Australia.' And I was like, 'Australia? Like, for good?'"*
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md), especially *Turns out…* and *Which was weird, because…*.
- End every round with **a question to the listener**: *"Has anyone ever surprised you like that?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Echo yourself (15 min)

1. Play your **round 1** recording.
2. Be the listener: **back-channel** quietly while it plays (uh-huh, right, oh yeah?), and **pause** at every surprising detail to give an **echo** — key-word ("Australia?") or auxiliary ("She is?") — plus a follow-up.
3. Target: **10 back-channels, 6 echoes, 4 follow-ups**. Tally them on paper.

### Part C: Echo ping-pong (10 min)

Say each sentence below as if a friend said it, then answer with an **auxiliary echo + reaction + follow-up**, fast. Go through the list twice.

1. I've started running every morning.
2. My boss doesn't use email.
3. We're getting a puppy.
4. I didn't go to the wedding.
5. My grandpa can speak four languages.
6. The concert was cancelled.
7. I've never tried sushi.
8. She's been to every country in South America.
