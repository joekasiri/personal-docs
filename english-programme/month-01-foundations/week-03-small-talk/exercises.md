# Week 3 exercises: Small talk
[Week 3 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-conversation-openers.md) · [Day 2](day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md) · [Day 3](day-3-answer-add-ask.md) · [Day 4](day-4-time-buyers.md) · [Day 5](day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** small talk is fast, so do everything **out loud and fast**. Don't aim for the perfect answer — aim for an answer that comes out in under 3 seconds. Where an exercise says **record**, record it and listen back once. Model answers are just examples: if your answer is natural and true for you, it's right.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Rapid-fire openers
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, timed

1. Read each opener as if someone just said it to you.
2. Answer in **under 5 seconds**: a short answer + a tiny detail + (where it fits) "You?" / "How about you?".
3. Do all 20. Then read the model answers, and do all 20 again with **different** details.

1. How's it going?
2. How are you doing?
3. What's up?
4. What've you been up to?
5. How was your weekend?
6. How've you been? (you haven't seen them for a year)
7. Long time no see!
8. How's work?
9. Busy day?
10. Got any plans for the weekend?
11. How was your vacation?
12. How's the new apartment?
13. How's your day going?
14. Is this your first time here?
15. So how do you know Mike?
16. How's the project going?
17. Did you have a good weekend?
18. What's new?
19. Are you here for the conference?
20. How are you holding up? (it's been a hard week)

### Exercise 1.2: Upgrade "fine"
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

Each answer below is closed — it gives the other person nothing to ask about. Say a **better answer** out loud: short answer + one detail + ask back. Then compare with the model.

1. A: How's it going? — B: Fine.
2. A: How was your weekend? — B: Good.
3. A: What've you been up to? — B: Nothing.
4. A: How's work? — B: OK.
5. A: What's up? — B: I'm fine, thank you.
6. A: How was the trip? — B: Nice.
7. A: Busy day? — B: Yes.
8. A: How've you been? — B: Good, and you?
9. A: Got any plans for the weekend? — B: No.
10. A: How's the new job? — B: It's fine.

6. A colleague just came back from two weeks' vacation.

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Auxiliary echoes
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud, timed

1. Read each statement, then answer with an **auxiliary echo** (You have? / She is? / You didn't?) with a **rising** voice ↗, plus a quick reaction.
2. Aim for 3 seconds per statement.
3. Check the key, then do all 20 again, faster.

1. I've been to Brazil three times.
2. I don't like chocolate.
3. My brother's a pilot.
4. We went to the new museum on Sunday.
5. I can't swim.
6. She's moving to Seattle.
7. They didn't come to the party.
8. I've never seen the ocean.
9. He works from home every day.
10. I was born in Canada.
11. We're getting married in June.
12. My manager doesn't use Slack.
13. I quit coffee last month.
14. The office is closing for a week.
15. I haven't finished the slides yet.
16. My cat can open doors.
17. They're not coming tonight.
18. I'll be in New York next week.
19. I didn't sleep at all last night.
20. Our team has six new designers.

### Exercise 2.2: Key-word echo + reaction + follow-up
**Time:** 13 min · **Type:** out loud

For each statement, do the **combo**: echo the surprising **key word** (rising ↗), then a [reaction](../week-01-storytelling/day-2-reaction-phrases.md), then a [follow-up](../week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md). Example: *"I drove to Montreal." → "Montreal? No way! How long did that take?"*

1. "I waited at the doctor's for four hours."
2. "My grandma just started a YouTube channel."
3. "We had a meeting with forty people."
4. "I ate a whole pizza by myself."
5. "My neighbor has eleven cats."
6. "I woke up at 4 a.m. to watch the game."
7. "Our client wants the app finished in two weeks."
8. "I ran into my ex at the supermarket."
9. "The tickets were three hundred dollars each."
10. "My flight got moved to Tuesday."
11. "I read the whole book in one night."
12. "They gave me a parking spot right next to the door."

### Exercise 2.3: Back-channel a monologue
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** record, then out loud

1. **Record** yourself reading the monologue below at a normal, relaxed speed. At each **✱**, stay silent for **2 seconds** (count in your head).
2. Play it back. At each silence, say a **back-channel** (uh-huh, mm-hm, right, oh yeah?, wow) — or an **echo** if something surprising just happened.
3. Use at least **8 different** back-channels and echoes. Tick them on paper.
4. Then do the same with 5 minutes of a real podcast where someone tells a story (pause it yourself).

> So last month my company decided we should all try a "no-meeting week". ✱ Like, no meetings at all, for five days. ✱ And at first everyone was super excited, right? ✱ But then on Monday, my manager sends this message, like, "Quick sync?" ✱ And I'm like… that's a meeting. ✱ So I don't reply. ✱ And then on Tuesday, three people from the design team start a "walk and talk". ✱ Which is basically a meeting, but outside. ✱ By Wednesday, there were like six "walks" a day. ✱ People were walking around the parking lot with laptops. ✱ And then Thursday, the CEO walks in and goes, "Why is nobody at their desk?" ✱ Turns out he'd forgotten about the no-meeting week. ✱ He'd planned an all-hands meeting. ✱ For Friday. ✱ So yeah. ✱ Our no-meeting week ended with the biggest meeting of the year.

### Answer key

**Exercise 2.1:**
1. You have? Wow! 2. You don't? Seriously? 3. He is? That's so cool! 4. Oh, you did? How was it? 5. You can't? 6. She is? Wow. 7. They didn't? How come? 8. You haven't? Never? 9. He does? Lucky! 10. You were? I didn't know that! 11. You are? Congrats! 12. He doesn't? / She doesn't? Seriously? 13. You did? Good for you! 14. It is? Why? 15. You haven't? Uh-oh. 16. She can? / He can? No way! 17. They're not? Oh, that's too bad. 18. You will? Nice! 19. You didn't? That's rough. 20. It does? Wow, that's a lot.

*Note on 13 and 4: the sentence has no helper ("I quit", "We went"), so the echo uses "did".*

**Exercise 2.2 — model combos:**
1. Four hours? That's rough. What happened? 2. YouTube? No way! What's it about? 3. Forty people? Seriously? Did anyone actually talk? 4. The whole pizza? Ha! How do you feel? 5. Eleven? You're kidding. Does it smell? 6. 4 a.m.? Wow. Was it worth it? 7. Two weeks? Oh no. Is that even possible? 8. Your ex? Oh no! What did you say? 9. Three hundred? Each? Who's playing? 10. Tuesday? Ugh, that's so annoying. How come? 11. In one night? Wow. What's the book? 12. Right next to the door? That's awesome! How'd you get that?

**Exercise 2.3 — possible back-channels, in order:** Mm-hm. · No meetings? · Right. · Uh-huh. · Ha, right. · OK. · Uh-huh. · Ha! · Six? · Wow. · Oh no! · No way! · Oh no. · Friday? · Ha. · (laugh) That's amazing.

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Bridge two topics
**Time:** 12 min · **Type:** out loud

Your friend says the first sentence. Jump to the **new topic** using a bridge (Speaking of… / That reminds me… / Oh, by the way… / Totally random, but…). Say it as one smooth chunk. Use each bridge at least twice.

1. "I've been so tired this week." → new topic: a good mattress you bought
2. "We went to an Italian restaurant last night." → new topic: your trip to Rome next month
3. "My sister just had a baby." → new topic: your friend's birthday party on Saturday
4. "The traffic was crazy this morning." → new topic: your new bike
5. "I've been watching a lot of cooking shows." → new topic: a cooking class you want to try together
6. "The client loved the new designs!" → new topic: the team dinner on Friday
7. "I finally cleaned my apartment." → new topic: you need to borrow their vacuum
8. "It's been raining all week." → new topic: a hiking trip you're planning
9. "Work's been really stressful." → new topic: a good yoga class you found
10. "I just finished a great book." → new topic: a podcast you think they'd like
11. "I can't believe it's already December." → new topic: holiday plans
12. "My phone's so slow." → new topic: you still have their charger

### Exercise 5.2: End the chat
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

End each conversation politely in **3 steps**: signal (Anyway… / Well…) + reason or nice comment + friendly goodbye with a future. Say each one twice, the second time faster.

1. A colleague at the coffee machine. You have a meeting in 2 minutes.
2. An old friend you bumped into on the street. You want to meet properly soon.
3. Someone you just met at a party. Your friends are leaving.
4. Your neighbor in the hallway. Your food is getting cold.
5. A friend who has a job interview tomorrow.
6. A phone call with your aunt that's gone on for 40 minutes.
7. A person at a meetup who you'd like to see again next month.
8. A colleague who's going on vacation tomorrow.

### Exercise 5.3: The full small-talk loop
**Time:** 18 min · **Type:** out loud, record scenario 3

For each scenario, perform a full conversation **playing both people** (turn your head for each one). The partner's key lines are given; **you** improvise your lines. Every one of your turns must use this week's skills, in this order:

1. **Open** ([Day 1](day-1-conversation-openers.md))
2. **Echo or back-channel** at least twice ([Day 2](day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md))
3. **Answer, add, ask back** at least 3 times ([Day 3](day-3-answer-add-ask.md))
4. **Time-buyer** for the hard question ([Day 4](day-4-time-buyers.md))
5. **Change topic** once, then **end** the chat (tonight)

**Scenario 1: Monday at the office (5 min)**
Partner's lines (use them in any order): *"Good, thanks! I went camping." · "It rained the whole time." · "So, do you think we'll finish the project this month?" (hard question) · "Oh yeah, I'll be there Friday."*

**Scenario 2: A friend's party (5 min)**
Partner's lines: *"I'm Alex, I'm Jordan's roommate." · "I just moved here from Texas." · "What's the best thing about living here?" (hard question) · "Oh, I love tacos."*

**Scenario 3: A design meetup (6 min, record it)**
Partner's lines: *"Yeah, it's my second time here." · "I'm a UX researcher at a bank." · "What's the hardest part of your job?" (hard question) · "Speaking of coffee, the one here is terrible."*
