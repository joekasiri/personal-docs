# Week 3 · Day 3: Answer, add, ask back

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- You can [open a chat](day-1-conversation-openers.md) and [show interest](day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md) while the other person talks. Tonight is about **your turn**: how to answer so the conversation never dies.
- In [Week 2 Day 4](../week-02-questions/day-4-question-tags-and-short-answers.md) you learned short answers ("Yeah, I did."). Tonight they become the first step of a three-step answer.
- Have ready: phone for recording, timer, a clip of two people chatting.

## Learning objectives

- Squash **of → "a"** in common chunks ("a lot of" → "a lodda", "kind of" → "kinda").
- Use the **answer, add, ask back** pattern for every question you get.
- Choose a good "add": a detail, a reason, a feeling or a mini-story.
- Ask back **specifically**, not only "And you?".
- Keep a 10-exchange "ping-pong" conversation going without one-word answers.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: of → "a"

Before a consonant, **of** often loses its V and becomes just **"a"** (the "uh" sound). Some of these are so common they have their own spelling.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| a lot of people | "a lodda people" (flap T!) |
| kind of | "kinda" |
| sort of | "sorta" |
| out of | "outta" |
| a cup of coffee | "a cuppa coffee" |
| one of my friends | "one a my friends" |
| some of them | "some a them" |
| because of the rain | "'cause a the rain" |

Before a vowel, the V usually stays: "a lot of apps" → "a lodda-vapps" (linking, from [Week 1 Day 3](../week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md#1-shadowing-20-min)).

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. There were a lot of PEOple. → "There were a lodda PEOple."
2. It's kind of FAR. → "It's kinda FAR."
3. One of my FRIENDS told me. → "One a my FRIENDS told me."
4. I ran out of TIME. → "I ran outta TIME."
5. Can I get a cup of COFfee? → "C'n I get a cuppa COFfee?"
6. Most of the TIME, I work from home. → "Most a the TIME, I work from home."
7. It was sort of WEIRD. → "It was sorta WEIRD."
8. Get out of HERE! → "Get outta HERE!"
9. Some of them were LATE. → "Some a them were LATE."
10. We stayed in because of the RAIN. → "We stayed in 'cause a the RAIN."
11. That's the best part of my DAY. → "That's the best part a my DAY."
12. I've got a lot of apps on my PHONE. → "I've got a lodda-vapps on my PHONE."

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"get to know me Q&A vlog"**, **"answering your questions casual"**, **"chatting with my roommate"**. People answering casual questions use "kinda", "a lot of" and "one of my" constantly.
- Shadow their **answers**: notice how they answer → add → sometimes ask back.
- 8 minutes on the sentences above, 12 on the clip.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Answer, add, ask back

### Explanation

The fastest way to kill a conversation is a one-word answer:

> A: "Did you have a good weekend?"
> B: "Yeah."
> A: "…Cool."

Now the other person has to start again from zero. Do that three times, and they give up. The fix is a simple 3-step pattern — **answer, add, ask back**:

| Step | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Answer** | Give the short answer. | "Yeah, it was nice!" |
| **2. Add** | Add **one** detail — something they can ask about. | "I finally tried that new ramen place." |
| **3. Ask back** | Ask them something related. | "Have you been?" |

> *"Did you have a good weekend?" → "Yeah, it was nice! I finally tried that new ramen place. Have you been?"*

That's 3 seconds of talking, and now the conversation has two directions to go: your ramen, or their experience. Use this for **every** question in small talk until it becomes automatic.

**What makes a good "add"?** One of these four:

| Type | Example (question: "How's work?") |
|---|---|
| A **detail** | "Busy! We're redesigning the whole app." |
| A **reason** | "Pretty stressful, 'cause we're launching next week." |
| A **feeling** | "Honestly, I'm kinda loving it right now." |
| A **mini-story** (1–2 sentences) | "Good! Funny thing happened, actually — my laptop died in the middle of a demo." |

Keep the add **short**: one sentence, maybe two. You're opening a door, not giving a speech.

**Ask back specifically.** "And you?" / "How about you?" is fine and safe. But a **specific** ask-back is even better, because it shows you're thinking about them:

> ❌ OK: "How about you?"
> ✅ Better: "Did you end up going to that concert?" · "How's the new apartment?" · "Are you a ramen person?"

**Recycle your question skills:** your ask-back can be any question from [Week 2](../week-02-questions/README.md) — direct, a tag ("You like ramen, right?"), or indirect ("Do you know if they deliver?").

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How about you? / What about you? | the safe ask-back | "…I mostly stayed home. How about you?" |
| Have you been? | they could have visited the place you mention | "We went to that new taco place. Have you been?" |
| Have you ever…? | you ask about their experience | "I tried surfing. Have you ever tried it?" |
| Are you into…? | you ask about their interests | "I've been watching a lot of basketball. Are you into sports?" |
| Do you…too? | you check if they share your habit | "I run before work. Do you run too?" |
| What are you up to this weekend? | the classic Friday ask-back | "…can't wait to sleep in. What are you up to this weekend?" |
| Did you end up…? | you remember something they planned | "…Oh, did you end up going to the wedding?" |
| Actually, … / Funny thing, … | you start your "add" with a mini-story | "Good! Actually, funny thing happened this morning…" |
| …, 'cause… | you add a reason | "Kinda tired, 'cause my neighbor had a party." |
| Honestly, … | you add an honest feeling | "Honestly, I'm kinda bored of my job." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: ping-pong at the coffee machine**

> **A:** Hey! How's it going?
> **B:** Pretty good! Kinda sleepy, 'cause I was up late watching the game. Did you see it?
> **A:** No, I missed it! I was at my sister's place. Who won?
> **B:** We did, in the last minute. It was crazy. Are you into basketball?
> **A:** Not really, honestly. I'm more of a soccer person. Do you play anything?
> **B:** I used to play tennis, but I haven't played in like two years. What about you, do you play soccer?
> **A:** Every Sunday, with some friends from work. You should come sometime!

**Dialogue 2: meeting someone at a meetup**

> **A:** So what do you do?
> **B:** I'm a product designer. I work on a fitness app — the kind that yells at you to drink water. What about you?
> **A:** Ha! I'm in marketing, for a travel company.
> **B:** Oh, nice. Do you get to travel a lot for work?
> **A:** Sometimes. I was in Mexico City last month, actually. Have you been?
> **B:** Not yet! It's on my list. What's the one thing I have to do there?

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ A: "Do you like your job?" B: "Yes." | ✅ "**Yeah, I do! I get to work on a lot of different projects. Do you like yours?**" — *answer, add, ask back.* |
| ❌ A: "How was your trip?" B: (3 minutes, no question) | ✅ Answer + **one** short add + ask back. — *if they want more, they'll ask a follow-up.* |
| ❌ Always "And you?" | ✅ Mix in **specific** ask-backs: "Have you been?", "Are you into…?" |
| ❌ Asking back about something unrelated | ✅ Ask back about the **same topic**: ramen → "Are you a ramen person?" — *it keeps the thread going.* |
| ❌ "I like it very much my job." | ✅ "I **really like my job**." — *"very much" goes after the object, and sounds formal; "really" is more natural.* |
| ❌ A: "How's work?" B: "Fine, and you?" | ✅ "**Busy, but good — we're launching next week. How's yours going?**" — *"fine" gives them nothing to ask about.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — your weekends (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about your typical weekend — and then about the best weekend you've had this year."**
> What do you usually do on Saturday and Sunday? Who with? Then: what made one weekend this year special? Where were you? What happened?

- This is a classic small-talk topic, so use lots of **adds**: details, reasons, feelings.
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and at least 3 "of" chunks (a lot of, kind of, one of my…).
- End every round with **a specific ask-back** to the listener: *"What's your perfect weekend?"* or *"Are you more of a stay-at-home person or a go-out person?"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Ping-pong with yourself (15 min)

Record a **10-exchange** small-talk conversation, playing both people (turn your head left for person A, right for person B, like in [Week 2 Day 5](../week-02-questions/day-5-interview-yourself.md)). **Every single turn** must be answer + add + ask back. Start with: *"Hey! Did you do anything fun this weekend?"*

Listen back. Mark every turn that broke the rule (no add, or no ask-back). Do it again until you have 10 perfect turns in a row.

### Part C: Rescue the dead chat (10 min)

Here's a conversation that died. Say it out loud, then **say B's lines again** with answer-add-ask. Do it twice.

> **A:** Hi! How are you? — **B:** Fine.
> **A:** Busy week? — **B:** Yes.
> **A:** Any plans for the weekend? — **B:** No.
> **A:** Have you been here long? — **B:** Two years.
> **A:** Do you like it? — **B:** Yes, it's OK.

**Model rescue** (check *after* your own version):
> **B1:** Pretty good, thanks! A little tired, 'cause I had a big deadline yesterday. How about you?
> **B2:** Yeah, kinda crazy. We're testing a new feature with users. Is your week busy too?
> **B3:** Not really, honestly. I'm just gonna sleep in and maybe see a movie. What are you up to?
> **B4:** Like two years, yeah. I moved here for this job. Are you from around here?
> **B5:** Yeah, I do! The team's super nice, and the coffee's free. Do you like your job?
