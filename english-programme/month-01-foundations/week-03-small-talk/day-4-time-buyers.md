## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: can → "c'n" vs CAN'T

This one is important for being understood. In a normal sentence, **can** is weak and short: **"c'n"** (kn). **Can't** is strong and long, with a clear vowel: **"CAN'T"** (the T is often almost silent — you'll study that in [Week 4](../week-04-vocabulary/README.md)).

| Sentence | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| I can go. | "I c'n GO." (short, quiet "c'n", stress on GO) |
| I can't go. | "I CAN'T go." (long, stressed "CAN'T") |

So Americans tell "can" and "can't" apart mostly by **stress and length**, not by the T. If you stress "can", people may hear "can't"!

**Exception:** at the end of a sentence or in short answers, "can" is strong: *Yes, I CAN. · I think I CAN.*

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

1. I can HELP you. → "I c'n HELP ya."
2. I CAN'T help you. → "I CAN'T help ya."
3. Can you SEE it? → "C'n ya SEE it?"
4. We can MEET at six. → "We c'n MEET at six."
5. I CAN'T beLIEVE it! → "I CAN'T b'LIEVE it!"
6. You can ASK her. → "You c'n ASK 'er."
7. She can't COME. → "She CAN'T come."
8. I can do it toMORrow. → "I c'n do it t'MORrow."
9. Can I ASK you something? → "C'n I ASK ya somethin'?"
10. Yes, I CAN. → "Yes, I CAN." (end = strong)
11. I can TRY. → "I c'n TRY."
12. We CAN'T be LATE. → "We CAN'T be LATE."

**Test yourself:** record sentences 1, 2, 6 and 7 in a random order. Listen back the next day. Can you hear which is which?

### Find a clip

Pause-and-repeat ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)).

- Search YouTube for **"answering hard questions podcast"**, **"questions I've never been asked"**, **"deep questions with friends"**. People answering tough questions use lots of time-buyers — perfect for tonight.
- Shadow the **first 3 seconds** of every answer. That's where the time-buyers are.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Time-buyers

### Explanation

Everyone needs time to think — native speakers too. The difference is **what they say while they think**. A learner goes silent or says "ummmm…". A native says *"Oh, good question…"* or *"Let me think…"* or *"I mean…"*.

Why does it matter?

- **Silence** feels awkward and makes people jump in or think you didn't understand.
- **Lots of "umm"** sounds nervous and unsure, and it's one of your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md).
- **Time-buyers** sound relaxed and natural. They tell the listener: *"I heard you, I'm thinking, an answer is coming."* And they give your brain the same 1–2 seconds an "umm" would.

Natives do say "um" sometimes — that's normal. The goal isn't zero. The goal is to replace **most** of your umms with real words.

**Match the time-buyer to the situation:**

| Situation | Time-buyers |
|---|---|
| A hard or unexpected question | Oh, good question. · Hmm, that's a tough one. · Let me think… · Oh man, let me see… |
| You need to remember something | Let me see… · What was it… · When was that… · Oh, it was… |
| A personal or sensitive question | Honestly? · Well… · How do I put this… |
| Giving an opinion | I mean… · I'd say… · I guess… · It's kind of hard to say, but… |
| Starting to explain something complicated | So… · OK, so… · The thing is… ([Week 1](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md)) |
| You want to say something surprising | You know what? · Actually… |

**Combine two** for a longer pause: *"Hmm, good question. Let me think…"* That gives you about 3 seconds — enough for almost anything.

**Don't use the same one every time.** "Good question" for every question sounds strange. Have 3–4 favourites and rotate them.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Oh, good question. | someone asks something you need to think about | "Oh, good question. Probably Lisbon." |
| Hmm, that's a tough one. | the question is really hard to choose or answer | "Hmm, that's a tough one. Pizza or tacos… tacos." |
| Let me think… | you need a second | "Let me think… I've been here about three years." |
| Let me see… | you're remembering or checking | "Let me see… the last time was in May, I think." |
| Well… | a soft start, often before a "not simple" answer | "Well… it's complicated." |
| I mean… | you start or clarify an opinion | "I mean, it's not bad. It's just expensive." |
| Honestly? | you're about to give a true, maybe surprising answer | "Honestly? I don't really like my job." |
| I'd say… | you give an estimate or careful opinion | "I'd say… like two hours?" |
| How do I put this… | you need to say something delicate | "How do I put this… he's very… confident." |
| It's kind of hard to say, but… | there isn't a simple answer | "It's kind of hard to say, but I think I'm happier here." |
| You know what? | you've just decided, or have a new idea | "You know what? I'll come with you." |
| Oh man, … | a big or emotional question | "Oh man, my worst day? OK, so…" |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: getting-to-know-you questions**

> **A:** So if you could live anywhere, where would it be?
> **B:** Oh, good question. Let me think… probably somewhere by the ocean. Like Portugal, maybe.
> **A:** Nice. Why Portugal?
> **B:** I mean, the food, the weather… and honestly? It's way cheaper than here.
> **A:** Ha, fair enough. What's the hardest thing about living here?
> **B:** Hmm, that's a tough one. I'd say… the winters. They're so long.

**Dialogue 2: at work**

> **A:** How long do you think the redesign will take?
> **B:** Let me see… we need research, then testing… I'd say six weeks?
> **A:** OK. And what do you think of the new logo?
> **B:** How do I put this… it's very… bright.
> **A:** You hate it.
> **B:** I don't hate it! It's kind of hard to say, but I think it needs more time.

**Same answer, with and without time-buyers:**

> ❌ *"Ummm… ummm… I think… uhh… Japan."*
> ✅ *"Oh, good question. Let me think… I'd say Japan."*

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ "Ummmm… uhhh… ummm…" (5 seconds) | ✅ "**Hmm, good question. Let me think…**" — *same time, but you sound relaxed and in control.* |
| ❌ "Good question!" for every question, even "What's your name?" | ✅ Use "good question" only for **real** thinking questions — *otherwise it sounds sarcastic.* |
| ❌ "Let me think about this question." | ✅ "**Let me think…**" — *keep it short; the long version sounds formal.* |
| ❌ "I mean" + nothing (the time-buyer becomes your new "umm") | ✅ Follow the time-buyer **with your answer** — *a time-buyer starts an answer; it doesn't replace it.* |
| ❌ "Honestly…" before every sentence | ✅ Use "honestly" for **honest or surprising** answers — *too much and it loses its meaning.* |
| ❌ Silence, then a full perfect sentence | ✅ Start talking **straight away** with a time-buyer, then build — *natives don't wait until the sentence is ready.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): Voice mode with Claude

Setup is in [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

### Steps

1. **Setup (3 min):** new chat, paste the prompt below, switch to voice mode, start recording on your phone.
2. **Tough-question round (15 min):** Claude asks you unexpected and hard questions. **Start every answer with a time-buyer** — never with "umm" or silence. Then answer, add, ask back.
3. **Small-talk round (15 min):** Claude plays a new colleague at the coffee machine. Use [openers](day-1-conversation-openers.md), [echo questions](day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md) and [answer, add, ask back](day-3-answer-add-ask.md).
4. **Feedback (7 min):** say "feedback". Write down every correction and Claude's umm count.
5. **Repeat (5 min):** say each corrected sentence 3 times.

### Prompt to paste

```
I'm practising casual spoken American English. My level is B2 and my goal is to speak fast and relaxed without thinking.

Please:
- Talk to me like a friendly American friend, casually, using contractions and everyday phrases.
- Keep your turns short (1–3 sentences) so I do most of the talking.
- Ask me follow-up questions so I have to keep going.
- Don't correct me during the conversation. Just remember my mistakes.
- When I say "feedback", stop and give me: my 5 most important mistakes with the correct version, 3 phrases I could have used to sound more natural, and one thing I did well.

Tonight's topic and focus:
Time-buyers instead of "umm". We'll do 2 rounds.
Round 1: ask me unexpected, slightly hard questions, one at a time, like "What's something you've changed your mind about?", "What would you do with a free year?", "What's the worst advice you've ever gotten?". Don't give me time to prepare.
Round 2: you're a new colleague I meet at the coffee machine on a Monday. Make normal small talk with me.
I'm trying to start every answer with a natural time-buyer like "Good question", "Let me think", "I mean", "Honestly?", "Hmm, that's a tough one" instead of "umm" or silence. In your feedback, tell me roughly how many times I said "umm" or "uhh", which time-buyers I used, and which ones I overused.
```

### What to focus on

- **Every answer starts with a word, not a silence.** Even "Well…" is better than "ummm".
- Use at least **6 different** time-buyers in round 1. Tick them off on paper.
- In round 2, every answer = answer + add + ask back.
- When Claude says something surprising, use an echo: "You did?"
