## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

### Tonight's sound: them → "'em" + all the weak forms

**Them** after a verb or preposition loses its "th" and becomes **"'em"** (sounds like "um"). It links to the word before.

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| tell them | "TELL-em" |
| call them | "CALL-em" |
| let them know | "LET-em know" |
| ask them | "ASK-em" |
| with them | "WITH-em" |

Tonight, you also review **all** of this week's weak forms together: ta, fer, 'n, a, c'n, 'em.

### Practice sentences

Say each 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed.

2. Just CALL them. → "Just CALL-em."
3. Let them KNOW. → "LET-em KNOW."
4. Did you ASK them? → "Didja ASK-em?"
5. I went with them to the BEACH. → "I went WITH-em ta the BEACH."
6. I can't FIND them. → "I CAN'T FIND-em."
7. Tell them I said HI. → "TELL-em I said HI."
8. Thanks for coming, and say hi to your MOM. → "Thanks fer COMin', 'n say hi ta yer MOM."
9. I can call them for you LATer. → "I c'n CALL-em fer ya LAYder."
10. I talked to them for an HOUR. → "I talked ta-'em fer an HOUR."
11. Some of them were GREAT. → "Some a-'em were GREAT."

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Changing topic and ending

### Explanation

**Part 1: Changing topic.** Natural conversations jump from topic to topic. The trick is to make the jump feel **connected**, even if it isn't really. Natives use a few bridge phrases:

| Bridge | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaking of…** | You take a word from what they just said and jump to a new topic about it. | A: "…we ate so much pasta." B: "Speaking of pasta, have you tried the new Italian place?" |
| **That reminds me…** | What they said made you remember something. | "That reminds me — did you ever get your camera back?" |
| **By the way… / Oh, by the way…** | A new topic, not connected. Sounds natural with "oh". | "Oh, by the way, are you coming on Friday?" |
| **Random, but…** / **Totally random, but…** | You admit it's not connected (very casual). | "Totally random, but do you know a good dentist?" |
| **Oh, before I forget…** | You suddenly remember something important. | "Oh, before I forget — I've got your charger." |
| **Anyway, …** / **So anyway, …** | You end a side topic and go back, or move on. | "Anyway, enough about me. How's your new job?" |
| **Going back to…** | You return to an earlier topic. | "Going back to the trip — when are you leaving?" |

**Part 2: Ending a chat.** Many learners feel stuck in a conversation because they don't know how to leave without sounding rude. Americans usually end in **3 steps**:

1. **Signal** that you need to go: *Anyway… · Well… · OK, so…*
2. **Give a reason (optional) or a nice comment:** *I should probably get going. · I gotta run, I've got a meeting. · It was great catching up.*
3. **Say a friendly goodbye with a future:** *Let's do this again soon! · See you Monday! · Let me know how it goes! · Say hi to Sam for me.*

> *"Anyway, I'll let you go — I know you're busy. It was so good to see you! Let me know how the interview goes."*

**"I'll let you go"** is a polite, very American way to end: it sounds like you're ending the chat *for them* ("you're busy"), even if *you* want to leave.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking of… | you jump from a word they used | "Speaking of coffee, have you been to the new place on 5th?" |
| That reminds me… | their topic makes you remember something | "That reminds me, I still owe you twenty bucks." |
| Oh, by the way… | you start an unconnected topic | "Oh, by the way, Jess says hi." |
| Totally random, but… | the new topic is clearly not connected | "Totally random, but do you like board games?" |
| Anyway, enough about me. | you've talked too long; give them a turn | "Anyway, enough about me. How was your trip?" |
| Going back to… | you return to an earlier topic | "Going back to what you said about the job — are you gonna apply?" |
| Anyway, I'll let you go. | the polite, classic ending | "Anyway, I'll let you go. Have a good one!" |
| I should probably get going. | you need to leave | "Oh wow, it's late. I should probably get going." |
| I gotta run. | casual, you're in a hurry | "Sorry, I gotta run — I'm late for a call." |
| It was great catching up. | you end a chat with someone you know | "It was great catching up! Let's do lunch soon." |
| Nice talking to you! | you end a chat with someone new | "Nice talking to you! Enjoy the rest of the party." |
| Let me know how it goes! | they have something coming (interview, trip) | "Good luck tomorrow! Let me know how it goes." |

### Examples

**Dialogue 1: a full small-talk loop**

> **A:** Hey! How's it going?
> **B:** Pretty good! Just got back from Denver, actually.
> **A:** Oh, nice! For work?
> **B:** Yeah, a conference. The food was amazing, though. I ate so much barbecue.
> **A:** Ha! Speaking of food, did you try the new lunch place downstairs?
> **B:** Not yet! Is it good?
> **A:** So good. Oh, that reminds me — are you coming to Lisa's goodbye lunch on Friday?
> **B:** Yeah, definitely. Anyway, I'll let you go, I know you've got that meeting.
> **A:** Ugh, yeah. Good to see you! Let me know if you wanna try the lunch place.
> **B:** For sure. See ya!

**Dialogue 2: ending with someone new at a party**

> **A:** …so yeah, that's how I ended up in Chicago.
> **B:** That's such a good story. Oh, I think my friends are leaving — I should probably get going.
> **A:** Oh, no worries!
> **B:** It was really nice talking to you. Good luck with the new apartment!
> **A:** Thanks! Nice to meet you too. Enjoy the rest of your night.

### Common mistakes

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Jumping to a new topic with no bridge: "…OK. I have a dog." | ✅ "**Oh, by the way**, did I tell you I got a dog?" — *a bridge makes the jump feel natural.* |
| ❌ "Speaking about food…" | ✅ "**Speaking of** food…" — *the fixed phrase is "speaking of".* |
| ❌ "I must go now. Goodbye." | ✅ "**Anyway, I should probably get going.** It was great catching up!" — *"I must go" sounds very formal and sudden.* |
| ❌ Just walking away when the chat slows down | ✅ Signal + nice comment + goodbye — *three short steps, about 5 seconds.* |
| ❌ "I let you go." | ✅ "**I'll let you go.**" — *you need "will" ('ll): it's an offer.* |
| ❌ Ending, then starting a new topic, then ending again… | ✅ Once you've said "I'll let you go", **actually go** — *a long goodbye is a very common (and funny) American problem.* |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

### Part A: 4/3/2 — a chance meeting (20 min)

Follow [The 4/3/2 drill](../../toolkit/drill-4-3-2.md).

**Prompt:**
> **"Tell me about a time you met someone by chance, and they became important in your life."**
> A friend, a partner, a mentor, a colleague, a roommate. Where were you? Who started talking — and how? What did you talk about? When did you realize they'd be important?

- Include the **opener** you or they used, and how the first chat **ended** ("And she goes, 'Anyway, I'll let you go — but here's my number.'").
- Use 4+ [connectors](../week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md).
- End every round with **a question to the listener**, then a **topic bridge** and an **ending**, like: *"Have you ever met someone important totally by accident? … Oh, that reminds me — I need to call my friend. Anyway, I'll let you go!"*
- Round 1: 4 min · rest · Round 2: 3 min · rest · Round 3: 2 min. Count your 3 numbers on round 3.

### Part B: Topic hopping (15 min)

Record a **5-minute** monologue where you talk to an imaginary friend and **change topic every 45–60 seconds**, using a different bridge each time. Start with work. Then: Speaking of… → That reminds me… → Oh, by the way… → Totally random, but… → Going back to… → end with "Anyway, I'll let you go." Listen back: did every jump feel connected?
