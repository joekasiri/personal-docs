# The 3/2/1 drill

[Programme home](../README.md) · [Toolkit](README.md) · Used from [Month 2](../month-02-speed/README.md) · Before this: [4/3/2](drill-4-3-2.md)

## What it is

The same idea as [4/3/2](drill-4-3-2.md), but faster: tell the same story, opinion or explanation in **3 minutes**, then **2 minutes**, then **1 minute**. The 1-minute round is about the length of one natural "turn" in a real conversation. That's exactly the skill you're training.

## How to do it

1. Read tonight's prompt. **15 seconds** to think. No notes.
2. **3 minutes**, recording.
3. **20-second rest.**
4. **2 minutes**, recording.
5. **20-second rest.**
6. **1 minute**, recording. Keep all the main points, lose everything else.
7. Count your [3 numbers](the-3-numbers.md) on the 1-minute round. Log them.

## What's different from 4/3/2

- **Less thinking time** (15 seconds instead of 30).
- **The 1-minute round must sound like conversation, not a speech.** Start the way people really do: "Oh man, so…", "OK so basically…", "Honestly? I think…"
- **End with a question** in the 1-minute round, like you're handing the turn to a friend: "Has that ever happened to you?", "What do you think?"
- From month 3, you'll do **one 3/2/1 round straight after a random-topic drill**, on whichever topic you found hardest.

## Not just stories

In month 2 you use 3/2/1 for:

| Week | Use it for | Example prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Stories | "The worst trip you've ever taken" |
| 6 | Opinions | "Is working from home better than working in an office?" |
| 7 | Explanations | "Explain how your job works to a 15-year-old" |
| 8 | Automatic answers | "Tell me about yourself" (you'll use this answer for the rest of your life) |

## Self-check after the 1-minute round

- [ ] I started talking within 2 seconds
- [ ] I used at least 2 connectors
- [ ] I used at least 2 of tonight's phrases
- [ ] I ended with a question or a clear finish ("So yeah, that's basically it.")
- [ ] It sounded like a conversation, not a presentation
