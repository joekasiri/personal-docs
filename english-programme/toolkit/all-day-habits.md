# All-day habits

[Programme home](../README.md) · [Toolkit](README.md)

The 2 hours at night are training. These habits are what make English **the language your brain reaches for first**. They need no extra time. You do them while you're doing something else.

## 1. Think in English (inner narration)

In your head, describe what you're doing as you do it, the way an American would say it out loud:

- "OK, I'm gonna grab a coffee first. Then I really need to reply to that email."
- "Ugh, the train's late again. Whatever, I'll just listen to something."
- "Wait, where did I put my keys? Oh, there they are."

When you hit a word you don't know, **don't stop**. Go around it ("the thing you use to open cans") and note the real word later.

## 2. Talk to yourself out loud

In the shower, while cooking, while walking alone, in the car. Even 5 minutes counts:

- Tell yourself about your day as if you're telling a friend
- Explain what you're cooking, step by step
- Argue with yourself about something in the news
- Repeat this week's phrases in new sentences

## 3. Switch your world to English

- Phone and computer language: English
- Social media: follow a few American creators who chat casually
- Background listening while doing chores: casual podcasts, not the news

## 4. The "how would an American say that?" game

When a sentence comes to mind in your own language, stop and ask: *how would an American say that?* If you don't know, write it down and look it up tonight, or ask Claude. These are the most useful phrases you'll ever log, because they're the things **you** actually want to say.

## 5. Catch-and-log

Whenever you hear a phrase you like in a podcast, a meeting or a show, add it to your [phrase log](phrase-log.md) straight away, with where you heard it.

## A realistic target

| Month | Daily habit goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | Inner narration 3 times a day, 1 minute each |
| 2 | Plus 5 minutes of talking to yourself out loud |
| 3–4 | Inner narration becomes your default when you're alone |
| 5–6 | You notice you sometimes *think* in English without deciding to |
