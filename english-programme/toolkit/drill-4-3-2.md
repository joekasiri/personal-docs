# The 4/3/2 drill

[Programme home](../README.md) · [Toolkit](README.md) · Used in [Month 1](../month-01-foundations/README.md) · Next step: [3/2/1](drill-3-2-1.md)

## What it is

You tell **the same story three times**: first in **4 minutes**, then **3 minutes**, then **2 minutes**. Same content, less time each round. Your brain is forced to find words faster, drop the slow parts and reuse the phrases that worked. By round 3 you're usually faster *and* smoother than in round 1.

## Why it works

- **Round 1 (4 min):** you search for words. Lots of pauses. That's fine.
- **Round 2 (3 min):** you already know what you want to say, so your brain spends its energy on *how*.
- **Round 3 (2 min):** the good phrases from rounds 1 and 2 come back automatically. This is what "talking without thinking" feels like, on a small scale.

## How to do it

1. **Read tonight's prompt** in your lesson. Give yourself **30 seconds** to remember the story. Don't write anything.
2. **Start a timer for 4 minutes** and start recording. Tell the story as if to a friend. Keep talking until the timer stops. If you finish early, add details.
3. **Rest 30 seconds.** Think about one phrase from tonight's lesson you want to use.
4. **3 minutes**, recording. Same story.
5. **Rest 30 seconds.**
6. **2 minutes**, recording. Same story. Every main point still has to be in.
7. **Listen to round 3 only** and count your [3 numbers](the-3-numbers.md). Write them in your [nightly log](nightly-log.md).

## Rules

- **Imagine a real listener.** A friend, a colleague, someone at a party. Talk *to* them.
- **Never stop for more than 2 seconds.** Stuck? Say "anyway…" and keep going.
- **Use tonight's phrases on purpose**, at least 3 times per round.
- **Don't correct yourself mid-sentence** in month 1. Finish the sentence badly, then carry on.

## A quick example (round 3, 2 minutes)

> "So basically, last month I had this huge presentation at work, right? And the thing is, I'd prepared for like a week. But then out of nowhere, the projector just dies. Like, completely. Everyone's staring at me. And I was like, OK… what do I do now? Turns out I knew the slides so well I could just *talk* it. Which was weird, because I'm usually super nervous. Long story short, my manager said it was the best one I've done. So, yeah. Sometimes things breaking is actually good."

## Common problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "I run out of story after 1 minute" | Add a scene (where were you? who was there?), a feeling (how did you feel?) and what people said |
| "Round 3 is worse than round 1" | You're trying to say everything faster. Instead, *cut* the boring parts |
| "I keep using 'and then… and then'" | Pick 3 connectors from [Week 1 Day 1](../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) before you start and use them on purpose |
