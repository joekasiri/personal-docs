# Live conversations

[Programme home](../README.md) · [Toolkit](README.md)

Practice at home builds the skill. **Live conversations prove it works under real pressure**, and they're the only way to lose the fear of speaking in front of people. You need 1 a week in months 1–2, 2 a week in months 3–4, and 3 a week in months 5–6.

## Where to find people

| Option | Good for | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Colleagues** | Easy to arrange, real work English | "Hey, wanna grab a coffee this week?" Most people say yes. Keep it 15 minutes. |
| **Language exchange apps** | Free, lots of native speakers | Look for people who want to learn *your* language. You split the time 50/50 between languages. |
| **Local meetups** | Groups, months 4–6 | Search a meetup site for "English conversation", "language exchange" or interest groups (hiking, board games, design) held in English |
| **Online tutors** | Guaranteed practice, corrections | Book "conversation practice", not "lessons". Tell them: "I want to talk, not study." |
| **Friends abroad / online communities** | Relaxed chat | Voice chats on community platforms around your hobbies |

## Before each conversation (5 minutes)

1. Read the **mission** in your lesson (every Day 6 has one).
2. Pick **3 phrases** from this week to use. Write them on your hand, a sticky note or your phone.
3. Prepare **2 questions** to ask the other person.
4. Remind yourself: *they care about the conversation, not your grammar.*

## During

- **Aim to talk at least 50% of the time.**
- **Use your 3 phrases.** Even if it's a bit forced, that's how they become yours.
- **When you don't understand,** say so naturally: "Sorry, say that again?", "Wait, what do you mean by…?"
- **When you get stuck,** go around the word. Don't stop.

## After (5 minutes)

Write down in your [nightly log](nightly-log.md):
- How long you talked, and with whom
- Which of your 3 phrases you used
- One moment you felt stuck, and what you could have said
- One moment that went well

## Nerves

Feeling nervous before speaking is normal, even for native speakers. Things that help:

- **Start small.** Week 1's conversation can be 10 minutes with a friendly colleague.
- **Say it up front:** "Just so you know, I'm practising my English, so be patient with me." People become kinder and you relax.
- **Plan your first line.** The first 30 seconds are the hardest. After that, it gets easier.
- **Count it as a win** whatever happens. Turning up is the hard part.

## Backup plan

If a conversation falls through, **don't skip it**. Do a 20–30 minute [voice mode session with Claude](voice-mode-with-claude.md) on the same mission, and add "Interrupt me sometimes and change topics, like a real person would" to the setup prompt.
