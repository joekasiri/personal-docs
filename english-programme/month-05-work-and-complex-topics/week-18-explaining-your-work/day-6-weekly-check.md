# Week 18 · Day 6: Weekly check
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5](../README.md) › [Week 18](README.md) · [← Previous: Success and failure stories](day-5-success-and-failure-stories.md) · [Next: Week 19 · News and current events →](../week-19-complex-topics/day-1-news-and-current-events.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 15 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 15 min

## Before you start

- Have your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) open with this week's numbers and last week's Day 6 numbers ([Week 17 weekly check](../week-17-meetings/day-6-weekly-check.md)).
- Have your recording app ready. You'll need a quiet room for the re-record test.
- Book your third live conversation of the week (see [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md)). This one should ideally be with someone who does **not** work in tech.
- Don't look at your project cards tonight. The test is unprepared.

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the **hardest clip of the week**. For most people that's the Day 4 interview clip (tough "why" answers) or the Day 2 demo walkthrough (fast, lots of contrast stress).

1. Speak-along with the whole clip once without stopping.
2. Pause-and-repeat the three sentences you still can't match.
3. Speak-along once more and record yourself. Listen: are you stressing the **new** information, or every word the same?

See [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**The prompt (unprepared, 2 minutes):**

> "A friend's parent who has never worked in an office asks you: 'So what do you actually do — and what's something you worked on recently?' Explain your job simply, walk them through one recent project, mention one thing you had to give up to make it work, and why it was worth it."

Steps:
1. Read the prompt once. Start recording **immediately**. No notes.
2. Speak for 2 full minutes. If you run out, add a "why?" layer or a lesson learned.
3. Listen back and count your 3 numbers (see [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)):
   - umms and uhhs
   - pauses longer than 2 seconds
   - different connectors (signposts like "So the starting point was…", "Where it got interesting was…", "The downside is…, but…", "Long story short…" count)
4. Write the numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), next to last week's Day 6 numbers.
5. Also note: how many jargon words slipped out without a plain-English explanation?

## 3. Skill check (15 min)

Rate yourself honestly for each day. **3** = I can do it at normal speed without thinking. **2** = I can do it, but I slow down or pause. **1** = I froze or avoided it.

| Day | I can… | Rating (1–3) | If you scored 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Your job for outsiders | explain my job in one sentence, 30 seconds and 2 minutes, with an analogy and no jargon | | Redo [Exercise 1.2](exercises.md#exercise-12-three-lengths) every night next week before shadowing (3 minutes). |
| 2. Walking through a project | walk through a project in five stages with signposts, in about 3 minutes | | Redo [Exercise 2.3](exercises.md#exercise-23-your-project-in-five-stages) with a different project card, then do one 3/2/1 round. |
| 3. Explaining trade-offs | say the choice, the cost, the gain and why it was worth it, calmly | | Redo [Exercise 3.3](exercises.md#exercise-33-four-move-trade-off-say-it-3-ways) and say one everyday trade-off out loud each morning. |
| 4. Answering "why?" questions | answer 4 "whys" in a row, starting with a short answer, using evidence | | Run Round 1 of the [Day 4 voice-mode prompt](day-4-answering-why-questions.md#3-speaking-drill-70-min-voice-mode-with-claude) again for 15 minutes this weekend. |
| 5. Success and failure stories | tell a success with shared credit and a failure that ends with "Now I always…" | | Redo [Exercise 5.2](exercises.md#exercise-52-story-builder--one-success-one-failure) and tell the failure story to a friend this week. |

Add up your score (5–15). 13–15: excellent, move on. 9–12: normal for this week — keep the 1s and 2s in your review block next week. 5–8: repeat the Day 1 and Day 2 lessons during next week's review time.

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

Live conversation #3 of the week. See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Who:** ideally someone outside tech — a friend's partner, a neighbour, a language exchange partner who works in a different field, a group at a meetup.

**Your mission: "the curious outsider".** Get the other person interested in your work for at least 10 minutes, and then become curious about theirs for at least 10 minutes.

Things to try:
1. Give the 30-second version of your job with a bridge to an app *they* use ("You know when you…?").
2. If they ask a follow-up, walk them through one project in under 3 minutes with at least four signposts.
3. Mention one trade-off: "The downside was…, but…".
4. When they ask "why?", start with a short answer: "Mostly because…".
5. Ask them about a success or failure in their own work, and ask "What would you do differently?"

**Backup if nobody is available:** voice mode with Claude ([voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)). Paste this:

```text
Let's have a 40-minute casual voice conversation. You're a nurse I've just met at a friend's dinner party. You know nothing about tech or design, and you're curious but sometimes confused. Ask me what I do. If I use jargon, look confused and ask "Wait, what does that mean?". Ask me about a project, why I made certain choices, and whether anything ever went wrong. Then tell me about your own job and let me ask you questions. At the end, give me: (1) every jargon word I used without explaining it, (2) whether my project explanation was easy to follow, (3) my 5 most important language mistakes, with corrected sentences. Casual American English.
```

3. **Carry into next week (Week 19: Complex topics):** next week you'll talk about news, tech, culture and money. Your job and project explanations are your "safe ground" — when a big topic gets hard, you can always bridge back to an example from your work ("Actually, we see this in my job…"). Pick your three strongest phrases from this week and write them at the top of next week's phrase log page.
