# Week 18: Explaining your work
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5: Work and complex topics](../README.md) · [← Week 17](../week-17-meetings/README.md) · [Week 19 →](../week-19-complex-topics/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you learn to talk about your own work so that anyone can follow it, and to do it without freezing. You will explain your design and product job to people outside tech, walk someone through a real project from start to finish, explain the trade-offs behind your decisions, survive a chain of "why?" questions, and tell a work success or failure story that people actually want to hear. Your work is the topic you know best in the world. By the end of the week it should also be a topic you can talk about in English for ten minutes, at normal speed, with anyone.

## How this builds on last week

Last week ([Week 17: Meetings](../week-17-meetings/README.md)) was about short, sharp turns inside a meeting: updates, clarifying, pushing back, suggestions, next steps. This week the turns get longer. In a meeting you speak for 30 seconds; when someone asks "So what was that project about?" you need to hold the floor for two or three minutes and keep it clear. You will reuse last week's update language ("So, quick update…") as the opening of a walkthrough, and last week's pushing-back language when someone challenges your trade-offs.

This week also goes much deeper than two earlier lessons. In [Week 4 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-5-talking-about-your-job.md) you learned the basics: what you do, a normal day, a recent problem. In [Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md) you learned to explain how things work and why you made a decision. We will not repeat those. This week is about **audience, structure and pressure**: changing your explanation for different listeners, structuring a long walkthrough, being honest about downsides, and staying calm when people keep asking "why?".

## Before you start

- Pick **three real projects** from your work (or from a course or side project). For each one, write five words on a card: the product, the problem, what you changed, the result, one thing that went wrong. Do not write sentences. You will use these cards all week.
- Have your recording app, phrase log and nightly log ready: [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md), [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
- Find one **product demo or design case study clip** for shadowing (search terms are in each lesson). Methods: [shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).
- Book your three live conversations for Days 2, 5 and 6. Ideal partners this week: a friend or relative who does **not** work in tech, a colleague from another team, a language exchange partner. See [live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- Reread your Week 17 Day 6 notes: [Week 17 weekly check](../week-17-meetings/day-6-weekly-check.md).

## Day 7: light day

Thirty minutes only. Put on a podcast or video where a **designer, engineer or founder is interviewed about their work** (search "product designer interview podcast", "how I built this style interview", "designer career story podcast"). Listen while walking. Do not take notes during the episode. Afterwards, ask yourself just two questions and answer them out loud in one minute each:

1. How did the guest explain their job to the host in the first five minutes? Was it simple or full of jargon?
2. When the host asked "why?", what was the first phrase the guest used before their answer?

Then rest. Your brain needs the quiet time to lock in the week.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] explain your job in one sentence, in 30 seconds and in 2 minutes, to someone who has never heard of product design
- [ ] walk someone through a project from the starting point to the result, with clear signposts, in about 3 minutes
- [ ] name the downside of a decision honestly and explain why it was still worth it
- [ ] answer three or four "why?" questions in a row without getting defensive or freezing
- [ ] tell a short work success story without bragging and a failure story without sounding weak
- [ ] stress the new, important word in each sentence so your listener knows what to remember
