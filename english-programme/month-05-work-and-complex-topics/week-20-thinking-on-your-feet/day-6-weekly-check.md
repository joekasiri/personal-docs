# Week 20 · Day 6: Weekly check

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 10 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 10 min · Live conversation 50 min · Review and report 15 min · Monthly check 20 min

This is the last night of Month 5, so tonight is both the **weekly check** and the **Month 5 check**.

## Before you start

- Have ready: your recording app, the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), your **Week 1 Day 1 baseline numbers**, your **Month 4 check numbers** (from [Week 16 Day 6](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/day-6-weekly-check.md)), and the feedback your partner gave you last night.
- **Live conversation #3** is tonight (50 minutes). If you can, make it a real work moment: a meeting where you present something, a 1:1 with your manager, or a meetup where people ask questions. Otherwise, a language partner or tutor who will ask you tough questions.
- Don't prepare the re-record topic. Read it only when you're about to press record.
- Use the materials in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials).

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 10 min)

Go back to the **hardest clip of the week**. For most people that's Day 4 (the calm, low voice under pressure) or Day 2 (fast surprise answers). Speak along with it once, all the way through. Then speak along a second time and focus only on the **first five words** of each answer: copy the opener and the tiny pause after it exactly. Method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**Prompt (unprepared):** read it, take a breath, start recording, and start talking within 2 seconds.

> **You're at a work event. A senior person from another team says: "So what's the hardest decision you've had to make at work this year? And honestly, would you make it again?"** Answer for 2 minutes.

Rules:

1. Open with a real opener ("Ooh, good question…", "Honestly?", "So there are two parts to that…"), not "umm".
2. Headline first: say what the decision was in your first or second sentence.
3. Include one moment where you defend the decision, and one where you admit something you'd do differently.
4. Don't stop. If you lose a word, go around it ([Week 11 Day 1: Paraphrasing](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-1-paraphrasing.md)).

Then listen back and count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md):

1. Umms and uhhs
2. Pauses longer than 2 seconds
3. Different connectors (not just "and" and "so")

Write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). Also note: **how many seconds until your first real word?** (Target: under 2.)

## 4. Live conversation (50 min)

**Your mission: think on your feet in front of someone.** Use the [live conversation mission card in Day 6 materials](exercises.md#live-conversation-mission-card).

Things to try:

1. Get at least **two questions you didn't expect**, and open each with a real opener (Day 2).
2. Share one idea or opinion about work and **defend it** once when they question it (Day 3).
3. **Give one piece of specific feedback** to the other person, positive or constructive (Day 5).
4. Ask them for feedback on something you said, and **receive it** with thank → clarify → reflect (Day 5).
5. If there's a question you can't answer, use "I don't know, but…" with a follow-up (Day 1).

Tips on partners, nerves and what to do if it goes quiet: [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Backup plan: voice mode with Claude.** If your live conversation falls through, paste this:

```text
Let's do a 45-minute "thinking on your feet" session in English. I'm a B2 speaker working in product design, and this is the end of a month practising meetings, explaining my work, complex topics and tough questions.

You are Jordan, a senior director from another department, meeting me for the first time over coffee. Start with small talk, then ask me about my work. Over the conversation:
- ask me at least four questions I won't expect (some about work, some personal, one big-picture "what would you change" question),
- challenge one of my ideas and push twice,
- ask me a question I probably can't answer, to see how I handle it,
- give me one piece of feedback about something I said, and see how I receive it,
- ask me for feedback on an idea of yours.

Stay in character the whole time. At the end, step out and tell me: how fast I started my answers, whether I gave headlines first, how I handled the challenge and the feedback, and my 5 most important language corrections.
```

## 6. Monthly check (20 min)

Follow the full procedure in [Monthly check](../../toolkit/monthly-check.md). Tonight's re-record test is your Month 5 recording.

**Month 5 targets (compared with your Week 1 Day 1 baseline):**

- **Umms and long pauses down about 45%.** Example: if your baseline was 20 umms, aim for 11 or fewer. If it was 8 long pauses, aim for 4 or fewer.
- **Connectors: 7 or more different ones** in 2 minutes, on the way to Month 6's target of 8.
- **Skill target from the plan:** you can run your part of a meeting and handle questions you didn't expect.

Fill in this table (copy it into your nightly log):

| | Week 1 baseline | Month 4 check | Month 5 check (tonight) | Change since baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Umms / uhhs | | | | % |
| Pauses over 2 seconds | | | | % |
| Different connectors | | | | + |

To work out the change: (baseline − tonight) ÷ baseline × 100. Example: (20 − 11) ÷ 20 × 100 = 45%.

**Listen to all three recordings in a row:** the Week 1 baseline, the Month 4 check and tonight. Write three sentences in your notes:

1. "The biggest difference I can hear is…"
2. "Something that hasn't changed yet is…"
3. "In Month 6, I want to…"

**If you hit the targets:** great. Month 6 is about making all of this automatic in longer, harder conversations.
**If you didn't:** don't just push on. Add one line to your Claude report: "I missed the Month 5 target for [umms / pauses / connectors]. Please suggest how to adjust Month 6." Common causes: too few live conversations (are you doing 3 a week?), skipping the review block, or practising silently instead of out loud.
