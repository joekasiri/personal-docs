# Week 20 exercises: Thinking on your feet
[Week 20 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-questions-after-a-talk.md) · [Day 2](day-2-unexpected-questions.md) · [Day 3](day-3-defending-an-idea.md) · [Day 4](day-4-difficult-conversations.md) · [Day 5](day-5-giving-and-receiving-feedback.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises.** Everything here is done **out loud**. Reading silently doesn't count this week. The whole point is to train your mouth to start fast under pressure. When an exercise says "record", record it and listen back once. When it says "write then say", write only a few key words, never full sentences, and then say the full answer. Check the answer key *after* you've spoken, not before. The model answers are examples, not the only correct answers: if yours is different but follows the pattern, it's right.

Exercises marked **(drill material)** are used in that day's speaking drill, not in the 20-minute main-skill block.

## Day 1 exercises

Lesson: [Questions after a talk](day-1-questions-after-a-talk.md)

### Exercise 1.1: Name it, then answer it

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

You just presented a redesign of your company's mobile app sign-up flow. Below are 10 questions from the audience.

1. Read each question out loud.
2. Say which type it is: **multi-part, challenge, off-topic, can't answer, comment, or loaded**.
3. Say a first move from the lesson, then finish with a 1–2 sentence answer. Make up any details you need.

**Questions:**

1. "How long did the research take, how many people did you talk to, and when does it go live?"
2. "Isn't this just making the screens prettier? What's the actual business value?"
3. "What's the drop-off rate on the second screen right now, exactly?"
4. "Since you're here, do you know if the design team is hiring next year?"
5. "I've been here eight years and we redesign this flow every two years and nothing changes. Just saying."
6. "Why did you decide to remove the option to sign up with email?" *(You didn't remove it. You moved it lower on the screen.)*
7. "How does this affect the Android app, and did legal approve the new consent text?"
8. "With respect, users hated the last redesign. Why should this one be different?"
9. "What's the conversion rate going to be after launch?"
10. "Why are you ignoring our older customers?" *(You tested with five people over sixty-five.)*

### Exercise 1.2: Headline first

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Each answer below gives the background first and the real answer last. Say a **headline-first** version: one sentence with the answer, then one sentence of reason. Say each new version twice, the second time faster.

1. **Q: Did the test work?** "So we started in March, and we had two groups, and the first group used the old version, and after four weeks we compared them, and yes, the new one was better by about ten percent."
2. **Q: Are we on track for the launch?** "Well, the engineers had some problems with the payment page last week, and then the designer was sick, but we caught up over the weekend, so yes, we're on track."
3. **Q: Why did you choose blue for the buttons?** "There was a lot of discussion, and we looked at the brand guide, and marketing had some ideas, and we tested three colours, and blue was the easiest for people to see."
4. **Q: Will this work on tablets?** "We designed it mainly for phones, and then we checked some screens on desktop, and we haven't had time to test tablets properly, so not yet, no."
5. **Q: How many users did you talk to?** "We had interviews over three weeks, some on video and some in person, in two cities, and in total it was eighteen people."
6. **Q: Is the old feature going away?** "We discussed it with the product team, and support, and we checked how many people use it, and it's about two percent, so yes, we're removing it in June."

### Exercise 1.3: Fix the reply

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

Each reply is weak, rude or unnatural. Say a better version out loud 3 times. Use the lesson's phrases.

1. Q: "What was the budget for this?" → "I don't know."
2. Q: "Isn't this the same thing the competitor did?" → "No. That's completely wrong."
3. Q: "And what about the pricing page redesign?" (off-topic) → "That is not the topic of my presentation."
4. Q: "Three quick questions: timeline, budget, and who's on the team?" → "Sorry, can you repeat all the question?"
5. Q: "When will the data be ready?" → "I will revert to you."
6. Q: "Why did you ignore accessibility?" (you didn't) → "Sorry, sorry, yes, maybe we should do more accessibility."
7. A colleague talks for a minute about how the team is too slow, then stops. → *(silence)* "…OK. Next question?"
8. Q: "Is this going to launch on time?" → "Umm… so the project started in January, and there were many changes, and…"

### Exercise 1.4: The 12 tough questions (drill material)

**Used in:** Day 1 speaking drill, Step 3. Answer each in 30–60 seconds after your 1-minute project talk. Start within 2 seconds. The type is in brackets so you can check your first move afterwards. Cover the brackets while you practise.

1. "So how long did this take, what did it cost, and would you do it the same way again?" *(multi-part)*
2. "Honestly, I don't see why this needed a whole project. Couldn't you just fix the one broken screen?" *(challenge)*
3. "What percentage of users actually saw the new design in the first week?" *(can't answer: assume you don't know the number)*
4. "This is great, but how are we handling the move to the new office next month?" *(off-topic)*
5. "I think the real problem is that we never talk to customers. Projects like this happen because nobody listens to support." *(comment)*
6. "Why did you decide to skip user testing?" *(loaded: you did test, with eight people)*
7. "Who made the final decision on this, you or the product manager?" *(possibly hostile: answer honestly and calmly)*
8. "What would you have done with twice the time?" *(open, unexpected)*
9. "The numbers you showed look good, but aren't they just because it was the holiday season?" *(challenge)*
10. "How does this connect to the company's goals for next year, and who's paying for phase two?" *(multi-part, the second part may be "can't answer")*
11. "Can you go back to slide three? I didn't really get the main point." *(clarification: re-explain in one sentence)*
12. "If this fails in six months, whose fault is it?" *(hostile)*

### Answer key: Day 1

**Exercise 1.1 (types + model answers)**

1. **Multi-part.** "So there are three things in there. Let me take them one at a time. Research took about four weeks, we talked to twelve people, and it goes live in March."
2. **Challenge.** "That's a fair challenge. It's not just prettier: the new flow cut sign-up time from three minutes to one, and in testing, more people finished it."
3. **Can't answer.** "I don't have the exact number in front of me, but it's somewhere around thirty percent. I'll check and get back to you by tomorrow."
4. **Off-topic.** "Ha, good question, and it's a bit outside what I covered today. Maybe ask Laura, she'd know. Happy to chat after."
5. **Comment.** "Thanks for that. If I understand your point, you're saying we need to make sure this one actually sticks? Totally agree, which is why we're tracking it for six months."
6. **Loaded.** "I can see why it looks that way, but we actually didn't remove it. We moved it lower on the screen. It's still there, just one tap further."
7. **Multi-part.** "OK, two parts. Android: it's the same design, launching two weeks later. And legal: yes, they approved the consent text last Friday."
8. **Challenge.** "That's a fair challenge. The difference this time is that we tested with real users three times before building anything, and we're rolling it out slowly."
9. **Can't answer.** "Honestly? I don't know yet. My guess is a small increase, but we'll have real numbers about two weeks after launch, and I'll share them then."
10. **Loaded.** "I'd push back on that a little. We tested with five people over sixty-five, and they actually found it easier than the old one."

**Exercise 1.2 (model answers)**

1. "Yes, the new version did about ten percent better. We compared it with the old one over four weeks."
2. "Yes, we're on track. We had a couple of problems last week, but we caught up over the weekend."
3. "Because it was the easiest colour for people to see. We tested three, and blue won."
4. "Not yet, no. We designed it for phones first, and we haven't tested tablets properly."
5. "Eighteen people. We did interviews over three weeks, in two cities."
6. "Yes, it's going away in June. Only about two percent of people use it."

**Exercise 1.3 (model answers)**

1. "I don't have that number in front of me, but I can find out and get back to you by Friday."
2. "That's a fair challenge. I can see why it looks similar. The big difference is…"
3. "That's a great question, and it's a bit outside what I covered today. Can we grab five minutes after?"
4. "Sure. Let me start with the timeline, and then come back to budget and the team." (If you forget one: "Remind me, what was the last one?")
5. "I'll get back to you on that by Thursday."
6. "I'd push back on that a little. We didn't ignore it. We tested with screen-reader users in week two, and we fixed three issues."
7. "Thanks for that. If I understand your point, you're saying we need more time for each project? That's fair. Let's talk about it after."
8. "The short answer is yes, we're on track. The longer answer is that we had a few changes, but we've planned for them."

**Exercise 1.4:** no fixed answers. Check each one: (1) did you start within 2 seconds? (2) was the headline in your first sentence? (3) did you stay calm on 2, 7, 9 and 12? (4) did you promise a real follow-up on 3 and 10?

### Exercise 2.2: Think-out-loud frames

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** record

Answer each question in about 1 minute using the 4 steps: **react → narrow (if needed) → think out loud → provisional answer**. Record all 6 in one file. Use this frame if you get stuck:

> "Ooh, [react]. Let me think out loud for a second. The obvious answer would be… but actually… So if I had to pick one, I'd say… What would change my mind is…"

4. "What's something your team does that wastes time?"
5. "Who's the best manager you've ever had, and why?"

### Exercise 2.3: Gap-fill dialogue

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Fill each gap with a phrase from the Day 2 lesson, saying it out loud. Then perform the whole dialogue twice, playing both parts, as fast as you can.

> **Paul (director):** Hey, random question. If we stopped doing all user research tomorrow, what would break first?
> **You:** (1) ________, that's a tough one. (2) ________ for the new products, or for the stuff we already have?
> **Paul:** New products.
> **You:** OK, (3) ________ for a second. The (4) ________ answer would be "we'd build the wrong thing". (5) ________, I think the first thing to break would be trust. Engineers would stop believing our designs, because we'd have no evidence.
> **Paul:** Interesting. You really think so?
> **You:** My (6) ________ says yes. I'd (7) ________ with the team, (8) ________ is that our biggest arguments always end with "what did users say?"
> **Paul:** Fair. And what would change your mind?
> **You:** (9) ________ if we had amazing product analytics instead. Then maybe we'd be OK for a while.

## Day 3 exercises

Lesson: [Defending an idea](day-3-defending-an-idea.md)

### Exercise 3.1: Four-step defence builder

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** write then say

For each proposal and objection, write **one key word** for each step (acknowledge, reason, evidence, offer). Then say the full defence out loud in about 45 seconds, without looking at your words. Say each one twice.

1. **Your proposal:** remove the "Skip" button from the onboarding survey.
   **Objection (PM):** "People will get annoyed and leave."
   **Your facts:** survey takes 20 seconds; answers let you personalise the home screen; in testing, 9 of 10 people finished it.
2. **Your proposal:** use real customer photos instead of stock photos on the website.
   **Objection (marketing):** "Real photos will look less professional."
   **Your facts:** in an A/B test last year, real photos got 12% more clicks; customers say stock photos feel "fake".
3. **Your proposal:** spend two weeks fixing old bugs instead of building new features.
   **Objection (sales director):** "Customers want new features, not bug fixes."
   **Your facts:** 40% of support tickets are about three old bugs; two big customers mentioned them in renewal calls.
4. **Your proposal:** move the "Delete account" button out of the hidden menu so it's easy to find.
   **Objection (growth lead):** "That's going to increase the number of people who leave."
   **Your facts:** some countries' rules expect deleting to be easy; hidden buttons create angry support emails and bad reviews.

### Exercise 3.2: Concede and protect

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

The challenger makes a point you **can't** fully answer. Concede the small point with "OK, you've got me there" or "I'll give you that", then protect your main idea with "But I don't think that means…". Say each answer twice.

1. "Your new dashboard has no dark mode. Lots of our users work at night."
2. "Your research only included people from one city."
3. "This design needs three new icons, and our illustrator is on vacation for a month."
4. "Your prototype was tested on the newest phones only."
5. "The new checkout adds one extra screen."
6. "You didn't talk to the support team before designing this."

### Exercise 3.3: Turn it around

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Turn each objection into a reason **for** your idea, using "That's actually why we…". Say each one out loud, then once more with falling intonation at the end.

1. "But our users are really busy." → *(your idea: a shorter, one-page settings screen)*
2. "But engineering is overloaded this quarter." → *(your idea: reuse existing design-system components)*
3. "But we don't know if people will like it." → *(your idea: a small test with 5% of users first)*
4. "But this is a big change for long-time users." → *(your idea: a "switch back to the old view" option for one month)*
5. "But our brand is supposed to be serious." → *(your idea: friendlier error messages, but no jokes)*
6. "But we've never done it this way before." → *(your idea: a two-week trial with a clear success number)*

### Exercise 3.4: Three challengers role cards (drill material)

**Used in:** Day 3 speaking drill, Step 3.

**The idea you're defending:** "We should launch the redesigned app to **all users next month**, not in a slow rollout over three months."

**Your facts (use any of these):**

- The redesign was tested with 30 users in three rounds. Task success went from 62% to 88%.
- Running the old and new app at the same time costs the engineering team about 20% of their time.
- A slow rollout means support has to answer questions about two different versions for three months.
- There's a "switch back to classic view" option for the first 30 days.
- The app has about 200,000 active users.

**Challenger 1: Rachel, Head of Customer Support.**
Personality: worried, practical, cares about her team.
Opening line: "If we launch to everyone at once and something goes wrong, my team gets two hundred thousand angry people on the same day. How is that a good idea?"
Second push: "The 'switch back' button is nice, but people won't find it. They'll just call us."
What could convince her: a support plan, extra staff in launch week, a clear in-app message.

**Challenger 2: David, Chief Financial Officer.**
Personality: direct, impatient, wants numbers.
Opening line: "Slow rollouts are safer. Why should I take the bigger risk?"
Second push: "Thirty users isn't two hundred thousand. Your test proves nothing at scale."
What could convince him: the cost of running two versions, a clear "stop" number (for example, roll back if sign-ins drop more than 5%).

**Challenger 3: Mei, Senior Engineer.**
Personality: calm, technical, sceptical.
Opening line: "We haven't tested the new app on the oldest Android phones. Some of our users still have them."
Second push: "If it crashes on old phones, we can't just 'switch back'. They won't be able to open the app at all."
What could convince her: you probably need to **give ground** here, for example keep old phones on the classic app until testing is done, and launch to everyone else.

**Final round (3 minutes):** read all three opening lines one after another and answer each in 30 seconds.

### Answer key: Day 3

**Exercise 3.1 (model defences)**

1. "That's a fair point, and it was our worry too. The whole point is to personalise the home screen, so people see the right things straight away. It only takes twenty seconds, and in testing nine out of ten people finished it. How about we track drop-off for the first two weeks, and if it goes up, we bring the skip button back?"
2. "I hear you. The whole point is to look trustworthy, not just professional. Last year's A/B test showed real photos got twelve percent more clicks, and customers told us stock photos feel fake. How about we try real photos on one page first and compare?"
3. "That's fair, they do want new features. But forty percent of our support tickets are about three old bugs, and two big customers mentioned them in renewal calls. So fixing them is protecting revenue. Let's do two weeks and measure the ticket numbers."
4. "I get the worry. The whole point is to avoid angry customers and legal problems: some countries expect deleting to be easy, and hidden buttons create bad reviews. People who want to leave will leave anyway. How about we add a short 'Before you go' screen with an option to pause instead?"

**Exercise 3.2 (model answers)**

1. "OK, you've got me there, no dark mode yet. But I don't think that means we hold the whole launch. Let's add it to the next release."
2. "I'll give you that, one city isn't ideal. But I don't think that means the findings are wrong. Let's do five remote interviews in other cities to check."
3. "OK, you've got me there. But I don't think that means we wait a month. We can use existing icons for now and swap them later."
4. "I'll give you that. But I don't think that means the design is wrong. Let's test it on two older phones this week."
5. "That's true, it's one more screen. But I don't think that means it's slower. In testing, people finished faster because each screen was simpler."
6. "OK, you've got me there. I should've talked to them first. But I don't think that means we start again. Let's do a session with them this week and adjust."

**Exercise 3.3 (model answers)**

1. "That's actually why we made it one page. Busy people don't want to click through five screens."
2. "That's actually why we used components from the design system. It's much less new work for them."
3. "That's actually why we're starting with five percent of users. We'll know before it's a big risk."
4. "That's actually why we added a 'switch back' option for the first month."
5. "That's actually why we kept it friendly but not funny. Clear and kind fits a serious brand."
6. "That's actually why we want a two-week trial with a clear number. If it doesn't work, we stop."

## Day 4 exercises

Lesson: [Difficult conversations](day-4-difficult-conversations.md)

### Exercise 4.1: Reorder the bad news

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Each message is in the wrong order. Say it out loud in the right order: **headline → ownership → short reason → options → next step**. Then say it again, faster, without looking.

**Message A (to your boss):**
a) "We can launch without the new search filters, or move the launch to the 20th."
b) "That's on me. I didn't plan enough time for testing."
c) "I'll be straight with you: we won't be ready for the 13th."
d) "I'll send you a short plan for both options by the end of today."
e) "Testing found problems with the search filters on slow connections."

**Message B (to a client):**
a) "What I can commit to is a full fix in next Tuesday's update."
b) "I wanted you to hear it from me first: some of your customers got the wrong email yesterday."
c) "A setting was changed during an update, and it sent the reminder email to the wrong list."
d) "We've already switched it off, and we can send an apology email today if you'd like."
e) "I'll call you tomorrow at ten to confirm everything's working."
f) "We should've caught it in testing, and we didn't."

**Message C (to your team lead):**
a) "I can either redo the slides tonight, or we can present the old version and share the corrected numbers after."
b) "I used last year's data by mistake."
c) "Heads-up: the numbers in tomorrow's presentation are wrong."
d) "That's on me, I should've double-checked."
e) "Let me know which you prefer in the next hour, and I'll get on it."

### Exercise 4.2: "I can't…, but I can…"

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Turn each flat "no" into a refusal with an alternative. Use "I'm not able to…, but I can…" or "What I can commit to is…". Say each one twice.

1. "No, we can't add the chat feature this month."
2. "No, I can't come to the client meeting on Friday."
3. "No, the design can't be finished by tomorrow."
4. "No, we can't make the logo bigger on every page."
5. "No, I can't share the research videos with you. They're private."
6. "No, we can't give you a discount."
7. "No, I can't review all twenty screens today."
8. "No, we can't change the colours. The brand team decides that."

### Exercise 4.3: Empathy, then action

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** record

Respond to each angry line out loud. **First**, one empathy sentence (then a short pause). **Then**, one action or option. Keep your voice low and slow. Record all 8.

1. Client: "We've been waiting three weeks for this. It's ridiculous."
2. Boss: "Why am I only hearing about this now?"
3. Colleague: "You changed my design without even telling me."
4. Client: "Your app deleted all my saved items. I'm furious."
5. Stakeholder: "This is the third time the date has moved."
6. Boss: "The CEO asked me about this and I had no idea what to say. That made me look bad."
7. Client: "I don't understand why something so simple takes so long."
8. Engineer: "Your designs keep changing after we've started building. It's driving the team crazy."

### Answer key: Day 4

**Exercise 4.1**

- **A:** c → b → e → a → d
- **B:** b → f → c → d → a → e (options and a next step: "We've already switched it off…", then the commitment, then the call)
- **C:** c → d → b → a → e

**Exercise 4.2 (model answers)**

1. "I'm not able to add the full chat feature this month, but I can add a simple 'contact us' button by next week."
2. "I'm not able to make it on Friday, but I can join a quick call on Thursday, or send a short video update."
3. "I'm not able to finish the whole design by tomorrow, but I can show you the main screen tomorrow and the rest on Thursday."
4. "I'm not able to make it bigger on every page, but I can make it bigger on the homepage, where it matters most."
5. "I'm not able to share the videos, because of privacy, but I can send you a summary with the key quotes."
6. "I'm not able to offer a discount, but what I can do is add two extra training sessions at no cost."
7. "I'm not able to review all twenty today, but I can review the five most important ones by five o'clock."
8. "I'm not able to change the colours myself, but I can set up a meeting with the brand team this week."

**Exercise 4.3 (model answers)**

1. "I understand, three weeks is a long time, and I'm sorry about that. What I can commit to is a working version by Thursday."
2. "That's fair. I should've flagged it sooner. Here's where we are, and here's what we can do."
3. "You're right, I should've told you first, and I'm sorry. Can we look at it together this afternoon?"
4. "That sounds really frustrating. Let me check right now if we can restore them from the backup."
5. "I get it. It's not what you wanted to hear, again. Here's what's different this time, and I'll send you weekly updates so there are no surprises."
6. "I'm sorry, that's on me. Let's agree that I'll send you a short update every Friday so you always have the latest."
7. "I understand why it looks simple from the outside. Can I walk you through what's involved? Then we can decide what to do first."
8. "That's fair, and I can hear it's been hard for the team. What if we lock the designs before each sprint starts, and any change waits for the next one?"

## Day 5 exercises

Lesson: [Giving and receiving feedback](day-5-giving-and-receiving-feedback.md)

### Exercise 5.1: Vague to specific

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** write then say

Turn each vague comment into **S-N-E-S** feedback: **S**ituation, what you **N**oticed, the **E**ffect, a **S**uggestion. Invent the details. Say each one out loud in about 30 seconds.

1. "Your presentation was confusing."
2. "Great job on the research!"
3. "The new homepage is too busy."
4. "You're always late with the files."
5. "I don't like the colours."
6. "Nice work in the meeting today."
7. "The error messages are bad."
8. "You talk too much in critiques."

### Exercise 5.2: Receive it well

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** record

Respond to each piece of feedback using all four steps: **thank → clarify → reflect → next step**. Record each answer (about 30 seconds).

1. Manager: "Your designs are good, but you don't share them early enough."
2. Senior designer: "The flow feels long."
3. PM: "In meetings, you sometimes go quiet and I'm not sure what you think."
4. Engineer: "The specs you send are hard to follow."
5. Colleague: "Your English is great, but sometimes you speak really fast when you're nervous."
6. Client: "The new design feels a bit cold."

### Exercise 5.3: Fix the feedback language

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence has a mistake (grammar, word choice, or tone). Say the correct version out loud 3 times.

1. "Thank you for your feedbacks."
2. "Can I give you a feedback about the deck?"
3. "Your design is bad in the mobile version."
4. "I noticed that you were lazy with the specs."
5. "Maybe possibly it is a little bit perhaps too long?"
6. "Yes but I did it like this because the PM said so, so it's not my problem."
7. "So what I'm hearing it's that the font is too small."
8. "Let me sit on that and come back."

### Exercise 5.4: Feedback situations (drill material)

**Used in:** Day 5 speaking drill, Step 1.

**Giving feedback (situations 1–4):**

1. **Your junior designer, Ana,** showed her first full design for a booking screen. The layout is clean and the spacing is great. But the main "Book now" button is grey, and in a quick test two people didn't notice it. Give her feedback: one positive, one suggestion.
2. **Your PM, Chris,** ran a planning meeting. It lasted 90 minutes instead of 45, and there was no agenda, so the team discussed the same topic three times. You like working with Chris. Give him feedback privately.

4. **A colleague, Lisa,** wrote the text for a new error message: "Error 403: Access denied. Contact administrator." Users don't know what it means. Give her feedback and suggest a better version.

**Receiving feedback (situations 5–8):** do each twice, once agreeing, once disagreeing calmly.

5. **Your manager, Priya:** "Your research reports are really long. Leadership doesn't read past the first page."
6. **A senior engineer, Ben:** "Your prototypes look great, but they show animations we can't actually build."
7. **A client, Mr. Lopez:** "The new logo you suggested looks too modern for our older customers."
8. **Your PM, Kim:** "You push back a lot in meetings. Sometimes it slows us down."

### Answer key: Day 5

**Exercise 5.1 (model answers)**

1. "In yesterday's presentation, I noticed you showed the results before explaining the problem. I think a few people didn't understand why the results mattered. What if you started with the problem slide?"
2. "Great job on the research. In the readout, the way you grouped the quotes into three themes made it super easy to follow. Keep doing that."
3. "Looking at the new homepage, I noticed there are six buttons above the fold. I think people won't know where to click first. I wonder if we could keep just two."
4. "For the last two handoffs, the files came in a day after we agreed, so the engineers had to wait. Could we agree on a time together, or is something blocking you?"
5. "On the settings page, I noticed the light grey text on white is hard to read. I think some users will struggle with it. What if we used a darker grey?"
6. "In today's meeting, when you summed up the decisions at the end, everyone left knowing exactly what to do. Really nice."
7. "I noticed the error messages say 'Invalid input' without saying what's wrong. People don't know how to fix it. What if each message said what to change, like 'Use at least 8 characters'?"
8. "In the last two critiques, I noticed you spoke for most of the time, and some of the juniors didn't share their ideas. Could we try going round the room first?"

**Exercise 5.2 (model answers)**

1. "Thanks, I appreciate you telling me. Can you give me an example? … So what I'm hearing is, people should see my work while it's still rough? That's fair. I'll share early sketches every Wednesday."
2. "Thanks. Is it the whole flow, or a specific part? … So what I'm hearing is, the middle steps feel repetitive. I'll try combining steps two and three."
3. "Thanks for telling me, that's useful. Can you give me an example? … So what I'm hearing is, you'd like me to say my opinion more clearly. I'll try to give my view before the end of each meeting."
4. "Thanks. Which part is hardest to follow? … So what I'm hearing is, the specs need clearer measurements. I'll add a table with the exact values."
5. "Oh, thanks, that's really helpful, actually. When did you notice it most? … So what I'm hearing is, I speed up when I'm nervous. I'll practise pausing more."
6. "Thanks for being honest. Can you tell me what feels cold: the colours, the photos, or the words? … So what I'm hearing is, it's mostly the colours. Let me sit with that and show you a warmer version on Monday."

**Exercise 5.3**

1. "Thanks for the feedback." *(feedback is uncountable)*
2. "Can I give you a bit of feedback on the deck?" *(no "a feedback"; "on" is more natural than "about")*
3. "The mobile version has a few issues." / "I noticed the buttons are hard to tap on mobile." *(talk about specifics, not "bad")*
4. "I noticed the specs were missing some measurements." *(describe the fact, not the person)*
5. "I think it might be a bit too long." *(soften once, not five times)*
6. "I see where you're coming from. I did it this way because the PM asked for it, but I'm happy to look at it again." *(no blame, stay open)*
7. "So what I'm hearing is that the font is too small." *(what I'm hearing **is**)*
8. "Let me sit with that and get back to you." *(fixed phrases: sit **with** that, get back to you)*

## Day 6 materials

### Re-record checklist

Tick these after listening to your 2-minute re-record:

- [ ] My first real word came within 2 seconds.
- [ ] I opened with a natural opener, not "umm".
- [ ] I said what the decision was in my first or second sentence (headline first).
- [ ] I defended the decision with a reason and a piece of evidence.
- [ ] I admitted one thing I'd do differently, calmly ("I'll give you that…", "If I'm honest…").
- [ ] I used at least 7 different connectors (for example: so basically, the thing is, turns out, at that point, in the end, which was weird because, long story short).
- [ ] I didn't stop when I lost a word. I went around it.
