## This week's goal

This is the week where the questions stop being friendly. Someone at the end of your talk asks a question with a hidden attack in it. Your manager asks something you didn't prepare for. A client says "That's not good enough." A teammate gives you feedback you don't agree with. In all of these moments, the danger is the same: you freeze, translate in your head, and either go silent or say too much. This week you build a set of **first moves**: the opening sentence you say in the first two seconds, which gives your brain time to find the real answer. By Sunday, you should be able to take a hard question, open calmly, give a short structured answer, and stay friendly while you do it.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 19](../week-19-complex-topics/README.md) you talked about big, complex topics and learned to give balanced views. You had time to think, and the topics were "out there" (news, tech, money, culture). This week the pressure is **personal and live**: the question is about *your* work, *your* idea, *your* mistake, and the other person is waiting for an answer right now. You'll reuse your balanced-view language from [Week 19 Day 5](../week-19-complex-topics/day-5-balanced-views.md), your polite push-back from [Week 17 Day 3](../week-17-meetings/day-3-pushing-back-politely.md) and your trade-off language from [Week 18 Day 3](../week-18-explaining-your-work/day-3-explaining-trade-offs.md). This is also the last week of Month 5, so Day 6 includes the monthly check.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Find a long-form interview with someone who gets tough questions for a living: a founder, a product leader or a designer on a business or tech interview podcast. Search for "founder interview tough questions", "product leader interview podcast" or "design leader fireside chat Q&A". Listen while you walk.

Your one task: **notice the first five words of every answer**. Each time the interviewer asks something, say out loud (quietly is fine) what the guest's opener was: "Yeah, so…", "Great question…", "Honestly…", "I'd push back on that a little…". Count how many answers start with a real opener and how many start with silence or "umm". You'll probably find that almost every confident speaker uses an opener. Notice which ones you would steal. Write two into your phrase log tonight, then stop. No recording, no drills. Rest is part of the plan: your brain locks in this week's practice while you sleep.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Handle the four hard question types after a talk (multi-part, hostile, off-topic, "I don't know") without freezing.
- [ ] Start any unexpected answer within two seconds, using a natural opener instead of "umm".
- [ ] Defend an idea you believe in, calmly, with a reason, evidence and an offer, and know when to give ground.
- [ ] Deliver bad news to a boss or client in a clear, honest and calm way, and stay calm when they push.
- [ ] Give feedback that is specific and kind, and receive feedback without getting defensive.
- [ ] Complete the Month 5 check and see how far your umms and long pauses have dropped since Week 1.
