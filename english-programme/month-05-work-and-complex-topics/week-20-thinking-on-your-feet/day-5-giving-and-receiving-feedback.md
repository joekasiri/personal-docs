## Learning objectives

- Give feedback that is **specific, about the work (not the person), and useful**, using the situation → what you noticed → effect → suggestion pattern.
- Give **positive** feedback that sounds real, not empty.
- Receive feedback without defending yourself straight away: **thank → clarify → reflect → next step**.
- Disagree with feedback calmly when you think it's wrong.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Focus: warm openers for giving and taking feedback.** Notice how good interviewees respond when someone praises or criticises them: "Oh, thank you, that means a lot", "That's really helpful, actually." The voice is **warm**: a slight smile while speaking, a gentle rise on the first word, and a soft, slower ending. Smile while you say these sentences. It really changes the sound.

| # | Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oh, thanks, that's really helpful, actually. | "oh, THANKS, that's REALLY helpful, AK-chully" (smile) |
| 2 | Can I give you a bit of feedback on the deck? | "c'n I GIVE you a bit of FEED-back on the deck?" |
| 3 | I really liked how you opened with the user story. | "I REALLY liked how you OPENED with the USER story" |
| 4 | One thing that might make it even stronger… | "ONE thing that might make it EVEN stronger…" |
| 5 | That's fair. I hadn't seen it that way. | "that's FAIR. I HADn't seen it that way" |
| 6 | Can you give me an example? | "c'n you GIMME an ex-AM-ple?" |
| 7 | So what I'm hearing is, the intro's too long. | "so what I'm HEAR-ing is, the IN-tro's too LONG" |
| 8 | I appreciate you telling me. | "I ap-PREE-shee-ate you TELL-ing me" |
| 9 | Honestly, that was one of the clearest demos I've seen. | "HON-estly, that was one of the CLEAR-est DEM-os I've seen" |

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Giving and receiving feedback

### Explanation

**Giving feedback.** Vague feedback ("It's good", "I don't like it") is useless and can feel personal. Useful feedback has four parts. Think **S-N-E-S**:

1. **Situation:** when and where. "In yesterday's demo…"
2. **Noticed:** what you saw or heard, as a fact. "…you spent about five minutes on the technical setup…"
3. **Effect:** what it caused. "…and I think the sales folks lost interest before the good part."
4. **Suggestion (or question):** what could work better. "What if you started with the live demo and did the setup at the end?"

Talk about the **work or the behaviour**, not the person. "The intro was long" not "You're boring". Soften with words you already know ("a bit", "might", "I wonder if…" from [Week 4 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md)), but don't soften so much that the message disappears.

**Positive feedback needs the same detail.** "Great job!" is nice but forgettable. "The way you showed the before-and-after side by side made the result super clear" tells people what to do again.

**Receiving feedback.** The natural reaction is to explain *why* you did it ("Yes, but the reason was…"). That sounds defensive, and the other person stops being honest with you. Instead:

1. **Thank:** "Thanks, I appreciate you telling me."
2. **Clarify:** "Can you give me an example?" / "Was it the whole thing or one part?"
3. **Reflect:** say it back. "So what I'm hearing is, the first section's too long."
4. **Next step:** "I'll cut it down for Thursday." or, if you need time: "Let me sit with that."

**If you disagree with the feedback,** do steps 1–3 first. *Then* give your view calmly: "I see where you're coming from. I did it that way because… but I'm open to trying it." You've shown you listened, so now they'll listen to you.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Can I give you a bit of feedback on…? | you ask permission before giving feedback | "Can I give you a bit of feedback on the onboarding screens?" |
| I really liked how you… | you give specific positive feedback | "I really liked how you used real user quotes in the deck." |
| One thing that might make it even stronger is… | you suggest an improvement gently | "One thing that might make it even stronger is a clear call to action at the end." |
| I noticed that… | you state a fact, not a judgement | "I noticed that the error message only shows up after you submit." |
| The effect was… / I think it meant that… | you explain the impact | "I think it meant that people didn't see the price until the last step." |
| What if you tried…? / I wonder if… | you suggest without ordering | "I wonder if the filters would work better on the left." |
| Thanks, I appreciate you telling me. | you receive critical feedback | "Thanks, I appreciate you telling me. That's not easy to say." |
| Can you give me an example? | the feedback is vague | "Can you give me an example of where it felt confusing?" |
| So what I'm hearing is… | you reflect it back | "So what I'm hearing is, the colours feel too playful for a bank." |
| That's fair. / That's a good catch. | you accept a valid point | "That's a good catch. I totally missed that." |
| I see where you're coming from. | you're about to disagree politely | "I see where you're coming from. I went with it because of the research, but I'm open to testing both." |
| Let me sit with that. | you need time to think about the feedback | "Let me sit with that and get back to you tomorrow." |
| Is there anything I could be doing differently? | you ask for feedback yourself | "We've been working together for a month. Is there anything I could be doing differently?" |
| What's one thing you'd change? | you want a focused answer | "If you could change one thing about the flow, what would it be?" |

### Examples

**Example 1: Giving feedback to a colleague after a demo**

> **You:** Hey Jen, can I give you a bit of feedback on the demo?
> **Jen:** Sure, go for it.
> **You:** First, I really liked how you started with the customer's problem. Everyone was nodding. One thing that might make it even stronger: in the middle, you spent about five minutes on the settings page, and I think the sales folks lost interest before the good part. What if you showed the alerts first and did settings at the end?
> **Jen:** Oh, that's a good catch. I didn't notice I spent that long.

**Example 2: Receiving critical feedback in a design critique**

> **Tom (senior designer):** Honestly, the new navigation feels cluttered.
> **You:** Thanks, I appreciate you being honest. Can you give me an example? Is it the whole bar, or a specific part?
> **Tom:** Mostly the icons. There are eight, and they all look the same weight.
> **You:** So what I'm hearing is, the problem is hierarchy, not the number of items?
> **Tom:** Yeah, exactly. Maybe make the main three bigger.
> **You:** That's fair. I'll try a version with three primary icons and show you on Thursday.

**Example 3: Disagreeing with feedback calmly**

> **Nina (PM):** I think we should get rid of the progress bar. It makes the form look long.
> **You:** I see where you're coming from. It does make the length visible. I kept it because in our last test, people said not knowing how long it would take was the most stressful part. But I'm open to testing both. Want to run a quick A/B test on it?
> **Nina:** Yeah, OK, that's fair.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Your design is bad." / "You're too slow in presentations."
✅ "I noticed the buttons are hard to find on mobile."
*Talk about the work or the behaviour, not the person.*

❌ "Great job!" *(and nothing else)*
✅ "Great job. The before-and-after slide made the result super clear."
*Specific praise tells people what to keep doing.*

❌ "Yes, but I did it because…" *(straight away)*
✅ "Thanks. Can you give me an example?" *(explain later)*
*Defending immediately stops people giving you honest feedback.*

❌ "Maybe, possibly, it's just a small thing, it's probably fine, but perhaps the colour is a little bit…"
✅ "One thing that might make it stronger: the colour's a bit low-contrast."
*Soften once. Too much softening hides the message.*

❌ "Thank you for your feedbacks."
✅ "Thanks for the feedback." / "Thanks for all the feedback."
*"Feedback" is uncountable: no "s", no "a feedback". Say "a bit of feedback" or "a piece of feedback".*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Step 1: Feedback role-plays (30 min)

Use the situations in [Exercise 5.4](exercises.md#exercise-54-feedback-situations-drill-material).

1. **Giving (12 min):** do situations 1–4. For each, say your S-N-E-S feedback out loud (about 1 minute), record it, then listen once. Was there a fact, an effect and a suggestion?
2. **Receiving (12 min):** do situations 5–8. Read the feedback out loud in the other person's voice, then respond. Do each one twice: first when you *agree* with the feedback, then when you *disagree*.
3. **3/2/1 (6 min):** use the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md), but shorter (90 / 60 / 30 seconds). Prompt: **"Tell a colleague about the most useful piece of feedback you've ever received, and what you changed because of it."**

### Step 2: Live conversation #2 (40 min)

**Your mission: a two-way feedback swap.** Ask your partner for honest feedback on your English, and give them feedback on something they share with you.

Set it up like this: "I'm practising giving and receiving feedback in English. Could you tell me honestly what I could improve in my speaking? And if you want, show me something you're working on and I'll give you my thoughts."

Things to try:

1. Ask: "Is there anything I could be doing differently when I speak?" or "What's one thing you'd change about my English?"
2. When they answer, use all four receiving steps: thank, clarify ("Can you give me an example?"), reflect ("So what I'm hearing is…"), next step.
3. Give one piece of specific positive feedback with "I really liked how you…".
4. Give one suggestion with "One thing that might make it even stronger…".
5. Write down their feedback straight after the conversation. Carry it into Day 6.

If it's a real work critique, use the same steps with the designs you're discussing. Partner tips: [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
