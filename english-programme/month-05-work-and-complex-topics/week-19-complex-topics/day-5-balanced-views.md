## Learning objectives

- Give a balanced answer in five parts: frame, side A, side B, weigh, land.
- Present the *best* version of the side you disagree with, so people trust your view.
- Turn a weak "it depends" into a strong one: "It depends on X. If…, then…; if not, then…".
- Concede a good point in a discussion without losing your whole position.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Feature: concluding signposts and the final fall.** At the end of a long, balanced answer, podcast hosts signal "here's my conclusion" with phrases like "So on balance…", "Where I land is…", "At the end of the day…". You can hear the conclusion coming because:

1. The speaker **slows down** for the signpost and often **pauses after it**.
2. The voice **falls clearly at the end** of the conclusion ↘. That fall says "I've decided. Your turn."
3. Before the conclusion, the "on the other hand" part often **rises**, because the thought isn't finished yet.

Practise. CAPITALS = stress, ↗ = rising, ↘ = falling, | = pause.

1. SO, | on BALANCE, | I think it's worth TRYING ↘.
2. Where I LAND is | it's a good idea, | but not for EVERYONE ↘.
3. On the ONE hand, it's CHEAPER ↗, | but on the OTHER hand, it's way SLOWER ↘.
4. I can see the argument for BOTH ↗, | HONestly.
5. To be FAIR to the other SIDE, | they've got a REAL point about COST.
6. Where it gets TRICKY | is when you think about OLDER people.
7. At the END of the day, | it comes down to TRUST ↘. ("at the END of the day" → "a-thee-END-uh-the-DAY")
8. If I HAD to pick, | I'd go with the SECOND option ↘.
9. That's a FAIR point, | and it kinda CHANGES things for me.
10. ALL things considered, | I'd say YES, | but with a FEW conditions ↘.

**Clip to find:** a discussion where two people disagree respectfully and each sums up their view at the end. Search: "both sides debate podcast", "respectful disagreement podcast", "two hosts disagree podcast", "debate club discussion podcast". Listen especially to the last minute of each person's turn.

**Method:** speak along with 2 minutes, three times ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)). Then pause-and-repeat three conclusions you hear, copying the final fall exactly.

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Balanced views

### Explanation

All week you've talked about big topics. Tonight you learn the structure that makes any complex answer sound mature and clear: the **balanced view**. It's what thoughtful people do at dinner parties, in meetings and in interviews. They don't just say "I think X". They show they've **understood both sides**, and *then* they decide.

**The five-part balanced answer:**

| Part | What you do | Phrases |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame | Say why it's not simple, or what the real question is | "It's a tricky one, because…", "I think the real question is…" |
| 2. Side A | The best argument for one side | "The best argument for it is…", "On one hand…" |
| 3. Side B | The best argument for the other side | "On the other hand…", "The flip side is…", "To be fair to the other side…" |
| 4. Weigh | Say which factor matters most, or where it gets difficult | "Where it gets tricky is…", "The question is which matters more…" |
| 5. Land | Commit to a view, and keep a door open | "On balance…", "Where I land is…", "If I had to pick…" |

You don't need all five every time. In a quick chat, Frame + Side A + Side B + Land in four sentences is perfect. In a longer discussion, you'll stretch each part.

**Make the other side strong.** If you describe the side you disagree with as stupid ("some people think X because they don't understand…"), nobody trusts your view. Describe it the way its best supporter would. "To be fair to the other side, they've got a real point about…". This is called giving the strongest version of the argument, and it's the most persuasive thing you can do.

**Upgrade "it depends".** "It depends" on its own sounds like you're avoiding the question ([Week 6 Day 5](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md) taught it as a softener). In a balanced view, always say **what** it depends on and **what you think in each case**: "It depends on the job. If it's mostly computer work, then yes; if it's hands-on, then honestly no."

**Conceding without collapsing.** When someone makes a good point, you can accept it and still keep your view: "That's a fair point, and I'll give you that. But I still think…". Or change your mind a little: "OK, that actually changes things for me a bit." Both sound confident.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's a tricky one, because… | You frame a question that isn't simple | "It's a tricky one, because both sides want the same thing, really." |
| I think the real question is… | You reframe a question to the key issue | "I think the real question is who pays for it." |
| The best argument for it is… | You give side A at its strongest | "The best argument for it is that it saves people a ton of time." |
| On one hand… on the other hand… | You present two sides | "On one hand it's cheaper; on the other hand, it's way less reliable." |
| The flip side is… | You turn to the other side | "The flip side is, small shops might not survive." |
| To be fair to the other side… | You give the opposing view respect | "To be fair to the other side, they've got a real point about safety." |
| Where it gets tricky is… | You show the hard part | "Where it gets tricky is people who can't afford it." |
| It depends on… If…, then…; if not… | You give a strong, specific "it depends" | "It depends on the city. If there's good transport, then sure; if not, it's a nightmare." |
| On balance… / All things considered… | You start your conclusion | "On balance, I think it's worth trying." |
| Where I land is… | You give your final view, casually | "Where I land is, yes, but slowly." |
| If I had to pick… | You choose when both sides are strong | "If I had to pick, I'd go with the four-day week." |
| That's a fair point, and I'll give you that. | You concede part of the argument | "That's a fair point, and I'll give you that. But I still think it's worth it." |
| That actually changes things for me a bit. | You change your mind partly | "Hmm, that actually changes things for me a bit. I hadn't thought about rural areas." |
| I can see it both ways, but… | You admit both sides are strong, then decide | "I can see it both ways, but I'd lean towards no." |
| I'd lean towards… | You give a soft but clear conclusion | "I'd lean towards keeping it optional." |

### Examples

**Example 1: a quick balanced view (four sentences)**

> **A:** Should kids have phones in school, do you think?
> **B:** It's a tricky one. On one hand, parents wanna be able to reach their kids. The flip side is, nobody's paying attention in class if there's a phone on the desk. Where I land is: phones in bags during lessons, out at lunch.

**Example 2: a full balanced view at a dinner**

> **A:** What do you think about making public transport free?
> **B:** Ooh, OK. I think the real question is who pays for it, right? Because it's never actually free.
> **A:** True.
> **B:** The best argument for it is that it gets cars off the road, and it really helps people who don't earn much. Like, if you're spending a big chunk of your pay on bus tickets, that's huge. To be fair to the other side, though, they've got a real point: it costs the city a ton, and the money has to come from somewhere, so maybe from taxes, or from other services. And some people say if it's free, it gets super crowded and nobody fixes anything.
> **A:** Yeah, I've heard that.
> **B:** Where it gets tricky is the quality. Free but terrible doesn't help anyone. So on balance, I'd lean towards making it free for students and older people first, and seeing what happens. That's just me, though. Where do you land?

**Example 3: conceding a point and keeping your view**

> **A:** I just think remote work is better for everyone. No commute, more time with family.
> **B:** I can see that. Where I'd push back a little is junior people. It's way harder to learn when you can't just lean over and ask someone.
> **A:** OK, that's a fair point, and I'll give you that. But you could fix that with mentoring calls, right?
> **B:** Maybe. Honestly, that actually changes things for me a bit. So maybe it's not remote versus office, it's more like, it depends on the stage of your career. If you're new, then more office days; if you're experienced, then go fully remote if you want.
> **A:** Yeah, I could live with that.

### Common mistakes

❌ "It depends." (and stop)
✅ "It depends on the job. If it's mostly on a computer, then yes. If not, probably not."
*A bare "it depends" sounds like you're avoiding the question. Say what it depends on.*

❌ "Some people think it's bad, but they're just afraid of change."
✅ "To be fair to the other side, they've got a real point about cost."
*Making the other side sound silly makes your own view less believable.*

❌ "From one side… from the other side…"
✅ "On one hand… on the other hand…"
*This is a fixed expression. Translating "from one side" from other languages sounds unnatural in English.*

❌ "In conclusion, I am agree with the first opinion."
✅ "So on balance, I'd go with the first option." / "Where I land is…"
*"In conclusion" sounds like an essay. And "agree" is a verb: "I agree", never "I am agree".*

❌ Giving both sides and then saying "So… yeah. Both are good."
✅ "I can see it both ways, but I'd lean towards the first one."
*A balanced view still ends with a decision. Otherwise it sounds like you have no opinion.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Warm-up drill (30 min)

**3/2/1 balanced view (20 min).** Use [the 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md). Motion: **"Cities should ban cars from their centres."** Give a full five-part balanced answer: frame, the best argument for, the best argument against, where it gets tricky, where you land. 3 minutes, then 2, then 1. In round 3, you'll need one sentence per part.

**Concede-and-continue round (10 min).** For each statement below, (a) say "That's a fair point, and I'll give you that…", (b) add your own "but…", and (c) land. 30–40 seconds each, out loud.

1. "Working four days a week would make people lazier."
2. "Social media has done more good than harm for small businesses."
3. "Everyone should learn to cook before they leave home."
4. "Kids today have it easier than their parents did."
5. "Tipping should be replaced by higher wages."
6. "Online friendships are just as real as in-person ones."
7. "Everyone should do at least one year working in customer service."
8. "It's better to be a generalist than a specialist."

### Part B: Live conversation #2 (40 min)

**Mission: have a real discussion about one complex question and give a full balanced view on it.** Choose a question from the [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials) topic bank, or ask your partner to choose. A good way in: "Can I ask you something random? I've been thinking about this all week…"

Things to try:

1. **Frame** the question before answering it ("I think the real question is…").
2. Give the **best version** of the side you don't agree with ("To be fair to the other side…").
3. Use **one strong "it depends"**: say what it depends on and what you think in each case.
4. **Concede** one point your partner makes ("That's a fair point, and I'll give you that…").
5. **Land** clearly, with a final fall in your voice, and hand over: "Where do you land?"

Afterwards, write down which of the five parts you skipped most.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, use voice mode ([Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)) and paste this:

```text
Let's have a 30-minute friendly discussion, like two friends at a cafe. You play Robin, who is curious and enjoys a good debate but is always respectful. Pick three questions from these topics: work, cities, technology, education, lifestyle. Avoid party politics and religion. For each question, ask for my view, then make one strong point for the side I didn't choose, so I can practise conceding and continuing. Keep your turns to 2-4 sentences. Don't correct me during the discussion. At the end, tell me: 1) which of these I did well and which I skipped: framing, both sides, weighing, landing, 2) three grammar or vocabulary corrections, 3) one sentence I could have said more naturally.
```
