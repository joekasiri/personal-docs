## Learning objectives

- Talk about money in a relaxed, polite way, without sharing (or asking for) private numbers.
- Describe costs and value with natural everyday phrases ("it adds up", "it's worth it", "it's a splurge").
- Weigh a big life choice out loud: name the options, the trade-off, what it comes down to, and your choice.
- Give both sides of a life-choice question before you say what you'd do.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Feature: signposting reasoning steps.** When people weigh a choice in a podcast, they often build a long answer in steps: "First of all…, then there's…, and on top of that…, so for me…". Each signpost is like a little door into the next part.

1. The signpost is said **quickly and lightly**: "FIRST of all" → "FIRS-tuh-VALL", "on TOP of that" → "on-TAH-puh-THAT".
2. After the signpost, **the key word** gets the big stress: "then there's the whole COMMUTE thing".
3. The final step ("so for ME…") **slows down** a little, because it's the conclusion.

Practise. CAPITALS = stress, | = tiny pause.

1. FIRST of all, | rent here is just CRAZY. ("FIRST of all" → "FIRS-tuh-VALL")
2. And THEN there's the whole COMMUTE thing, | which is like an HOUR each WAY.
3. On TOP of that, | you've got TAXES, | INSURANCE, | all that STUFF. ("on TOP of that" → "on-TAH-puh-THAT")
4. The OTHER thing is, | I'd be FAR from my FAMILY.
5. It's not just about the MONEY, | it's about the LIFESTYLE.
6. When you ADD it all up, | it's actually not THAT much CHEAPER.
7. It REALLY comes down to | what you VALUE more. ("comes down to" → "kumz-DOWN-tuh")
8. If money WEREN'T an issue, | I'd TOTALLY do it.
9. So for ME, | PERSONALLY, | the FREEDOM wins.
10. But THAT'S just me. | I know a LOT of people would go the OTHER way.

**Clip to find:** two or three people talking about money decisions or life choices in a relaxed way. Search: "personal finance podcast conversation", "money talk podcast friends", "renting vs buying podcast discussion", "career change podcast conversation", "work life balance podcast chat". Avoid sales-style videos that try to sell you a course.

**Method:** speak along with 2 minutes, three times ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)). Then pause-and-repeat any sentence with two or more signposts.

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Money and life choices

### Explanation

**Talking about money politely.** In the US (and many other places), it's normal to talk about money *in general* ("rent is crazy here", "that's a lot for a coffee"), but personal numbers are often private. Many Americans don't ask "How much do you earn?" or "How much did your apartment cost?" unless they're close friends. Here's how to stay comfortable:

- **Talk in general, not in personal numbers.** "It's not cheap" or "it's more than I wanted to spend" instead of the exact amount.
- **If someone asks you directly and you don't want to answer**, deflect lightly: "Ha, let's just say it wasn't cheap." "More than I'd like to admit."
- **If you want to ask**, ask about *ranges* or *experience*, not numbers: "Is it way more expensive than where you were before?"

**Weighing a life choice.** Questions like "Would you rather rent or buy?", "Would you take a job you love for less money?" or "Would you move abroad?" have no right answer. A strong answer has four parts:

| Part | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The options | Say what the choice is between | "So it's basically a stable job versus doing something I actually love." |
| 2. Both sides | Give one real point for each option | "The stable job means I can save and not stress. But the other one, I'd actually be excited to go to work." |
| 3. What it comes down to | Name the value that decides it | "For me, it really comes down to how much risk I can handle right now." |
| 4. Your choice + a door open | Say what you'd do, and that others might choose differently | "So right now, I'd probably take the stable one. But ask me again in five years." |

The key phrase of the night is **"It comes down to…"**. It helps you skip the endless list of pros and cons and say what really matters.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's not cheap. / It wasn't cheap. | You talk about cost without giving a number | "The course wasn't cheap, but it was worth it." |
| Let's just say… | You politely avoid giving a number | "How much was it? Ha, let's just say I'm eating rice for a month." |
| It adds up. | Small costs become big | "Coffee every day doesn't seem like much, but it adds up." |
| It's worth it. / It's not worth it. | You judge value | "Paying more to live close to work is so worth it. I get two hours back a day." |
| It's a bit of a splurge. | You spend more than usual on something nice | "The headphones were a bit of a splurge, but I use them every day." |
| I'm trying to cut back on… | You want to spend less | "I'm trying to cut back on food delivery." |
| It's out of my budget. / It's a bit much for me right now. | Something is too expensive for you | "I'd love to go, but it's a bit much for me right now." |
| It's a trade-off between… and… | You name the choice | "It's a trade-off between money and freedom." |
| It comes down to… | You name the deciding value | "It comes down to whether you want stability or adventure." |
| The way I look at it… | You explain your personal logic | "The way I look at it, you can always earn more money, but you can't get time back." |
| …outweighs… | One side is more important for you | "For me, being near family outweighs the higher salary." |
| If money weren't an issue, I'd… | You imagine without limits (recycles [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/day-1-if-i-could.md)) | "If money weren't an issue, I'd take six months off to travel." |
| At this point in my life… | Your answer depends on your stage of life | "At this point in my life, I'd rather rent. I don't know where I'll be in five years." |
| I can see why people would… | You respect the other choice | "I can totally see why people would buy, though. It's your own place." |
| That's just me, though. | You close your view and leave room for others | "I'd pick the experience over the stuff. That's just me, though." |

### Examples

**Example 1: rent or buy?**

> **A:** So are you ever gonna buy a place, or are you a renter for life?
> **B:** Ha, good question. It's a trade-off between flexibility and, like, security, I guess. If you rent, you can just pack up and move whenever. But you're paying someone else's mortgage, you know? And if you buy, it's yours, you can paint the walls, whatever. But you're kind of stuck.
> **A:** Right.
> **B:** For me, it really comes down to whether I know where I'll be in five years. And at this point in my life, I really don't. So I'd rent, for now. But I can totally see why people buy. My sister bought last year and she loves it. That's just me, though. What about you?

**Example 2: a job you love for less money**

> **A:** Would you take a job you loved if it paid way less?
> **B:** Hmm. First of all, it depends how much less. Like, ten percent, sure. Half? That's a whole different question. Then there's the question of what "love" means. A lot of people love their hobby, but when it's your job, it's different.
> **A:** True.
> **B:** The way I look at it, you spend, like, a third of your life at work. So enjoying it matters a lot. But on top of that, money stress is real. It kinda ruins everything. So for me, I'd take a little less for a job I really liked. Not a lot less. Being able to sleep at night outweighs the dream job. If money weren't an issue, though? I'd be a furniture designer tomorrow.

**Example 3: avoiding a personal number, politely**

> **A:** Your apartment's so nice! What are you paying for this, if you don't mind me asking?
> **B:** Ha, let's just say it wasn't cheap. It's a bit of a splurge, honestly. But I work from home a lot, so for me it's worth it. Before this I had a tiny place with no light, and it was, like, really getting to me.
> **A:** Totally get that.
> **B:** And I'm trying to cut back on other stuff, like food delivery. It adds up so fast.

### Common mistakes

❌ "How much is your salary?" (to a colleague or new friend)
✅ "Is it pretty well-paid, that kind of role?" or just "Is the pay OK?"
*Exact personal numbers can feel private. Ask about the general picture.*

❌ "It's not deserve it."
✅ "It's not worth it."
*To talk about value, use "worth it". "Deserve" is about people getting what they earned.*

❌ "If money is not a problem, I will travel."
✅ "If money weren't an issue, I'd travel."
*For an imagined, unreal situation, use the second conditional: "if + past, would".*

❌ "Renting is better. Buying is a mistake." (said as fact)
✅ "At this point in my life, renting makes more sense for me. But I can see why people buy."
*Life choices depend on the person. Make your answer personal and respect the other side.*

❌ Listing ten pros and cons and never deciding.
✅ "…but really, it comes down to one thing: how much risk I can handle."
*"It comes down to…" stops the endless list and shows you've actually thought about it.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): voice mode with Claude

Tonight Claude is your discussion partner. You'll talk through life-choice questions, and **every time, you give both sides before you give your view**. Claude will push you with "why?" and with the side you didn't choose.

### Round 1: Money small talk (15 min)

Start voice mode and paste this:

```text
Let's practise relaxed small talk about money, like two friends catching up. You play Alex, a friend who just moved to a new apartment and recently bought something expensive. Ask me about the cost of living where I live, things I've splurged on, and what I'm trying to cut back on. Sometimes ask me a slightly too personal money question (like how much I pay for rent) so I can practise avoiding it politely. Keep your turns short (1-3 sentences). Don't correct me yet. After 12 minutes, tell me which phrases about money I used well and one I should add.
```

### Round 2: Three life choices, both sides (40 min)

Paste this:

```text
Now let's discuss life choices. Ask me these three questions, one at a time, and spend about 12 minutes on each:
1) Would you rather rent or buy a home, and why?
2) Would you take a job you love for 30% less money?
3) Would you move to another country for a better career if it meant leaving your family and friends?
For each one: first ask me to give BOTH sides before my opinion. Then, whatever I choose, argue the other side politely and ask "why?" at least twice. Push me to say what it "comes down to". Keep your turns short. Don't correct my English during the discussion. After each question, give me one sentence of feedback on how balanced and clear my answer was.
```

What to focus on:

1. **Both sides first.** Before you say what you'd do, give one real point for each option.
2. **"It comes down to…"** at least once per question.
3. **Signposts**: "First of all…", "Then there's…", "On top of that…", "So for me…". Use the ones you shadowed.
4. **Stay calm when Claude argues the other side.** Use "I can see why people would…" and then explain why you still choose yours (or change your mind; that's fine too).
5. **No silences longer than 2 seconds.** If you're stuck, use a time-buyer from [Week 3 Day 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) and keep going.

### Round 3: Corrections (15 min)

Paste this:

```text
Now please give me feedback on the whole conversation: 1) my five most important grammar or vocabulary mistakes, each with my original sentence and a corrected natural version, 2) two places where my answer wasn't balanced or I didn't really decide, and a better way to say it, 3) three natural phrases for weighing choices that I didn't use but should. Then ask me the third question again so I can give a better 2-minute answer using your corrections.
```

Write the corrections into your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md) while Claude talks. Then give the improved 2-minute answer and record it on your phone too.
