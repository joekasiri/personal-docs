## Learning objectives

- Explain a tech idea in one plain sentence before you discuss it.
- Talk about how technology is changing work and life, using impact language ("it's changed the way…", "it's made… way easier").
- Choose the right level of certainty for a prediction, from "no way" to "definitely".
- Express mixed feelings about technology without sounding scared or like an advert.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Feature: long sentences with lists and contrasts.** When podcast hosts talk about tech, they often list things ("it's faster, it's cheaper, and it's everywhere") and then contrast ("but the catch is…"). Two sound patterns make this easy to follow:

1. **Lists go up, up, then down.** Each item rises a little, and the last one falls: "it's FASTER ↗, it's CHEAPER ↗, and it's EVERYWHERE ↘."
2. **The contrast word gets a little extra stress and a pause after it:** "BUT | the catch is…", "The THING is, though | …".

Practise. CAPITALS = stress, ↗ = rising, ↘ = falling, | = tiny pause.

1. It's FASTER ↗, it's CHEAPER ↗, and honestly it's kinda FUN ↘.
2. It's GREAT for first drafts, | BUT | I wouldn't trust it with the final VERSION.
3. The UPside is it saves you HOURS; | the DOWNside is you stop LEARNING the basics.
4. I use it for NOTES ↗, for EMAILS ↗, and SOMEtimes for CODE ↘.
5. Ten years ago NObody had one, | and now it's, like, in EVERY pocket. ("in EVERY pocket" → "in-EV-ree-PAH-kit")
6. It's not that it's BAD, | it's that we're using it for EVERYthing.
7. The THING is, though, | it's only as good as the stuff you GIVE it.
8. I could SEE it replacing the boring PARTS of my job, | but not the whole THING.
9. It's made my job EASIER ↗, FASTER ↗, and a little bit WEIRDER ↘.
10. I'm not WORRIED exactly, | I'm just... curious where it's GOING. ("going" → "GO-in")

**Clip to find:** two or three hosts discussing a new technology, how they use it, and whether it's good. Search: "tech podcast hosts discuss AI", "tech news roundtable podcast", "designers talk about AI tools podcast", "future of work podcast conversation". Pick a clip where they disagree a bit.

**Method:** speak along with a 2-minute stretch three times ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)). Then pause-and-repeat any sentence with a list in it, copying the up-up-down melody.

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Tech and AI

### Explanation

Tech and AI come up everywhere now: at work, at dinner, with your parents. You work in design and product, so people will often ask *you* what you think. These conversations go wrong in three ways:

1. **Too technical.** You use words the listener doesn't know. Fix: start with **one plain sentence** that explains the thing ("It's basically a tool that writes a first draft for you").
2. **Too certain.** "AI will replace all designers." Fix: pick the **right level of certainty**. Most predictions about tech are guesses, so sound like it.
3. **Too one-sided.** Either "it's amazing" or "it's terrible". Fix: most people have **mixed feelings**, and saying so makes you sound thoughtful.

**The certainty scale.** In [Week 10 Day 5](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/day-5-future-plans-and-predictions.md) you learned to make predictions with "gonna", "will", "might" and "probably". Tonight, use a full scale, from most to least sure:

| How sure? | Say… |
|---|---|
| 100% | "It's definitely gonna…", "There's no question it'll…" |
| 80% | "It'll probably…", "I'd be surprised if it didn't…" |
| 60% | "There's a good chance…", "I could see it…" |
| 50% | "It could go either way.", "Honestly, who knows?" |
| 30% | "I doubt it'll…", "I'm not convinced it'll…" |
| 5% | "No way it'll…", "I really can't see it…" |

**The impact pattern.** To talk about how tech changes things, use "it's changed the way…", "it's made X way easier/harder", "it used to be…, now…". Then add a **personal example** from your own work. Personal examples make big topics real and stop the conversation from becoming abstract.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's basically a… that… | You explain the tech in one plain sentence | "It's basically a tool that turns a rough sketch into a clickable prototype." |
| It's changed the way we… | You describe a big change | "It's changed the way we do research. We summarise interviews in minutes now." |
| It's made… way easier / harder | You describe a specific effect | "It's made first drafts way easier, but reviewing them is harder." |
| It used to be…, now… | You compare before and after | "It used to take a week to test an idea. Now it takes an afternoon." |
| I've been using it for… | You add your own experience | "I've been using it for writing UX copy, and it's actually pretty decent." |
| The upside is… the downside is… | You give mixed feelings in one breath | "The upside is speed; the downside is everything starts to look the same." |
| It's a double-edged sword. | Something is good and bad at once | "AI for hiring is a double-edged sword. Faster, but who checks it's fair?" |
| The catch is… | You add the hidden problem | "It's free. The catch is, you're paying with your data." |
| I'd be surprised if it didn't… | You're about 80% sure | "I'd be surprised if every design tool didn't have it in two years." |
| I could see it… | You're about 60% sure | "I could see it taking over the boring parts of my job." |
| I'm not convinced it'll… | You're doubtful but polite | "I'm not convinced it'll replace real user research." |
| It could go either way. | You genuinely don't know | "Will people trust it for medical stuff? It could go either way." |
| It's only as good as… | You explain a limit | "It's only as good as the information you give it." |
| What worries me a bit is… | You share a concern calmly | "What worries me a bit is junior designers not learning the basics." |

### Examples

**Example 1: explaining your job to a friend's parent**

> **A:** So do you use that AI stuff at work?
> **B:** Yeah, a bit. It's basically a tool that gives you a first draft of something: text, images, even a rough screen layout.
> **A:** So it does your job for you?
> **B:** [laughs] Not really. It's more like a really fast intern. It's made the first draft way easier, but somebody still has to decide what's actually good. It used to take me a whole morning to write all the error messages for an app. Now I get a draft in two minutes and spend the morning fixing it.
> **A:** Huh. Is it gonna replace you?
> **B:** I'm not convinced it'll replace designers. I could see it taking over the boring parts, though. Honestly? It could go either way in, like, ten years. Who knows.

**Example 2: mixed feelings with a colleague**

> **A:** Did you try the new auto-layout AI thing in our design tool?
> **B:** Yeah, I've been using it for a couple weeks. The upside is it's crazy fast. The downside is everything starts to look kinda the same, you know? Same spacing, same cards.
> **A:** Totally.
> **B:** And what worries me a bit is the junior folks. If it does the layout for you every time, when do you learn *why* the layout works? It's a double-edged sword. I'd be surprised if we weren't all using it by next year, though.

**Example 3: talking about a tech story (using yesterday's shape)**

> **A:** Did you see that thing about the delivery drones?
> **B:** No, what?
> **A:** So basically, a grocery chain's testing drone delivery in a few suburbs. Apparently it's, like, fifteen minutes door to door. The catch is, they can only carry something like two kilos. So it's great for medicine or a forgotten ingredient, not your weekly shopping.
> **B:** That's kinda cool.
> **A:** Yeah. My take is, it's a fun idea, but I doubt it'll be normal anytime soon. There's noise, there's safety, there's people's privacy. It could go either way. Would you use it?

### Common mistakes

❌ "AI will take all the jobs. It is sure."
✅ "I could see it changing a lot of jobs. Some will probably disappear, but new ones will show up too."
*Big, certain predictions sound naive and start arguments. Use the certainty scale.*

❌ "It uses an LLM with a diffusion pipeline to generate…" (to someone outside tech)
✅ "It's basically a tool that writes a first draft for you."
*Explain in one plain sentence first. Add technical detail only if they ask.*

❌ "I am using it since one year."
✅ "I've been using it for about a year."
*For something that started in the past and continues now, use the present perfect continuous + "for".*

❌ "It made the work more easy."
✅ "It's made the work way easier."
*"Easy" is a short adjective: "easier", not "more easy". Use "way" or "a lot" to intensify casually.*

❌ "Technology is good and bad." (and then silence)
✅ "The upside is…, but the downside is… For me, the upside wins, mostly."
*Saying "it's good and bad" isn't a view. Name the upside and downside, then say which wins for you.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Warm-up drill (30 min)

**3/2/1 (20 min).** Use [the 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md). Prompt: **"How has technology changed your job in the last few years, and where do you think it's going?"** Include: one plain-sentence explanation, one "it used to be…, now…", one personal example, one upside and downside, and one prediction with a clear level of certainty. Tell it in 3 minutes, then 2, then 1.

**Rapid-fire certainty round (10 min).** Answer each question in about 20 seconds. Start every answer with a phrase from the certainty scale, then give one reason.

1. Will people still use keyboards in 20 years?
2. Will most cars drive themselves in your lifetime?
3. Will AI ever design a whole app without a human?
4. Will people stop learning foreign languages because of instant translation?
5. Will paper books disappear?
6. Will most people work from home one day?
7. Will we have a phone-free generation of teenagers?
8. Will video calls ever feel as good as meeting in person?
9. Will smart glasses replace phones?
10. Will robots do most housework?
11. Will people trust AI doctors?
12. Will cash disappear completely?

### Part B: Live conversation #1 (40 min)

**Mission: have a real conversation about technology and how it's changing things, and keep it two-sided.** Good opener: "Have you been using any of those AI tools at work? I'm curious how other people use them."

Things to try:

1. Explain one tool or idea in **one plain sentence** before talking about it.
2. Give **one personal example** with "it used to be…, now…".
3. Use **two different levels** from the certainty scale (for example "I'd be surprised if…" and "I'm not convinced…").
4. Say **one mixed feeling** ("The upside is…, the downside is…").
5. Ask **two follow-up questions** about *their* experience, and react to what they say before giving your view.

Right after the call or chat, write down: one moment you sounded too certain or too technical, and one phrase you wish you'd had.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, do the same conversation in voice mode with Claude ([Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)). Paste this:

```text
Let's have a relaxed 30-minute conversation about technology and AI, like two coworkers chatting over coffee. You play Sam, a friendly product manager who's a bit more excited about AI than I am. Ask me how I use tech at work, share your own (invented) experiences, and gently disagree with me sometimes. Keep your turns short (2-4 sentences) so I do most of the talking. Don't correct me during the chat. At the end, give me: 1) three places where I sounded too certain or not certain enough, with a better phrase for each, 2) my three most common grammar mistakes with corrections, 3) two natural phrases you used that I should steal.
```
