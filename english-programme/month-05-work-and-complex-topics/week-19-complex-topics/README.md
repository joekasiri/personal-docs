# Week 19: Complex topics
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5: Work and complex topics](../README.md) · [← Week 18](../week-18-explaining-your-work/README.md) · [Week 20 →](../week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you leave the office and step into the big conversations: the news, technology and AI, culture, money and life choices. These are the topics that come up at dinner, at a meetup or on a long lunch break, and they're exactly where B2 speakers freeze. Not because they don't have opinions, but because the ideas are complicated and the sentences get long. By the end of the week you can talk about a big topic for three to five minutes without losing your way. You'll summarise a story you heard, explain both sides fairly, weigh them, and land on your own view, all in relaxed spoken English. You'll do it with signposts ("The background is…", "The flip side is…", "Where I land is…") that guide the listener, and keep *you* on track too.

## How this builds on last week

Last week you explained your own work: your job, your projects, your trade-offs, and your "why". That was a topic you know better than anyone else. This week the topics belong to everybody, and you often know only part of the story. So the skill shifts from "explain what I know" to "talk well about what I only half know, and stay fair". The trade-off language from [Week 18 Day 3](../week-18-explaining-your-work/day-3-explaining-trade-offs.md) comes back on Day 4 (money and life choices), and the "why?" pressure from [Week 18 Day 4](../week-18-explaining-your-work/day-4-answering-why-questions.md) comes back every time someone asks you to justify your view.

You already know how to give an opinion ([Week 6 Day 1](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-1-giving-opinions.md)), hedge it ([Week 6 Day 5](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md)), talk when you're unsure ([Week 11 Day 3](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/day-3-talking-when-youre-unsure.md)) and argue a side you don't agree with ([Week 12 Day 5](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-5-arguing-the-other-side.md)). This week does **not** re-teach those. It puts them together into longer, structured, balanced answers.

## Day 7: light day

30 minutes only. Go for a walk and listen to one episode (or 25 minutes of one) of a discussion podcast where two or three people talk through a big question: the future of work, how money habits differ between generations, what makes cities nice to live in. While you listen, do one simple thing: **every time a host signposts** ("the flip side is…", "here's the thing though…", "so where I land is…"), say it quietly to yourself. Don't write anything down. When you get home, say out loud the three signposts you remember best. Then rest. Your brain needs a quiet day to lock in a heavy week.

- [ ] summarise a news-type story in under a minute, say where you heard it, and separate facts from your opinion
- [ ] talk about technology and AI with the right level of certainty, without sounding either scared or like an advert
- [ ] compare your home culture with the US carefully, explaining customs without stereotyping
- [ ] talk about money and big life choices politely, and weigh a choice out loud
- [ ] give a balanced answer: frame the question, present both sides fairly, weigh them and land on your view
