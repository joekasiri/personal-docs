# Week 19 · Day 6: Weekly check
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5](../README.md) › [Week 19](README.md) · [← Previous: Balanced views](day-5-balanced-views.md) · [Next: Week 20 · Questions after a talk →](../week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/day-1-questions-after-a-talk.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 15 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 15 min

## Before you start

- Have your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) open with this week's numbers from Days 1–5.
- Open your recording app and [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) guide.
- Confirm tonight's **live conversation #3** (60 min). Ideally make it a small group (3+ people) or someone you haven't talked to about big topics before. See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- Have the [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials) open: the topic bank and the balanced-view checklist.

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the hardest clip of the week, probably the one with the longest sentences or the fastest disagreement. Speak along with it twice without pausing ([Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md)). Then speak along a third time and **exaggerate the signposts**: make "the flip side is…", "where I land is…" and "on top of that…" slightly slower and clearer than the speaker does. Finish by saying the five signposts you like best from this week, each in a new sentence of your own.

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**Unprepared prompt.** Don't read it until you're ready to press record. Then start talking within 3 seconds:

> **"A friend says: 'Everyone should live abroad for at least a year at some point in their life.' What do you think?"**

Talk for **2 minutes**. Try to include:
- a frame ("I think the real question is…")
- the best argument for and the best argument against
- one comparison between back home and somewhere else ("whereas…")
- one money or life-choice point ("It comes down to…")
- a clear landing with a final fall in your voice

Then:
1. Listen back once and count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms/uhhs, pauses longer than 2 seconds, and different connectors.
2. Write them in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) next to last week's numbers.
3. Listen a second time and tick the [balanced-view checklist](exercises.md#day-6-materials). Which part did you skip?

## 3. Skill check (15 min)

Rate yourself honestly for each day. **3** = I can do this easily, even under pressure. **2** = I can do it, but I have to think. **1** = I still freeze or forget.

| Day | Topic | I can… | Rating (1–3) | If you scored 1… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [News and current events](day-1-news-and-current-events.md) | summarise a story in five steps, say where I heard it and how sure I am, and keep facts and opinion separate | | Redo Exercise 1.2 with two new stories from the [random topic cards](../../toolkit/random-topics.md), recording each one. |
| 2 | [Tech and AI](day-2-tech-and-ai.md) | explain a tech idea in one plain sentence, give a personal before-and-after, and make predictions at the right level of certainty | | Redo Exercise 2.2 and then answer five of the Day 2 rapid-fire questions again, recording them. |
| 3 | [Culture and differences](day-3-culture-and-differences.md) | compare cultures with softened generalisations, explain a custom in four steps, and handle a stereotype calmly | | Redo Exercise 3.2 with two new customs, and say Exercise 3.3's model comebacks five times each. |
| 4 | [Money and life choices](day-4-money-and-life-choices.md) | talk about money without personal numbers and answer a life-choice question in four parts | | Redo Exercise 4.3, then ask Claude in voice mode for three more life-choice questions. |
| 5 | [Balanced views](day-5-balanced-views.md) | give a five-part balanced view, make the other side strong, and concede a point without collapsing | | Redo Exercise 5.3 with three new motions from the topic bank, recording each one. |

Any day you scored **1**: do its fix on Day 7 instead of the listening task, or in the first 10 minutes of next week's Day 1. Any day you scored **2**: use that day's key phrases in tonight's live conversation on purpose.

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

**Mission: lead a real discussion on one big question.** This time *you* bring the topic. Choose one question from the [Day 6 materials topic bank](exercises.md#day-6-materials) before you start, and bring it up naturally: "OK, random question, but I've been thinking about this all week. Do you think…?"

Things to try:

1. **Bring it up with a hook** (Day 1), and give a little background if it's a news-type topic.
2. **Use one personal example** and one level of certainty from the scale (Day 2).
3. **Ask for their perspective** if they're from a different place: "What's the equivalent where you're from?" (Day 3).
4. **Say what it comes down to** (Day 4).
5. **Land clearly, then hand over**: "Where I land is… Where do you land?" (Day 5). And concede at least one good point someone else makes.

If it's a group, make sure everyone speaks: use "What do you think, [name]?" from [Week 13 Day 2](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/day-2-handing-over.md). After the conversation, write down the moment you felt most fluent and the moment you got lost.

**Backup plan:** if nobody is available, use voice mode ([Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md)) and paste this:

```text
Let's simulate a relaxed discussion between three friends at dinner. You play two characters: Jess, who tends to agree with me but asks lots of "why?" questions, and Omar, who usually takes the other side but is always friendly. I'll bring up a big question and lead the discussion. Make both characters react to me and to each other, keep each turn short (1-3 sentences), and leave space for me to talk most. Stay away from party politics and religion. Don't correct me during the conversation. After about 40 minutes, tell me: 1) how well I framed, balanced and landed my views, 2) my five most important mistakes with corrections, 3) whether I let the others speak enough.
```

**Carry into next week:** Week 20 is [Thinking on your feet](../week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/README.md): questions after a talk, unexpected questions, defending an idea, difficult conversations and feedback.
