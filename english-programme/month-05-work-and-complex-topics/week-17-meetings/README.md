# Week 17: Meetings
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5: Work and complex topics](../README.md) · [← Week 16](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md) · [Week 18 →](../week-18-explaining-your-work/README.md)

## This week's goal

This week you learn to **run your part of a meeting** in English without freezing. Meetings are where most people at work go quiet: everyone is busy, people talk fast, and you feel that a slow sentence wastes everybody's time. You will practise the five moves that make up most of what you actually say in a meeting: giving an update, asking for clarification, pushing back politely, making a suggestion, and summing up the next steps. Each move has a small set of ready-made chunks. By Day 6 you should be able to walk into a real meeting (or a mock one) and use all five moves at least once, at normal speed.

## How this builds on last week

Last week ([Week 16: Speaking in front of people](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md)) you spoke while a group listened: introductions, stories, toasts and updates, questions, recovering from mistakes. A meeting is the same pressure, but **shorter and more back-and-forth**. You don't hold the floor for two minutes; you speak for 20–60 seconds, someone reacts, and you need to answer straight away. You'll recycle the update structure from [Week 16 Day 3](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/day-3-toasts-and-updates.md) and the turn-taking from [Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md), and add the work chunks on top.

## Before you start

- Write down (keywords only) **two or three meetings you really have**: a standup, a design review, a planning meeting, a 1:1 with your manager. For each one: who's there, what you usually say, what you find hardest.
- Pick **one real project** you're working on now. You will give updates about it all week. Note: what's done, what's next, one problem, one date.
- Book your three live conversations for **Days 2, 5 and 6**. The best options this week are real work meetings where you speak at least twice. If your work is not in English, use a language partner or tutor and give them the role cards in [exercises.md](exercises.md#day-6-materials). See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- Find two or three shadowing clips of people **in meetings or meeting-style talk**. Search terms are in each lesson. Method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).
- Have your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md) and [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) ready.

## Day 7: light day

Thirty minutes only. Find a podcast episode or video where **a team talks through a real project together** — two or three people planning, disagreeing and deciding (search "startup team meeting recording", "product team podcast planning episode", "design critique live session", "founders discuss product decisions podcast"). Listen while you walk. Don't take notes. When you get home, answer these out loud, one minute each:

1. When someone disagreed, what did they say **first**, before the actual disagreement? ("Yeah, I hear that…", "Totally, and…")
2. How did the conversation end? Did anyone sum up who does what? What words did they use?

Then rest. Seriously. The week was a lot.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] give a 30–60 second project update with a clear structure: done, next, problem, what you need
- [ ] deliver bad news (a delay, a bug, a missed target) calmly, with a reason and a new plan
- [ ] ask for clarification by paraphrasing back and asking about scope, deadline or priority
- [ ] push back on an idea, a deadline or a request without sounding rude or weak
- [ ] make a suggestion at the right strength, and respond to other people's suggestions
- [ ] close a meeting with a 30-second recap: what we decided, who does what, by when
