## Learning objectives

- Paraphrase back what someone said to check you understood ("So what you're saying is…").
- Ask about the three things that are most often unclear at work: **scope, deadline, priority**.
- Ask what a vague word or an acronym means without feeling stupid.
- Use a soft, rising "checking" tone so clarification sounds smart, not lost.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's focus: the soft checking tone.** When you check something in a meeting, your voice should sound curious and relaxed, not worried. Two patterns:

1. **Paraphrase + rise.** "So you want it by FRIDAY? ↗" — a statement with a rising end. It means "I think this is right, please confirm."
2. **Soft openers.** "Just to make sure I get it…" is said quickly and lightly ("juss-ta-make-SHUR-I-get-it"), then a small pause, then the important part.

| # | Sentence (CAPITALS = stressed) | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Just to make SURE I get it… | "juss-ta-make-SHUR-I-ged-it" |
| 2 | So you want the NEW version by FRIDAY? ↗ | rising end |
| 3 | When you say "SIMPLE", do you mean FEWER steps? | "do you mean" → "d'ya-MEAN" |
| 4 | Sorry, what does "MVP" stand for HERE? | "what does" → "wuh-duz" |
| 5 | Are we talking about the WHOLE app, or just CHECKOUT? | rise on "app", fall on "checkout" |
| 6 | Is that a MUST-have or a NICE-to-have? | "nice-to-have" → "NICE-tuh-hav" |
| 7 | Can you give me an EXAMPLE? | "give me an" → "gimme-uh-n" |
| 8 | Just so I'm CLEAR — this replaces the OLD flow? ↗ | pause after "clear" |
| 9 | What does "DONE" look like for you? | "look like" → "LOOK-lyke" |
| 10 | Which one's the PRIORITY this week? | "which one's" → "WITCH-wunz" |
| 11 | OK, so if I'm HEARING you right… | "hearing you" → "HEER-in-ya" |
| 12 | Got it. That makes a LOT more sense now. | "got it" → "GAH-dit" |

**Method:** speak-along with your clip twice, then pause-and-repeat the 2–3 hardest sentences. Then the 12 sentences above twice: first slowly with the tone exaggerated, then at normal speed. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

**Clip to find:** people asking each other questions in a work discussion or Q&A. Search YouTube for "design critique session real", "product manager stakeholder meeting example", "client kickoff meeting roleplay", "tech podcast host asks guest to clarify".

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Asking for clarification

### Explanation

At work, the most expensive mistake is not a grammar mistake. It's **doing the wrong task well**. You spend three days on a full redesign, and your manager only wanted a new button colour. Clarification questions stop that from happening.

Many learners don't ask because they're afraid it shows they didn't understand the English. But native speakers ask clarification questions **all the time**, because people at work are vague all the time. "Can you clean this up a bit?" "We need it ASAP." "Make it more modern." Nobody knows exactly what those mean. Asking makes you look careful and senior.

**Three tools:**

**1. Paraphrase back.** Say what you understood in your own words, then let them confirm. This is the strongest tool, because even if your English isn't perfect, they'll correct the content.
- "So what you're saying is we keep the old layout, but change the colours?"
- "Just to make sure I get it: you want three options, not one?"

**2. Ask about scope, deadline and priority.** Most unclear tasks are unclear in one of these three ways:

| Unclear about… | Ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope** (how big? what's included?) | "Are we talking about X or Y?" / "Does that include…?" | "Does that include the mobile version too?" |
| **Deadline** (when exactly?) | "When do you need it by?" / "What does ASAP mean here?" | "When you say soon, is that this week or this month?" |
| **Priority** (how important compared to other work?) | "Is that more important than…?" / "Should I drop X for this?" | "Should I pause the settings work for this?" |

**3. Ask about a word.** When someone uses a vague word ("simpler", "cleaner", "pop") or an acronym, ask what it means **in this context**: "When you say cleaner, what do you have in mind?" This sounds expert, not lost — designers ask this constantly.

**Finish the loop.** After the answer, confirm and close: "Got it, that makes sense." / "Perfect, that's really helpful." Don't just go silent.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Just to make sure I get it, … | you're about to paraphrase | "Just to make sure I get it, you want the old menu back?" |
| So what you're saying is… | you sum up their point | "So what you're saying is the price is the main problem?" |
| If I'm hearing you right, … | a softer way to paraphrase | "If I'm hearing you right, we're not launching in March." |
| When you say ___, do you mean…? | a word is vague | "When you say simpler, do you mean fewer screens?" |
| What does ___ look like for you? | you want a concrete picture | "What does 'done' look like for you on this?" |
| Are we talking about ___ or ___? | the scope is unclear | "Are we talking about the whole app or just the home screen?" |
| Does that include…? | you check what's in scope | "Does that include the emails?" |
| When do you need it by? | no deadline was given | "When do you need it by, roughly?" |
| Is that a must-have or a nice-to-have? | you check priority | "Is the dark mode a must-have or a nice-to-have?" |
| Sorry, what does ___ stand for? | an acronym is new | "Sorry, what does QBR stand for?" |
| Can you give me an example? | an idea is abstract | "Can you give me an example of a screen you like?" |
| Can you walk me through…? | you need the steps | "Can you walk me through how the data gets there?" |
| Got it, that makes sense | you close the loop | "Got it, that makes a lot more sense now." |
| Let me just play that back | you're about to repeat the plan | "Let me just play that back so I don't get it wrong." |

### Examples

**1. A vague request from a manager**

> **Manager:** Can you take a look at the settings page? It feels kind of messy.
> **You:** Sure. Just to make sure I get it — are we talking about a full redesign, or more of a clean-up?
> **Manager:** Oh, just a clean-up. Like, fix the spacing, group things better.
> **You:** Got it. And when do you need it by?
> **Manager:** No rush. End of next week?
> **You:** Perfect. And is that more important than the onboarding work, or should I do it after?
> **Manager:** After is fine.
> **You:** Cool, that makes sense.

**2. Paraphrasing back in a design review**

> **Dana (PM):** I like it, but I think it needs to feel more premium. Right now it's a bit... I don't know, generic?
> **You:** OK. When you say premium, do you mean the visuals — like colours and type — or more the experience, like fewer steps?
> **Dana:** The visuals, mostly. It looks like every other app.
> **You:** So if I'm hearing you right, the flow is fine, but you want it to look more like *our* brand?
> **Dana:** Exactly. That's exactly it.
> **You:** Great. Can you give me an example of an app that feels premium to you?
> **Dana:** Hmm. Maybe a hotel booking app, something like that — lots of white space, big photos.
> **You:** Super helpful. Got it.

**3. An acronym and a deadline**

> **Marcus (engineer):** We should be fine as long as it's in before code freeze. Just send me the specs and the QA notes.
> **You:** Sorry, quick question — when is code freeze exactly?
> **Marcus:** The 15th. After that, nothing new goes in.
> **You:** Got it. And when you say QA notes, do you mean the list of edge cases, or something else?
> **Marcus:** Yeah, the edge cases. Like, what happens if there's no internet, empty states, that kind of thing.
> **You:** OK, let me just play that back: specs and edge cases to you before the 15th.
> **Marcus:** Yep, perfect.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't understand." | "Just to make sure I get it — you mean…?" | "I don't understand" stops the conversation and sounds like a language problem. Paraphrasing keeps it moving. |
| "What do you mean?" (flat, falling) | "When you say *simple*, what do you mean?" | Name the exact word. A bare "What do you mean?" can sound like a challenge. |
| "When do you need it?" — "ASAP." — "OK." | "When you say ASAP, is that today or this week?" | ASAP, soon and quick mean different things to different people. Pin them down. |
| "So you are saying that we should to change the colours?" | "So you're saying we should change the colours?" | No "to" after "should". And use contractions: "you're". |
| "Can you explain me what does it mean?" | "Can you explain what it means?" | Explain *something* (no "me"), and normal word order inside the question. See the indirect-questions rule from [Week 2 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-3-indirect-questions.md). |
| Asking five questions in a row | Ask one, listen, confirm, then ask the next | Too many questions at once feels like an interrogation. |

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Tonight: **30 minutes of drill** to warm up, then **40 minutes of live conversation #1**.

### Part A: 3/2/1 warm-up (10 min)

**Prompt:** "Tell me about a time someone gave you a task that was unclear. What did they say, what did you think they meant, and how did you find out what they really wanted?" (If you can't think of one, invent one: a friend asks you to "help a bit" with their move, and it turns out to be the whole day.)

Tell it in **3 minutes**, then **2**, then **1**. Record the 1-minute version. Use casual reported speech from [Week 5 Day 3](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/day-3-casual-reported-speech.md): "She was like, 'Can you clean it up a bit?', and I was like, 'Wait, what does that mean?'" See the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part B: Clarification role-play (20 min)

Read each brief out loud as if the other person is saying it to you. Then, out loud: ask **at least four clarification questions** (one paraphrase, one about scope, one about deadline, one about priority), invent the answers yourself, and finish with "Let me just play that back…" and a one-sentence summary. Record it.

**Brief 1 (from a marketing lead):** "Hey, so we're doing a big campaign next month and we need a landing page. Nothing crazy. Just something that looks good and gets people to sign up. Can you have something soon?"

**Brief 2 (from a founder):** "I was looking at the app last night and honestly the home screen is too busy. People don't know where to start. Can we make it simpler? I want this fixed before the investor meeting."

**Brief 3 (from an engineer):** "So we're moving to the new component library, which means some of your designs won't work anymore. We'll need updated specs for the forms and probably the modals. Not urgent-urgent, but we're starting the migration in the next sprint."

**Brief 4 (from a friend, non-work):** "Hey, can you help me plan my sister's birthday thing? It's gonna be small. I just need help with the place and maybe food. It's sometime next month."

### Part C: Live conversation #1 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md) for finding and preparing partners.

**Best options:** a real work meeting where someone gives you or the team a task; a 1:1 with your manager; a coffee chat where a colleague explains what their team does; a language exchange partner who tells you about their job.

**Your mission:** understand something properly by asking clarification questions — don't just nod.

Things to try:

1. **Paraphrase back at least twice**: "Just to make sure I get it…", "So what you're saying is…"
2. Ask **one "When you say ___, do you mean…?"** question about a vague word they use.
3. Ask about **scope, deadline or priority** at least once.
4. If they use an acronym or word you don't know, ask: "Sorry, what does ___ stand for?"
5. Close the loop every time: "Got it, that makes sense."

If it's not a work conversation, ask your partner to explain **their job or a hobby you know nothing about** (a sport, a game, a craft), and use the same five moves.

**Backup (if no partner):** voice mode with Claude. Paste this:

```text
Let's do a 40-minute speaking practice in voice mode. You are Jordan, a product manager at a fitness app, and I'm the product designer on your team. Give me a new project, but explain it in a vague, messy way, like real managers do: use vague words like "cleaner", "soon", "a quick thing", "more engaging", and one or two acronyms without explaining them. Don't give me the details unless I ask the right clarification questions about scope, deadline and priority. After about 15 minutes, switch to a second project with a different manager personality (very fast and busy). Keep your turns short, like in a real meeting. At the very end, step out of the role and tell me: 1) which clarification questions I asked well, 2) any moments where I just said "OK" when I should have asked, 3) up to 5 English mistakes with corrections.
```

Afterwards, write down two questions you asked that worked well, and one moment where you should have asked but didn't.
