# Week 17 · Day 6: Weekly check

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 15 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 15 min

## Before you start

- Have ready: your recording app, the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), your numbers from [Week 16 Day 6](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/day-6-weekly-check.md) (your month 4 check), and your notes from this week's two live conversations.
- **Live conversation #3** is tonight — the big one. Ideally a real meeting where you can use all five moves. If not, a language partner or tutor who will play a meeting with you, using the role cards in [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials).
- Don't read the re-record prompt until you're ready to press record.

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the **hardest clip of the week** — probably the push-back clip from [Day 3](day-3-pushing-back-politely.md) or the brainstorm from [Day 4](day-4-making-suggestions.md). Speak along with it twice without pausing. Then pick the 12 shadowing sentences from any day this week and say them at full speed, paying attention to:

- the light, upbeat update tone (Day 1)
- the soft rising check (Day 2)
- the fall-rise and tiny "but" (Day 3)
- the open rise on suggestions (Day 4)
- the calm falling tone on decisions (Day 5)

See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**The prompt (unprepared — read it, then start recording straight away):**

> "You're in a team meeting. Your manager turns to you and says: 'Can you walk us through where your project is, what's worrying you, and what you need from us? And if you think we should change anything about the plan, say so.' Talk for 2 minutes."

1. Record **2 minutes**. Don't stop, even if you lose your way. Use your [time-buyers](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) and keep going.
2. Listen back once. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md):
   - umms and uhhs
   - pauses longer than 2 seconds
   - different connectors (not just "and" and "so")
3. Write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) next to last week's numbers.
4. Quick bonus check: did you use a **headline** at the start? A **suggestion**? A clear **end** ("That's it from me")?

## 3. Skill check (15 min)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Asking for clarification | Your manager says: "Can you make the dashboard cleaner? Soon-ish." Ask three clarification questions. | …paraphrase back and ask about scope, deadline and priority. | | Redo [Exercise 2.2](exercises.md#exercise-22-pin-down-the-vague-word) and two briefs from the [Day 2 drill](day-2-asking-for-clarification.md#3-speaking-drill-70-min). |

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: run your part of a full meeting, using all five moves.**

**Option A — a real meeting:** any work meeting where you can speak several times (a planning meeting, a design review, a team sync). Before it starts, decide which project you'll update on and one suggestion you'll make.

**Option B — a practice meeting with a partner or tutor:** give them one of the role cards from [Day 6 materials](exercises.md#day-6-materials) and run a 20-minute meeting, then swap roles or run a second card. Then chat normally for the rest of the hour about how meetings work in your partner's job or country.

Things to try (tick them off afterwards):

1. **Update:** start with a headline ("So, quick update on… Overall, we're…").
2. **Clarify:** paraphrase back at least once ("Just to make sure I get it…").
3. **Push back:** on one idea, deadline or request, with an alternative.
4. **Suggest:** at least two suggestions, at different strengths.
5. **Sum up:** offer to recap at the end ("Do you mind if I recap quickly?") with owners and deadlines.

**Backup (if no partner):** voice mode with Claude:

```text
Let's do a 45-minute mock meeting in voice mode for my English speaking practice. I'm a product designer. You play a product manager (Taylor), an engineer (Chris) and a sales lead (Morgan); say each name before they speak. The meeting is a weekly product sync about a new booking feature for a fitness-class app that is two weeks behind schedule. During the meeting, make sure I have to: give an update with bad news, clarify a vague request, push back on an unrealistic idea from Morgan, make suggestions, and sum up next steps at the end. Talk at natural speed, interrupt me sometimes, and keep turns short. Don't correct me during the meeting. When I say "end meeting", give me a score from 1 to 3 for each of the five moves, one example of something I said well, and up to 5 corrections.
```

## 5. Review and report (15 min)

1. Fill in tonight's row in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): the 3 numbers, the live conversation, the skill check scores.
2. Send this to Claude (copy, fill in, paste):

```text
Week 17 (Meetings) report.
Re-record test, 2 minutes: umms ___, long pauses ___, connectors ___ (last week: umms ___, pauses ___, connectors ___).
Skill check (1–3): updates ___, clarification ___, pushing back ___, suggestions ___, summing up ___.
Live conversations this week: Day 2 ___, Day 5 ___, Day 6 ___ (who/where, and how it went in one line each).
The move I found hardest in real meetings: ___.
A moment I froze or translated in my head: ___.
Please: 1) tell me what my numbers say about my progress, 2) give me 5 short meeting situations to practise the move I found hardest, 3) suggest one thing to focus on in Week 18 (Explaining your work).
```

3. **Carry into next week:** next week you'll talk about your work at length — walkthroughs, trade-offs, "why?" questions. Keep using the update opener ("So, quick update…") as the way into a project walkthrough, and the push-back phrases when someone questions your decisions.
