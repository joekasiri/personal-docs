## Before you start

- Yesterday you pushed back, and the last step was always an **alternative**. Tonight you learn to make suggestions on their own — not only as a reaction to a bad idea, but to move a meeting forward.
- In [Week 13 Day 5: Building on others' ideas](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/day-5-building-on-others-ideas.md) you learned "Yeah, and…" and "Adding to that…". You'll recycle those tonight. The new part is **choosing the strength** of a suggestion, and **responding** to other people's suggestions.
- Tonight's speaking drill is **voice mode with Claude**: two full mock meetings. Have the meeting brief from [Exercise 4.3](exercises.md#exercise-43-prepare-your-meeting-moves) ready. Read [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) if you need a reminder of the set-up.

## Learning objectives

- Make suggestions at three strengths: **open** ("What if we…?"), **medium** ("I'd suggest…"), **strong** ("I really think we should…").
- Know which strength to use and when.
- Respond to other people's suggestions: accept, build, park or decline.
- Use all four meeting moves from this week in one mock meeting.
- Sound open and relaxed with a rising, tentative tone.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's focus: the tentative, open tone.** Open suggestions usually rise at the end, like a question, even when they aren't grammatically questions. The rise invites other people in: "What if we tried a SHORTER version? ↗". Words like "maybe", "kind of" and "just" are quick and light.

Strong suggestions are the opposite: steady, slower, falling at the end: "I REALLY think we should TEST this first. ↘"

| # | Sentence (CAPITALS = stressed) | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What if we TRIED a shorter version? ↗ | "what if we" → "wuh-DIF-we" |
| 2 | Could we MAYBE start with just ONE screen? ↗ | "could we" → "kood-we" |
| 3 | How about we SPLIT it into two parts? ↗ | "how about" → "HOW-bout" |
| 4 | Just THROWING it out there, but… ↗ | "throwing it out" → "THROW-in-id-out" |
| 5 | One OPTION would be to ask SUPPORT. | "would be" → "wood-bee" |
| 6 | Would it make SENSE to test it FIRST? ↗ | "would it" → "WOO-dit" |
| 7 | I'd SUGGEST we keep the OLD menu for now. | "I'd suggest" → "aid-sug-JEST" |
| 8 | I REALLY think we should TEST this first. ↘ | slow, steady, falling |
| 9 | Building on what PRIYA said… | "building on" → "BILL-din-on" |
| 10 | Ooh, I LIKE that. What if we ALSO… ↗ | warm "ooh", rise at the end |
| 11 | That's a GOOD idea. Let's PARK it for now. | "park it" → "PAR-kit" |
| 12 | Let's GO with that. | falling, decisive |

**Method:** speak-along with your clip twice, then pause-and-repeat the 2–3 hardest sentences. Then say sentences 1–7 with a rising, open tone and sentence 8 with a strong, falling tone. Feel the difference. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

**Clip to find:** a brainstorm or planning conversation where people throw out ideas. Search YouTube for "team brainstorming session real", "product ideation workshop recording", "podcast hosts brainstorm ideas", "startup planning meeting".

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Making suggestions

### Explanation

A meeting without suggestions goes in circles. Many learners have good ideas in meetings but say nothing, because by the time they've built the sentence, the moment is gone. The fix: have **one ready-made opener for each strength** and use it the second the idea comes.

**Three strengths:**

| Strength | When to use it | Openers |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** (low pressure) | brainstorming; you're not sure; you're new or junior; the topic isn't yours | "What if we…?", "Just throwing it out there, but…", "Could we maybe…?", "How about…?" |
| **Medium** (a clear proposal) | you've thought about it; it's your area; the group needs a direction | "One option would be to…", "I'd suggest we…", "Would it make sense to…?", "My suggestion would be…" |
| **Strong** (you really believe it) | there's a real risk; you have evidence; it's your expertise | "I really think we should…", "I'd strongly recommend…", "We need to…" |

Most learners make one of two mistakes: they're **too weak** on things they're the expert on ("Maybe, I don't know, maybe we could, maybe…"), or **too strong** when brainstorming ("We must do X"). Match the strength to the moment. As the designer, you can be strong about user experience — that's your job.

**Suggestion + reason.** A suggestion lands better with a short "because": "What if we tested with five users first? That way we'd catch the big problems before launch." Useful linkers: "That way…", "so we…", "which means…".

**Responding to suggestions.** Half of meeting talk is reacting to *other* people's ideas. Four responses:

| Response | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Accept** | "Let's go with that." / "Yeah, that works for me." / "Love that." |
| **Build** | "Ooh, I like that. And what if we also…?" / "Building on that…" |
| **Park** (good, but not now) | "Good idea — let's park it for now and come back to it." / "Can we add that to the list for next sprint?" |
| **Decline** (softly) | "I like the thinking, but I'm not sure it solves the main problem." / "That's interesting. I'm worried it'd take too long, though." |

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What if we…? | an open, low-pressure idea | "What if we showed the price earlier?" |
| Just throwing it out there, but… | a wild or unfinished idea | "Just throwing it out there, but what if we killed the tutorial?" |
| Could we maybe…? | a very soft idea | "Could we maybe try it on one screen first?" |
| How about…? | a casual idea (+ -ing or + we) | "How about we meet again on Thursday?" |
| One option would be to… | a clear, neutral proposal | "One option would be to ask support for the top complaints." |
| Would it make sense to…? | a proposal as a question | "Would it make sense to talk to legal first?" |
| I'd suggest we… | a medium-strong proposal | "I'd suggest we keep the old menu for now." |
| I really think we should… | a strong recommendation | "I really think we should test this before launch." |
| That way, … | you add the benefit | "That way, engineering can start on Monday." |
| Building on what ___ said, … | you add to someone's idea | "Building on what Priya said, we could add a reminder email." |
| Let's go with that | you accept | "OK, let's go with that." |
| Let's park it for now | good idea, wrong time | "Love it, but let's park it for now." |
| I like the thinking, but… | you decline softly | "I like the thinking, but I'm not sure users would find it." |
| Can I suggest something? | you need to get the floor first | "Sorry, can I suggest something quickly?" |

### Examples

**1. Open suggestion in a brainstorm**

> **Dana (PM):** So people sign up, and then like 40 percent of them never come back. Ideas?
> **You:** Just throwing it out there, but what if we sent a short "here's what to do next" message the day after they sign up?
> **Priya (marketing):** Ooh, I like that. And building on that, we could make it personal — like, use their name and the thing they signed up for.
> **You:** Yeah, totally. That way it doesn't feel like spam.
> **Dana:** Love it. Let's go with that.

**2. Strong suggestion when it's your expertise**

> **Marcus (engineer):** We could just put all the settings on one long page. Easier to build.
> **You:** I get that it's easier, but I really think we should group them. When we tested the long page last year, people couldn't find the notification settings at all. I'd suggest four groups — account, privacy, notifications, payment. That way it's still one page, just organised.
> **Marcus:** OK, four groups on one page... yeah, that's doable.

**3. Parking and declining**

> **Leo (designer):** What if we also redid the icons while we're at it?
> **You:** Good idea — they do look a bit old. But let's park it for now, because we've only got two weeks. Can we add it to the list for next quarter?
> **Leo:** Sure.
> **Priya:** And what about adding a video on the home screen?
> **You:** I like the thinking, but I'm worried it'd slow the page down a lot, especially on phones. One option would be a short animation instead?
> **Priya:** Hmm, yeah, could work.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "We must change the colours." (in a brainstorm) | "What if we tried different colours?" | "Must" sounds like an order. Too strong for brainstorming. |
| "Maybe... I don't know... maybe we could, maybe, test?" | "I really think we should test this." | When it's your expertise, be clear. Too many "maybes" hide your idea. |
| "What if we will test it first?" | "What if we test it first?" / "What if we tested it first?" | No "will" after "what if". Present or past simple. |
| "How about to meet on Thursday?" | "How about meeting on Thursday?" / "How about we meet on Thursday?" | "How about" + -ing or + a full clause, never "to". |
| "I suggest you to use a bigger font." | "I'd suggest using a bigger font." / "I'd suggest we use a bigger font." | "Suggest" doesn't take "someone to". |
| "No." (to someone's idea) | "I like the thinking, but I'm worried it'd take too long." | Decline the idea, not the person. Start with something positive. |

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Voice mode with Claude — two mock meetings

Tonight you run your part of two full meetings with Claude playing several people. Use all four moves from this week: **update** (Day 1), **clarify** (Day 2), **push back** (Day 3), **suggest** (tonight). Tomorrow you'll add the fifth: summing up. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Before you start:** open your notes from [Exercise 4.3](exercises.md#exercise-43-prepare-your-meeting-moves). Keywords only — no full sentences.

### Round 1: The onboarding meeting (25 min)

Paste this into Claude, then switch to voice mode:

```text
Let's run a mock product meeting in voice mode, for my English speaking practice. I'm a product designer at a language-learning app. You play three people and say each person's name before they speak:
- Dana, the product manager: friendly but always worried about deadlines.
- Marcus, an engineer: practical, often says things are harder than they look.
- Priya, the marketing lead: full of ideas, sometimes too many.
The meeting is about this problem: 40% of new users quit during the first week. The launch of a fix is planned in 3 weeks.
Run the meeting like a real one, about 20 minutes:
1) Dana asks me for a quick update on the onboarding redesign.
2) Someone gives me a vague request I need to clarify.
3) Priya or Dana suggests something too big for the timeline, so I need to push back.
4) Ask the group for ideas so I can make suggestions, and have the others react to my ideas (sometimes accept, sometimes build on them, sometimes park or decline them).
Keep each person's turns short (1–3 sentences), talk at natural speed, and interrupt me once or twice like real colleagues do. Don't correct me during the meeting. When I say "end meeting", step out of the role and give me: 1) the moves I did well, 2) one moment where my suggestion was too weak or too strong, 3) up to 5 English mistakes with corrections, 4) two natural phrases a native speaker would have used in my place.
```

Focus on:
- Starting your update with the headline.
- At least **three suggestions**, at different strengths.
- Responding to at least two of *their* suggestions (accept, build, park or decline).

### Round 2: The planning meeting (25 min)

Start a new message (same voice session is fine):

```text
New meeting, same three people. This time it's sprint planning for the next two weeks, and there's too much work: a new payment screen, a bug in the notifications, a redesign of the profile page, and a request from the founder to add a "streaks" feature. The team can only do about half of it. I'm the designer and need to help the team decide what to do first. Make Dana push for the founder's request, Marcus warn about the bug, and Priya want the profile redesign for a campaign. Ask me direct questions, push back on my suggestions at least twice so I have to defend them or adjust them, and at one point ask me "So what do you suggest?" and wait for a full answer. Same rules as before: short turns, natural speed, no corrections until I say "end meeting", then the same 4-part feedback.
```

Focus on:
- Using **"If we add X, something else has to move"** and **"I can do X or Y — which works better?"** from [Day 3](day-3-pushing-back-politely.md).
- Giving a **strong** suggestion at least once (it's your area: user experience).
- Not going silent when someone pushes back: answer in under 3 seconds, even with a time-buyer from [Week 3 Day 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md).

### Round 3: Redo the hardest moment (15 min)

Ask Claude:

```text
Let's replay the hardest moment from the two meetings, the one where I struggled most. Play that moment again, and I'll answer again. Then do it a third time with a slightly harder version (more pressure, faster speech). After each try, give me one quick tip only.
```

### After the session (5 min)

Write down the corrections Claude gave you. Pick the **two natural phrases** Claude suggested and add them to your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Note your general feeling: when did you think in English, and when did you translate?
