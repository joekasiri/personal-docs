## Learning objectives

- Close a meeting with a 30-second recap: **decisions → actions (owner + deadline) → open questions → close**.
- Say deadlines naturally: "by end of day Thursday", "early next week", "before the next sprint".
- Give owners to tasks politely: "Marcus, can you take…?", "I'll own…".
- Park off-topic items and end a meeting on time: "Let's take that offline."
- Use a calm, falling "decision" tone and list intonation.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's focus: the calm decision tone.** When someone sums up a meeting, their voice sounds settled. Statements **fall at the end** (↘), and lists go rise, rise, fall (you practised lists on [Day 1](day-1-giving-updates.md)). Names and dates get the stress, because those are the new, important information.

| # | Sentence (CAPITALS = stressed) | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OK, so let me just RECAP. ↘ | "let me just" → "lemme-juss" |
| 2 | We DECIDED to launch on ANDROID first. ↘ | falling at the end |
| 3 | MARCUS, you're on the BUG fix. ↘ | pause after "Marcus" |
| 4 | PRIYA's gonna send the COPY by WEDNESDAY. | "Priya's gonna" → "PREE-yaz-gunna" |
| 5 | And I'll have the SCREENS ready by end of day THURSDAY. | "end of day" → "en-da-DAY" |
| 6 | The ONE thing we didn't decide is the PRICE. | stress the open point |
| 7 | Let's take THAT one OFFLINE. ↘ | "that one" → "THAT-wun" |
| 8 | Does that sound RIGHT to everyone? ↗ | rise at the end: a real check |
| 9 | Did I MISS anything? ↗ | "did I" → "DID-eye" |
| 10 | I'll send a quick SUMMARY after this. | "after this" → "AF-ter-this" |
| 11 | We're a little over TIME, so let's WRAP up. | "wrap up" → "RAP-up" |
| 12 | Great, THANKS everyone. ↘ | warm and final |

**Method:** speak-along with your clip twice, then pause-and-repeat the 2–3 hardest sentences. Then say the 12 sentences as if you're closing a real meeting: slow down slightly on the names and dates. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

**Clip to find:** the end of a meeting or the end of a podcast where the host wraps up. Search YouTube for "end of meeting action items example", "sprint planning wrap up", "project kickoff meeting recap", "podcast host wraps up episode".

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Summing up next steps

### Explanation

Meetings fail at the end more than anywhere else. Everyone talked, people nodded, and then nobody knows who's doing what. The person who says "OK, so next steps…" in the last two minutes is often seen as the person who ran the meeting — even if they said very little before that. This is a huge opportunity for you: it's a **short, predictable** piece of English that you can prepare completely.

**The recap pattern (30–45 seconds):**

| Part | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Signal | Tell people you're summing up | "OK, so let me just recap." |
| 2. Decisions | What did we decide? (1–3 things) | "We decided to launch on Android first and keep the old menu for now." |
| 3. Actions | Who does what by when | "Marcus, you're on the bug fix by Wednesday. Priya's sending the copy by Thursday. And I'll have the final screens by end of day Friday." |
| 4. Open questions | What's still not decided, and how you'll handle it | "The one thing we didn't decide is the price. Dana, can you check with finance?" |
| 5. Check and close | Confirm and finish | "Does that sound right? Did I miss anything? ... Great, I'll send a quick summary. Thanks, everyone." |

**Owner + task + deadline.** Every action needs all three. "Someone should look at the bug" is not an action. "Marcus, can you look at the bug by Wednesday?" is. If you're not sure who should do it, ask: "Who wants to take this one?"

**Natural deadlines.** Americans at work rarely say "on the 14th of March at 5 p.m." They say:
- "by end of day" (EOD) = before people go home today
- "by end of day Thursday" / "by Thursday EOD"
- "by end of week" = by Friday
- "early next week" = Monday or Tuesday
- "before the next sprint" / "before the launch"
- "first thing tomorrow" = early tomorrow morning

**Parking and ending.** When the meeting goes off-topic or runs over time, someone needs to stop it, politely:
- "That's a great point, but let's take it offline." (= discuss it later, outside this meeting, with fewer people)
- "Let's put a pin in that." (= come back to it later)
- "We're a little over time, so let's wrap up."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| OK, so let me just recap | you start the summary | "OK, so let me just recap before we go." |
| So, next steps… | you move to actions | "So, next steps: Marcus is on the bug." |
| We decided to… / We agreed that… | you state a decision | "We agreed that the launch stays on the 30th." |
| ___, you're on… | you name an owner | "Priya, you're on the emails." |
| ___, can you take…? | you ask someone to own a task | "Leo, can you take the icons?" |
| I'll own… / I'll take… | you take a task | "I'll own the handoff doc." |
| by end of day ___ | you give a deadline | "by end of day Thursday" |
| early next week | a soft deadline | "I'll have a first draft early next week." |
| The one thing we didn't decide is… | an open question | "The one thing we didn't decide is the price." |
| Who wants to take this one? | no obvious owner | "OK, the FAQ page — who wants to take this one?" |
| Let's take that offline | something off-topic or too detailed | "Great question, but let's take that offline." |
| Let's put a pin in that | come back to it later | "Let's put a pin in the logo for now." |
| Did I miss anything? | you check the recap | "That's what I've got. Did I miss anything?" |
| I'll send a quick summary | you promise a written follow-up | "I'll send a quick summary in Slack after this." |
| Let's wrap up | you end the meeting | "We're at time, so let's wrap up." |

### Examples

**1. A full recap at the end of a design review**

> **Dana (PM):** OK, we've got like two minutes left.
> **You:** Cool, so let me just recap. We decided to go with option B for the home screen, and to keep the old navigation for now. Next steps: Marcus, you're on checking if the new cards work on older phones — by Wednesday? 
> **Marcus:** Yep, Wednesday's fine.
> **You:** Great. Priya's sending the final copy by end of day Thursday. And I'll have the updated screens ready early next week. The one thing we didn't decide is the colour of the "Save" button — I'll do two versions and we can decide in Slack. Did I miss anything?
> **Dana:** Nope, that's it.
> **You:** Awesome. I'll send a quick summary after this. Thanks, everyone.

**2. Parking an off-topic discussion**

> **Leo:** Actually, while we're here — I really think we need to talk about the whole icon style. It's kind of a mess.
> **You:** Totally agree, it is. But that's a bigger conversation. Can we put a pin in it and take it offline? Maybe the two of us on Thursday?
> **Leo:** Yeah, fair. Thursday works.
> **You:** Cool. So, back to next steps…

**3. When nobody owns a task**

> **You:** And the last thing is the help article for the new feature. Who wants to take this one?
> **(silence)**
> **You:** Priya, would it make sense for your team to own it, since it's customer-facing?
> **Priya:** Hmm, yeah, OK. We can do it. Not this week, though.
> **You:** Totally fine. Before the launch on the 30th?
> **Priya:** Yeah, that works.
> **You:** Perfect.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "So we will see. OK, bye." | "So, next steps: Marcus, you're on the bug, by Wednesday." | A meeting without clear actions often means nothing happens. |
| "Someone needs to check the bug." | "Marcus, can you check the bug by Wednesday?" | Every action needs an owner and a deadline. |
| "Until Thursday I will send it." | "I'll send it by Thursday." | "By" = at or before that time (a deadline). "Until" = continuing up to that time. |
| "I will do the screens until end of the day." | "I'll have the screens done by end of day." | Same mistake, very common. Deadlines use "by". |
| Retelling the whole meeting in order | Decisions, then actions, then open questions | A recap is a summary, not a replay. 30–45 seconds. |
| "We decided this, yes? Correct?" | "Does that sound right? Did I miss anything?" | "Correct?" sounds like a test. These phrases sound like a team check. |

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Tonight: **30 minutes of drill** to warm up, then **40 minutes of live conversation #2**.

### Part A: 3/2/1, your last real meeting (12 min)

**Prompt:** "Sum up the last real meeting you were in (or the last group plan you made with friends): what it was about, what was decided, who's doing what, and what's still open."

Do it in **3 minutes** (include some background), then **2**, then **1** (pure recap: decisions, actions, open questions). Record the 1-minute version. See the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part B: Recap the transcript (18 min)

Read this meeting transcript out loud once (play all the parts). Then close the book and give a **30–45 second recap** using the pattern. Record it. Then read the model recap below and do it once more, better.

> **Dana:** OK, so the main issue is the app store rating dropped from 4.5 to 4.1 since the update.
> **Marcus:** Most of the bad reviews mention the app being slow to open. I think it's the new animation on the start screen.
> **You:** Can we just remove the animation?
> **Marcus:** We could make it shorter. Removing it completely takes longer, because other things depend on it.
> **Dana:** OK, let's make it shorter. Marcus, how long would that take?
> **Marcus:** Two days, maybe. I can have it by Wednesday.
> **Priya:** We should also reply to the bad reviews. Say we're fixing it.
> **Dana:** Yes. Priya, can your team do that this week?
> **Priya:** Sure, by Friday.
> **Dana:** And I want to know if it's only older phones. Can someone check the data?
> **(silence)**
> **You:** I can look at it with the analytics team. I'll have something by Thursday.
> **Leo:** Should we also rethink the whole start screen? It's kind of busy.
> **Dana:** Good question, but that's bigger. Not today.
> **Marcus:** Also, are we doing the release on Thursday or Monday?
> **Dana:** Hmm. Let's decide that once the fix is ready.

**Model recap:**
"OK, so let me just recap. We agreed the main problem is the app opening slowly, probably because of the start animation, so we're making it shorter, not removing it. Next steps: Marcus, you're on the shorter animation by Wednesday. Priya's team is replying to the bad reviews by Friday. And I'll check the data with analytics — whether it's only older phones — by Thursday. Two things are still open: the release day, Thursday or Monday, which we'll decide when the fix is ready, and Leo's point about redesigning the start screen, which we'll take offline. Did I miss anything? ... Great, I'll send a quick summary. Thanks, everyone."

Then do one more round with **your own correction from last night**: take the meeting you had with Claude (Round 1 or 2) and give a 30-second recap of it from memory.

### Part C: Live conversation #2 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Best options:** a real work meeting (offer to sum up at the end: "Do you mind if I recap quickly?"); a planning chat with a colleague; a language exchange where you **plan something together** — a trip, a dinner, a weekend, a small project.

**Your mission:** be the person who sums up and turns talk into next steps.

Things to try:

1. Near the end, say **"OK, so let me just recap"** and do the full pattern.
2. Give every action an **owner and a deadline** (even in a casual plan: "So you're booking the restaurant by Friday, and I'm sending everyone the address.").
3. Use one **natural deadline** phrase: "by end of day", "early next week", "first thing tomorrow".
4. If the talk goes off-topic, try **"Let's put a pin in that"** once.
5. Finish with **"Did I miss anything?"**

**Backup (if no partner):** voice mode with Claude. Paste this:

```text
Let's do a 40-minute speaking practice in voice mode. You play two colleagues, Sam (a product manager) and Alex (an engineer), and say their names before they speak. I'm the product designer. We're in a messy meeting about planning a new "saved items" feature for a shopping app. Talk naturally, go off-topic sometimes, make decisions in an unclear way, and don't give owners or deadlines to tasks unless I ask. Every 10 minutes, Sam should say "OK, we've got a couple of minutes left" so I have to give a recap with decisions, owners, deadlines and open questions. Do this 3 times with different parts of the project. Then do one final short meeting where you run over time and I have to wrap up politely. At the end, step out of the role and give me: 1) which recaps were clear, 2) any action I gave without an owner or deadline, 3) up to 5 English mistakes with corrections.
```

Afterwards, write down your best recap sentence and one thing you'd say differently next time.
