# Week 17 · Day 3: Pushing back politely
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 5](../README.md) › [Week 17](README.md) · [← Previous: Asking for clarification](day-2-asking-for-clarification.md) · [Next: Making suggestions →](day-4-making-suggestions.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Main skill 20 min · Speaking drill 70 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- In [Week 6 Day 3: Disagreeing politely](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-3-disagreeing-politely.md) you learned to disagree with an **opinion** in a casual chat ("I see what you mean, but…"). We won't repeat those phrases. Tonight is **pushing back at work**: on a plan, a deadline, a request or a decision — often with someone more senior, and with real consequences.
- Pushing back usually means someone will push back on *you*. Reread [Week 13 Day 3: Holding your turn](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/day-3-holding-your-turn.md) so you can finish your point if they interrupt.
- Think of one real decision at work you didn't agree with. You'll use it in the drill.

## Learning objectives

- Push back in four steps: **acknowledge → concern → reason → alternative**.
- Push back on the three classic things: scope ("more features"), time ("faster") and quality ("skip testing").
- Say no to a request without saying the word "no".
- Know when to stop pushing and "disagree and commit".
- Use a fall-rise tone on "I hear you, but…" so it sounds respectful.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's focus: the fall-rise and the light "but".** When people push back politely, the first part ("I hear you", "That makes sense") goes **down and then slightly up**: "I HEAR you ↘↗…". That small rise at the end says "there's more coming, and it's a 'but'". Then the word **"but" is light and quick** — the stress goes on the concern after it. If you hit "BUT" hard, you sound angry.

| # | Sentence (CAPITALS = stressed) | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I HEAR you ↘↗… but I'm a little WORRIED about the timeline. | "but" is tiny: "b'm-a-liddle-WORRIED" |
| 2 | That makes SENSE ↘↗. My only CONCERN is the testing. | pause after "sense" |
| 3 | I'm not SURE we can do it by FRIDAY. | "not sure" → "nahd-SHUR" |
| 4 | Can we take a STEP back for a second? | "take a" → "tay-kuh" |
| 5 | I'd PUSH back a little on that. | "I'd push" → "aid-PUSH" |
| 6 | I GET why we want it. I just don't think it's the RIGHT time. | stress the contrast: GET / RIGHT |
| 7 | What if we did HALF now and HALF in the next sprint? | rise at the end |
| 8 | If we ADD this, something ELSE has to move. | "has to" → "HAS-tuh" |
| 9 | I wanna FLAG a risk before we decide. | "wanna" → "wanna", light |
| 10 | Help me UNDERSTAND the reason for the rush. | "help me" → "HEL-pmee" |
| 11 | I'm not gonna DIE on this hill, but I wanted to SAY it. | "gonna" → "gunna" |
| 12 | OK, I still have DOUBTS, but I'm on BOARD. | falling tone at the end = decision accepted |

**Method:** speak-along with your clip twice, then pause-and-repeat the 2–3 hardest sentences. Then the 12 sentences above: first with an exaggerated fall-rise, then naturally. Record sentences 1, 6 and 12 and check that "but" is light. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

**Clip to find:** two or more people discussing and disagreeing respectfully about a decision. Search YouTube for "founders disagree podcast product decision", "design critique feedback session", "product manager negotiate scope with engineering", "business podcast debate respectfully".

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Pushing back politely

### Explanation

At work you'll often hear ideas you think are wrong: a deadline that's too short, a feature nobody needs, a decision to skip user testing. Saying nothing is bad for the product. Saying "No, that's wrong" is bad for you. **Pushing back** is the middle way: you raise your concern clearly, but you show you're on the same team.

**The four steps:**

| Step | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Acknowledge | Show you heard and understand *why* they want it | "I hear you — the launch date matters a lot to the sales team." |
| 2. Concern | Name the problem, softly | "My concern is we haven't tested the new flow with anyone." |
| 3. Reason / evidence | One fact, number or example | "Last time we skipped testing, we had to redo the whole sign-up." |
| 4. Alternative | Offer another way forward | "What if we test with five users this week and launch on the 20th?" |

The **alternative** is the most important part. Push-back without an alternative sounds like complaining. Push-back with an alternative sounds like leadership.

**The three things you'll push back on most:**

- **Scope** ("Can we also add…?"): "If we add that, something else has to move. Which one should we drop?"
- **Time** ("Can we do it by Friday?"): "Friday's tight. I can do a rough version by Friday, or a finished one by Wednesday next week."
- **Quality** ("Let's skip testing"): "I'd push back a little on skipping it. Even one day of testing would catch the big problems."

**Saying no without "no".** At American workplaces, a straight "No" to a colleague is rare. People say no by offering a trade-off or a different option: "I can't take that on this week, but I could look at it on Monday." / "I'm not the best person for that — Leo knows the payments side better."

**Disagree and commit.** Sometimes you push back and the group still decides the other way. Then you say so and move on: "OK, I still have some doubts, but I'm on board. Let's do it." This is a normal and respected phrase in tech companies. It shows you're honest **and** a team player.

**Softeners make it work.** Notice how much softening is in every example: "a little", "my only concern", "I'm not sure", "What if…". Compare: "This won't work" (hard) vs "I'm not sure this'll work as it is" (soft). Same message, very different reaction.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I hear you, but… | you acknowledge, then disagree | "I hear you, but I think we're rushing it." |
| That makes sense. My only concern is… | you agree with the goal, not the plan | "That makes sense. My only concern is the cost." |
| I'd push back a little on that | you openly (but softly) disagree | "I'd push back a little on that — users really like the old menu." |
| I'm not sure we can… by… | a deadline is too tight | "I'm not sure we can get it done by Friday." |
| Can we take a step back? | the group is jumping to a solution | "Can we take a step back and look at what problem we're solving?" |
| I wanna flag a risk | you see a danger | "I wanna flag a risk: the legal team hasn't seen this." |
| Help me understand… | you want the reason, without attacking | "Help me understand why we need it before the launch." |
| If we add X, something else has to move | scope is growing | "If we add dark mode, something else has to move." |
| What if we… instead? | you offer an alternative | "What if we launch on Android first instead?" |
| I can do X, or Y — which works better? | you give a choice instead of a no | "I can do a rough version Friday, or a polished one Tuesday. Which works better?" |
| I can't take that on right now, but… | you say no to extra work | "I can't take that on right now, but I can look at it next week." |
| I'm not gonna die on this hill, but… | it's not that important to you | "I'm not gonna die on this hill, but I'd keep the old icon." |
| I still have doubts, but I'm on board | you disagree and commit | "I still have doubts, but I'm on board. Let's try it." |
| Fair point. I hadn't thought of that | they changed your mind | "Fair point. I hadn't thought about the support calls." |

### Examples

**1. Pushing back on scope**

> **Dana (PM):** So I was thinking we could also add a referral feature to the launch. Like, invite a friend, get a discount. It's quick, right?
> **You:** I hear you, and I think referrals could be great for growth. My only concern is the timeline. We're already pretty tight for the 30th, and a referral flow is maybe five or six new screens, plus the emails.
> **Dana:** Hmm. Five or six screens?
> **You:** Yeah, at least. So if we add it, something else has to move. What if we launch on the 30th as planned and do referrals as the first thing after?
> **Dana:** OK, that's fair. Let's put it first in the next sprint.

**2. Pushing back on a senior person (quality)**

> **Director:** Honestly, I don't think we need user testing for this one. We know what users want. Let's just ship it.
> **You:** I get why — we're under a lot of time pressure. I'd push back a little on skipping it, though. Last time we redesigned sign-up without testing, we had to redo half of it after launch.
> **Director:** That was a different situation.
> **You:** Totally, it was. But even two days of testing with five people would catch the big stuff. Could we do that and still launch next week?
> **Director:** Two days? ...Fine. But no more than two.
> **You:** Deal. Thanks.

**3. Saying no to extra work**

> **Colleague:** Hey, could you also design the new newsletter template? It shouldn't take long.
> **You:** Ah, I'd love to help, but I can't take that on this week — I'm on the checkout handoff until Friday. I could look at it on Monday, if that works? Or Leo might be free sooner.
> **Colleague:** Monday's fine, actually.
> **You:** Cool, I'll put it in my calendar.

**4. Disagree and commit**

> **You:** I still think the green is a bit too bright for the "Delete" button. But I hear what you're all saying about the brand.
> **Marcus:** We could test both?
> **Dana:** We don't have time to test both. Let's go with the brand green.
> **You:** OK. I still have doubts, but I'm on board. Let's go with it and see what the data says after launch.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "No, that's a bad idea." | "I hear you, but I'm a little worried about…" | Too direct for most American workplaces. Acknowledge first. |
| "I hear you, BUT…" (loud, hard "but") | "I hear you ↘↗… but I'm a little worried…" | A heavy "but" cancels the acknowledgement. Keep it light. |
| "This is impossible." | "I'm not sure we can do it by Friday. I could do X by Friday, or Y by Tuesday." | "Impossible" ends the talk. A choice keeps it going. |
| "I don't agree, because it's wrong." | "My concern is the testing. Last time we skipped it, we had to redo sign-up." | Give a real reason or example, not just "it's wrong". |
| Pushing back, losing, then going quiet and angry | "OK, I still have doubts, but I'm on board." | Say clearly that you accept the decision. Silence looks like you're still fighting. |
| "Sorry, sorry, maybe I'm wrong, sorry, but maybe…" | "Can I flag one risk?" | Over-apologising makes your point sound weak. One softener is enough. |

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Tonight is a **mixed drill**. Record everything. Tools: [Random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md), [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part A: Warm-up, "the other side" topics (12 min)

For each statement, talk for about 2 minutes. **Push back** on it, using the four steps — even if you secretly agree. (Arguing a side you don't believe is a skill you built in [Week 12 Day 5](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-5-arguing-the-other-side.md).)

1. "Every meeting should be 15 minutes, maximum."
2. "Cameras should always be on in video calls."
3. "Designers should learn to code."
4. "Open-plan offices are better for teamwork."
5. "Nobody should send work messages after 6 p.m."

### Part B: 3/2/1, make your case (20 min)

**Prompt:** "Your team wants to launch the new app design next week. You think it needs two more weeks of testing. Make your case to the team: acknowledge why they want to launch, explain your concern, give a reason or example, and offer an alternative."

Do it in **3 minutes**, then **2**, then **1**. Record the 1-minute version and check: did you include all four steps? Is the alternative clear? See the [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part C: Push-back role-plays (30 min)

Read the other person's line out loud (or record yourself reading it, then play it back). Answer straight away. Then read their next line and answer again. Do each card twice.

**Card 1: The extra feature (PM)**
- PM: "We need to add dark mode before launch. It's a quick one, right?"
- PM: "But our competitors all have it. People will notice."
- PM: "OK, so what would you drop if we added it?"

**Card 2: The impossible deadline (manager)**
- Manager: "The client moved the meeting up. Can you have the full prototype by tomorrow morning?"
- Manager: "I know it's tight, but this client is really important."
- Manager: "What *can* you have by tomorrow?"

**Card 3: The senior person's idea (founder)**
- Founder: "I want the sign-up form to ask for phone number, address and birthday. More data is better."
- Founder: "But we need that data for marketing."
- Founder: "Fine. What's your suggestion?"

**Card 4: The colleague's request (marketing)**
- Marketing: "Can you redo all the app store screenshots this week? We're changing the slogan."
- Marketing: "It's just changing the text, really."
- Marketing: "OK, when could you do it?"

**Card 5: Your real situation**
Think of the real work decision you didn't agree with (from "Before you start"). Push back on it out loud for 1 minute, using all four steps. Then imagine they still say no, and **disagree and commit** in one or two sentences.

### Part D: Listen back (8 min)

Listen to Card 2 and Card 5. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) for Card 5 and write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). Check one thing: did you offer an **alternative** every time?
