# Week 17 exercises: Meetings
[Week 17 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-giving-updates.md) · [Day 2](day-2-asking-for-clarification.md) · [Day 3](day-3-pushing-back-politely.md) · [Day 4](day-4-making-suggestions.md) · [Day 5](day-5-summing-up-next-steps.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use this page.** Every exercise is done **out loud**. When an exercise says "write then say", write keywords only, never full sentences, and then speak. When it says "record", record yourself and listen back once. Answer keys and model answers are at the end of each day's section: look at them only **after** you've said your own answer. Model answers are examples, not the only correct answer. If yours is clear, polite and natural, it's right. After checking, say the model answer once out loud too — that's how the chunks become yours.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Update builder
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last two

Each situation gives you notes. Turn them into a 30–45 second meeting update using the pattern **headline → done → next → blockers → ask → close**. Start with "So, quick update on…" and finish with "That's it from me." Don't read the notes word for word: speak.

1. **Food-delivery app, "track your order" screens.** On track. Done: all 6 screens, tested with 4 users. Next: handoff to engineering. Blockers: none. Ask: nothing.
2. **Banking app, sign-up flow.** A little behind (3 days). Done: first 3 screens. Next: ID-check screen. Blocker: waiting on the security team's rules. Ask: Sam (security) to send rules by Wednesday.
3. **Design system, new buttons.** On track. Done: primary + secondary buttons, documented. Next: form fields. Blocker: engineers using old buttons on new pages. Ask: Marcus to remind the team to use the new ones.
4. **Travel app, search results page.** On track but a risk. Done: wireframes approved by PM. Next: visual design. Risk: photos from hotels are low quality. Ask: decision from Dana on whether to use placeholder images.
5. **User research, interviews.** Done: 8 of 10 interviews. Next: last 2 on Thursday, then a summary. Early finding: people don't trust the "recommended" label. Ask: nothing yet, results next Monday.
6. **Your real project.** Use your notes from the [Week 17 overview](README.md#before-you-start). Record this one twice.

### Exercise 1.2: Bad news, said well
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence is a bad way to give bad news: too much apology, too vague, or too dramatic. Say a better version in **three short sentences: fact, reason, plan**.

1. "I'm so, so sorry, it's really my fault, I couldn't finish the screens, I'm sorry."
2. "There are some problems with the project, maybe it will be late, I don't know."
3. "It's a disaster. The testing was terrible. Nobody could use it."
4. "The launch is not possible on the 10th."
5. "The numbers went down. Sorry."
6. "We have a bug. It's very bad. Everything is broken on Android."
7. "I didn't do the research because I had too much work, sorry, really."
8. "I think maybe we can't do the feature, because of reasons from engineering."

### Exercise 1.3: Rapid follow-ups
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud, timed (under 15 seconds each)

You've just given an update about a checkout redesign that is one week late. Your manager and colleagues ask these questions. Answer each one **in under 15 seconds**, in one or two sentences. If you don't know, say so and say when you'll know. Go straight through without stopping, then do the list again faster.

1. "When will it be ready?"
2. "What's causing the delay?"
3. "Do you need anything from me?"
4. "Is that gonna affect the marketing launch?"
5. "Can we do part of it on time?"
6. "Who else knows about this?"
7. "How confident are you about the new date?"
8. "Did the testing find anything else?"
9. "What would help you go faster?"
10. "Will it work on tablets too?"
11. "Have the engineers seen the new designs?"
12. "Can you send me a short summary of this?"

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Play it back
**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

Each speaker says something long or unclear. Paraphrase it back in one sentence with **"Just to make sure I get it…"**, **"So what you're saying is…"** or **"If I'm hearing you right…"**, and end with a soft rising tone. Use a different opener each time.

1. **PM:** "I don't think we should kill the old dashboard yet, because a lot of our bigger customers are still on it, but at the same time we can't keep fixing two versions forever."
2. **Engineer:** "The animation's fine on new phones, but on older ones it drops frames, so either we simplify it or we turn it off for older devices."
3. **Manager:** "This quarter, I'd rather we did fewer things really well than lots of things halfway."
4. **Client:** "We love the design, it's just that our CEO thinks the blue is too cold, and she's kind of the one who decides."
5. **Researcher:** "Most people said they liked the feature in the interviews, but when we watched them use it, almost nobody actually used it."
6. **Marketing:** "We need the page live before the campaign starts, but the campaign date isn't totally fixed yet, it depends on the budget meeting."
7. **Founder:** "I'm not saying the onboarding is bad. I'm saying people don't get to the 'wow' moment fast enough."
8. **Support lead:** "We get about thirty tickets a week about passwords, and half of them are people who never got the reset email."

### Exercise 2.2: Pin down the vague word
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud, rapid

Each request has a vague word or phrase (in bold). Ask **one** clarification question about it. Try to use each of these at least once: "When you say ___, do you mean…?", "What does ___ look like for you?", "Are we talking about ___ or ___?", "Is that a must-have or a nice-to-have?", "When do you need it by?", "Sorry, what does ___ stand for?"

1. "Can you make the home screen **pop** a bit more?"
2. "We need the report **ASAP**."
3. "Just give it a **quick look**."
4. "It should feel more **modern**."
5. "Let's **clean up** the settings."
6. "Make sure it works for **everyone**."
7. "We need the new logo **on the app**."
8. "Can you put together the **PRD** comments?"
9. "It'd be good to have **some** options."
10. "Let's make it **simpler**."
11. "We should **improve** retention."
12. "Can you do the **mobile** version too?"

### Exercise 2.3: Fill the gaps in a kickoff meeting
**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

Read the dialogue out loud, playing both parts. Fill each gap with a clarification phrase from Day 2. Then read it again faster, without looking at your gaps.

> **Jordan (PM):** OK, so the new project is the loyalty programme. Basically, points for every order. Leadership wants it soon.
> **You:** Cool. (1) ___ "soon", is that this quarter or next?
> **Jordan:** Ideally before the summer. So, end of Q2.
> **You:** Got it. And (2) ___ the whole programme — points, rewards, the profile page — or just collecting points?
> **Jordan:** Good question. Just collecting points for now. Rewards come later.
> **You:** OK. (3) ___: for the first version, people only see their points, they can't spend them yet?
> **Jordan:** Exactly.
> **You:** And (4) ___ the emails too, like "You earned 50 points"?
> **Jordan:** Hmm. Emails would be nice, but not essential.
> **You:** So emails are a (5) ___?
> **Jordan:** Yeah, a nice-to-have.
> **You:** Perfect. One more — you mentioned it needs to be "on brand". (6) ___ for you? Is there an example?
> **Jordan:** Like the gift-card page we did last year. That one felt really us.
> **You:** Oh, nice, that helps a lot. OK, (7) ___ so I don't get it wrong: points only, no rewards yet, emails if we have time, end of Q2, and the gift-card page as the style example.
> **Jordan:** That's it.
> **You:** Great. (8) ___.

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Soften it
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Each sentence is too blunt for most American workplaces. Say a softer version that keeps the same message. Use a different softener each time ("I'm not sure…", "My only concern is…", "I'd push back a little…", "I hear you, but…", "Help me understand…", "I wanna flag…").

1. "That's wrong."
2. "We can't do it by Friday."
3. "Your idea won't work."
4. "Why do we need this?"
5. "No, I won't do the newsletter."
6. "That's a waste of time."
7. "We're not testing enough."
8. "You didn't think about mobile."
9. "This deadline is crazy."
10. "I disagree with everyone."

### Exercise 3.2: The four-step push-back
**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** out loud, record two of them

For each situation, push back in four steps: **acknowledge → concern → reason → alternative**. 30–45 seconds each.

1. **The PM** wants to add a full chat feature to the app two weeks before launch. You know it's at least 10 new screens and needs a moderation plan.
2. **A stakeholder** from sales wants to remove the onboarding completely "because people hate tutorials". Your research shows people who finish onboarding stay twice as long.
3. **Your manager** wants you to present the new designs to the CEO tomorrow. You've only finished half and you haven't tested any of them.
4. **An engineer** says the animation you designed is "impossible" and wants to remove it. You think a simpler version is possible and it helps people understand what happened.
5. **A client** wants their logo three times bigger on every screen. It pushes the main content down so people have to scroll to see the "Book" button.

### Exercise 3.3: No without "no"
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud, rapid

Say no to each request without using the word "no". Offer another time, a smaller version, a different person, or a choice.

1. "Can you join the marketing workshop all day Thursday?"
2. "Could you quickly design five banner ads by tomorrow?"
3. "Can you take over Leo's project while he's on vacation, on top of yours?"
4. "Would you mind presenting at the all-hands meeting this Friday?"
5. "Can you redo the whole icon set this sprint?"
6. "Can you review my deck tonight? It's like 40 slides."

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: The suggestion ladder
**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Say each idea three times: **open** (What if we…? / Just throwing it out there, but… / Could we maybe…?), **medium** (One option would be to… / I'd suggest we… / Would it make sense to…?), and **strong** (I really think we should… / I'd strongly recommend…). Add a short benefit with "That way…" to the medium or strong version.

1. Test the new design with five users before launch.
2. Show the price on the first screen.
3. Send a reminder email to people who leave items in their cart.
4. Remove the "Skip" button from onboarding.
5. Have a 15-minute design review every Monday.
6. Use one font instead of three.

### Exercise 4.2: Accept, build, park or decline
**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

Colleagues make these suggestions in a meeting. Respond to each one with the reaction in brackets. Keep it to one or two sentences.

1. "What if we added a progress bar to the sign-up?" (**accept**)
2. "We could let people log in with their phone number." (**build**)
3. "Why don't we redesign the whole app while we're at it?" (**park**)
4. "Let's put a video tutorial on every screen." (**decline**)
5. "How about we ask support for the top five complaints?" (**accept**)
6. "We could add a 'favourites' button to the menu." (**build**)
7. "What if we translated the app into ten languages this month?" (**park**)
8. "We should hide the 'Cancel subscription' button so fewer people cancel." (**decline** — and say why)

### Exercise 4.3: Prepare your meeting moves
**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** write keywords, then say

Tonight's voice-mode meetings are about a **language-learning app where 40% of new users quit in the first week**. You're the designer on the onboarding redesign. Prepare your moves in **keywords only**, then say each one out loud twice.

1. **Your update (30 seconds).** Invent your progress: what's done, what's next, one blocker. Example keywords: "new welcome screens done · tested 5 users · next: daily-goal screen · blocker: waiting on copy".
2. **Three suggestions** for keeping new users, one at each strength. Ideas to choose from (or use your own):
   - let people pick a daily goal (5, 10 or 15 minutes)
   - send a friendly reminder the next day
   - start with a 1-minute lesson before asking people to sign up
   - show progress after the first lesson ("You learned 10 words!")
   - make the first week's lessons shorter
3. **One push-back line**, ready for when someone suggests something too big: "If we add ___, something else has to move."
4. **One clarification question**, ready for when someone is vague: "When you say ___, do you mean…?"
5. **Two responses** to other people's ideas: one "build" ("Ooh, I like that, and what if we also…?") and one "park" ("Let's park it for now…").

Say the full set once more as quickly as you can. You're ready for the meeting.

1. "That's a really important conversation, but it's too big for ten minutes. Can we put a pin in it and set up a separate meeting?"
2. "We're a little over time, so let's wrap up. I think we can sort out the details in Slack."
3. "This sounds important, but maybe it's one for you two — can you take it offline and let us know what you decide?"
4. "Great question. I don't think anyone knows right now. Can someone own finding out? ... OK, I'll look into it and get back to everyone by Thursday."
5. "OK, so let me just recap quickly before we go."
6. "Does that sound right to everyone? ... OK, I'll take that as a yes. I'll send a quick summary. Thanks, everyone."

## Day 6 materials

### Role cards for a practice meeting

Use these on Day 6 if you're practising with a language partner or tutor instead of a real meeting. Give your partner **their** card only. Each meeting takes about 20 minutes. You always play the designer.

**Meeting 1: The late launch**

- **Your card (designer):** You're redesigning the booking flow of a fitness-class app. You're about two weeks behind, because testing showed people couldn't find the "Cancel booking" option. You've fixed it. New date: the end of next month. You think the team should launch a smaller version first (booking only, no waiting list). At the end, sum up next steps.
- **Partner card (product manager):** You're worried about the delay because you promised leadership a launch next month. Ask for an update. Ask at least one vague question ("Can we speed it up somehow?"). Suggest adding a "bring a friend" feature to the launch "to make up for the delay" (it's too big — see if the designer pushes back). Be open to the smaller-version idea, but ask what you'd lose. Let the designer sum up at the end.

**Meeting 2: Too much work**

- **Your card (designer):** Next sprint, the team can only do about half of this list: a new profile page, a fix for confusing notifications, a "dark mode", and a new payment screen. You think the notifications fix and the payment screen matter most (lots of support tickets, and payment affects money). Make suggestions, push back on dark mode, and sum up.
- **Partner card (engineering lead):** You think dark mode is easy and users keep asking for it on social media. You're worried the payment screen is risky to change. Use one acronym without explaining it (for example "QA" or "MVP") and see if the designer asks. Agree to drop one thing if the designer gives good reasons. At the end, ask "So what are the next steps?"

**Meeting 3: The unhappy client**

- **Your card (designer):** You're showing a website design to a client who runs a small chain of bakeries. They asked for "modern and simple". You used lots of white space and big photos. Clarify what they mean if they're unhappy, push back politely if they want to add too much to the home page, suggest a solution, and sum up what you'll change and by when.
- **Partner card (client):** You like it, but it feels "a bit empty". You want the menu, opening hours for every shop, a newsletter sign-up, a video and customer reviews all on the home page. Use vague words like "warmer", "more life", "make it pop". Be a little stubborn at first, then accept a good compromise.
