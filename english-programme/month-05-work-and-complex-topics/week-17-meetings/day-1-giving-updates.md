We won't repeat that. A **meeting update** is shorter (30–60 seconds), it follows a fixed pattern, and people interrupt you with questions. Tonight is about that pattern, about giving bad news, and about answering the follow-up questions.

## Learning objectives

- Give a 30–60 second update using the pattern: **headline → done → next → blockers → what I need**.
- Start an update with a natural opener ("So, quick update…") and finish it cleanly ("That's it from me").
- Give bad news (a delay, a bug, a missed number) calmly: fact, reason, new plan.
- Answer short follow-up questions in under 15 seconds.
- Sound light and confident with an upbeat update tone and list intonation.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's focus: the update tone.** When Americans give a quick update in a meeting, they sound light and a bit upbeat, even when the news isn't great. Two things make this happen:

1. **The opener is quick and squashed.** "So, quick update" sounds like "so-KWICK-update". Only one word is strong.
2. **Lists go rise, rise, fall.** When you list things, your voice goes up on each item and down on the last one: "We finished the screens ↗, the copy ↗, and the icons ↘." The fall tells people "that's the end of the list".

Softening also matters. Meeting talk is full of small words that make you sound relaxed: "just", "a little", "kind of", "should be". Say them quickly and lightly.

| # | Sentence (CAPITALS = stressed) | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So, QUICK update on the CHECKOUT stuff. | "so-KWICK-update on the CHECK-out stuff" |
| 2 | We FINISHED the screens ↗, the COPY ↗, and the ICONS ↘. | rise, rise, fall |
| 3 | This week I'm gonna FOCUS on the HANDOFF. | "I'm gonna" → "ahm-gunna" |
| 4 | It's taking a LITTLE longer than we THOUGHT. | "a little" → "uh-LIDDLE" |
| 5 | We're kinda BLOCKED on the PAYMENT API. | "kinda" light; "API" = "ay-pee-EYE" |
| 6 | It SHOULD be ready by THURSDAY. | "should be" → "shoodbee" |
| 7 | The ONE thing I need is FEEDBACK from Dana. | pause after "need is" |
| 8 | Overall we're in pretty GOOD shape. | "pretty good" → "priddy GOOD" |
| 9 | The BAD news is we're about a WEEK behind. | "about a" → "uh-bowda" |
| 10 | But the GOOD news is the TESTING went really WELL. | "GOOD news" contrasts with "BAD news" |
| 11 | That's PRETTY much it from ME. | "pretty much" → "priddy-much", falling tone |
| 12 | Happy to go into MORE detail if you WANT. | "happy to" → "HAP-ee-tuh" |

**Method:** speak-along with your whole clip twice (no pausing). Then pause-and-repeat only the 2–3 hardest sentences from the clip. Then say the 12 sentences above twice, the second time faster. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

**Clip to find:** someone giving a short work update or explaining what their team is doing. Search YouTube for "daily standup meeting example", "weekly status update meeting", "work vlog day in the life product designer meeting", "startup team weekly sync".

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Giving updates

### Explanation

A meeting update is not a story and not a report. It's a **headline with four short parts**. People in meetings are listening for one thing: "Do I need to do anything, or worry about anything?" A good update answers that in under a minute.

**The pattern:**

| Part | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Headline | One sentence: the overall state | "So, quick update on onboarding: we're on track." |
| 2. Done | What's finished since last time (2–3 things max) | "We finished the new welcome screens and tested them with five users." |
| 3. Next | What you're doing now / this week | "This week I'm doing the handoff to engineering." |
| 4. Blockers / risks | Anything slowing you down (or "no blockers") | "The only risk is the copy — we're still waiting on marketing." |
| 5. Ask | What you need from the room (if anything) | "So, Priya, if you could get us the copy by Wednesday, that'd be great." |

Then finish with a clear end: "That's it from me." or "Any questions?"

**Why the headline first?** Because if your manager only hears one sentence, it should be that one. "We're on track", "We're a little behind", "We've hit a problem" — say it first, then explain.

**Bad news: fact, reason, plan.** When something has gone wrong, don't hide it in the middle and don't over-apologise. Say it simply:

1. **Fact:** "We're about a week behind on the checkout redesign."
2. **Reason (one sentence):** "The user testing found a bigger problem than we expected with the address form."
3. **Plan:** "So the new date is the 14th, and I'll update you on Monday if anything changes."

Notice: no "I'm so sorry", no long excuses. In American work culture, calm and clear sounds professional. A lot of apology sounds like panic.

**Follow-up questions.** After an update, people often ask quick questions: "When will it be ready?", "Do you need anything from me?", "Is that gonna affect the launch?" Answer in one or two sentences. If you don't know, say so and say when you'll know: "Not sure yet — I'll know by tomorrow."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So, quick update on… | you start your update | "So, quick update on the settings redesign." |
| Overall, we're on track / a little behind | you give the headline | "Overall, we're on track for the launch next week." |
| Since last week, we've… | you say what's done | "Since last week, we've finished all the mobile screens." |
| This week I'm focusing on… | you say what's next | "This week I'm focusing on the empty states." |
| The main blocker right now is… | something is stopping you | "The main blocker right now is we don't have access to the analytics." |
| We're waiting on… | you depend on someone else | "We're waiting on legal to sign off on the wording." |
| No blockers on my end | everything's fine | "No blockers on my end, so we're good." |
| The one thing I need is… | you ask the room for help | "The one thing I need is a decision on the colours by Friday." |
| Heads-up: … | you warn people early | "Heads-up: the release might slip a couple of days." |
| It's taking a little longer than we thought | you explain a delay gently | "The research is taking a little longer than we thought." |
| The new date is… / We're now looking at… | you give a new plan | "We're now looking at the 20th instead of the 14th." |
| I'll keep you posted | you promise more updates | "I'll keep you posted if anything changes." |
| That's it from me | you finish your turn | "That's it from me. Any questions?" |
| Happy to go into more detail | you offer more info without giving it all | "Happy to go into more detail after the meeting." |

### Examples

**1. A daily standup (good news)**

> **Dana (PM):** OK, let's go round. You wanna start?
> **You:** Sure. So, quick update on onboarding. Overall we're on track. Yesterday I finished the last two welcome screens and sent them to Marcus. Today I'm focusing on the empty states — like, what people see before they've added anything. No blockers on my end. That's it from me.
> **Dana:** Awesome, thanks.

**2. A weekly status meeting (bad news)**

> **Manager:** How's the checkout redesign looking?
> **You:** So, honest update: we're about a week behind. The user testing showed people were really confused by the address form, so we're redesigning that part. The good news is the rest of the flow tested really well. We're now looking at the 21st instead of the 14th. I'll keep you posted if anything changes.
> **Manager:** OK. Does that affect the marketing launch?
> **You:** It might. I'm meeting Priya tomorrow to check. I'll let you know by end of day.
> **Manager:** Cool, thanks for flagging it.

**3. An update with an ask**

> **You:** Quick update on the design system. Since last week we've added the new button styles and the form fields, so engineering can start using them. Next up is the date picker. The main blocker right now is that we don't have a final decision on the brand colours. So, Leo, the one thing I need is a yes or no on the blue by Friday. If we get that, we're in good shape.
> **Leo:** Yeah, I can do that. I'll ping you Thursday.
> **You:** Perfect. That's it from me.

**4. Handling a follow-up question you can't answer yet**

> **Marcus (engineer):** Wait, will the new screens work on tablets too?
> **You:** Good question. Not sure yet, honestly — we designed for phones first. I'll check with the team and get back to you by tomorrow.
> **Marcus:** Sounds good.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "So, about the project, last Monday I started to look at the research, and then on Tuesday…" | "So, quick update: we're on track. We finished the research and…" | Headline first. Don't tell it like a diary. |
| "I'm so sorry, I'm really sorry, it's my fault, we are late…" | "We're about a week behind. Here's why, and here's the new date." | Too much apology sounds like panic. Fact, reason, plan. |
| "We are doing the screens and the copy and the icons and the testing and…" | "We finished the screens, the copy ↗, and the icons ↘." | Maximum three things, and drop your voice on the last one so people know the list is over. |
| "The project is in progress." | "We're about 70 percent done — the screens are finished, the testing isn't." | Vague updates make people ask more questions. Give something concrete. |
| "I have finished the screens yesterday." | "I finished the screens yesterday." / "I've finished the screens." | With a finished time (yesterday, last week), use past simple, not present perfect. |
| (silence, then) "…That's all." | "That's it from me. Any questions?" | End clearly and confidently so the next person knows it's their turn. |

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Tonight is a **mixed drill**: a random-topic warm-up, a 3/2/1 round on your own update, and four role-plays. Record everything. Tools: [Random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md), [3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

### Part A: Warm-up, random topics (12 min)

Pick each topic and start talking **immediately** for 1.5–2 minutes. No preparation. Work-flavoured topics tonight:

1. The best meeting you've ever been in, and why it was good
2. Working from home versus working in an office
3. A tool or app you use every single day at work
4. The most annoying thing that happens in meetings
5. What your workday looked like yesterday, from waking up to going to bed

### Part B: 3/2/1, your real project update (20 min)

**Prompt:** "Give your team an update on the project you're working on right now: where it is, what's done, what's next, any problems, and what you need."

1. Speak for **3 minutes**. In the long version you can give more detail and examples. Record it.
2. Speak for **2 minutes**. Same update, fewer details. Record it.
3. Speak for **1 minute**. This is the real meeting version. Headline first. Record it.
4. Now do a bonus **30-second** version — the one you'd give in a daily standup.
5. Listen to the 1-minute version. Did you start with the headline? Did you end with "That's it from me"?

### Part C: Role-plays (30 min)

Read each card, then say your update out loud and record it. After each update, answer the three follow-up questions on the card, one at a time, out loud, each in under 15 seconds. Then do each card a **second time**, faster.

**Card 1: Daily standup (good news)**
You're the designer on a food-delivery app. Yesterday you finished the new "track your order" screens. Today you'll do the handoff to the engineers. No blockers.
Follow-ups: "Did you test the screens with anyone?" / "When can engineering start?" / "Do you need anything from me?"

**Card 2: Status meeting (bad news)**
You're redesigning the sign-up flow for a banking app. You're about two weeks behind, because the security team asked for an extra step (a code sent by text message) and you had to redesign three screens. The new date is the end of next month.
Follow-ups: "Why didn't we know about this earlier?" / "Can we launch without the extra step?" / "Is the new date realistic?"

**Card 3: Cross-team update (mixed news)**
You're presenting results to engineering, marketing and customer support. The new checkout launched two weeks ago. Good: 12% more people finish checkout. Bad: there's a bug on some Android phones where the "Pay" button is hidden. Next step: the fix goes out on Thursday. You need support to tell you if customers keep reporting it.
Follow-ups: "How many people are affected by the bug?" / "Should we pause the launch?" / "What exactly do you need from support?"

**Card 4: Surprise update (no notes)**
Your manager says at the start of a meeting: "Before we start, can you give everyone a quick update on whatever you're working on?" Talk for 45 seconds about your **real** current work. No notes.
Follow-ups: "What's the biggest risk right now?" / "When will we see something?" / "Anything you need from the team?"

### Part D: Listen back (8 min)

Listen to your Card 2 and Card 4 recordings. Count your [3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md) for Card 4 (umms, long pauses, different connectors) and write them in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md). Write down one phrase you wanted to say but couldn't.
