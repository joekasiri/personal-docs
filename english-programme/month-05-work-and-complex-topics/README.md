# Month 5: Work and complex topics
[Programme home](../README.md) · [← Month 4: Real-life pressure](../month-04-real-life-pressure/README.md) · [Month 6: Consolidation →](../month-06-consolidation/README.md)

## What this month is about

For four months you have practised the English of everyday life: stories, questions, small talk, opinions, groups, jokes, phone calls and speaking in front of people. This month you take all of that into the two places where most people freeze hardest: **work** and **serious topics**. A meeting where your manager is listening, a project walkthrough for someone outside your field, a conversation about technology or money where you have to give a real view, a question you didn't see coming after you've presented something.

These situations are hard for a simple reason: the stakes feel higher. In a chat with a friend, a pause is fine. In a meeting, a pause feels like everyone is waiting for you. So your brain switches back to translating, checking every word, and the "talking without thinking" you built in months 1–4 disappears. The fix is the same as always: own the **chunks** for these situations ("So, quick update…", "Just to make sure I get it…", "The downside is…, but…", "On one hand…, on the other…") so well that they come out on their own, and practise under pressure until the pressure feels normal.

You also have a big advantage this month: **you know your work**. As a designer working on products, you already think in clear steps — problem, research, options, trade-offs, decisions, results. This month you learn to say that thinking out loud in natural American English, at normal speed, to anyone: a product manager, an engineer, a client, a friend's parent at dinner.

This month builds directly on month 4. In month 4 you learned to hold your turn in a group, handle calls and stand up in front of people. Now those skills get a job: running your part of a meeting, presenting your work, and answering tough questions in the moment. There is also more speaking than ever — 70 minutes of speaking drill every night and **three live conversations a week**. Use real work moments whenever you can.

## Goals for the month

By the end of month 5 you can…

- [ ] give a clear 60-second update in a meeting, ask for clarification without sounding lost, and push back politely on an idea you disagree with
- [ ] sum up a meeting in 30 seconds: what was decided, who does what, and by when
- [ ] explain your job to someone outside tech, and walk anyone through one of your projects from start to finish in about 3 minutes
- [ ] explain a trade-off honestly ("The downside is…, but…") and answer three or four "why?" questions in a row without freezing
- [ ] talk for 3–5 minutes about a complex topic (technology, AI, culture, money, a news story) and give a balanced view, not just one side
- [ ] handle questions you didn't expect — after a talk, from a tough boss or client — and give and receive feedback calmly
- [ ] get your umms and long pauses down by about 45% compared with your Week 1 Day 1 baseline

## How your nights change this month

Less studying, more speaking. The main skill is now only 20 minutes; the speaking drill grows to 70.

| Block | Minutes | What changes from month 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Shadowing | 15 | Work talk, interviews, demos and discussion podcasts. **Speak-along** with whole clips; pause-and-repeat only for the 2–3 hardest sentences. |
| Main skill | 20 | One work or complex-topic skill per night, with 2–4 short exercises. |
| Speaking drill | 70 | Bigger and more varied (see below). |
| Review | 15 | Spaced review **out loud**: yesterday's phrases, last week's, last month's. |

**The speaking drill, day by day:**

| Day | Speaking drill (70 min) |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Mixed drills built around the lesson: a random-topic warm-up, a 3/2/1 round, and role-plays with role cards |
| Day 2 | 30 min drill + **live conversation #1** (40 min). Backup: voice mode with Claude |
| Day 3 | Mixed drills, like Day 1 |
| Day 4 | **Voice mode with Claude** for the whole block (mock meeting, "why?" chain, two-sided discussion, tough boss or client) |
| Day 5 | 30 min drill + **live conversation #2** (40 min). Backup: voice mode with Claude |
| Day 6 | Weekly check: re-record test + **live conversation #3** |
| Day 7 | Light day: 30 minutes of listening, then rest |

The month ends with [Week 20 Day 6](week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/day-6-weekly-check.md), which is both the weekly check and the **month 5 check**. You will record a 2-minute unprepared talk, count your 3 numbers, have your third live conversation of the week, and then follow the procedure in [Monthly check](../toolkit/monthly-check.md).

**Month 5 targets (compared with your Week 1 Day 1 baseline):**

| Number | Target |
|---|---|
| Umms and uhhs | down about **45%** |
| Pauses longer than 2 seconds | down about **45%** |
| Different connectors in 2 minutes | **7 or more** (on the way to month 6's target of 8) |
| Skill | you can run your part of a meeting and handle questions you didn't expect |

Compare tonight's recording with your baseline **and** with your month 4 check. If the numbers aren't going down, don't just push on: tell Claude in your Day 6 report and adjust the plan before month 6.
