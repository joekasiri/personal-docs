# Week 8 · Day 3: Sentence starter bank

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Do [Day 1](day-1-sixty-second-answers.md) and [Day 2](day-2-rapid-fire-answers.md) first.
- In Month 1 you learned [time-buyers](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) ("Let me think…", "Good question…", "I mean…"). They fill silence while you think. Tonight's **sentence starters** are different: they don't buy time, they **start your actual answer** and give it a shape.
- Have a die (or a random number app) for the drill, or just close your eyes and point.
- Have a fast podcast or vlog clip ready (search terms in section 1).

## Learning objectives

- Start any answer within 1 second with a ready-made starter.
- Choose a starter that fits the type of question (favourite, opinion, experience, big question, unknown).
- Use 15+ starters from a personal starter bank.
- Hear and say "gotta", "got a" and "gonna" in fast speech.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: "gotta", "got a" and "gonna" in fast speech.** Three forms that sound almost the same:

- **gotta** = have got to / have to: "I gotta go." → "I GAH-da go"
- **got a** = have got a: "I've got a good one." → "I've GAH-da good one"
- **gonna** = going to: "I'm gonna say pizza." → "I'm GUN-na say pizza"

In fast speech the T becomes a quick D sound (the flap T you met in [Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md)). Many sentence starters use them: "It's gotta be…", "I've got a good one", "I'm gonna go with…".

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. Oh, it's GOTta be pizza. → "oh, it's GAH-da be PIZza"
2. I've got a GOOD one. → "I've GAH-da GOOD one"
3. I'm gonna go with the FIRST one. → "I'm GUN-na go with the FIRST one"
4. Honestly, I gotta SAY… → "honestly, I GAH-da SAY"
5. OK, I'm gonna be HONest.
6. You've GOTta try it. → "ya GAH-da TRY it"
7. HANDS down, the beach. → "HANDZ down, the BEACH"
8. Ooh, that's a BIG one.
9. It's FUNny you ask, because…
10. The first thing that comes to MIND is… → "the first thing th't comes ta MIND is"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "podcast hosts answer listener questions", "would you rather podcast", "this or that questions vlog" or "favourite things Q and A". You want speakers answering many different questions quickly.

1. Find a 40–60 second part with 3–4 questions and answers.
2. Listen twice. How does each answer start? Write down every starter you hear.
3. Speak along 5 times, playing only the person answering.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with just the first 5 words of each answer, 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Sentence starter bank

### Explanation

Here's what happens when you freeze: someone asks a question, and your brain tries to plan the **whole** answer before you say anything. That takes too long. Natives don't plan the whole answer. They start with a ready-made **starter**, and the starter tells their brain what kind of answer is coming.

For example, "What's the best city you've been to?":

- "Oh, it's gotta be…" → now your brain knows it needs **one name**. "…Lisbon."
- "Ooh, it's a tie between… and…" → now it needs **two names**.
- "The first one that comes to mind is…" → **one name, not necessarily the best**, so there's no pressure.

The starter is like a rail for your train. Once you're on it, the rest follows.

**Starters vs time-buyers.** A time-buyer ("Hmm, good question… let me think…") gives you silence-free thinking time, but you still have to start the answer after it. A starter **is** the start of the answer. You can combine them when you really need time: "Ooh, good question. Honestly, my first thought is…".

**Build your bank by question type:**

| Question type | Starters |
|---|---|
| **Favourite / best** ("What's your favourite…?") | Oh, easy: … · It's gotta be… · Hands down, … · Ooh, it's a tie between… and… |
| **Opinion** ("What do you think about…?") | Honestly, my first thought is… · I'm gonna be honest… · OK, so for me… · I'd probably say… |
| **Experience** ("Have you ever…?", "Tell me about a time…") | Oh, I've got a good one. · The first thing that comes to mind is… · Actually, yeah, this one time… |
| **Big / deep** ("What's the most important…?") | Ooh, that's a big one. · The short answer is…, but… · There are a few things, but the main one is… |
| **Don't know** ("What's the population of…?") | I've never really thought about it, but I guess… · No idea, but if I had to guess… |
| **Description** ("What's it like?") | The best way to describe it is… · It's kind of like… · Picture… |
| **Surprising / personal** | It's funny you ask, because… · Oh man, where do I start? · I've actually thought about this a lot. |

You already know some of these from earlier weeks ("Honestly, I think…", "This one time…", "It's kind of like…"). Tonight they become a single bank you can grab from instantly.

**Pick your favourites.** You don't need all of them. Choose **2 starters per question type** that feel natural to you, and use those until they're automatic.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Oh, easy: … | the answer is obvious to you | "Favourite food? Oh, easy: tacos." |
| It's gotta be… | you choose one thing quickly | "Best season? It's gotta be fall." |
| Hands down, … | you're 100% sure it's the best | "Hands down, the best trip I've ever taken." |
| Ooh, it's a tie between… and… | you can't choose between two | "Ooh, it's a tie between Rome and Mexico City." |
| Honestly, my first thought is… | opinion, first reaction | "Honestly, my first thought is it's too expensive." |
| OK, so for me… | personal answer to a general question | "OK, so for me, it's all about the people." |
| Oh, I've got a good one. | you have a story ready | "Embarrassing moment? Oh, I've got a good one." |
| The first thing that comes to mind is… | you take the first idea, no pressure | "The first thing that comes to mind is my grandma's kitchen." |
| Ooh, that's a big one. | a deep or difficult question | "Ooh, that's a big one. I guess… my family?" |
| The short answer is…, but… | you give a quick answer, then more | "The short answer is yes, but it's complicated." |
| I've never really thought about it, but I guess… | you don't have a ready opinion | "I've never really thought about it, but I guess I'd pick the mountains." |
| It's funny you ask, because… | the question connects to something recent | "It's funny you ask, because I was just talking about this yesterday." |

### Examples

**Example 1: rapid questions at a dinner**

> A: What's the best thing you ate this year?
> B: Oh, easy: this tiny dumpling place in Queens. Like, life-changing.
> A: Best city you've ever been to?
> B: Ooh, it's a tie between Lisbon and Seoul. Lisbon for the vibe, Seoul for the food.
> A: Something you'd never do again?
> B: Oh, I've got a good one. A twelve-hour bus ride. Never again.

**Example 2: a big question**

> A: What's the most important thing in a job, for you?
> B: Ooh, that's a big one. The short answer is the people, but it's more than that. Like, I've had jobs with great people where I was still bored. So I'd probably say people *and* learning something new.

**Example 3: a question you've never thought about**

> A: If you could live in any decade, which one?
> B: Hmm, I've never really thought about it, but I guess… the nineties? Like, no smartphones, but still good music. And you could still afford an apartment.

**Example 4: "it's funny you ask"**

> A: Do you ever think about moving back home?
> B: It's funny you ask, because I literally talked to my mom about this last night. Honestly, my first thought is no, but… I dunno, ask me again in five years.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ (5 seconds of silence while planning the whole answer) | ✅ "It's gotta be…" + answer | Start with a starter. Let the answer come while you talk. |
| ❌ My favourite food is… my favourite food is… | ✅ Oh, easy: … | Repeating the question is slow. Use a starter. |
| ❌ It must be pizza. | ✅ It's gotta be pizza. | "It must be" sounds like a logical guess. "It's gotta be" = it's definitely my choice. |
| ❌ There is a tie between Rome and Paris. | ✅ It's a tie between Rome and Paris. | Fixed chunk: "It's a tie between…". |
| ❌ The first thing coming to my mind is… | ✅ The first thing that comes to mind is… | Fixed chunk, no "my". |
| ❌ Hands down, I think maybe pizza? | ✅ Hands down, pizza. | "Hands down" is strong. Don't hedge after it. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md). Tonight you **don't know the prompt in advance**: roll a die (or pick a number from 1–6 without looking) and use that prompt.

**The surprise prompts:**

1. What's the best decision you've ever made?
2. What's something you used to love but don't anymore?
3. What's the most useful thing you've learned at work?
4. If you could change one thing about where you live, what would it be?
5. What's a skill you wish you had?
6. What's the best day you've had this year?

**Rules for tonight:** start within **1 second** with a starter from your bank. In the 3-minute round, use at least **5 different starters** (one for each new part of your answer: "The short answer is…", "Oh, I've got a good one…", "Honestly, my first thought is…").

1. **Roll (no planning).** Read the prompt, start recording, start talking.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Count your starters. How long before your first word?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).**
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Starters count as connectors.

**Second roll (about 15 minutes):** roll again (re-roll if it's the same number) and do 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log your personal starter bank** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): the 2 starters you chose for each question type in Exercise 3.3 (that's up to 14 phrases; that's OK tonight).
2. **Cover the log.** For each question type, invent a question and answer it with one of your starters, from memory.
3. **Say Day 2's 5 short-answer phrases from memory** as answers to quick questions.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
