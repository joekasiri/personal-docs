## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: "betcha", "getcha" and "gotcha".** When "you" comes after a word ending in T, the T and the Y join into a **"ch"** sound:

- "I bet you…" → **"I betcha…"**
- "I'll get you one." → **"I'll getcha one."**
- "Got you." → **"Gotcha."** (= I understand / I caught you)
- "Don't you think…?" → **"Doncha think…?"**
- "What do you want?" → **"Whatcha want?"**

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times. Notice the contractions too: they're part of the same fast rhythm.

1. I BETcha it's closed. → "I BET-cha it's CLOSED"
2. GOTcha. That makes SENSE. → "GOT-cha. that makes SENSE"
3. I'll GETcha a coffee. → "ah'll GET-cha a COFfee"
4. DONcha think it's too EARly? → "DON-cha think it's too EARly?"
5. WHATcha up to TOnight? → "WHAT-cha up ta TOnight?"
6. I'd've told you, but I FORgot. → "I'd-uv TOLD ya, but I fer-GOT"
7. It's been AGES. → "its-bin AY-jiz"
8. I should've CALLED. → "I SHOULD-uv CALLED"
9. That'd be aWESome. → "THAD-d be AWsome"
10. We'll SEE. → "wull SEE"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "friends chatting podcast unfiltered", "vlog talking to camera fast", "catching up with my best friend podcast" or "casual phone call vlog". Choose very relaxed, fast speech.

1. Find a 40–60 second part.
2. Listen twice. Count the contractions you hear (you'll be surprised).
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with any sentence that has "should've", "would've", "it's been", "I'd" or "gotcha", 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Contractions in your own speech

### Explanation

You understand contractions. You've read them a thousand times. But in your **own** speech, under pressure, you might still say "I am going", "I have been there", "It is been a long time". There are three problems with full forms in casual speech:

1. **They're slow.** "I would have" is three heavy words. "I'd've" is one quick sound.
2. **They sound formal**, like an email or a speech.
3. **They sound emphatic.** "I DO like it" or "I am NOT tired" can sound like you're arguing. So "I am tired" can sound like "I *am* tired, OK?"

Native speakers contract almost every time they can. Tonight's goal is to make contractions your **default**.

**The contraction map:**

| Full form | Contraction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I am / you are / it is / that is / there is / what is | I'm · you're · it's · that's · there's · what's | "That's crazy." "What's up?" |
| I have / we have / it has | I've · we've · it's | "I've been there." "It's been ages." |
| I will / it will / that will | I'll · it'll · that'll | "I'll text you." "That'll be fun." |
| I would / it would / that would | I'd · it'd · that'd | "I'd love to." "That'd be great." |
| would have / should have / could have / must have | would've · should've · could've · must've | "You should've told me." "It must've been hard." |
| I had (past perfect) | I'd | "I'd already left." |
| do not / did not / is not / was not / have not | don't · didn't · isn't · wasn't · haven't | "I haven't seen it." |
| will not / cannot / would not / could not | won't · can't · wouldn't · couldn't | "I won't be long." |
| let us | let's | "Let's go." |
| where did / how did / what did (spoken) | where'd · how'd · what'd | "Where'd you go?" "How'd it go?" |

**'s and 'd have two meanings:**

- **'s = is or has.** "She's tired" (is). "She's been busy" (has: look for *been* or a past participle after it).
- **'d = would or had.** "I'd love to" (would: a base verb follows). "I'd already eaten" (had: a past participle follows).

**would've / should've / could've** sound like "would-uv", "should-uv", "could-uv". (Never write "would of": it's a spelling mistake that comes from this sound.) In Month 3 you'll hear an even faster version ("woulda", "shoulda") when you study [hypotheticals](../../month-03-spontaneity/README.md).

**When NOT to contract:**

- **Positive short answers at the end:** "Are you tired?" "Yes, I am." (not "Yes, I'm.") "Is it open?" "Yeah, it is." (not "Yeah, it's.") Negative short answers are fine: "No, it isn't."
- **Emphasis:** when you're correcting someone or insisting: "I DID call you!" "I am NOT going."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's been (ages / a while / a crazy week). | talking about a period of time up to now | "It's been ages! How are you?" |
| I've been (+ -ing)… | what you've been doing recently | "I've been working way too much." |
| I'd love to. / I'd rather… | accepting / preferring | "I'd love to, but I'd rather not drive." |
| That'd be (great / awesome). | reacting to an offer or idea | "You'll pick me up? That'd be amazing." |
| I'll (text you / let you know). | quick promises and plans | "I'll let you know tonight." |
| You should've (told me / come). | something that didn't happen, gently | "You should've come! It was so fun." |
| I would've (+ past participle), but… | you wanted to, but something stopped you | "I would've called, but my phone died." |
| It must've been (hard / amazing). | guessing about someone's past experience | "Three flights in one day? That must've been exhausting." |
| How'd it go? / Where'd you go? | fast past questions | "How'd the interview go?" |
| I haven't (+ past participle) yet. | not yet | "I haven't seen it yet. No spoilers!" |
| There's no way… | strong "impossible" | "There's no way I'm getting up at five." |
| Gotcha. | "I understand" (casual) | "Meet at the side door, not the front." "Gotcha." |

### Examples

**Example 1: full forms vs contractions**

Full forms (slow and stiff):
> "I am sorry I did not call you. I would have called, but I have been so busy. It is been a crazy week. I will call you tomorrow."

Contractions (natural):
> "Sorry I didn't call. I would've, but I've been so busy. It's been a crazy week. I'll call you tomorrow."

**Example 2: catching up with a friend**

> A: Hey! It's been ages!
> B: I know, right? What've you been up to?
> A: Oh man, I've been working nonstop. I should've taken a vacation in the summer, but I didn't, and now I'm dead.
> B: That must've been rough. Hey, we're doing a barbecue Saturday, if you're free.
> A: That'd be awesome. I'll bring drinks.
> B: Gotcha.

**Example 3: 's and 'd in one chat**

> A: Where's Sam? He's usually here by now. (is / is)
> B: He's been sick all week. (has)
> A: Oh no. I'd have called him if I'd known. (would / had)
> B: I'd text him. He'd like that. (would / would)

(Natives might say "I'd've called him if I'd known." Both are fine.)

**Example 4: when not to contract**

> A: You're not coming tonight, are you?
> B: I am! I said I'd come. (emphasis: "I AM")
> A: Is it far?
> B: Yeah, it is. Like forty minutes. (short answer: no contraction at the end)

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ It is been a long time. / It's being a long time. | ✅ It's been a long time. | "It's been" = it has been. |
| ❌ I would of called you. | ✅ I would've called you. | "Would've" = would have. "Would of" is a spelling mistake from the sound. |
| ❌ Yes, I'm. / Yeah, it's. | ✅ Yes, I am. / Yeah, it is. | No contraction at the end of a positive short answer. |
| ❌ I'd went there before. | ✅ I'd gone there before. | 'd = had here, so it needs a past participle. |
| ❌ I am going to text you, I will call you later. | ✅ I'm gonna text you, I'll call you later. | Full forms sound stiff and slow in casual speech. |
| ❌ I have not saw it. | ✅ I haven't seen it. | Contraction + past participle. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): voice mode with Claude

Tonight is **30 minutes of fast Q&A** with Claude. Claude asks quick questions, and you answer with everything from this week: 60-second answers, rapid-fire answers, starters and contractions. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

1. **Set up (5 min).** Headphones on, notebook open. Start voice mode and paste or say this prompt:

```
I'm a B2 English learner and this is my speed review week. Let's do 30 minutes of fast Q&A, like a friendly American getting to know me at a party.
Mix three kinds of questions:
1. Big "getting to know you" questions (Tell me about yourself, What do you do, Where are you from, What do you do for fun, What brought you here).
2. Quick rapid-fire questions that need short answers (You a morning person? Busy week? Ever been to Japan?).
3. Questions that make me use contractions, like "What would you have done?", "How long have you been…?", "What should you have done differently?", "Where'd you…?".
Go fast. Don't wait long after my answers, just react briefly and ask the next question. Every 10 minutes, stop and give me quick spoken feedback: up to 3 mistakes with the natural version, and any time I used a full form like "I am" or "I would have" where a contraction sounds more natural.
Use casual American English.
```

2. **Q&A (30 min)** in three 10-minute blocks, with Claude's feedback after each block.
3. **Write corrections (10 min).** Write each correction as *what I said → better version*. Say each better version 3 times, fast, with contractions.

**Focus tonight:** speed over perfection. Start every answer within 1 second (use your starters from Day 3). If you notice you said "I am" or "I have", don't stop. Just say the next sentence with a contraction.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): 3 from tonight ("It's been ages", "I would've…, but…", "That'd be awesome") and 2 corrections from Claude.
2. **Cover the log.** Say each correction's better version from memory.
3. **Say your Day 3 starter bank from memory**, with one contraction in each answer ("It's gotta be… I've been there like five times").
4. Write in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) how many full forms Claude caught and how it compares with the number you counted before tonight.
