## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: speak-along with all the fast forms.** This week you practised *lemme, gimme, dunno, gotta, gonna, betcha, gotcha* and contractions. Tonight you hear them all in one clip and speak along at full speed.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Each sentence mixes several fast forms. Say each 3 times, getting faster.

1. Lemme tell you what HAPpened. → "lemme tell ya wha' HAPpened"
2. I dunno, I feel like it's gotta be the FIRST one.
3. I betcha he's gonna be LATE again. → "I BET-cha he's gonna be LATE again"
4. Gimme a sec, I've gotta find the PHOTO.
5. It's been CRAzy, honestly.
6. I should've known, you know what I MEAN? → "I SHOULD-uv known, y'know whad I MEAN?"
7. Gotcha. So whatcha gonna DO?
8. I'd've said something, but I didn't wanna be RUDE.
9. That'd be AWEsome, lemme check my CALendar.
10. Dunno about YOU, but I'm STARving.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "unfiltered friends podcast", "storytime and hot takes podcast", "chatty vlog walk and talk" or "two friends catch up podcast". Choose a 2-minute part where someone tells a story *and* says what they think about it.

1. Listen to the 2 minutes once.
2. Speak along with the first minute 3 times.
3. Speak along with the second minute 3 times.
4. Speak along with the full 2 minutes twice without stopping. Record the last one and compare it with your Day 5 recording from [Week 5](../week-05-better-stories/day-5-retelling-films-and-shows.md). Can you keep up better now?

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Combining story, opinion and question

### Explanation

Real conversation is not "story time", then "opinion time", then "question time". People mix them in one turn. Someone tells a short story, says what they think about it, then throws a question to the other person. That's what makes a conversation flow, and it's exactly what natives do without thinking.

The shape is **S-O-Q**:

1. **Story** (20–40 seconds): a mini story with a fast scene, maybe one quote, and an ending (Week 5).
2. **Opinion** (1–2 sentences): what you think about it, or what you learned (Week 6).
3. **Question** (1 sentence): hand it over (Month 1 questions).

> **S:** "So last week I'm in a meeting, and my manager goes, 'Can everyone turn their cameras on?' And literally nobody does."
> **O:** "Which, honestly, I get. I feel like cameras on all day is exhausting."
> **Q:** "What's it like at your work? Do people keep their cameras on?"

You can also flip it: **O-S-Q** (opinion first, then the story as an example, then the question). That's O-R-E-O from [Week 6, Day 4](../week-06-opinions/day-4-giving-reasons-and-examples.md) with a question at the end.

**The bridges** are the secret. They connect the parts so it doesn't sound like three separate answers:

| From → to | Bridge chunks |
|---|---|
| Story → opinion | Which, honestly… · And that made me realise… · So now I'm kinda convinced that… · Anyway, the point is… · Which is why I think… |
| Opinion → question | Has that ever happened to you? · What would you have done? · Am I crazy, or…? · What's your take? · Is it just me, or…? · Do you think I overreacted? |
| Opinion → story (O-S-Q) | Like, just the other day… · Case in point: … · Let me give you an example. |

**When someone else does S-O-Q to you,** respond with **react → answer the question → your own mini story or opinion**. That's how a chain starts, and suddenly you've talked for 20 minutes.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Which, honestly, … | you add your opinion about the story you just told | "…and she just left. Which, honestly, fair." |
| And that made me realise… | the story taught you something | "And that made me realise I never take breaks." |
| So now I'm kinda convinced that… | the story changed your opinion | "So now I'm kinda convinced that cheap shoes are a scam." |
| Anyway, the point is… | you move from the story to your main idea | "Anyway, the point is, always check the date." |
| Which is why I think… | the story is a reason for your opinion | "Which is why I think meetings should be shorter." |
| Has that ever happened to you? | you ask if they've had the same experience | "Has that ever happened to you?" |
| What would you have done? | you ask what they'd do in your situation | "I just said nothing. What would you have done?" |
| Am I crazy, or…? | you check if they agree with your (maybe strange) opinion | "Am I crazy, or is brunch kind of overrated?" |
| Is it just me, or…? | the same, softer | "Is it just me, or is everyone tired this week?" |
| What's your take? | you ask for their opinion | "That's how I see it. What's your take?" |
| Do you think I overreacted? | you ask them to judge your reaction | "I sent him a really long message. Do you think I overreacted?" |
| Let me give you an example. | opinion first, then the story | "People here are super friendly. Let me give you an example." |

### Examples

**Example 1: S-O-Q at a party**

> "OK, so this was last weekend. I'm at the grocery store, and this guy in front of me pays for the old lady behind him. Like, just out of nowhere. And she goes, 'Oh, honey, you didn't have to.' And he's like, 'My grandma would've wanted me to.' || I almost cried in the cereal aisle. Which, honestly, made me realise how rarely you see stuff like that now. Has anything like that ever happened to you?"

**Example 2: O-S-Q**

> "Honestly, I feel like open offices are the worst. Let me give you an example. Last Tuesday, I'm trying to focus on this design, right, and three people start a meeting literally next to my desk. With a speakerphone. Needless to say, I got nothing done. Am I crazy, or are open offices just a bad idea?"

**Example 3: responding to someone's S-O-Q (react → answer → your own S-O-Q)**

> A: …so I ended up paying for the whole dinner. Do you think I overreacted when I said something?
> B: No way, that's rough. I would've said something too. Actually, that reminds me, my roommate does this thing where he "forgets" his wallet. Like, every time. So now I'm kinda convinced he does it on purpose. Is it just me, or is that super common?

**Example 4: a mix of Month 1 and Month 2 phrases**

> "So basically, I've been trying to wake up at six (Month 1 connector). It's been a disaster (Week 8 contraction). Like, the first day, I'm up, I'm ready, and next thing I know (Week 5 suspense), it's 9:30, and I'm asleep on the couch. Turns out (Month 1 connector) I'm just not a morning person. And honestly, I feel like (Week 6 opinion) the whole 'wake up at five' thing is overrated. What's your take?"

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ (story) …and that's it. (long silence) My opinion is… | ✅ …which, honestly, … | Use a bridge so the parts flow. |
| ❌ What do you think about this? | ✅ What's your take? / Has that ever happened to you? | A specific question is easier to answer and sounds more natural. |
| ❌ What would you do? (about a past situation) | ✅ What would you have done? | Past situation → "would have done". |
| ❌ It made me realised… | ✅ It made me realise… | "Make someone do", base form. |
| ❌ Is it only me or…? | ✅ Is it just me, or…? | Fixed chunk with "just". |
| ❌ (5-minute story, no question) | ✅ (40-second story, opinion, question) | Keep the story short and hand it over. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Your hardest story from Month 1, retold the Month 2 way."** Use the story with your worst numbers from Month 1.

**Rules for tonight:** fast scene (Week 5, Day 1), at least one suspense moment (Day 2), two quotes (Day 3), a clean ending (Day 4), **then** an opinion with a bridge and a question (S-O-Q). Use contractions throughout (Day 4 of this week).

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords. Write only your bridge and your question.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Compare with your Month 1 recording of this story (if you kept it). What's different?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** All of S-O-Q in 60 seconds.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Write them next to this story's Month 1 numbers.

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "A time you changed your mind about something."** Use O-S-Q: the opinion you have now, the story that changed it, a question. 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "Which, honestly…", "And that made me realise…", "What would you have done?", "Is it just me, or…?", "What's your take?".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory inside a 20-second S-O-Q.
3. **Choose the 10 best phrases from this whole week** and say each one from memory. Then choose the **10 best from the whole month** (look back at Weeks 5–7) and say those too. Mark any you forgot. Those are your focus for the Month 2 check.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
