# Week 8 exercises: Automatic answers
[Week 8 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-sixty-second-answers.md) · [Day 2](day-2-rapid-fire-answers.md) · [Day 3](day-3-sentence-starter-bank.md) · [Day 4](day-4-contractions-in-your-own-speech.md) · [Day 5](day-5-combining-story-opinion-question.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** everything is **out loud**, and this week **speed matters more than perfection**. Use a timer. If you hesitate, don't restart: keep going and do the item again at the end. Record when the exercise says so. Model answers are in each day's answer key. For the "about you" questions, the models are examples about an invented person: replace the details with your own life.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Build your skeletons
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** write keywords, then say

1. For each of the 8 questions, write a **4-keyword skeleton** (one word or short phrase per part): headline · 2 details · spice · back to you. Don't write full sentences.
2. Read the model answer for that question, then cover it and say **your own** answer from your keywords (about 45–60 seconds).
3. Keep your skeletons. You'll use them all week and on Day 6.

| # | Question | Headline | Details | Spice | Back to you |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself. | | | | |
| 2 | What do you do? | | | | |
| 3 | Where are you from? | | | | |
| 4 | What do you do for fun? | | | | |
| 5 | What brought you here? | | | | |
| 6 | What are you working on these days? | | | | |
| 7 | What's a normal weekend like for you? | | | | |
| 8 | How long have you been learning English? / Where did you learn your English? | | | | |

**Model answers** (an invented person: Dani, a product designer who moved from Lisbon to Toronto):

1. **Tell me about yourself.** "So I'm Dani, I'm originally from Lisbon, but I've been living in Toronto for about three years. I work in product design at a travel start-up. In my free time, I'm really into cooking, like, way too into it. My friends call me 'the soup guy'. Long story. What about you?"
2. **What do you do?** "I'm a product designer. Basically, I figure out how our app works for people, like what happens when you book a trip. These days I'm mostly working on the checkout. The best part is testing with real users. The worst part is when they can't find the 'pay' button that I *thought* was obvious. What do you do?"
3. **Where are you from?** "Originally Lisbon, in Portugal. But I've been here three years now. Honestly, the thing I miss most is the light. Like, in Lisbon it's sunny and golden all the time, and here in February it's just… gray. Have you ever been to Portugal?"
4. **What do you do for fun?** "I'm kind of a homebody, honestly. I cook a lot, like, big complicated stuff on Sundays. And I've started climbing. I'm terrible, but it's so fun. Last week I finally got to the top of this wall I've been trying for a month. What about you, are you into sports?"
5. **What brought you here?** "Work, mainly. I got an offer from a start-up here, and I thought, OK, one year, let's try it. That was three years ago. Ha. What brought me to *this* meetup was a friend, she said it's the best way to meet people. Is it your first time?"
6. **What are you working on these days?** "So we're redesigning the checkout in our app. It's kind of a mess right now. People drop out halfway, and we're trying to figure out why. We've tested with like ten people so far, and it turns out the problem is the hidden fees. So yeah, fun times. What about you, anything big at work?"
7. **What's a normal weekend like for you?** "Most weekends, Saturday's for the city, like markets, brunch, seeing friends. Sunday's my lazy day: I cook something huge and watch a show. Honestly, I feel like I need one totally quiet day, or the week's a disaster. How about you, are you more of a go-out person or a stay-in person?"
8. **How long have you been learning English?** "Since school, technically, but I only started really *speaking* it a few years ago, when I moved here. The first six months were rough. I'd plan every sentence in my head before I said it. It's a lot better now, but I still freeze sometimes in big groups. Do you speak any other languages?"

### Exercise 1.2: 10 – 30 – 60
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** timed out loud, record 2

1. For each of the 8 questions, say your answer at **three lengths**, using a timer:
   - **10 seconds:** headline + back to you.
   - **30 seconds:** headline + 1 detail + spice + back to you.
   - **60 seconds:** full skeleton.
2. Go straight from one length to the next. Don't stop between them.
3. Record the full set for the two questions you find hardest.

### Exercise 2.3: Just right
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** fix, then out loud

1. Each answer below is **too short** (cold) or **too long** (didn't notice it was a quick question). Say a **just-right** version: short answer + tiny add-on (and maybe a quick question back).

2. A: "Busy day?" B: "Well, so, in the morning I had a meeting at nine, and then another at ten, and then I had to finish the slides, which took two hours because the template was broken, and then lunch, and then…"

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Contract it
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Say each sentence with **every possible contraction** (and "gonna" / "wanna" where natural).
2. Say it twice: the second time at full speed.

1. I am not sure, but I think it is going to rain.
2. It has been a long time since I have seen you.
3. I would love to come, but I have got to work.
4. You should have told me. I would have helped you.
5. We will see. I do not know yet.
6. That would be amazing. I will bring the snacks.
7. She has been working here for two years.
8. I had already left when he called.
9. There is no way I am doing that again.
10. What did you do last night? Where did you go?
11. It must have been really hard.
12. They are not coming. They have got a family thing.
13. I could have gone, but I did not want to.
14. Let us get coffee. It will be quick.
15. He is the guy who has been calling me.

### Exercise 4.2: Is or has? Would or had?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** decide, then say

1. Say what each **'s** or **'d** means: *is / has / would / had*.
2. Then say the sentence out loud, fast.

1. She's been to Japan twice.
2. He's my brother's best friend.
3. I'd love a coffee.
4. I'd never seen snow before that trip.
5. It's been raining all day.
6. It's raining again.
7. We'd better leave now.
8. She'd already eaten when we got there.
9. That'd be great, thanks.
10. He's finished the project.
11. What's he doing here?
12. I'd go if I had more time.

### Exercise 4.3: Contraction-heavy answers
**Time:** 20 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last 4

1. Each question is built to make you use contractions. Answer in 20–30 seconds, using **at least 3 contractions** in each answer.
2. Record questions 9–12. Listen: did you use any full forms? Say those sentences again with contractions.

1. How long have you been living where you live now?
2. What would you have done differently in your last job (or at school)?
3. What should you have packed on your last trip, but didn't?
4. What's the longest you've ever waited for something?

9. What have you been watching lately?
10. Who's someone you should've stayed in touch with?
11. What's been the best part of this month?

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Bridge it
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each line is a mini story. Say it, then add a **bridge** + **opinion** + **question** (S-O-Q). Use a different bridge and a different question each time (see the [Day 5 key phrases](day-5-combining-story-opinion-question.md)).
2. About 20–30 seconds each.

1. "My neighbour knocked on my door at midnight to give me a package that was delivered to him by mistake."
2. "I waited 45 minutes for a table, and they gave it to a famous guy who arrived after me."
3. "My boss replied to my 3-page email with one word: 'Sure.'"
4. "I tried a 'quick' 10-minute recipe and it took me two hours."
5. "A stranger on the train helped me carry my suitcase up three flights of stairs."
6. "My friend posted a photo of me sleeping at her party."
7. "I left my laptop on the bus and the driver kept it safe for me."
8. "My team spent a month on a feature, and the client said, 'We don't need that anymore.'"

### Exercise 5.2: Month 1 + Month 2 mix
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record 3

1. For each prompt, give a **30–40 second turn** that uses **one phrase from the Month 1 column** and **one phrase from the Month 2 column** (choose any, but don't repeat a phrase).
2. Cross out each phrase when you've used it. Try to use all of them by the end.
3. Record any 3 turns.

| Month 1 phrases | Month 2 phrases |
|---|---|
| So basically… | So this was (last summer)… |
| Turns out… | You're not gonna believe this. |
| Long story short… | And I'm like, "…" |
| The funny part is… | Needless to say… |
| At that point… | Honestly, I feel like… |
| I ended up (+ -ing)… | I see what you mean, but… |
| figure out | Take (my brother), for example. |
| a piece of cake / a long shot (any Month 1 idiom) | I guess it depends on… |
| pretty (good / bad) | The way it works is… |
| What've you been up to? | It came down to… |

**Prompts:**

1. Tell me about the last time you got lost.
2. What do you think of the city you live in?
3. How did you choose your job?
4. Tell me about a really good meal.
5. What's the most useful app on your phone, and how does it work?
6. Tell me about a time you were late for something important.
7. Is it better to travel alone or with friends?
8. What's something that went wrong this week?

### Exercise 5.3: Turn chains
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record 2

1. Read each turn (person A). Respond with **react → answer the question → your own S-O-Q** (about 30–40 seconds).
2. Then imagine A answers your question, and respond again in 2–3 sentences.
3. Record any 2 chains.

1. A: "So I get to the airport, and my flight's been moved to the next day. Like, they just didn't tell me. Which, honestly, I think should be illegal. Has that ever happened to you?"
2. A: "Last week my manager asked everyone to come into the office on Fridays. And like, half the team just… didn't. Is it just me, or are Friday office days kind of pointless?"
3. A: "I finally deleted social media from my phone last month. First week, I kept grabbing my phone for no reason. Now? I feel way calmer. What's your take? Could you do it?"
4. A: "My friend borrowed my car and brought it back with no gas. And I didn't say anything. What would you have done?"

### Answer key

**Exercise 5.1 (model answers):**
1. "…to give me a package that was delivered to him by mistake. Which, honestly, is so nice, but also, midnight? Is it just me, or is that a weird time to knock?"
3. "…with one word: 'Sure.' And that made me realise nobody reads long emails. Do you write long emails, or are you a 'sure' person?"
5. "…up three flights of stairs. So now I'm kinda convinced people are nicer than we think. Has a stranger ever helped you like that?"
8. "…'We don't need that anymore.' Anyway, the point is, always check before you build. Has that ever happened on your team?"

**Exercise 5.3 (model, 4):**
> "Oh no, that's rough. Honestly, I would've said something, but nicely, like, 'Hey, next time can you fill it up?' Actually, that reminds me, my brother does this with my phone charger. He borrows it and I never see it again. Like, I've lost four chargers to him. So now I'm kinda convinced I should just buy him one. Am I crazy, or is that a fair solution?"
