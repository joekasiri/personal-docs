## Learning objectives

- Answer the 8 most common "getting to know you" questions in about 60 seconds each.
- Use the shape **headline → 2 details → spice → back to you**.
- Make a 10-second, 30-second and 60-second version of each answer.
- Hear and say "lemme" and "gimme" naturally.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: "lemme" and "gimme".** In fast speech, "let me" becomes **"lemme"** and "give me" becomes **"gimme"**. The T and the V disappear and the words join. You'll hear them all the time: "Lemme think", "Gimme a sec". You don't *have* to say them like this, but it makes you faster and sound more relaxed.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. Lemme THINK. → "LEM-me THINK"
2. Gimme a SEC. → "GIM-me a SEC"
3. Lemme tell you about my JOB. → "lemme tell ya about my JOB"
4. Gimme a BREAK. → "gimme a BRAKE"
5. OK, lemme start from the beGINning.
6. Can you gimme a HAND? → "c'n ya gimme a HAND?"
7. Lemme know if you're COMing. → "lemme KNOW if ya COMin'"
8. Gimme like five MINutes.
9. Lemme see… I've been here about two YEARS.
10. Don't gimme that LOOK. → "don' gimme that LOOK"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "day in my life vlog talking fast", "get ready with me chatty vlog", "introduce myself vlog casual" or "Q and A vlog rapid fire". Choose a fast, relaxed speaker talking about themselves.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where they talk about who they are or what they do.
2. Listen twice.
3. Speak along 5 times. Don't slow down. If you fall behind, skip ahead and catch up.
4. Last 3 minutes: find any "lemme", "gimme", "gonna" or "wanna" in the clip and speak along with those sentences 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Sixty-second answers

### Explanation

You will be asked "What do you do?" and "Where are you from?" hundreds of times in your life. If you build the answer from zero each time, you'll hesitate every time. If you have a ready answer, it comes out in a second, and the confidence of the first 10 seconds carries you through the rest of the conversation.

But don't memorise a **script** word for word. Scripts sound robotic, and if someone asks the question a slightly different way, the script breaks and you freeze. Instead, build a **skeleton**: 4 keywords you always use, with flexible chunks around them.

The shape:

1. **Headline (1 sentence):** the direct answer. "I'm a product designer."
2. **Two details:** where, how long, what exactly. "I work at a travel start-up. Basically, I design how the app works."
3. **Spice (1–2 sentences):** something a bit personal, funny or surprising: a mini story or an opinion. "Honestly, the best part is watching people use something I made. The worst part is when they can't find the button."
4. **Back to you:** a question that hands the conversation over. "What about you, what do you do?"

That's about 30–60 seconds. **Spice** is what makes you memorable instead of sounding like a CV. **Back to you** keeps the conversation going (you know this from [answer, add, ask back](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md)).

**Three lengths.** In real life, people don't always want 60 seconds. Practise each answer at three lengths:

- **10 seconds** (a quick chat in a lift): headline + back to you.
- **30 seconds** (normal small talk): headline + 1 detail + spice + back to you.
- **60 seconds** (when someone's really interested, or at an interview-style meetup): the full shape.

**The 8 questions you'll prepare this week:**

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. What do you do?
3. Where are you from?
4. What do you do for fun?
5. What brought you here? (to this city / company / event)
6. What are you working on these days?
7. What's a normal weekend like for you?
8. How long have you been learning English? / Your English is really good, where did you learn?

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So I'm originally from…, but I've been living in… for… | where you're from + where you live now | "So I'm originally from São Paulo, but I've been living in Toronto for about three years." |
| I work in (field) at (company type)… | the headline of your job | "I work in product design at a travel start-up." |
| Basically, I… | you explain your job simply | "Basically, I figure out how the app should work for people." |
| These days, I'm mostly working on… | what you're doing right now | "These days, I'm mostly working on our new booking flow." |
| In my free time, I'm really into… | your hobbies | "In my free time, I'm really into climbing." |
| I'm kind of a (homebody / foodie / night owl). | a quick description of your personality | "I'm kind of a homebody, honestly." |
| What brought me here was… | why you came to a place | "What brought me here was a job, actually." |
| Long story, but… | you skip a long background | "Long story, but I ended up here for my master's." |
| Most weekends, I… | your typical weekend | "Most weekends, I try to get out of the city." |
| The best part is… / The worst part is… | spice about your job or city | "The best part is the people. The worst part is the winters." |
| I've been here about (two years) now. | how long | "I've been here about two years now, and I love it." |
| What about you? / How about you, what do you do? | back to you | "Anyway, that's me. What about you?" |

### Examples

**Example 1: "What do you do?" at three lengths**

10 seconds:
> "I'm a product designer at a travel start-up. How about you?"

30 seconds:
> "I'm a product designer. I work at a travel start-up, so basically I design how the app works, like booking flights and stuff. Honestly, the best part is when people actually use something I made. What about you?"

60 seconds:
> "So I'm a product designer. I work at this travel start-up, it's like fifty people. Basically, I figure out how the app should work, like what happens when you book a flight, which buttons go where. These days I'm mostly working on the checkout, which is… let's say, a work in progress. Ha. The best part is testing with real users. Like, last week, this woman found a bug in like ten seconds that we'd missed for a month. The worst part is meetings. Anyway, what about you, what do you do?"

**Example 2: "Where are you from?"**

> "So I'm originally from Brazil, from São Paulo, but I've been living here for about three years now. Long story, but I came for a job and just… stayed. Honestly, the thing I miss most is the food. Like, real Brazilian barbecue. Have you ever tried it?"

**Example 3: "What do you do for fun?"**

> "Hmm, I'm kind of a homebody during the week, so mostly cooking and watching way too many shows. But most weekends, I try to get out of the city, like hiking or just driving somewhere random. Last month we found this tiny lake two hours away, totally empty. What about you, are you an outdoors person?"

**Example 4: "What brought you here?" at a meetup**

> "What brought me here? Honestly, I'm trying to practise my English more, and a friend told me about this group. Lemme see, it's my second time? Everyone's super nice. How about you, have you been coming for a while?"

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ I am working as a designer since three years. | ✅ I've been working as a designer for three years. | Present perfect continuous + "for" with a period of time. |
| ❌ I'm from Brazil, I live in Toronto. That's it. | ✅ I'm originally from Brazil, but I've been living in Toronto for three years. And… | Connect the facts and add spice. |
| ❌ (reciting a memorised script, word for word) | ✅ (keywords + flexible chunks) | Scripts sound robotic and break when the question changes. |
| ❌ My hobbies are reading, travelling and music. | ✅ In my free time, I'm really into… Like, last weekend I… | A list of hobbies sounds like a form. Pick one and give an example. |
| ❌ (answer, then silence) | ✅ (answer, then "What about you?") | Always hand back the conversation. |
| ❌ I work in a start-up. | ✅ I work at a start-up. / I work in tech. | "At" + company, "in" + field. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md). Tonight the rounds get shorter in a natural way: 3 minutes is an "interview" version, 1 minute is the real-life version.

**Prompt A: "So, tell me about yourself."**

**Rules for tonight:** use your skeleton from Exercise 1.1 (where you're from, what you do, what you're into, one spice), include one mini story (Week 5), one opinion (Week 6), and end with "What about you?".

1. **Plan (3 min).** Look at your skeleton for question 1. Then cover it.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** This is the "long coffee chat" version. Add more stories.
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Which part sounded most natural? Which part did you hesitate on?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** This is your real-life, every-day version. You should be able to say it tomorrow to anyone.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "What do you do for fun?"** 2 minutes, 1 minute, then the 30-second version and the 10-second version.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "I'm originally from…, but I've been living in… for…", "Basically, I…", "These days, I'm mostly working on…", "In my free time, I'm really into…", "What brought me here was…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory in a new sentence about yourself.
3. **Say 5 phrases from Week 7 from memory**, one from each day (a sequence chunk, a decision chunk, a comparison, a problem/solution chunk, a check).
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
