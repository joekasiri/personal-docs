**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Do [Day 1: Sixty-second answers](day-1-sixty-second-answers.md) first. Tonight is the opposite: very short answers, very fast.
- In Month 1 you learned grammatical short answers ("Yeah, I did.", "No, it isn't.") in [question tags and short answers](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-4-question-tags-and-short-answers.md). Tonight is about the **casual reaction words** natives use instead, plus a tiny add-on.
- Have a timer that shows seconds. You'll need it for the drill.
- Have a fast Q&A clip ready (search terms in section 1).

## Learning objectives

- Answer quick questions in under 10 seconds with natural short answers ("Yeah, totally", "Not really", "Kind of").
- Add a tiny add-on (3–8 words) so the answer isn't just one word.
- Use frequency answers naturally ("Every now and then", "All the time").
- Answer 30 rapid-fire questions in a row without freezing.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: "dunno" and "I dunno".** "I don't know" is often said as **"I dunno"**, and when it's very fast, almost **"I'unno"** with a falling-rising melody. It's casual, but extremely common. People use it to really say they don't know, *and* as a soft start: "I dunno, I feel like…" (a little like the hedges from [Week 6](../week-06-opinions/day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md)).

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. I DUNno. → "I DUN-no"
2. Dunno, MAYbe? → "DUN-no, MAY-be?"
3. I dunno, I feel like it's too EARly.
4. Yeah, TOtally. → "yeah, TOH-dally"
5. Not REALly. → "nod REE-lee" (flap T)
6. KIND of. → "KIND-a"
7. Oh, for SURE. → "oh, fer SHER"
8. Every now and THEN. → "ev-ry now 'n THEN"
9. Ha, I WISH. → "ha, I WISH"
10. NAH, not really. → "NAH, nod really"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "street interview quick questions", "rapid fire questions vlog", "this or that challenge couple" or "73 questions style interview casual". Choose short, fast answers.

1. Find a 40–60 second part with many quick questions and answers.
2. Listen twice.
3. Speak along 5 times, **only as the person answering**. Mute yourself for the questions.
4. Last 3 minutes: pause after each question and give **your own** quick answer before the speaker does.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Rapid-fire answers

### Explanation

Not every question needs a 60-second answer. Many questions in casual conversation are **quick**: "You coming tonight?", "Busy week?", "You like spicy food?". Learners often freeze on these because they try to build a full, correct sentence ("Yes, I like spicy food very much"). By the time it's ready, the moment has passed.

Natives answer quick questions with two pieces:

1. **A short answer word or chunk** (1–3 words): "Yeah, totally." / "Not really." / "Kind of."
2. **A tiny add-on** (3–8 words): "…as long as it's not too hot." / "…I'm wiped." / "…but I'm trying."

> "Busy week?" → "Big time. Three deadlines."
> "You like it here?" → "Yeah, totally. It's growing on me."

That's it. Under 10 seconds. If the other person wants more, they'll ask a follow-up.

**Not too short, not too long.** One word ("Yes.") can sound cold. Two minutes sounds like you didn't understand it was a quick question. Short answer + tiny add-on is the sweet spot.

**The answer bank:**

| Meaning | Short answers |
|---|---|
| **Strong yes** | Yeah, totally. · Oh, for sure. · Definitely. · Big time. · A hundred percent. |
| **Normal yes** | Yeah. · Pretty much. · I think so. · Yeah, kinda. |
| **Partly** | Kind of. · Sort of. · Yes and no. · Depends. · Ish. (very casual: "Ready?" "Ish.") |
| **Normal no** | Not really. · Nah, not really. · Not yet. · Not anymore. · Not much. |
| **Strong no** | Not at all. · No way. · Not a chance. · Never, actually. |
| **I wish / funny** | I wish! · Ha, guilty. · Don't remind me. · Who, me? |
| **How often** | All the time. · Pretty often. · Every now and then. · Once in a while. · Hardly ever. · Once or twice. |
| **Don't know** | I dunno. · No idea. · Good question. · Not sure, actually. |

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Yeah, totally. | strong, relaxed yes | "You coming Friday?" "Yeah, totally. Wouldn't miss it." |
| Not really. | soft no | "You into sports?" "Not really. I watch the World Cup, that's it." |
| Kind of. / Sort of. | partly yes | "Did you finish it?" "Kind of. Like, eighty percent." |
| Big time. | a strong yes, especially for something intense | "Tired?" "Big time." |
| Pretty much. | almost completely yes | "So you're done for the day?" "Pretty much." |
| Not yet. / Not anymore. | no for now / no, but it used to be yes | "You seen it?" "Not yet." "You still play guitar?" "Not anymore." |
| Every now and then. | sometimes | "You cook much?" "Every now and then." |
| All the time. | very often | "You use that app?" "All the time." |
| I wish! | you'd like it to be true, but it isn't | "Going on holiday soon?" "I wish!" |
| Ha, guilty. | you admit something a bit embarrassing | "Did you eat the last cookie?" "Ha, guilty." |
| Don't remind me. | the topic is painful or annoying | "Isn't your exam tomorrow?" "Don't remind me." |
| No idea. / I dunno. | you don't know | "What time does it start?" "No idea. I'll check." |

### Examples

**Example 1: a quick chat in the kitchen at work**

> A: Busy week?
> B: Big time. Two launches.
> A: Oof. You going to the team lunch?
> B: Yeah, totally. I need the break.
> A: Ha, same. You tried the new ramen place yet?
> B: Not yet. Is it good?
> A: Pretty good. Kinda pricey, though.

**Example 2: getting to know someone, fast**

> A: You like living here?
> B: Yeah, kinda. It's growing on me.
> A: You a coffee person?
> B: Oh, for sure. Like, three a day.
> A: Into hiking?
> B: Every now and then. Mostly in summer.
> A: You ever been to Mexico?
> B: Never, actually. It's on my list.

**Example 3: funny short answers**

> A: Did you go to the gym this week?
> B: Ha. Don't remind me.
> A: Did you stay up watching that show again?
> B: Guilty. Four episodes.
> A: Going anywhere for the holidays?
> B: I wish!

**Example 4: too short → just right**

> Too short:
> A: Do you like your job? B: Yes.
>
> Just right:
> A: Do you like your job? B: Yeah, most days. The people are great.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Yes. (and silence) | ✅ Yeah, totally. Wouldn't miss it. | One word can sound cold. Add 3–8 words. |
| ❌ Yes, I like spicy food very much because… (30 seconds) | ✅ Oh, for sure. The spicier the better. | A quick question wants a quick answer. |
| ❌ Not yet. I have not seen it. | ✅ Not yet. Is it good? | Don't repeat the full sentence. Add something new or ask back. |
| ❌ Sometimes yes, sometimes no. | ✅ Every now and then. / Depends. | Use the natural chunk. |
| ❌ I don't know. (flat, then silence) | ✅ I dunno, maybe? I'll check. | Add a tiny next step or guess. |
| ❌ Never ever I went to Mexico. | ✅ Never, actually. | Use the short chunk: no need for a full sentence. |

### Practise

Do these out loud, in order. Full content is in [this week's exercises](exercises.md#day-2-exercises).

1. **Exercise 2.1: Short answer + add-on** (15 min): answer 20 quick questions using the answer bank, under 10 seconds each.
2. **Exercise 2.2: How often?** (10 min): answer 12 "how often" questions with frequency chunks.
3. **Exercise 2.3: Just right** (15 min): fix 10 answers that are too short or too long, then say the right-size version.

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Tonight's drill has two parts: **30 rapid-fire questions**, then a **3/2/1** on the question you answered worst. See [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Part 1: rapid-fire (20 min).**

1. Record yourself. Read each question out loud (or have someone read them), then answer in **under 10 seconds** with a short answer + add-on.
2. If you take more than 10 seconds, mark the question with a ✗ and move on. Don't stop.
3. Do all 30. Then do them all again, faster, trying to get zero ✗.

1. You a morning person?
2. Busy week?
3. You like your job?
4. You ever been to Japan?
5. You cook much?
6. Coffee or tea?
7. You into sports?
8. Did you sleep OK?
9. You seen any good movies lately?
10. You still live in the same place?
11. You like the city?
12. Going anywhere this summer?
13. You play any instruments?
14. Do you work from home?
15. You good with directions?
16. Did you have a good weekend?
17. You a cat or a dog person?
18. You get nervous before presentations?
19. You use social media much?
20. Have you finished that project yet?
21. You read much?
22. Are you a planner?
23. You like spicy food?
24. Ever been on TV?
25. You go out much during the week?
26. You good at remembering names?
27. Is your family nearby?
28. You learn languages easily?
29. You coming to the thing on Friday?
30. You like surprises?

**Part 2: 3/2/1 (20 min).** Choose the question you found hardest (one with a ✗). Now give it the **long** version: 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute, recording each. Use your Day 1 shape (headline → details → spice → back to you).

**Listen (5 min).** Listen to your 1-minute round and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Also write down your number of ✗ from Part 1, round 2.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "Big time.", "Pretty much.", "Every now and then.", "I wish!", "Don't remind me."
2. **Cover the log.** Ask yourself 5 quick questions and answer with tonight's phrases from memory.
3. **Say Day 1's 5 phrases from memory**, as a 30-second "Tell me about yourself".
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers and your ✗ count in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
