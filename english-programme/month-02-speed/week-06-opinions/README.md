# Week 6: Opinions
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 2: Speed](../README.md) · [← Week 5](../week-05-better-stories/README.md) · [Week 7 →](../week-07-explaining/README.md)

## This week's goal

Stories are about the past. Opinions happen *right now*, and you can't rehearse them because you don't know what people will ask. That's why opinions are where many B2 speakers freeze: "What do you think about remote work?" and suddenly the brain is empty. This week you learn a small set of opinion chunks that start your answer immediately, so your brain can find the content while your mouth is already moving. You'll give opinions, agree, disagree without sounding rude, back up what you say with reasons and examples, and hedge when you're not sure. By Day 6 you should be able to answer "What do you think about…?" in under 3 seconds and keep talking for 30 seconds or more.

## How this builds on last week

Last week you learned to tell [better stories](../week-05-better-stories/README.md). This week, a short story becomes the **example** that supports your opinion: "I feel like open offices are terrible. Like, last week I'm trying to focus, and three people start a meeting right next to my desk…". In Month 1 you learned softeners such as "kinda", "not that…" and "a little bit" in [intensifiers and softeners](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-3-intensifiers-and-softeners.md). Those words make an opinion milder. This week is about the bigger chunks around them: how to *start*, *react to* and *support* an opinion.

## Day 7: light day

30 minutes only. Walk and listen to a relaxed opinion podcast, two or three people chatting about everyday topics (search "hot takes podcast" or "friends debate everyday topics"). Your one task: **every time a speaker disagrees, notice how they start**. Do they say "Yeah, but…", "I don't know…", "Really?" or something else? Note your favourite 3 in your phrase log when you get home. Then rest.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Start an opinion in under 3 seconds with a ready-made chunk ("Honestly, I feel like…").
- [ ] Show how strong your opinion is, from "I'm 100% sure" to "I'm kinda torn".
- [ ] Agree in different ways and add your own point instead of just saying "yes".
- [ ] Disagree politely, without sounding rude and without staying silent.
- [ ] Back up an opinion with a reason and a real example.
- [ ] Hedge when the honest answer is "it depends", and still say something useful.
- [ ] Keep talking about an opinion for 30–60 seconds.
