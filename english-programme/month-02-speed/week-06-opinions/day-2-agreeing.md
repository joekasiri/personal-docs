## Learning objectives

- Agree in 12+ natural ways instead of only "Yes, I agree".

- Agree **and add** your own point, so the conversation keeps going.
- Sound enthusiastic with strong stress on the agreement word.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: strong stress in agreement.** When people agree in casual speech, the agreement word is **loud, long and high**, then the voice falls: "TO-tally ↘", "I KNOW, right? ↗". Agreement that sounds flat ("yes… I agree…") sounds bored, even if you mean it. Tonight, exaggerate.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times, with energy.

1. TOtally. → "TOH-dally" (flap T)
2. I KNOW, right? (voice goes up on "right")
3. TELL me about it.
4. EXactly. That's what I'm SAYing.
5. Same HERE.
6. Me NEIther. → "me NEE-ther"
7. Oh, a hundred PERcent.
8. You're TELLing me. → "y'r TELLin' me"
9. That's SO true.
10. I couldn't agree MORE. → "I c'dn't agree MORE"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "friends chat podcast", "best friends podcast", "roommates podcast" or "couples podcast funny". Look for a clip where two people agree with and build on each other ("Yes! And also…").

1. Find a 30–40 second part with at least 3 agreements.
2. Listen twice.
3. Speak along 5 times, copying the energy of the agreement words.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the person who agrees, mute your voice for the other person.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Agreeing

### Explanation

Agreeing sounds easy, but many learners only have "Yes" and "I agree". Both are correct, but in casual conversation they can sound flat, and they **end the conversation**. If someone says "This weather is awful" and you say "Yes, I agree", there's nowhere to go.

Natural agreement has two parts:

1. **An agreement chunk**, said with energy: "Totally!", "I know, right?", "Tell me about it."
2. **An addition**: your own reason, example or a new point. "Totally. And it's supposed to rain all weekend too."

The addition is what keeps the conversation alive. It's the same idea as [answer, add, ask back](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md) from Month 1, but for opinions.

**Strength of agreement:**

| Strength | Chunks |
|---|---|
| **Very strong** | Couldn't agree more. · A hundred percent. · Exactly! · Absolutely. |
| **Strong, casual** | Totally. · I know, right? · For sure. · Oh, for real. |
| **Because you've lived it too** | Tell me about it. · You're telling me. · Same here. |
| **Softer** | Yeah, I'm with you on that. · That's fair. · Good point. |

**Me too or me neither?** Match the sentence:

- Positive: "I love this place." → "Me too." / "So do I."
- Negative: "I don't like mornings." → "Me neither." / "Neither do I." (not "Me too")
- With *can / am / have*: "I can't swim." → "Me neither." / "Neither can I." · "I'm exhausted." → "Me too." / "So am I."

"Me too" and "Me neither" are the most common in casual speech. "So do I" / "Neither do I" are a bit more careful, but also fine.

**Tell me about it / You're telling me** mean "I know exactly what you mean, I've experienced it too". They are agreement, not a request for information. Careful: if you say "Tell me about it" with a rising, curious voice, it sounds like "please tell me more". Say it with a falling, tired voice.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Totally. | quick, strong, casual agreement | "Totally. It's way too expensive." |
| I know, right? | you agree with something you also noticed | A: "This coffee is amazing." B: "I know, right?" |
| Exactly. / That's exactly it. | they said exactly what you think | "Exactly. That's what I've been saying." |
| Tell me about it. | you've experienced the same problem (falling voice) | A: "Mondays are brutal." B: "Tell me about it." |
| You're telling me. | the same, even stronger | A: "This project is a nightmare." B: "You're telling me. I've been on it for a year." |
| Same here. | you feel or do the same | "Same here. I can't work in silence." |
| Me too. / Me neither. | short agreement with "I…" statements | A: "I don't get it." B: "Me neither." |
| A hundred percent. | very strong agreement | "A hundred percent. Best decision we ever made." |
| Couldn't agree more. | very strong, a little more serious | "Couldn't agree more. We need more time." |
| For sure. / Oh, for real. | casual agreement, very common with younger speakers | "Oh, for real. That show is so good." |
| I'm with you on that. | calm agreement, often with something a bit controversial | "I'm with you on that. The old logo was better." |
| That's so true. | you agree and you think it's a smart point | "That's so true. I never thought about it that way." |

### Examples

**Example 1: agree and add**

> A: I feel like everyone's always on their phones at dinner now.
> B: Oh, totally. And it's not just kids. My dad's the worst. He reads the news through the whole meal.
> A: Ha! Mine too.

**Example 2: tell me about it**

> A: This commute is killing me.
> B: Tell me about it. I was on the train for an hour and a half this morning. The signal was down again.
> A: Ugh. Same.

**Example 3: negative agreement**

> A: I don't really like big parties.
> B: Me neither. I'd way rather have, like, four people over for dinner.
> A: Exactly. You can actually talk.

**Example 4: a work meeting, softer**

> A: Honestly, I think we should test it with users before we build anything.
> B: I'm with you on that. Last time we skipped testing, we had to redo the whole flow.
> C: A hundred percent. Let's book a few sessions next week.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ I am agree. | ✅ I agree. / Totally. | "Agree" is a verb: no "am". |
| ❌ A: "I don't like coffee." B: "Me too." | ✅ B: "Me neither." | Negative statement → "me neither". |
| ❌ Yes. (and silence) | ✅ Yeah, totally. And… (add something) | A bare "yes" stops the conversation. |
| ❌ Tell me about it? (rising, curious) | ✅ Tell me about it. ↘ (falling, tired) | With a rising voice, it sounds like you want more information. |
| ❌ I'm agree with you completely. | ✅ I completely agree. / Couldn't agree more. | No "am", and "completely" usually goes before "agree". |
| ❌ So I do. | ✅ So do I. | Word order: so + do + I. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: Your friend says, "Honestly, I think everyone should learn to cook."** Agree with them, then build on it: why you agree, a short story (Week 5 skills), and one new point they didn't say.

**Rules for tonight:** start with an agreement chunk in the first second, use at least **3 agreement chunks** and **2 opinion chunks** from Day 1, and add at least one new point.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords. Choose your story.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** Imagine your friend sometimes adds something. Agree again each time ("Exactly, and…").
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did your agreement sound enthusiastic or flat?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).**
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): A colleague says, "We have way too many meetings."** Agree ("Tell me about it…"), give an example from your week, and suggest one fix. 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "I know, right?", "Tell me about it.", "Me neither.", "I'm with you on that.", "A hundred percent."
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory as a reply to a statement you invent.
3. **Say Day 1's 5 opinion chunks from memory.** For each one, give an opinion, then agree with yourself using a new chunk. Example: "If you ask me, brunch is overrated." "Totally. And it's always so crowded."
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
