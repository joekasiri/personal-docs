## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: stressing the opinion word.** In an opinion, the speaker makes one word **strong**: louder, longer and a bit higher. Often it's the opinion verb, the word "really", the "not", or the person ("ME"). The rest is fast. Changing the stress changes the meaning: "I REALLY think so" (I'm sure) vs "I really THINK so" (I think so, but I'm not sure).

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each sentence 3 times. Capitals = stressed.

1. I REALLY don't think so.
2. Honestly, I feel like it's OVERrated. → "honestly, I feel like it's OH-ver-ray-ded" (flap T)
3. If you ask ME, it's a waste of MONey.
4. I'm ALL for it.
5. I'm not SOLD on it. → "I'm not SOLD on it" ("not" is quick, flap T: "nod")
6. Personally, I'd say the FIRST one.
7. The way I SEE it, it's just not worth it. → "the way I SEE it, it's jus' not WORTH it"
8. I'm kinda TORN, actually.
9. No, I DEFinitely think it's better.
10. For ME, it's all about the FOOD.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube or a podcast app for "hot takes podcast", "unpopular opinions chat", "friends debate podcast" or "food opinions debate". Choose 2–3 people chatting, not a news show.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where one person gives a strong opinion.
2. Listen twice. Which words are stressed?
3. Speak along 5 times, copying the stress exactly.
4. Last 3 minutes: pause after each opinion sentence and repeat just that sentence, 3 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Giving opinions

### Explanation

When someone asks "What do you think about…?", the hardest part is the **first two seconds**. If nothing comes out, you freeze, and freezing makes the next sentence even harder. The solution: always start with a **ready-made opinion chunk**. While you say "Honestly, I feel like…", your brain has time to choose the actual opinion.

Natives do this constantly. They also use the chunk to show **how strong** the opinion is. Think of it like a volume button:

| Strength | Chunks |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | I'm 100% (for / against)… · I'm all for… · No, I definitely think… · I'm a huge fan of… |
| **Medium** | Honestly, I think… · I feel like… · If you ask me… · The way I see it… · Personally, I'd say… |
| **Weak** | I'm pretty sure… · I kinda feel like… · I'm not sold on… |
| **Torn** | I'm kinda torn on… · I go back and forth on… |

Three simple rules:

1. **"I feel like" is a thinking phrase.** In American English, "I feel like it's too expensive" means "I think it's too expensive". It's not about emotions. It's the most common casual opinion chunk. (It's followed by a full sentence, not a noun: "I feel like it's…", not "I feel like this idea".)
2. **"Honestly" makes you sound relaxed and sincere.** "Honestly, I think it's overrated." It doesn't mean you were lying before.
3. **Give the opinion, then one quick reason.** Tonight, keep the reason short ("…because it's too expensive"). On [Day 4](day-4-giving-reasons-and-examples.md) you'll learn to build longer reasons and examples.

**"Hot take"** means a surprising or unpopular opinion. People use it as a fun warning: "OK, hot take: pizza is better cold."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I feel like… | the most common casual way to give an opinion | "I feel like people work way more from home." |
| Honestly, I think… | you want to sound relaxed and sincere | "Honestly, I think the first version was better." |
| If you ask me… | you give your opinion, maybe a bit strongly | "If you ask me, that meeting could've been an email." |
| The way I see it… | you explain your point of view | "The way I see it, it's just not worth the money." |
| Personally, I'd say… | you choose between options | "Personally, I'd say the beach. Way more relaxing." |
| For me, it's all about… | you say what matters most to you | "For me, it's all about the coffee." |
| I'm all for… | you strongly support something | "I'm all for a four-day week." |
| I'm not sold on… | you're not convinced yet | "I'm not sold on the new design." |
| I'm kinda torn (on that). | you can see both sides | "Living downtown? I'm kinda torn on that." |
| I'm pretty sure… | you think it's true but you're not 100% | "I'm pretty sure it's more expensive now." |
| No, I definitely think… | strong opinion, often after a question | "No, I definitely think it's a good idea." |
| OK, hot take: … | a surprising or unpopular opinion | "OK, hot take: I don't like brunch." |

### Examples

**Example 1: a quick question at work**

> A: What do you think of the new office layout?
> B: Honestly? I feel like it's way too open. You can't focus. If you ask me, they should've kept a few quiet rooms.
> A: Yeah, it's loud.

**Example 2: showing strength**

> A: Pineapple on pizza. Yes or no?
> B: Oh, I'm all for it. 100%.
> C: I'm kinda torn, honestly. Sometimes it's great, sometimes it's just… wet.
> D: Hot take: it's the best pizza. Fight me.

**Example 3: a medium opinion with a quick reason**

> A: Are you gonna get the new phone?
> B: I'm not sold on it, to be honest. The way I see it, it's basically the same as the old one, but like, three hundred bucks more.

**Example 4: personal preference**

> A: City break or beach holiday?
> B: Personally, I'd say city break. For me, it's all about the food and walking around. I get bored at the beach after like a day.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ In my opinion, I think that… | ✅ Honestly, I think… / In my opinion, … | "In my opinion" + "I think" says the same thing twice. |
| ❌ I feel like this idea. | ✅ I like this idea. / I feel like this idea is great. | "I feel like" needs a full sentence after it. |
| ❌ According to me, … | ✅ If you ask me, … / For me, … | "According to" is for other people and sources, not yourself. |
| ❌ For my opinion, … | ✅ In my opinion, … / For me, … | Wrong preposition. |
| ❌ I am agree / I am think… | ✅ I think… | "Think" is a normal verb: no "am". |
| ❌ (silence for 4 seconds) …I think it's good. | ✅ Honestly, I feel like… it's actually pretty good. | Start with a chunk immediately, then find the content. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Is remote work better than working in the office?"**

**Rules for tonight:** start within 3 seconds with an opinion chunk, use at least **4 different chunks** from tonight, show the strength of your opinion, and include one short story from your own work life (a Week 5 skill).

1. **Plan (3 min).** Decide your opinion and its strength. Write max 5 keywords.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** How many seconds before your first word? Which chunks did you use?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).** Use a chunk you didn't use in round 1.
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Opinion in the first sentence, story in the middle, opinion again at the end.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Opinion chunks count as connectors.

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "Are smartphones making people less social?"** 2 minutes, 1 minute, then a 30-second version that starts with "OK, hot take:".

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "I feel like…", "If you ask me…", "I'm all for…", "I'm not sold on…", "I'm kinda torn on that."
2. **Cover the log.** Say tonight's 5 chunks from memory, each with a different topic.
3. **Say 5 phrases from Week 5 from memory**, one from each day (a scene opener, a suspense phrase, a quote verb, an ending, a "show" phrase).
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
