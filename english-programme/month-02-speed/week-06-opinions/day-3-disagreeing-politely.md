# Week 6 · Day 3: Disagreeing politely

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Do [Day 1](day-1-giving-opinions.md) and [Day 2](day-2-agreeing.md) first. Tonight's disagreements often start with a small agreement.
- Have a clip of friends disagreeing in a friendly way (search terms in section 1).
- Think of 3 popular opinions you don't agree with. You'll use them in the drill.

## Learning objectives

- Disagree in 10+ polite ways, from soft to direct.
- Use the pattern: **acknowledge → but → your view → reason**.
- Use contrastive stress so your disagreement is clear but not aggressive.
- React calmly when someone disagrees with *you*.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: contrastive stress.** When you disagree or correct something, you stress the **two words that are different**: "It's not BAD, it's just EXPENSIVE." "I see what you MEAN, but…". This stress makes your point clear without needing strong words. The tone stays friendly: smile while you say it.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. I see what you MEAN, but… → "I see wha' chu MEAN, but"
2. It's not BAD, | it's just exPENsive.
3. I don't think it's the PRICE, | I think it's the QUALity.
4. Yeah, but it's not THAT simple. → "yeah, but it's not THAT simple"
5. I'm not so SURE about that.
6. Really? I always thought it was the OTHer way around.
7. I get THAT, | but what about the KIDS?
8. Not NECessarily.
9. Maybe for YOU, | but not for ME.
10. OK, let's agree to disaGREE. (smile)

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "friends debate podcast", "couple argues about food podcast", "would you rather debate" or "hot takes reaction podcast". Choose friendly disagreement, not angry arguments.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where someone disagrees.
2. Listen twice. How do they start? Where's the stress?
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the disagreement sentences, 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Disagreeing politely

### Explanation

Many learners stay quiet when they disagree, because they're worried about sounding rude in a foreign language. Or they say "No, you're wrong", which *is* too strong in most casual situations. Americans disagree all the time, but they **wrap** the disagreement in friendly language.

The pattern:

1. **Acknowledge** (show you heard them): "I see what you mean…", "Yeah, I get that…", "That's fair…"
2. **But**
3. **Your view**: "…I kinda see it differently." / "…I feel like it's the opposite."
4. **A reason** (short): "…because most people I know hate it."

> "I see what you mean, but I feel like it's the opposite. Most people I know work *better* from home."

**How soft or direct?**

| Level | Chunks | Use with… |
|---|---|---|
| **Very soft** | I'm not so sure about that. · Hmm, maybe, but… · I don't know, I feel like… | people you don't know well, managers, clients |
| **Medium** | I see what you mean, but… · I get that, but… · That's fair, but… · Not necessarily. | most everyday conversations |
| **Direct (friendly)** | Yeah, but… · Really? I always thought… · I kinda see it differently. | friends, colleagues you know |
| **Very direct (only with close friends, with a smile)** | No way. · Come on! · I couldn't disagree more. | close friends, joking |

**Questions are softer than statements.** "Don't you think it's a bit expensive?" is softer than "It's too expensive."

**When someone disagrees with you:** stay relaxed. "Fair enough." "OK, you've got a point." "Ha, OK, let's agree to disagree." You don't need to win. Keeping the conversation friendly *and* still saying what you think is the goal.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I see what you mean, but… | the standard polite disagreement | "I see what you mean, but I think it's too risky." |
| Yeah, but… | casual, quick disagreement with friends | "Yeah, but it's like twice the price." |
| I'm not so sure about that. | soft disagreement, especially with facts | "I'm not so sure about that. I read it's actually cheaper now." |
| I don't know, I feel like… | very soft, you're a bit unsure | "I don't know, I feel like people just want it simple." |
| I get that, but… | you understand their reason but still disagree | "I get that, but we can't keep delaying it." |
| That's fair, but… | you accept part of their point | "That's fair, but the old version was way faster." |
| Really? I always thought… | you're surprised and you believed the opposite | "Really? I always thought New York was more expensive." |
| I kinda see it differently. | calm, clear disagreement | "I kinda see it differently. For me, it's about the people." |
| Not necessarily. | you disagree with a general rule | A: "Expensive means better." B: "Not necessarily." |
| Don't you think…? | you disagree with a question (very soft) | "Don't you think it's a bit early to decide?" |
| Maybe for you, but… | the opinion depends on the person | "Maybe for you, but I can't sleep with the window open." |
| Let's agree to disagree. | you end the disagreement in a friendly way | "OK, OK, let's agree to disagree." |

### Examples

**Example 1: friends, casual**

> A: Honestly, I think cats are way better than dogs.
> B: Yeah, but cats don't even care about you!
> A: That's not true! Mine sleeps on my head every night.
> B: OK, fair, but that's because your head is warm.
> A: Ha! Let's agree to disagree.

**Example 2: at work, soft**

> A: I feel like we should launch on Monday.
> B: I see what you mean, but don't you think it's a bit risky? We haven't tested the payment flow yet.
> A: Hmm, that's fair.
> B: Maybe we could do Wednesday? That gives us two days.

**Example 3: a surprising fact**

> A: Coffee's really bad for you.
> B: Really? I always thought it was kinda good for you, in small amounts. I'm not so sure about that one.
> A: Hmm, maybe I'm thinking of energy drinks.

**Example 4: someone disagrees with you**

> A: If you ask me, the city's way better than the suburbs.
> B: I kinda see it differently. I grew up in the suburbs, and it's so much calmer. And you get a yard.
> A: OK, you've got a point about the yard. But the commute, though!
> B: Ha, fair enough.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ No, you're wrong. | ✅ Hmm, I'm not so sure about that. | Too strong for most situations. Wrap it. |
| ❌ I don't agree with you. (flat, then silence) | ✅ I see what you mean, but I feel like… | Acknowledge, then give your view and a reason. |
| ❌ I am disagree. | ✅ I disagree. / I don't agree. | "Disagree" is a verb: no "am". |
| ❌ I see what do you mean. | ✅ I see what you mean. | Inside a sentence, normal word order (the [indirect question rule](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-3-indirect-questions.md)). |
| ❌ Yes, but no. | ✅ Yeah, but… / Kind of, but… | "Yes, but no" is confusing; give the "but" part clearly. |
| ❌ (silent nodding while disagreeing inside) | ✅ "That's fair, but…" | Staying silent is the habit we're fixing. Say *something*. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: Someone says, "Everyone should go to university."** Disagree politely (even if you partly agree): acknowledge their point, give your view, a reason, and an example or short story.

**Rules for tonight:** start with an acknowledgement in the first second, use at least **3 disagreement chunks**, ask one "Don't you think…?" question, and end in a friendly way ("Fair enough…", "Let's agree to disagree").

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** Imagine the other person pushes back twice. Respond each time.
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you sound friendly? Was the contrastive stress clear?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).**
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): A colleague says, "Working from cafés is the best way to work."** Disagree softly ("I don't know, I feel like…"), with an example. 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "I see what you mean, but…", "I'm not so sure about that.", "Really? I always thought…", "Not necessarily.", "Let's agree to disagree."
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory as a reply to an opinion you invent.
3. **Say Day 2's 5 agreement phrases from memory.** Then, for practice, say the *opposite*: agree with one statement, disagree with the next.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

## Expected outcome

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] Disagree in at least 8 polite ways without looking.
- [ ] Use acknowledge → but → view → reason.
- [ ] Choose a softer or more direct chunk depending on who you're talking to.
- [ ] Stay friendly when someone disagrees with you.
