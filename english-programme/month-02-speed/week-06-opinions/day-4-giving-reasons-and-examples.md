## Learning objectives

- Support every opinion with a reason **and** a real example.
- Use the shape **Opinion → Reason → Example → Opinion again (O-R-E-O)**.
- Use 10+ chunks to introduce reasons and examples.
- Debate 3 topics with Claude for 30 minutes.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: stress on the example.** When people give an example, they often slow down a little and put strong stress on the **new thing**: "Like, take my MOM…", "For ONE thing…", "PLUS, it's way cheaper." The chunk before it ("like, take") is fast and small.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. Like, take my MOM, for example. → "like, take my MOM, f'r example"
2. For ONE thing, it's way too EXpensive. → "f'r WUN thing"
3. And PLUS, nobody even USES it.
4. The MAIN reason is TIME.
5. I mean, LOOK at New YORK.
6. Like when you're on the PHONE, and…
7. Case in POINT: | my BROther.
8. That's WHY I stopped going.
9. The THING is, | it never WORKS. → "the THING is, it never WORKS"
10. Just the other DAY, | I was trying to…

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "debate podcast casual", "friends argue podcast", "two hosts discuss everyday topics" or "should you rent or buy podcast". Choose a speaker who gives examples from their own life.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where someone gives a reason and an example.
2. Listen twice. Mark where they switch from opinion to example.
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the example part, copying the slow-down and stress.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Giving reasons and examples

### Explanation

An opinion without a reason sounds empty: "I think cities are better." OK… why? And a reason without an example sounds like a textbook: "Because there are more opportunities." The **example** is what makes people listen, and it's also what makes *you* fluent, because examples come from your life and you don't have to invent them.

Use the **O-R-E-O** shape:

1. **O**pinion: "Honestly, I feel like cities are better."
2. **R**eason: "The main reason is, there's just so much more to do."
3. **E**xample: "Like, take last weekend. I went to a food market, a free concert and a museum, and I spent like twenty bucks."
4. **O**pinion again (short): "So yeah, for me, cities win."

That's about 30 seconds of talking, from one simple opinion.

**Examples can be:**

- **a person:** "Take my brother, for example…"
- **a moment:** "Just the other day…" / "Like when…"
- **a place or thing:** "I mean, look at Tokyo…"
- **a mini story** (Week 5 skills!): "So last month, I'm at the airport…"

**Two or more reasons?** Use "For one thing… And on top of that…" or "Plus…". Don't list five reasons. Two is perfect for casual talk.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The main reason is… | you give your most important reason | "The main reason is, it's just way cheaper." |
| For one thing… | you start the first of two reasons | "For one thing, the commute is terrible." |
| And on top of that… / Plus… | you add a second reason | "Plus, you can't bring your dog." |
| The thing is… | you introduce the key reason or problem | "The thing is, nobody reads the long emails." |
| Like, for example… | the most common way to start an example | "Like, for example, my last apartment had no windows in the kitchen." |
| Take (my sister / Paris), for example. | your example is a person or a place | "Take my sister, for example. She's never had a car." |
| I mean, look at… | you point to something everyone knows | "I mean, look at how much rent costs now." |
| Like when… | your example is a typical situation | "Like when you're tired and you just want to order food." |
| Just the other day… | your example is a recent story | "Just the other day, I waited forty minutes for a bus." |
| Case in point: … | your example proves the point (a bit dramatic, often funny) | "People never read instructions. Case in point: me, yesterday, with the new desk." |
| That's why… / Which is why… | you connect the reason back to your opinion | "Which is why I always book the early flight." |
| Because otherwise… | you show what happens without your idea | "You need a plan, because otherwise you spend all day deciding." |

### Examples

**Example 1: O-R-E-O**

> A: Do you think it's worth buying a car in the city?
> B: Honestly? No. The main reason is, you just don't need one. Like, take me, for example. I sold my car last year, and I use the subway and bikes and the occasional taxi. And I'm saving like four hundred bucks a month. So yeah, I'd say skip the car.

**Example 2: two reasons + a quick story**

> A: Why don't you like open-plan offices?
> B: For one thing, it's so loud. And on top of that, there's no privacy. Just the other day, I'm on a call with a client, and my coworker behind me starts eating chips. Like, loud chips. The client goes, "Is it raining there?" That's why I always book a meeting room now.

**Example 3: disagreeing with a reason and example (Day 3 + tonight)**

> A: I feel like everyone should have a side hustle.
> B: I see what you mean, but I'm not so sure. The thing is, people are already super tired. I mean, look at my friends. Most of them work like fifty hours a week. Because otherwise they can't pay rent. Adding a side hustle on top of that? That's how you burn out.

**Example 4: "case in point"**

> "People never read the instructions. Case in point: I spent three hours building a bookshelf last night, and I found the instructions in the box afterwards. Under the screws."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ For example my brother he never cooks. | ✅ Take my brother, for example. He never cooks. | "Take X, for example" sounds natural. Don't double the subject ("my brother he"). |
| ❌ Because it's cheaper. (as a full answer) | ✅ Mainly because it's cheaper. Like, … | "Because…" alone sounds unfinished in conversation. Add an example. |
| ❌ The main reason is because… | ✅ The main reason is (that)… | "Reason is because" says "because" twice. (Natives say it too, but avoid it.) |
| ❌ For instance, like, for example… | ✅ Like, for example… | One example signal is enough. |
| ❌ Firstly… secondly… thirdly… fourthly… | ✅ For one thing… And plus… | Numbered lists sound like a presentation, not a chat. |
| ❌ (giving a general fact) "Many people think…" | ✅ (giving your own example) "Like, my roommate…" | Personal examples are faster to find and more interesting. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): voice mode with Claude

Tonight you **debate 3 topics** with Claude. Claude takes the opposite side. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**The 3 topics:**

1. Is it better to rent or to buy a home?
2. Should phones be banned in schools?
3. Is it better to work at a big company or at a start-up?

**Steps:**

1. **Set up (5 min).** Headphones on, notebook open. Start voice mode and paste or say this prompt:

```
I'm a B2 English learner practising casual opinions and debate. Let's debate 3 topics, one at a time, about 8 minutes each:
1. Is it better to rent or to buy a home?
2. Should phones be banned in schools?
3. Is it better to work at a big company or at a start-up?
For each topic: ask me my opinion, then take the opposite side, even if you don't agree. Push back politely 3 or 4 times, like a friend at dinner, not like a TV debate. Keep your turns short (2-4 sentences) so I talk more than you. If I only give a reason without an example, ask me "Like what?" or "Do you have an example?".
After each topic, give me quick spoken feedback: up to 3 mistakes with the natural American version, and tell me if I used opinion chunks, polite disagreement and real examples. Then move to the next topic.
Use casual American English.
```

2. **Topic 1 (8 min), Topic 2 (8 min), Topic 3 (8 min).** Use O-R-E-O in every answer. Agree with Claude at least once per topic ("OK, that's fair, but…").
3. **Switch sides (6 min).** Ask Claude: "Now let's switch: I'll argue the side I don't agree with for topic 3." This is hard, and that's the point.
4. **Write corrections (10 min).** Write each correction as *what I said → better version*. Say each better version 3 times.

**Focus tonight:** never answer with only a reason. Every time, add "Like, for example…" or "Take…". If you can't think of a real example, invent one. Nobody's checking.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): 3 from tonight ("The main reason is…", "Take X, for example", "Case in point") and 2 corrections from Claude.
2. **Cover the log.** Say each correction's better version from memory in a new sentence.
3. **Say Day 3's 5 disagreement phrases from memory**, each followed by a reason and an example.
4. Write in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) how many corrections you got and which topic was hardest.
