# Week 6 exercises: Opinions
[Week 6 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-giving-opinions.md) · [Day 2](day-2-agreeing.md) · [Day 3](day-3-disagreeing-politely.md) · [Day 4](day-4-giving-reasons-and-examples.md) · [Day 5](day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** everything is **out loud**. You don't need to have a real opinion on every topic: if you don't care about something, invent an opinion and defend it. That's still great practice. Record when the exercise says so. Model answers are in each day's answer key. They're examples, not the only right answers.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Opinion starters 5 times
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each chunk, give **5 different opinions**, one for each topic in the row. One sentence each.
2. Start each sentence immediately. Don't think first.

| Chunk | Topics |
|---|---|
| I feel like… | coffee · your city · weekends · email · winter |
| Honestly, I think… | social media · your job · fast food · cars · reality TV |
| If you ask me… | meetings · gyms · weddings · phones · public transport |
| The way I see it… | working late · renting · online shopping · tipping · school uniforms |
| Personally, I'd say… | cats or dogs · tea or coffee · summer or winter · books or films · early or late |
| I'm all for… | four-day weeks · working from home · free museums · bike lanes · naps |
| I'm not sold on… | smart watches · open offices · electric scooters · brunch · AI art |
| OK, hot take: … | breakfast · holidays · a famous food · a famous city · a popular app |

### Exercise 1.2: Turn up the volume
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each topic, give your opinion **three times**: weak, medium and strong. Use a different chunk each time (see the strength table in the [Day 1 lesson](day-1-giving-opinions.md)).
2. Add a 3–5 word reason to each.
3. Then say which one is your *real* opinion: "OK, but honestly, the real me is the medium one."

1. Pineapple on pizza
2. Working from home every day
3. Living in a big city
4. Video calls with the camera on
5. Getting up at 5 a.m.
6. Learning a language with apps only
7. Going to the cinema (instead of streaming)
8. Having a smartphone before age 12
9. Open-plan offices
10. Surprise parties

### Exercise 1.3: 3-second rule
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, timed

1. Read a question, start a timer, and **start talking within 3 seconds** with an opinion chunk.
2. Talk for about 15–20 seconds: opinion + quick reason.
3. If you miss the 3 seconds, start again with the same question.
4. Do all 25. If you finish early, do the ones you found hardest again.

1. What do you think of your city's public transport?
2. Is breakfast the most important meal of the day?
3. What's the best season of the year?
4. Are video games a waste of time?
5. What do you think about tipping?
6. Is it better to have a lot of friends or a few close friends?
7. What do you think of your phone?
8. Should people work four days a week?
9. Are online reviews useful?
10. Is it OK to wear sneakers to a wedding?
11. What do you think of working in cafés?
12. Is it better to travel alone or with friends?
13. Are e-books better than paper books?
14. What do you think about dating apps?
15. Is it worth paying more for organic food?
16. What do you think of the last film you saw?
17. Should everyone learn to drive?
18. Is it rude to look at your phone during dinner?
19. What's the most overrated food?
20. What do you think about your company's office (or your school)?
21. Is it better to rent or buy?
22. Are dogs better than cats?
23. Is it worth going to university?
24. What do you think about voice messages?
25. Is it better to be early or exactly on time?

### Answer key

**Exercise 1.2 (model answers, topic 1: pineapple on pizza):**
- Weak: "I'm not sold on it, honestly. It's kinda sweet."
- Medium: "I feel like it's actually pretty good. The salty-sweet thing works."
- Strong: "I'm all for it. Best topping, 100%."

**Exercise 1.3 (model answers):**
- Q1: "Honestly, I feel like it's pretty good. The subway's fast, but the buses are always late."
- Q4: "If you ask me, no, not at all. It depends how much you play, but they're great for relaxing."
- Q19: "OK, hot take: avocado toast. It's literally bread and a vegetable, for fifteen bucks."
- Q25: "Personally, I'd say early. Like, five minutes early. Exactly on time always feels late to me."

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Agree and add
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Agree with each statement using a **different** chunk each time (Totally / I know, right? / Tell me about it / Exactly / Same here / A hundred percent / Couldn't agree more / For sure / I'm with you on that / That's so true / You're telling me / Me too or Me neither).
2. Then **add** one thing: a reason, an example or a new point.
3. Say each answer twice: the second time faster and with more energy.

1. "This weather is terrible."
2. "I don't think I could live without my phone."
3. "Mondays are the worst."
4. "That new café downstairs is amazing."
5. "Flights are so expensive now."
6. "I feel like nobody answers their phone anymore."
7. "Our team needs fewer meetings."
8. "I really don't like crowded places."
9. "Honestly, the first season of that show was the best."
10. "It's so hard to find a good apartment here."
11. "I think everyone should take a real holiday every year."
12. "I can't work with music on."

### Exercise 2.2: Me too or me neither?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Respond to each statement with **Me too** or **Me neither**, then add 3–5 words.
2. Second round: respond with **So do / am / can / have I** or **Neither do / am / can / have I**.

1. I love spicy food.
2. I don't drink coffee.
3. I'm so tired today.
4. I can't swim very well.
5. I've never been to Canada.
6. I hate waiting in line.
7. I'm not a morning person.
8. I have two brothers.
9. I don't really watch TV.
10. I can speak a little Spanish.
11. I've been really busy this week.
12. I didn't sleep well last night.

### Exercise 2.3: Agreement chains
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** gap-fill, then perform both parts, then improvise

1. Fill each gap with an agreement chunk that fits (more than one answer is possible).
2. Read each dialogue out loud, both parts, with strong stress on the agreement.
3. Then continue each dialogue for 3 more lines, improvising, still agreeing and adding.

**Dialogue 1: at a café**
> A: This place is so good. I feel like they make the best coffee in the city.
> B: (1) ______. And the pastries! Have you tried the cinnamon one?
> A: Oh my god, yes. I come here like three times a week.
> B: (2) ______. I'm basically paying their rent at this point.

**Dialogue 2: after a long meeting**
> A: That meeting could've been an email.
> B: (3) ______. Two hours, and we decided nothing.
> A: And I don't think anyone even read the doc before.
> B: (4) ______. I'm pretty sure I was the only one.
> A: We need, like, a "no doc, no meeting" rule.
> B: (5) ______. I'd vote for that.

**Dialogue 3: planning a trip**
> A: I don't wanna do another trip where we're running around all day.
> B: (6) ______. Last time I needed a holiday after the holiday.
> A: Right? I feel like we should just pick one place and relax.
> B: (7) ______. Maybe a beach town? Somewhere small.

### Answer key

**Exercise 2.1 (model answers):**
1. "Tell me about it. It's been raining for like a week straight."
2. "Me neither. I use it for literally everything, maps, work, banking."
3. "Ugh, you're telling me. I had four meetings before lunch today."
4. "I know, right? Their croissants are crazy good."
5. "Totally. I looked at flights to Chicago and it was like six hundred bucks."
6. "That's so true. Everyone just texts now."
7. "A hundred percent. Half of them could be a quick message."
8. "Same here. I'd rather stay home than go to a packed bar."
9. "Exactly. After that it just got kinda weird."
10. "Tell me about it. I looked at twelve places before I found mine."
11. "Couldn't agree more. Like, really switching off, no laptop."
12. "Me neither. I need total silence, or I can't think."

**Exercise 2.2:**
Round 1: 1 Me too · 2 Me neither · 3 Me too · 4 Me neither · 5 Me neither · 6 Me too · 7 Me neither · 8 Me too · 9 Me neither · 10 Me too · 11 Me too · 12 Me neither.
Round 2: 1 So do I · 2 Neither do I · 3 So am I · 4 Neither can I · 5 Neither have I · 6 So do I · 7 Neither am I · 8 So do I · 9 Neither do I · 10 So can I · 11 So have I · 12 Neither did I.

**Exercise 2.3 (possible answers):** (1) I know, right? (2) Same here. / Me too. (3) Tell me about it. / You're telling me. (4) Exactly. / That's so true. (5) A hundred percent. / I'm with you on that. (6) Me neither. / Same here. (7) Totally. / For sure.

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Soften it
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each disagreement below is too direct for most situations. Make it polite using **acknowledge → but → view (→ reason)**.
2. Say your version out loud with contrastive stress.

1. "No. That's wrong."
2. "That's a bad idea."
3. "You're wrong, London isn't more expensive than New York."
4. "No, the old design was better."
5. "That's not true. People do read emails."
6. "No, we shouldn't launch on Friday."
7. "That's stupid. Cats are better."
8. "No. It's too expensive."
9. "You don't understand. It's more complicated."
10. "I don't agree. Remote work is better."

### Exercise 3.2: Yes, but…
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last 4

1. Disagree politely with each opinion. Use a **different** chunk each time.
2. Give a reason. For the last 4, add a quick example too.
3. Record the last 4 and listen: do you sound friendly?

1. "Summer is the best season, no question."
2. "Social media is just bad for everyone."
3. "Expensive restaurants are always better."
4. "You should always take the job that pays the most."
5. "Reading the news every day is important."
6. "Kids today are lazy."
7. "Working out at home is just as good as going to the gym."
8. "It's rude not to answer a message for a day."
9. "Big cities are too stressful to live in."
10. "Everyone should have a five-year plan."
11. "You can't learn a language after thirty."
12. "Tipping should be banned."

### Exercise 3.3: Disagreement ping-pong
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** perform both parts, then improvise

1. Read each script, both parts, out loud. Fill the gaps with disagreement chunks.
2. Then improvise 4 more lines: keep disagreeing politely, and end in a friendly way ("Fair enough", "Let's agree to disagree").

**Script 1: Friends choosing a restaurant**
> A: Let's go to that burger place again. It's the best in town.
> B: (1) ______, but we went there last week. Don't you wanna try something new?
> A: Why? We know it's good.
> B: (2) ______, but there's that new Thai place everyone's talking about.
> A: Thai food's always too spicy.
> B: (3) ______. You can ask for mild.

**Script 2: Colleagues and a deadline**
> A: I think we should just ship it on Friday. It's ready.
> B: (4) ______. We haven't tested it on older phones.
> A: Nobody uses older phones.
> B: (5) ______. Like half our users in some countries do.
> A: OK, but the client wants it Friday.
> B: (6) ______ we ask them for two more days?

**Script 3: Roommates and the heating**
> A: It's freezing. Can we turn the heating up?
> B: (7) ______. It's like 70 degrees in here.
> A: That's cold!
> B: (8) ______, I'm wearing a T-shirt.

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.1 (model answers):**
1. "Hmm, I'm not so sure about that."
2. "I see what you mean, but I'm not sure it'll work. It feels kinda risky."
3. "Really? I always thought New York was more expensive."
4. "I get that, but I kinda see it differently. I feel like the new one's clearer."
5. "I'm not so sure. I feel like *some* people read them, at least the short ones."
6. "That's fair, but don't you think Friday's a bit risky? If something breaks, nobody's around."
7. "Ha, I see what you mean, but I'm totally team cat."
8. "I get it, but it's a little out of my budget."
9. "Yeah, I don't know, I feel like it's not that simple."
10. "I see what you mean, but for me, remote work is actually better. I focus way more."

**Exercise 3.2 (model answers, selected):**
1. "Yeah, but it's so hot! I feel like fall is way better."
3. "Not necessarily. Some of the best food I've had was from a food truck."
6. "I'm not so sure about that. I feel like kids today are just busy in different ways."
9. "Maybe for some people, but I love it. Like, take my neighbourhood: I can walk everywhere, and there's always something going on."
11. "Really? I always thought that was a myth. My mom started English at forty-five, and she's pretty fluent now."

**Exercise 3.3 (possible answers):** (1) I see what you mean / Yeah (2) I get that / That's fair (3) Not necessarily (4) I'm not so sure about that (5) I don't know, I feel like that's not true / Really? I always thought… (6) Don't you think / Maybe (7) Really? I always thought it was warm / I'm not so sure about that (8) Maybe for you, but…

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Add a reason and an example
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each line below is an opinion. Build it into an **O-R-E-O** answer: Opinion → Reason (with a chunk) → Example (with a chunk) → Opinion again.
2. About 20–30 seconds each. Use your real life for examples, or invent them.

1. "Honestly, I think everyone should learn to cook."
2. "If you ask me, phones should stay out of the bedroom."
3. "I feel like small companies are more fun to work for."
4. "I'm not sold on smart home devices."
5. "Personally, I'd say trains are better than planes for short trips."
6. "I'm all for working from home two days a week."
7. "I feel like people don't take enough breaks at work."
8. "Honestly, I think live music is always better than recorded music."
9. "If you ask me, the best way to see a city is on foot."
10. "I feel like most apps send way too many notifications."

### Exercise 4.2: Example in 5 seconds
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. For each reason, give a personal example within **5 seconds**, starting with a chunk (*Like, for example… · Take my… · Just the other day… · Like when… · I mean, look at… · Case in point:*).
2. Keep it to 1–2 sentences. Then do the whole list again with different examples.

1. "…because the rent here is crazy."
2. "…because people never read long messages."
3. "…because it's way too loud."
4. "…because nobody plans ahead."
5. "…because the instructions are always confusing."
6. "…because the food is incredible."
7. "…because traffic is a nightmare."
8. "…because kids learn so fast."
9. "…because good customer service is really rare."
10. "…because everything takes longer than you think."

### Exercise 4.3: 45-second O-R-E-O
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** timed out loud, record the last two

1. Choose your opinion on each topic and give a **45-second** answer (use a timer).
2. Include two reasons ("For one thing… Plus…") and one example. Finish with "That's why…" or "So yeah, for me…".
3. Record topics 5 and 6. Listen for umms between the reason and the example.

1. Is it better to have a car or not in a big city?
2. Should people be allowed to bring dogs to the office?
3. Is it better to study in the morning or at night?
4. Are holidays better at home or abroad?
5. Should meetings have a maximum length of 30 minutes?
6. Is it worth paying for a streaming service?

### Answer key

**Exercise 4.1 (model answer, 1):**
> "Honestly, I think everyone should learn to cook. The main reason is, it saves you so much money. Like, take my roommate. He used to order food every night, and he was spending like six hundred bucks a month. Now he cooks three times a week and he's saving a ton. So yeah, everyone should learn at least a few meals."

**Exercise 4.1 (model answer, 10):**
> "I feel like most apps send way too many notifications. For one thing, it's distracting. And on top of that, half of them aren't even useful. Case in point: my pizza app sent me four notifications yesterday. I didn't order pizza. That's why I turn them all off now."

**Exercise 4.2 (model answers):**
1. "Like, for example, my one-bedroom costs more than my parents' whole house payment."
2. "Just the other day, I wrote a super detailed message, and my coworker replied, 'So what time?'"
5. "Case in point: I built a desk last weekend and I had three screws left over."
9. "Like when you call your bank and wait forty minutes just to talk to a robot."

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Depends on what?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each question, say **"I guess it depends on…"** + what it depends on.
2. Then give **two cases**: "If…, then… But if…, then…".
3. Don't give your lean yet. That's Exercise 5.2.

1. Is it better to live near your work?
2. Should you tell a friend if you don't like their partner?
3. Is it worth buying expensive shoes?
4. Is it a good idea to work with friends?
5. Should kids have a phone?
6. Is it better to take a gap year?
7. Are long holidays better than short ones?
8. Is it OK to cancel plans last minute?
9. Should you always say yes to your boss?
10. Is it better to rent furniture or buy it?

### Exercise 5.2: The full hedge
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record 3

1. Answer each question with the full pattern: **hedge → depends on → case 1 → case 2 → your lean**.
2. Use a different hedge each time (I guess it depends · Yes and no · In a way · It's not that simple · Depends who you ask · I'm on the fence · It's hard to say · I could go either way).
3. Each answer about 30–40 seconds. Record any 3 and check you gave your lean.

1. Is remote work good for your career?
2. Is it better to have kids young or later?
3. Is AI good or bad for designers?
4. Is it worth moving to another country for a job?
5. Is it better to be a specialist or to know a bit about everything?
6. Are big weddings worth the money?
7. Should you follow your passion or follow the money?
8. Is it better to live in the same city as your family?

### Exercise 5.3: Quick hedges
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** rapid out loud, under 10 seconds each

1. Answer each small-talk question with a **short hedge** (Kind of, yeah · Sort of · Yes and no · In a way · Not really, but… · Probably? · I could go either way) + **one detail**.
2. Under 10 seconds per answer. Go through the list twice.

1. Do you like your job?
2. Are you a morning person?
3. Do you like spicy food?
4. Are you good at cooking?
5. Do you like living here?
6. Are you into sports?
7. Do you enjoy flying?
8. Are you a planner?
9. Do you like parties?
10. Is your English better now?
11. Do you like your neighbourhood?
12. Are you a dog person?
13. Do you watch a lot of TV?
14. Do you like your commute?
15. Are you competitive?
16. Do you like surprises?
17. Is your family close?
18. Do you like shopping?
19. Are you busy this week?
20. Do you miss your hometown?

### Answer key

**Exercise 5.1 (model answers):**
1. "I guess it depends on the city. If it's a big city with bad traffic, then yeah, for sure. But if it's a small town, it doesn't really matter."
5. "I guess it depends on the age. If they're like sixteen, then yeah, sure. But if they're seven, I'm not so sure."
8. "I guess it depends on why. If you're sick, then of course. But if you just don't feel like going, that's kinda rude."

**Exercise 5.2 (model answer, 3):**
> "Hmm, yes and no. I guess it depends on how you use it. If you use it to do boring stuff faster, like resizing images or writing first drafts, then yeah, it's amazing. But if companies think they can replace designers with it, then that's a problem, because it doesn't really understand users. Personally, I lean towards good. It's a tool, like any other."

**Exercise 5.3 (model answers):**
1. "Kind of, yeah. The work's fun, the meetings not so much."
2. "Not really, but I'm getting better."
5. "Yes and no. I love the food, but the winters are rough."
8. "Sort of. I plan trips, but not weekends."
16. "Depends on the surprise! Cake, yes. A surprise party, no."
20. "In a way. I miss the food more than the place."

## Day 6 materials

### Re-record test prompt

> **"What's something most people love that you don't really like? Why?"**

Unprepared. Start within 5 seconds. Record exactly 2 minutes.

### Opinion checklist (tick while you listen)

| ✔ | Did I… |
|---|---|
| | start with an opinion chunk within 3 seconds? |
| | show how strong my opinion is? |
| | give at least two reasons ("For one thing… Plus…")? |
| | give at least one personal example ("Like, take my…")? |
| | admit the other side somewhere ("I get why people love it, but…")? |
| | use a hedge where it was honestly complex? |
| | finish with my opinion again ("So yeah, for me…")? |
| | stress the key words? |

Score: count the ticks (out of 8). Write it in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
