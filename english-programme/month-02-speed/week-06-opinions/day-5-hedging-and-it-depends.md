## Learning objectives

- Hedge naturally when you can't give a simple yes or no.
- Always say **what** it depends on, and give both cases.
- Use the pattern: **hedge → depends on → case 1 → case 2 → your lean**.
- Use the fall-rise melody that tells the listener "there's a 'but' coming".

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: the fall-rise melody.** When a speaker hedges, their voice often goes **down and then up** on the key word: "I GUESS… (↘↗)", "KIND of… (↘↗)", "In a WAY… (↘↗)". This melody means "yes, but…" or "not completely". It tells the listener to wait for more. A flat "yes" (↘) sounds finished.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times. `↘↗` = fall-rise.

1. I GUESS ↘↗ it depends. → "I GUESS it d'pends"
2. KIND of, ↘↗ yeah. → "KINDa, yeah"
3. YES and no. ↘↗
4. In a WAY, ↘↗ but…
5. It's hard to SAY. ↘↗
6. I could go EIther way. ↘↗
7. Depends who you ASK. ↘↗
8. I'm kinda on the FENCE. ↘↗
9. Not REALly, ↘↗ but…
10. PROBably? ↘↗ I mean, if it's CHEAP…

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "street interview would you rather", "casual interview podcast questions", "two friends answer questions podcast" or "Q and A vlog". Look for people answering questions where they think for a second and say "it depends" or "kind of".

1. Find a 30–40 second answer with a hedge.
2. Listen twice and notice the melody.
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along only with the first sentence of each answer (where the hedge is), 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Hedging and "it depends"

### Explanation

Some questions don't have a simple answer. "Is social media good or bad?" "Do you like your job?" "Is it worth living abroad?" If you say only "yes" or "no", it's not honest. But if you say only "It depends" and stop, it sounds like you're avoiding the question, or like you froze.

A **hedge** is a way of saying "the answer is not 100% yes or no". Hedging is good. Natives do it all the time. The rule is: **a hedge is the start of your answer, not the whole answer.**

The pattern:

1. **Hedge:** "I guess it depends."
2. **Depends on what:** "…on what kind of job you have."
3. **Case 1:** "If you work in tech, then yeah, it's amazing."
4. **Case 2:** "But if you're like a nurse or a teacher, it's not even an option."
5. **Your lean:** "For me personally? I'd probably go hybrid."

> "Is remote work better?" → "I guess it depends on the person. If you live alone, it can get kinda lonely. But if you have a long commute, it's a game changer. Personally, I'd say two days in the office is perfect."

**Your lean** is important. It shows you have an opinion, even if it's not black and white. Use "Personally, I'd probably…", "If I had to choose…", "For me, I lean towards…".

**Short hedges for quick questions.** For yes/no questions in small talk, a short hedge + one detail is enough:

- "Do you like spicy food?" → "Kind of, yeah. Not *super* spicy, though."
- "Is your job stressful?" → "Yes and no. The work's fine, it's the meetings."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I guess it depends (on…). | the most common hedge; always say on what | "I guess it depends on the price." |
| Kind of, yeah. / Sort of. | partly yes | "Do you like your new place?" "Kind of, yeah. It's small, though." |
| Yes and no. | there's a yes part and a no part | "Yes and no. I love the city, but I miss my family." |
| In a way. / In some ways. | it's partly true | "In a way, it's easier. You don't have to commute." |
| To some extent. / Up to a point. | partly agree (a bit more careful) | "I agree with that, up to a point." |
| It's hard to say. | you're not sure what the answer is | "It's hard to say. I've only been there twice." |
| I could go either way. | you like both options equally | "Pizza or burgers? Honestly, I could go either way." |
| Depends who you ask. | different people have different opinions | "Is it a good school? Depends who you ask." |
| I'm on the fence (about it). | you haven't decided yet | "I'm on the fence about moving." |
| It's not that simple. / It's not black and white. | the question is more complex than it looks | "It's not that simple. There's the visa, the rent, the language…" |
| If it's (X), then yeah. But if… | you give two cases | "If it's a short trip, then yeah. But if it's two weeks, no way." |
| If I had to choose, I'd… / I lean towards… | you give your lean after hedging | "If I had to choose, I'd go with the smaller company." |

### Examples

**Example 1: full pattern**

> A: Do you think it's worth learning to code?
> B: Hmm, I guess it depends on what you want to do. If you wanna be a developer, then obviously, yeah. But if you're in, like, design or marketing, it's more of a bonus. Personally, I'd say learn the basics. It helps you talk to engineers.

**Example 2: quick small-talk hedges**

> A: Are you a morning person?
> B: Kind of, yeah. Like, I'm up at six, but I'm not *talking* until eight.
> A: Ha. Do you like living downtown?
> B: Yes and no. I love being close to everything, but the noise at night… ugh.

**Example 3: hedging + disagreeing softly (Day 3)**

> A: Honestly, I think everyone should live abroad at least once.
> B: I mean, up to a point. It's not that simple, though. Like, if you have kids, or your parents are sick… For me, it was amazing, but I get why some people can't.

**Example 4: "I could go either way" → still choose**

> A: Beach or mountains?
> B: Ooh, I could go either way, honestly. But if I had to choose… mountains. I burn way too easily.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ It depends. (and silence) | ✅ It depends on the price. If it's under fifty bucks, sure. | Always say what it depends on, and give the cases. |
| ❌ It depends of the weather. | ✅ It depends on the weather. | "Depend on", not "depend of". |
| ❌ It's depend. / It's depends. | ✅ It depends. | "Depends" is a verb: "it depends". |
| ❌ Yes and no. (and nothing else) | ✅ Yes and no. Yes because…, but no because… | Explain both parts. |
| ❌ Hedging every single answer | ✅ Hedge when it's honestly complex; be direct when you're sure | Too many hedges sounds like you have no opinions. |
| ❌ I'm on the fence of it. | ✅ I'm on the fence about it. | "On the fence about". |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Is social media good or bad for people?"**

**Rules for tonight:** use the full hedge pattern, give both cases with a reason and an example each (Day 4), and finish with your lean. Use at least **4 hedging phrases** and **2 opinion chunks** from Day 1.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Decide what it depends on. Max 5 keywords.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you give your lean at the end?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Hedge, two quick cases, lean.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "Is it better to live alone or with roommates?"** 2 minutes, 1 minute, then a 30-second version that starts with "Honestly, I could go either way, but…".

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "I guess it depends on…", "Yes and no.", "I could go either way.", "I'm on the fence about…", "If I had to choose, I'd…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory as the start of an answer to a question you invent.
3. **Choose the 10 best phrases from this whole week** (2 from each day) and say each one from memory. Mark any you forgot. Those are your focus for Day 6.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
