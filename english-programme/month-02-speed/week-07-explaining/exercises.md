# Week 7 exercises: Explaining
[Week 7 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-explaining-how-things-work.md) · [Day 2](day-2-explaining-decisions.md) · [Day 3](day-3-comparing-things.md) · [Day 4](day-4-explaining-problems-and-solutions.md) · [Day 5](day-5-checking-understanding.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** everything is **out loud**, as if you're explaining to a friend sitting in front of you. Imagine their face: are they following? Record when the exercise says so. Model answers are in each day's answer key. They're one natural version, not the only right one.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Put it in order
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. The steps of each process are mixed up. Put them in order in your head (or write the numbers).
2. Say the full process out loud with a big picture first and **different** sequence chunks (*First you… · Then… · After that… · Once you've… · Make sure you… · And that's it*).
3. Say it twice: the second time faster, with pauses between steps.

**Process A: Using a bike-share bike**
- (a) ride to any other station
- (b) scan the code on the bike with the app
- (c) push the bike into an empty dock until it beeps
- (d) download the app and add a card
- (e) check the tyres and brakes before you ride

**Process B: Making cold brew coffee**
- (a) pour it through a filter
- (b) mix coarse coffee with cold water in a big jar
- (c) leave it in the fridge for 12 to 18 hours
- (d) add ice and milk, or drink it black
- (e) stir it so all the coffee gets wet

**Process C: Running a user test (work)**
- (a) watch where they get stuck, and don't help
- (b) write down the 3 biggest problems
- (c) find 5 people who use the product
- (d) give them a simple task, like "buy a ticket"
- (e) make a quick clickable prototype

### Exercise 1.2: Big picture in one sentence
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Explain what each thing **is and what it's for** in **one sentence**, using "So basically, it's a… that…" or "It's kind of like a…, but…".
2. Under 10 seconds each. Do the list twice.

1. A dishwasher
2. A calendar app
3. A library card
4. A food delivery app
5. A password manager
6. A coworking space
7. A smart watch
8. A group chat
9. A design system (or any tool from your job)
10. A podcast

### Exercise 1.3: Explain it in 5 steps
**Time:** 20 min · **Type:** out loud, record 3

1. Explain each process with **big picture → 3–5 steps → a tip ("The trick is…" / "Make sure you…") → done**.
2. About 45–60 seconds each. Speak in thought groups.
3. Record any 3 and listen: can you hear the pauses between steps?

1. How to make a good cup of tea (or your favourite drink)
2. How to book a flight online
3. How to set up a new phone
4. How to get from your home to your work
5. How to do laundry without ruining your clothes
6. How to plan a birthday dinner for a friend
7. How to prepare for a job interview
8. How to do one task that's part of your job, explained for someone outside your field

### Answer key

**Exercise 1.1 (order):** A: d, e, b, a, c · B: b, e, c, a, d · C: e, c, d, a, b

**Exercise 1.1 (model, Process B):**
> "So basically, cold brew is coffee made with cold water instead of hot. First you mix coarse coffee with cold water in a big jar. Make sure you stir it so all the coffee gets wet. Then you leave it in the fridge for like 12 to 18 hours. Once it's ready, you pour it through a filter. And that's it, you add ice and milk, or drink it black."

**Exercise 1.2 (model answers):**
1. "So basically, it's a machine that washes your dishes for you."
3. "It's a card that lets you borrow books for free."
5. "It's kind of like a safe, but for all your passwords. You only remember one."
6. "It's basically an office you rent by the day, with lots of different people working there."
9. "So basically, it's a set of rules and parts, like buttons and colours, that everyone on a team uses, so the whole app looks and works the same."

**Exercise 1.3 (model, 2):**
> "So basically, you just need a booking site or the airline's app. First you put in where you're going and the dates. Then you compare the prices. The trick is to check the 'flexible dates' option, because sometimes one day earlier is way cheaper. Once you've picked a flight, you add your passport details and pay. Make sure you check the baggage rules before you pay. And that's it, you get the ticket by email."

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Build the decision
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Turn each set of notes into a full decision explanation (about 40 seconds): **decision → options → what it came down to → reasons + example → how it turned out**.
2. Use at least 3 decision chunks each time, and one "The downside is…, but…".

1. **Apartment.** Options: a small flat downtown vs a bigger one 30 minutes out. Came down to: space. Example: you can now have friends over for dinner. Downside: the commute. Now: happy.
2. **New laptop.** Options: expensive and fast vs cheap and light. Came down to: you travel a lot. Example: you take it on the train every day. Downside: it's a bit slow for big files. Now: mostly happy.
3. **Holiday.** Options: Japan vs Mexico. Came down to: price of flights. Example: Mexico was half the price. Downside: it was hot. Now: want to go to Japan next year.
4. **Team tool (work).** Options: keep the old project tool vs switch to a new one. Came down to: everyone can see the same thing. Example: no more "which version is the latest?". Downside: two weeks of learning it. Now: much faster.
5. **Course.** Options: an evening class vs an online course. Came down to: your schedule. Example: you work late twice a week. Downside: less speaking practice. Now: you added conversation practice.
6. **Launch date (work).** Options: launch before the holidays vs wait until January. Came down to: risk. Example: half the team was on holiday. Downside: a competitor launched first. Now: launch went smoothly.

### Exercise 2.2: Why not the other one?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Someone asks why you *didn't* choose something. Answer in 1–2 sentences with *We decided against… because… · The main reason we didn't go with… was… · It just made more sense to…*.
2. Under 15 seconds each. Invent the details.

1. "Why didn't you take the job in London?"
2. "Why didn't you guys use the red logo?"
3. "Why didn't you buy a car?"
4. "Why didn't you go to the party?"
5. "Why didn't you fly? The train takes forever."
6. "Why didn't you choose the bigger hotel?"
7. "Why didn't your team build the mobile app first?"
8. "Why didn't you study design at university?"
9. "Why didn't you move in with your friends?"
10. "Why didn't you get the cheaper phone?"

### Exercise 2.3: Looking back
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each decision, explain it in 2–3 sentences, then reflect with **"Looking back, I'd…"**, **"Looking back, I should've…"** or **"Looking back, it was the right call, because…"**.
2. After the 8 below, do 2 of your own real decisions, about 1 minute each.

1. You took a job for the money, not the people.
2. You moved to a new city without knowing anyone.
3. You said no to a trip with friends because of work.
4. You bought the cheapest flight, with a 10-hour stopover.
5. You switched from design to product (or another career change).
6. You learned English with apps only for two years.
7. You adopted a dog while living in a small apartment.
8. You chose to launch a product quickly, with some bugs.

### Answer key

**Exercise 2.1 (model, 4):**
> "So we ended up switching to a new project tool. We were looking at two options: keep the old one, which everyone knew, or switch. In the end, it came down to one thing: everyone seeing the same thing. Like, with the old one, we'd always have these 'Wait, which version is the latest?' conversations. The downside was two weeks of everyone learning it, but honestly? Looking back, it was the right call. We're way faster now."

**Exercise 2.2 (model answers):**
1. "The main reason I didn't go with London was the cost. The salary sounded great, but rent would've eaten half of it."
2. "We decided against red because it looked too much like an error message."
5. "It just made more sense to take the train. It's city centre to city centre, so it's actually faster."
7. "We decided against mobile first because most of our users are on laptops at work."

**Exercise 2.3 (model, 4):**
> "I bought the cheapest flight to Bangkok, with a ten-hour stopover. I thought, OK, I'll save two hundred bucks. Looking back, I should've paid more. I was so tired I lost the first day of the trip."

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Modifier ladder
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Compare each pair **three ways**: (a) with *a bit / slightly*, (b) with *way / a lot*, (c) with *not as… as*.
2. Use the adjective given, or choose your own.

1. A train / a bus (comfortable)
2. Summer / winter (hot)
3. Your phone / your laptop (fast)
4. A coffee shop / your home (noisy)
5. Your city / a small village (expensive)
6. A cat / a dog (easy to look after)
7. A video call / a phone call (tiring)
8. Your first job / your current job (interesting)
9. Cooking at home / eating out (cheap)
10. Email / chat messages (formal)

### Exercise 3.2: Better for what?
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record 3

1. For each pair, say what **each one is better for**, give the **main difference**, and finish with a **recommendation** ("If you want…, go with…").
2. Use at least one of: *X wins on…, but Y wins on… · whereas · compared to · it's not even close · they're basically the same*.
3. About 30 seconds each. Record any 3.

1. Living in the city centre vs the suburbs
2. Working from home vs working in the office
3. Paper notebook vs notes app
4. Taking the train vs driving for a weekend trip
5. A big supermarket vs a local market
6. Booking a hotel vs renting an apartment on holiday
7. Video calls vs meeting in person (at work)
8. A big company vs a small start-up
9. Reading a book vs listening to the audiobook
10. Two tools or apps you use at work

### Exercise 3.3: Fix it, then say it
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** correct, then out loud

**Part 1 (5 min).** Each sentence has one mistake. Say the correct version out loud.

1. My new apartment is more bigger than the old one.
2. The train is very cheaper than flying.
3. It's not so fast like my old phone.
4. Her job is similar than mine.
5. This café is better that the one downstairs.
6. The second option is the more better one.
7. London is more expensive as Berlin.
8. It's a little more cheap.
9. Compared with the last version, this one is much more easy.
10. They're basically same.

**Part 2 (10 min).** Give two **one-minute comparisons** (record both):
- "Compare your hometown with the city you live in now." (If it's the same city, compare it ten years ago vs now.)
- "Compare two jobs or roles you've had (or two classes, if you were a student)."

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.1 (model, 1):** (a) "The train's a bit more comfortable than the bus." (b) "The train's way more comfortable." (c) "The bus isn't as comfortable as the train."

**Exercise 3.2 (model, 2):**
> "Working from home is way better for focus, whereas the office is better for, like, quick questions and actually knowing your team. The main difference is energy. At home I'm calm, in the office I'm more creative. If you're doing deep work, go with home. If you're starting a new project, I'd say go in."

**Exercise 3.3 Part 1:**
1. "My new apartment is bigger than the old one." / "…way bigger…"
2. "The train is way / much / a lot cheaper than flying."
3. "It's not as fast as my old phone."
4. "Her job is similar to mine."
5. "This café is better than the one downstairs."
6. "The second option is the better one."
7. "London is more expensive than Berlin."

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Notes to explanation
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Turn each set of notes into a full explanation (about 40 seconds): **problem → impact → cause (at first we thought… but…) → fix → now**.
2. Use at least 4 chunks from the [Day 4 key phrases](day-4-explaining-problems-and-solutions.md).

1. **Wi-Fi at home.** Problem: kept disconnecting every evening. Impact: couldn't do video calls. First thought: the internet company. Real cause: the neighbour's new router on the same channel. Fix: changed the channel in the settings. Now: perfect.
2. **Sign-up page (work).** Problem: lots of people started signing up but didn't finish. Impact: 60% dropped out. First thought: the page was slow. Real cause: the form asked for a phone number, people didn't trust it. Fix: made the phone number optional. Now: dropout halved.
3. **Car.** Problem: a strange noise when braking. Impact: scary on the highway. First thought: the brakes. Real cause: a small stone stuck near the wheel. Fix: the mechanic took it out in two minutes. Now: fine, and only cost $20.
4. **Team communication (work).** Problem: designers and engineers kept building different versions. Impact: two weeks wasted. First thought: people weren't reading the docs. Real cause: the docs were in three different places. Fix: one shared page and a 10-minute weekly check-in. Now: much better.
5. **Sleep.** Problem: kept waking up at 4 a.m. Impact: tired at work. First thought: stress. Real cause: coffee after 3 p.m. Fix: no coffee after lunch. Now: still trying, but better.
6. **Online order.** Problem: package never arrived. Impact: it was a birthday present. First thought: stolen. Real cause: wrong apartment number. Fix: the neighbour brought it up a week later. Now: you always double-check addresses.

### Exercise 4.2: What we thought vs what it was
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** rapid out loud

1. Make a sentence with **"At first I/we thought…, but it turned out…"** for each situation.
2. Then say it again with **"Turns out…"** instead (more casual).

1. Your phone wouldn't charge. (the cable / dust in the port)
2. The client didn't reply for a week. (they were angry / they were on holiday)
3. Your plant was dying. (too little water / too much water)
4. The app was slow for users. (the servers / huge images)
5. Your colleague seemed cold. (she didn't like you / she was just shy)
6. The meeting room was always booked. (lots of meetings / one person booked it every day "just in case")
7. Your coffee tasted strange. (old coffee / the machine needed cleaning)
8. Your neighbour was making noise every night. (a party / he was learning the drums)

### Exercise 4.3: Everyday problems in 60 seconds
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** timed out loud, record the last two

1. Explain each problem in **60 seconds** with the full shape. Use a real problem from your life if you have one, or invent the details.
2. Record numbers 5 and 6.

1. A problem with an apartment or house you've lived in
2. A problem with travel (lost luggage, a missed connection, a wrong booking)
3. A problem with technology (a phone, a laptop, an app)
4. A problem with a neighbour or roommate
5. A problem at work that your team solved
6. A problem you haven't solved yet (use "We're still trying to figure out…" / "For now, the workaround is…")

### Answer key

**Exercise 4.1 (model, 2):**
> "So the problem was, lots of people started signing up but never finished. Like, sixty percent just left halfway. At first we thought the page was too slow, but it turned out it was the form. We were asking for a phone number, and people didn't trust it. So what we did was, we made the phone number optional. And since then, the dropout's been cut in half."

**Exercise 4.2 (model answers):**
1. "At first I thought it was the cable, but it turned out there was dust in the port." / "Turns out it was just dust in the port."
3. "At first I thought it needed more water, but it turned out I was giving it way too much."
6. "At first we thought there were just lots of meetings, but it turned out one guy was booking it every day, just in case."
8. "At first I thought he was having parties, but turns out he's learning the drums."

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Where to check
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Read each explanation. Add **2–3 checks** in the best places: after the big picture, after the hardest part, and at the end. Use different checks (*Make sense so far? · You with me? · Does that make sense? · You know what I mean? · Does that help?*).
2. Say the full explanation with the checks, and **pause for 2 seconds** after each one.

**A. Directions**
> "OK, so the easiest way is the subway. You take the green line to Central, which is about six stops. At Central you change to the red line, going north, not south, that's the confusing part. You get off at Park Street, and the café is right outside exit B."

**B. A work tool**
> "So basically, it's where we keep all our designs. Every project has its own page. Inside each page, there are different versions, and the newest one is always at the top. If you want to leave feedback, you just click anywhere on the design and type a comment. The designer gets a notification."

**C. A recipe**
> "It's a really simple pasta. You fry garlic in a lot of olive oil, but really slowly, so it doesn't burn. That's the trick. Then you add chilli and the cooked pasta, plus a little of the pasta water, and you mix it really fast so it gets creamy. Lemon and parsley at the end, and done."

### Exercise 5.2: Say it simpler
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each sentence is too complicated for casual conversation. Say it, then rescue it with **"Sorry, that was a lot. So basically…"** or **"Let me put it another way…"** + a simple version (and an everyday comparison if you can).
2. Say only the simple version a second time, faster.

1. "The subscription renews automatically on a monthly basis unless you cancel it at least 48 hours before the renewal date."
2. "We're prioritising features based on a combination of user impact, technical effort and strategic alignment."
3. "The heating system is controlled by a thermostat that turns the boiler on when the temperature falls below the level you've set."
4. "The new policy means employees can choose their working location on two out of five days, subject to manager approval."
5. "A prototype is an early interactive version of a product that we use to test ideas before investing in full development."
6. "The airline will compensate passengers for delays of more than three hours, provided the delay was within their control."
7. "Our app uses your location data to show nearby options, but only while the app is open, and it doesn't store it."
8. "The rent includes water and heating, but electricity and internet are billed separately each month."

### Exercise 5.3: The lost listener
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** perform both parts, then improvise

1. Read each script out loud, both parts. When the listener gets lost, **you** (B) must rescue the explanation: rephrase, use an example, then check again. Fill the gaps.
2. Then do each one again without reading, improvising your rescue.

**Script 1: Explaining your job**
> A: So what do you actually do?
> B: I'm a product designer. I work on the user experience of our app.
> A: The user… what?
> B: (1) ______ Basically, I decide how the app works for people. Like, (2) ______ you want to buy a ticket. I make sure it takes three taps, not ten. (3) ______
> A: Oh, OK! Yeah, that makes sense.

**Script 2: Explaining a game**
> B: So each player gets five cards, and you can either play one or swap one, but if you swap you can't play on your next turn, unless you have a joker, and…
> A: Wait, wait.
> B: (4) ______ Let me start again. You just want to get rid of all your cards. That's it. (5) ______
> A: Yeah.
> B: Cool. So every turn, you play one card. The swapping stuff we can learn later.

**Script 3: Confirming (you're the listener)**
> A: So the meeting's moved to Thursday, but only for the design team. Product's still Wednesday, and then we all meet Friday.
> B: (6) ______ So you mean I go Thursday and Friday, but not Wednesday?
> A: Exactly.

**Script 4: Directions on the phone**
> B: …so you go past the station, take the second left, then a quick right after the bakery. (7) ______
> A: Kind of… I got the station part.
> B: OK, (8) ______ Just go to the station first. Call me when you're there, and I'll walk you from there. (9) ______
> A: Perfect, thanks.

### Answer key

**Exercise 5.2 (model answers):**
1. "Sorry, that was a lot. So basically, it renews every month. If you wanna stop it, cancel two days before."
2. "Let me put it another way: we do the stuff that helps users most and isn't crazy hard to build first."
3. "So basically, when it gets cold, the heating turns on by itself. Like a fridge, but the opposite."
4. "So basically, you can work from home two days a week, if your manager's OK with it."
5. "Let me put it another way. It's like a rough draft of the app that you can click on, so we can test it before we build the real thing."
6. "So basically, if the flight's more than three hours late and it's their fault, they pay you."
8. "So basically, water and heating are included. You pay electricity and internet yourself."

**Exercise 5.3 (possible answers):** (1) Sorry, let me put it another way. (2) imagine (3) Does that make sense? (4) Sorry, that was a lot. (5) You with me? / Make sense so far? (6) Got it. (7) You with me? / Does that make sense? (8) let me put it another way. (9) Does that help?

## Day 6 materials

### Re-record test prompt

> **"Explain something you're good at, and how someone could learn it."**

Unprepared. Start within 5 seconds. Record exactly 2 minutes.

### Explanation checklist (tick while you listen)

| ✔ | Did I… |
|---|---|
| | start with a one-sentence big picture ("So basically, it's…")? |
| | give steps in a clear order with different sequence chunks? |
| | speak in thought groups, with pauses between steps? |
| | include a tip ("The trick is…", "Make sure you…")? |
| | explain why (a reason, decision or comparison)? |
| | use an everyday comparison or example ("It's kind of like…")? |
| | check understanding at least once? |
| | finish clearly ("And that's it.", "So basically…")? |

Score: count the ticks (out of 8). Write it in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

### "Explain it to me" topics for the live conversation

Ask your partner 2–3 of these, listen, and confirm you understood ("Got it. So you mean…?"). Be ready to answer them yourself too.

1. What do you actually do at work, day to day?
2. What's the best way to get around this city?
3. How did you end up living here?
4. What's a recipe you're really good at?
5. Which is better here: renting or buying?
6. What's an app you use every day, and how does it work?
7. What's the hardest part of your job?
8. What's a problem you solved recently?
9. How do you usually plan a trip?
10. What's a hobby you could teach someone?
11. Why did you choose your job or what you studied?
12. What's the difference between this neighbourhood and the one next to it?
