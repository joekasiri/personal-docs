# Week 7 · Day 3: Comparing things

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Do [Day 1](day-1-explaining-how-things-work.md) and [Day 2](day-2-explaining-decisions.md) first. A comparison is often the middle part of a decision.
- Hedges from [Week 6, Day 5](../week-06-opinions/day-5-hedging-and-it-depends.md) ("I guess it depends…") are useful tonight, because many comparisons depend on the person.
- Think of pairs you know well: two cities, two jobs, two phones, two apps, two coffee places, two ways of working.

## Learning objectives

- Compare two things with natural modifiers (*way, a lot, a bit, not as… as*).
- Say what each option is **better for**, instead of only "X is better".
- Use 10+ comparison chunks ("The main difference is…", "X wins on…").
- Finish a comparison with a recommendation ("If you want…, go with…").

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: the rhythm of comparisons.** In a comparison, the stress lands on the **modifier** and the **two things being compared**: "It's WAY cheaper, | but it's not as FAST." The little words (*it's, than, as, the*) are tiny. "Than" sounds like "th'n", "as" sounds like "'z".

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. It's WAY better for WORK, | but the other one's better for GAMes.
2. It's a LOT cheaper th'n the old one.
3. It's not as FAST 'z the other one. → "it's not 'z FAST 'z the other one"
4. They're pretty SIMilar, | except for the PRICE.
5. The MAIN difference is | the BATtery.
6. Chicago wins on FOOD, | but Denver wins on WEATHer.
7. If you want CHEAP, | go with the FIRST one.
8. It's not even CLOSE.
9. They're basically the SAME thing.
10. It's kind of a CHEAPer version of the other one.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube for "which is better vlog comparison", "living in city A vs city B", "phone comparison casual review" or "coffee taste test vlog". Choose a casual, chatty reviewer.

1. Find a 30–40 second comparison.
2. Listen twice. Which words are stressed?
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the sentences that contain "better", "cheaper", "more" or "than", 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Comparing things

### Explanation

You already know comparative grammar ("bigger", "more expensive", "the best"). The problem in conversation is that learners use it in a flat, textbook way: "London is bigger than Paris. London is more expensive than Paris." That's correct, but it doesn't help the listener decide anything.

Natural comparisons do three things:

1. **They use modifiers** to show *how much* different:

| A little | A lot | Not |
|---|---|---|
| a bit / a little / slightly (cheaper) | a lot / way / much (cheaper) | not as (cheap) as · not quite as (good) as |

"Way" is the most casual and the most common in American speech: "way better", "way more expensive", "way too slow".

2. **They say what each thing is better *for*.** Not "X is better" but "X is better **for** work, Y is better **for** travel". Almost nothing is better at everything.

3. **They end with a recommendation**, often with "if": "If you want something cheap, go with X. If you care about quality, Y's worth it."

**Useful patterns:**

- "X **wins on** price, but Y **wins on** quality." (a fun, casual way to compare)
- "The main difference is…" (when they're mostly similar)
- "X is…, **whereas** Y is…" (a bit more careful, good at work)
- "**Compared to** X, Y is…"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| X is way better for…, but Y… | you compare what each is good for | "The laptop's way better for work, but the tablet's better for travel." |
| X is a lot (cheaper / faster) than Y. | a big difference | "The train's a lot cheaper than flying." |
| X is a bit / slightly (bigger)… | a small difference | "The new one's a bit heavier." |
| X isn't as (good / fast) as Y. | a negative comparison (sounds softer than "worse") | "It isn't as fast as my old one." |
| They're pretty similar, except (for)… | mostly the same, one difference | "They're pretty similar, except the camera." |
| The main difference is… | you point to the key difference | "The main difference is the price." |
| X wins on (price), but Y wins on (quality). | casual way to compare two strengths | "Denver wins on weather, but Chicago wins on food." |
| …whereas Y… | you contrast two things, a bit more careful | "My old job was super structured, whereas this one's pretty chaotic." |
| Compared to X, Y is… | you use one thing as the reference | "Compared to my last apartment, this place is huge." |
| It's not even close. | one thing is MUCH better | "Which coffee's better? It's not even close. The small place." |
| They're basically the same. | there's no real difference | "Honestly, they're basically the same. Get the cheaper one." |
| If you want (A), go with X. | you recommend based on what someone needs | "If you want quiet, go with the second hotel." |

### Examples

**Example 1: comparing two cities**

> A: So which do you like more, Austin or Seattle?
> B: Ooh, tough one. Austin wins on weather, for sure. It's way sunnier. But Seattle's a lot more interesting for food, and it's so green. The main difference is the vibe, honestly. Austin's super relaxed, whereas Seattle's more… serious? If you want sun and music, go with Austin. If you like hiking and coffee, Seattle.

**Example 2: two tools at work**

> A: Should we use the new design tool or stick with the old one?
> B: They're pretty similar, except for collaboration. With the new one, everyone can edit at the same time, whereas the old one is basically one person at a time. It's a bit slower, though. But compared to the time we lose sending files around? It's not even close.

**Example 3: a quick small-talk comparison**

> A: How's the new gym?
> B: Honestly, it's a lot better than my old one. It's not as cheap, but it's way cleaner, and it's open 24 hours.

**Example 4: comparing with a hedge (Week 6)**

> A: Is it better to work at a big company or a start-up?
> B: I guess it depends what you want. Big companies are way more stable, and you learn how things are "supposed" to work. Start-ups are a lot more chaotic, but you learn ten times faster. If you're early in your career, I'd say go with the start-up.

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ It's more cheaper. | ✅ It's cheaper. / It's a lot cheaper. | Short adjectives take *-er* without "more". |
| ❌ It's very cheaper. | ✅ It's way / much / a lot cheaper. | "Very" doesn't go with comparatives. |
| ❌ It's bigger that the old one. | ✅ It's bigger than the old one. | "Than", not "that". |
| ❌ It's not so good like the other one. | ✅ It's not as good as the other one. | The pattern is "not as… as". |
| ❌ It's similar than mine. / It's different than mine. | ✅ It's similar to mine. / It's different from mine. | "Similar to". "Different from" (Americans also say "different than", but "from" is always safe). |
| ❌ The more better option is… | ✅ The better option is… | "Better" is already a comparative. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Compare two places you've lived or worked."** (Two cities, two apartments, two offices, two companies.)

**Rules for tonight:** at least **3 different modifiers** (a bit, way, not as… as), say what each place is better **for**, use "X wins on…, but Y wins on…" once, and end with a recommendation ("If you want…, go with…"). Include one short example or story.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords: 2 for each place, 1 for the recommendation.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you use any "more + -er" or "very + -er" mistakes?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Two strengths each, main difference, recommendation.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "Compare two apps, tools or devices you use."** (Two phones, two music apps, two design tools, two ways to get to work.) 2 minutes, 1 minute, then 30 seconds ending with "It's not even close."

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "X is way better for…", "It isn't as… as…", "The main difference is…", "X wins on…, but Y wins on…", "If you want…, go with…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory, comparing a different pair.
3. **Say Day 2's 5 decision phrases from memory** about a decision where you compared two options.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
