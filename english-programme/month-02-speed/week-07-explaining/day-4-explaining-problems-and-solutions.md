## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: problem → pause → solution.** When people explain a problem, they often slow down and pause just after describing it, then switch to a lighter, faster voice for the solution, like a relief. `||` = pause. Capitals = stress.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. So the PROBlem was, || it kept CRASHing.
2. What HAPpened was, | the update broke the LOGin.
3. At FIRST we thought it was the WIfi, || but it TURNED out it was the ROUTer.
4. So what we DID was, | we just RESTARTed everything.
5. The FIX was actually super SIMple.
6. For NOW, | the workaround is to do it MANually.
7. We're STILL trying to figure out WHY. → "we're still tryin' ta figure out WHY"
8. And since THEN, | it's been FINE.
9. The ROOT of it was | a typo. || ONE typo.
10. So to stop it happening aGAIN, | we added a CHECK.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "what went wrong story podcast", "tech support story", "I fixed my own car vlog", "home repair fail and fix vlog" or "startup failure story podcast". Choose someone explaining a problem casually.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where they explain what went wrong and how they fixed it.
2. Listen twice. Notice the change in voice from problem to solution.
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the solution sentences, 5 times each.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Explaining problems and solutions

### Explanation

"What happened?" "Why is it broken?" "How did you fix it?" Explaining a problem is part story (Week 5), part explanation (this week). Learners often get stuck because they try to explain every technical detail. The listener only needs five things:

1. **The problem:** "So the problem was, the app kept logging people out."
2. **The impact** (why it mattered): "Like, every five minutes. People were furious."
3. **The cause**, often with "at first we thought… but": "At first we thought it was the servers, but it turned out it was a setting someone changed."
4. **The fix:** "So what we did was, we changed it back and added an alert."
5. **Now:** "And since then, it's been fine." / "We're still trying to figure out why it happened."

**Tense map:**

| Part | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The problem and the cause (finished story) | past simple / past continuous | "It kept crashing." "We were losing users." |
| "It kept + -ing" | for something that happened again and again | "It kept freezing." |
| Now | present / present perfect | "It's fine now." "Since then, it's been fine." |
| Still unsolved | present continuous | "We're still trying to figure it out." |

**Simplify technical stuff.** For someone outside your field, swap details for an everyday comparison: "Basically, two parts of the app weren't talking to each other. Like two people on the phone, both waiting for the other one to speak."

**Workaround** = a temporary fix, not the real solution. "For now, the workaround is to restart it every morning."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So the problem was… | you start explaining a problem | "So the problem was, nobody could pay." |
| What happened was… | you tell the story of the problem | "What happened was, the update deleted everyone's settings." |
| It kept (+ -ing)… | the problem happened again and again | "My laptop kept shutting down." |
| At first we thought…, but… | your first guess was wrong | "At first we thought it was the battery, but…" |
| It turned out (that)… / Turns out… | the real cause | "It turned out the cable was broken." |
| The root of it was… | the deep, original cause | "The root of it was bad communication, honestly." |
| We figured out that… | you discovered the cause | "We figured out that it only happened on old phones." |
| So what we did was… | you explain the solution | "So what we did was, we split it into two smaller steps." |
| The fix was (pretty simple / a nightmare). | you describe the solution | "The fix was super simple: one line of code." |
| For now, the workaround is… | there's only a temporary fix | "For now, the workaround is to log in twice." |
| We're still trying to figure out… | it's not solved yet | "We're still trying to figure out why it happens." |
| Since then, it's been… | what's happened from the fix until now | "Since then, it's been totally fine." |

### Examples

**Example 1: work problem, full shape**

> A: What was all the drama yesterday?
> B: Oh man. So the problem was, the checkout page kept freezing. Like, people would click "pay" and nothing would happen. We were losing, like, hundreds of orders. At first we thought it was the payment company, but it turned out it was our own fault. Someone added a huge image to the page, and it was so slow it just timed out. So what we did was, we took the image off, and it worked right away. Since then, it's been fine. Now we check page speed before every release.

**Example 2: everyday problem**

> A: Did you fix your shower?
> B: Kind of. What happened was, the water kept going cold after like two minutes. At first I thought it was the heater, so I called this guy, and he was like, "Nope, heater's fine." Turns out it was the little valve thing, I forget what it's called. The real fix is gonna take a week, so for now, the workaround is… very fast showers.

**Example 3: simplifying for an outsider**

> A: So what exactly broke? I don't really get tech stuff.
> B: Basically, two parts of the app weren't talking to each other. It's kind of like you send a text, and your friend's phone gets it, but it never shows them a notification. So they never reply. That's what was happening, but with orders.

**Example 4: still unsolved**

> "My phone keeps dying at like 40%. We're still trying to figure out why. The store says it's the battery, my brother says it's an app. For now, I just carry a charger everywhere."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ It kept to crash. | ✅ It kept crashing. | "Keep" + -ing. |
| ❌ The problem was that it was a problem with the… | ✅ The problem was, the… | Say the problem directly. |
| ❌ It turned out that the cable is broken. | ✅ It turned out the cable was broken. | Past story → past tense after "it turned out". |
| ❌ We solved it, the problem. | ✅ We solved it. / We fixed the problem. | Don't double the object. |
| ❌ Since then, it was fine. | ✅ Since then, it's been fine. | "Since then" → present perfect. |
| ❌ (5 minutes of technical detail to a non-expert) | ✅ "Basically, it's kind of like…" | Simplify with an everyday comparison. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min): voice mode with Claude

Tonight you **explain 3 things from your life or work** to Claude. Claude plays a curious friend who doesn't work in your field. See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md).

**Choose your 3 things before you start:**

1. **How something works:** a tool, app or process from your work, or a machine or system at home (Day 1).
2. **A decision:** why you or your team chose something, and what you compared (Days 2 and 3).
3. **A problem you solved:** at work or at home (tonight's lesson).

**Steps:**

1. **Set up (5 min).** Headphones on, notebook open. Start voice mode and paste or say this prompt:

```
I'm a B2 English learner practising explaining things clearly in casual conversation. I'm going to explain 3 things to you, one at a time: how something works, a decision I made, and a problem I solved.
Please act like a curious friend who doesn't work in my field. While I explain:
- interrupt sometimes with natural questions like "Wait, what's that?", "Why not just…?", "So what happened next?" or "How's that different from…?"
- if I use a technical word, ask me what it means, so I have to explain it simply.
Keep your turns short so I do most of the talking.
After each explanation, give me quick spoken feedback: up to 3 mistakes with the natural American version, and whether my explanation was easy to follow (1 to 3). Then say "OK, what's next?".
Use casual American English.
```

2. **Explanation 1 (8 min), Explanation 2 (8 min), Explanation 3 (8 min).**
3. **Reverse (6 min).** Ask Claude: "Now you explain something from your 'work' to me, and I'll ask questions." Practise asking "Wait, how does that work?" and "So what was the problem?".
4. **Write corrections (10 min).** Write each correction as *what I said → better version*. Say each better version 3 times.

**Focus tonight:** when Claude asks "What's that?", don't freeze. Use "Basically, it's kind of like…" and keep going.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md): 3 from tonight ("What happened was…", "It kept + -ing", "For now, the workaround is…") and 2 corrections from Claude.
2. **Cover the log.** Say each correction's better version from memory in a new sentence.
3. **Say Day 3's 5 comparison phrases from memory**, comparing an old way of doing something with a new way.
4. Write in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) how many corrections you got and which explanation was easiest to follow.
