## Learning objectives

- Check understanding naturally at the right moments ("Does that make sense?", "You with me?").
- Pause and really listen after a check.
- Rephrase simply when someone is lost ("Let me put it another way…", "So basically…").
- Confirm *you* understood when someone explains to you ("Got it. So you mean…?").

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: rising checks and the pause after.** Check questions usually go **up** at the end ("Does that make SENSE? ↗"), and then the speaker **stops** for a second to let the listener answer. "You know what I mean?" is often very fast and squashed: "y'know whad I mean?". In the sentences, `↗` = voice rises, `[pause]` = stop and wait.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times, and really pause where it says so.

1. Does that make SENSE? ↗ [pause] → "does that make SENSE?"
2. You know what I MEAN? ↗ [pause] → "y'know whad I MEAN?"
3. You with ME? ↗ [pause] → "you with ME?"
4. Am I making SENSE? ↗ [pause]
5. Sorry, | did I LOSE you? ↗ [pause]
6. OK, let me put it ANother way.
7. So BASically, | it's like a SHORTcut.
8. It's kinda CONfusing, right? ↗ [pause]
9. Got it. | So you MEAN… the BLUE one?
10. Does that HELP? ↗ [pause]

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube for "teacher explains casually", "cooking class talking to students", "explaining to my friend vlog" or "tutorial with a friend". Choose a clip where someone explains *to another person* who responds.

1. Find a 30–40 second part with at least two checks.
2. Listen twice. Notice the rise and the pause.
3. Speak along 5 times, playing only the explainer.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along only with the check questions, then stay silent during the other person's answer.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Checking understanding

### Explanation

When you explain something, you can't see inside the listener's head. If they get lost early, everything after that is wasted. Natural speakers **check** regularly, not by asking "Do you understand?" (which can sound like a teacher), but with soft, friendly chunks.

**When to check:**

1. After the **big picture** (Day 1): "So basically, it's an app for splitting bills. Make sense so far?"
2. After a **complicated** step or idea.
3. At the **end**: "Does that help?"

Not after every sentence. That sounds nervous.

**How to check:**

- Use a rising voice, then **pause**. Really wait. Look at their face, or listen on the phone. This pause is also a little rest for your brain.
- If they say "Yeah, totally", keep going.
- If they look lost or say "Kind of…", **rephrase simply**: "OK, let me put it another way…", "So basically…", "Like, imagine…". Use an everyday comparison (Day 4) or an example.

**"You know what I mean?" and "right?"** are used in two ways: as a **real check** (pause after it), or as a quick **filler** inside a sentence ("It's just, you know, kinda annoying, right?"). Both are natural. Just don't use them in every sentence.

**When someone explains to you,** show you understood by **summarising back**: "Got it. So you mean the blue button, not the red one?" This is called *confirming*. It's faster than saying "Sorry, I didn't understand". (Asking people to repeat when you're really lost is a whole lesson in Month 4.)

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Does that make sense? | the standard, friendly check | "So you pay once and that's it. Does that make sense?" |
| Make sense so far? | you check in the middle of an explanation | "That's the first part. Make sense so far?" |
| You know what I mean? | you check if they get the feeling or idea | "It's not bad, it's just… boring, you know what I mean?" |
| You with me? / Are you with me? | casual check during steps | "Then you go left at the second light. You with me?" |
| Am I making sense? | you worry *your* explanation is confusing | "Sorry, am I making sense? I'm kinda rambling." |
| Did I lose you? | you think they're lost | "Did I lose you? That part's confusing." |
| Sorry, that was a lot. | you said too much at once | "Sorry, that was a lot. Basically, it's just two steps." |
| Let me put it another way. | you're going to rephrase | "Let me put it another way: it's like Netflix, but for books." |
| So basically… | you give a short, simple summary | "So basically, you just press it twice." |
| Like, imagine… | you explain with an example | "Like, imagine you're at a restaurant, and…" |
| Does that help? | at the end, especially when someone asked for help | "Try restarting it first. Does that help?" |
| Got it. So you mean…? | you confirm you understood (listener side) | "Got it. So you mean we meet at the station, not the café?" |

### Examples

**Example 1: checking and rephrasing**

> A: So what does your team actually do?
> B: So basically, we design how the app works. Like, which buttons go where, what happens when you tap something. Does that make sense?
> A: Kind of… like, the colours and stuff?
> B: Hmm, a little, but not really. Let me put it another way. Imagine you're building a house. The colours are like the paint. We're more like the person who decides where the doors and stairs go. You with me?
> A: Oh, OK, got it. So you're like the architect.
> B: Exactly.

**Example 2: in the middle of instructions**

> "OK, so first you take the 42 bus to the end of the line. Then you walk past the big supermarket, and there's a little path on the right. Make sense so far? … Cool. So you take that path, and the house is the blue one at the end. Does that help?"

**Example 3: you're the listener, confirming**

> A: So we're gonna move the launch, but only for mobile. The web version stays the same.
> B: Got it. So you mean the web goes out Monday, and the app comes later?
> A: Exactly.

**Example 4: "that was a lot"**

> "…and then there's the monthly plan, which is different from the yearly one, because with the yearly one you get the extra storage, but not the video stuff, unless you… Sorry, that was a lot. So basically: if you just need storage, get the yearly one. That's it."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Do you understand me? | ✅ Does that make sense? | "Do you understand?" can sound like a teacher or a little rude. |
| ❌ Is it clear? | ✅ Make sense so far? / You with me? | "Is it clear?" sounds formal and a bit cold. |
| ❌ (checking after every sentence) | ✅ (checking after the big picture, after a hard part, and at the end) | Too many checks sound nervous. |
| ❌ Does that make sense? [keeps talking immediately] | ✅ Does that make sense? [pause, wait for an answer] | A check is a real question. Wait. |
| ❌ (listener) "Yes, yes." [but you're lost] | ✅ (listener) "Kind of. So you mean…?" | Pretending to understand causes bigger problems later. |
| ❌ Let me say it in other way. | ✅ Let me put it another way. | Fixed chunk: "put it another way". |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Teach someone a skill you have."** (Riding a bike, making a good presentation, taking a good photo with a phone, packing a suitcase, making coffee properly, a design skill.)

**Rules for tonight:** big picture first, steps in thought groups (Day 1), **at least 3 checks** (after the big picture, after the hardest step, at the end), and **one rephrase** ("Let me put it another way…" + an everyday comparison). After each check, pause for 2 seconds, as if someone is answering.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords. Decide which step is hardest (that's where your rephrase goes).
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you really pause after each check?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Keep at least 2 checks.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). (Your planned 2-second pauses after checks don't count as long pauses.)

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "Explain the most confusing part of your job"** to a friend who knows nothing about it. Use "Let me put it another way…" at least twice. 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "Does that make sense?", "Make sense so far?", "Let me put it another way.", "Sorry, that was a lot.", "Got it. So you mean…?".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory inside a mini-explanation.
3. **Choose the 10 best phrases from this whole week** (2 from each day) and say each one from memory. Mark any you forgot. Those are your focus for Day 6.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
