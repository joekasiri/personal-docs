## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: thought groups.** A *thought group* is a small chunk of words that belongs together, said in one breath, with a tiny pause after it. Good explainers speak in thought groups, so the listener can follow each step. Learners often do the opposite: either one long breathless sentence, or a pause in the *wrong* place (in the middle of a chunk). In the sentences, `|` = small pause between thought groups, `||` = longer pause between steps.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times. Keep each group smooth and connected, then pause.

1. So basically | the way it works is || you scan the code | and it opens the menu.
2. First you open the APP, || then you tap the little PLUS | in the corner.
3. Once you've done THAT, || it asks for your EMAIL.
4. Make sure you SAVE it | before you close it. → "make sher ya SAVE it"
5. The TRICK is | to do it SLOWly.
6. And THAT'S it. || You're DONE.
7. Basically | it's like a CALendar | but for the whole TEAM.
8. What HAPpens is | the water heats up | and then it goes through the COFfee.
9. If you press it TWICE, || it turns OFF.
10. Don't forGET | to unplug it | when you're done.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube for "explain like I'm five", "how it works explained simply", "casual cooking tutorial" or "app tutorial talking". Choose a relaxed speaker, not a robot voice.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where the speaker explains steps.
2. Listen twice. Where are the pauses? How long is each thought group?
3. Speak along 5 times, pausing exactly where they pause.
4. Last 3 minutes: pause after each step and repeat that step alone.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Explaining how things work

### Explanation

When people explain something badly, they usually jump straight into details: "So you click on the settings, no wait, first the profile, then there's a menu, and you have to, umm…". The listener is lost after 10 seconds.

A good casual explanation has four parts:

1. **Big picture (one sentence).** What is it, and what's it for? "So basically, it's an app that splits bills between friends."
2. **Steps (3–5, not 15).** Each step is one thought group, with a sequence chunk: "First you…", "Then…", "Once you've…".
3. **A tip or warning.** The thing people get wrong: "The trick is…", "Make sure you…", "Don't forget to…".
4. **Done.** "And that's it." / "And you're done." This tells the listener the explanation is finished (just like the story endings in [Week 5, Day 4](../week-05-better-stories/day-4-endings-and-punchlines.md)).

**Use "you".** In explanations, "you" means "anyone", not the listener. "First you add the water" = "First, a person adds the water." It's more natural than "one" or "we" in casual speech.

**Use the present simple.** "You press the button, it turns green, and then it starts." Not "will press" or "are pressing".

**"Once you've…"** is a great chunk: it means "after you finish". "Once you've added the water, you close the lid."

**"The way it works is…" / "What happens is…"** are perfect for explaining how a *machine or system* works (not instructions): "What happens is, the app checks your location and finds the nearest driver."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So basically, it's a (thing) that… | big picture in one sentence | "So basically, it's a tool that turns your notes into tasks." |
| The way it works is… | you explain a system or machine | "The way it works is, you pay a monthly fee and borrow any bike." |
| What happens is… | you describe what happens inside, step by step | "What happens is, the water heats up and gets pushed through the coffee." |
| First you… | the first step | "First you open the app and sign in." |
| Then… / After that… | the next steps | "Then you pick a time. After that, you get a confirmation." |
| Once you've (done X)… | the next step starts after the last one finishes | "Once you've added everyone, just hit 'split'." |
| Make sure you… | something important not to forget | "Make sure you save it before you close the tab." |
| The trick is (to)… | the secret to doing it well | "The trick is to let the pan get really hot first." |
| Don't forget to… | a common mistake | "Don't forget to turn it off at the wall." |
| If you (do X), it'll… | you explain what happens in a certain case | "If you hold it for three seconds, it'll restart." |
| It's kind of like a… | you compare it to something familiar | "It's kind of like a group chat, but for files." |
| And that's it. / And you're done. | you signal the end | "Then you hit send, and that's it." |

### Examples

**Example 1: an app (big picture → steps → tip → done)**

> A: What's that app you always use for dinners?
> B: Oh, it's for splitting bills. So basically, the way it works is, one person pays, and the app figures out who owes what. First you make a group with your friends. Then you add the bill, and you can split it equally or by item. Once you've done that, everyone gets a notification. The trick is to add the bill right away, because otherwise you forget. And that's it.

**Example 2: a machine ("what happens is")**

> A: How does your coffee machine work? It's so fast.
> B: So what happens is, you put a little capsule in the top, right? And when you press the button, it pushes hot water through the capsule really fast. Make sure you put a cup there first, though. I've made that mistake. Twice.

**Example 3: at work, a design process**

> A: How do you guys test a new design?
> B: It's pretty simple, actually. First we make a quick prototype, like a clickable version. Then we find five people who use the product. Once they've tried it, we watch where they get stuck. The trick is to not help them. Like, at all. Even when it's painful. And then we fix the worst problems and test again.

**Example 4: a quick "if you…"**

> "It's kind of like a normal remote, but if you hold the round button, it'll turn on the voice thing. And if you press it twice, it goes back to the home screen."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ (starts with step 7) "So you go to settings, no wait, first…" | ✅ "So basically, it's a … First you…" | Big picture first, then steps in order. |
| ❌ First you will open the app, then you will click… | ✅ First you open the app, then you click… | Use the present simple for instructions. |
| ❌ After you finished it, you close it. | ✅ Once you've finished it, you close it. / After you finish it… | Present perfect ("once you've") or present, not past. |
| ❌ One must press the button. | ✅ You press the button. | "One" is very formal. Use "you". |
| ❌ Firstly, secondly, thirdly, fourthly… | ✅ First… then… after that… once you've… | Vary your sequence chunks. Numbered lists sound like a manual. |
| ❌ It's like how you say… a thing for… | ✅ It's kind of like a (calendar / group chat)… | Compare with something familiar to explain quickly. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Explain how to make your favourite meal"** (or drink, or snack) to a friend who has never cooked it.

**Rules for tonight:** big picture first, maximum 5 steps, at least **6 different** sequence/instruction chunks, one "The trick is…", and finish with "And that's it" or "And you're done." Speak in thought groups.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords (one per step).
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** You can add a short story about the first time you made it (Week 5 skills).
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you start with the big picture? Can you hear pauses between steps?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Big picture, 3 steps, the trick, done.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Sequence chunks count as connectors.

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "Explain how a tool or app you use at work works"** to someone who doesn't work in your field. 2 minutes, 1 minute, then 30 seconds with only the big picture and the trick.

2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory, explaining a different everyday thing.
3. **Say 5 phrases from Week 6 from memory**, one from each day (an opinion chunk, an agreement, a polite disagreement, a reason/example chunk, a hedge).
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
