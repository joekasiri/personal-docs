## Learning objectives

- Explain a decision with the shape **decision → options → what it came down to → reasons → how it turned out**.
- Say what you *didn't* choose and why.
- Use 10+ decision chunks ("We went with…", "It came down to…", "What tipped it was…").
- Reflect on a past decision ("Looking back, I'd…").

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: pausing before the reason.** When people explain a decision, there's often a small pause **just before the key reason**, and the reason gets strong stress. It's like a mini suspense moment (you practised those in [Week 5, Day 2](../week-05-better-stories/day-2-building-suspense.md)). `|` = small pause, `||` = longer pause.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each 3 times.

1. We went with the SMALLer one, | mainly because of the PRICE.
2. In the END, || it came down to LOCation.
3. What tipped it was | the COMmute.
4. We ended up going with BLUE. → "we ended up goin' with BLUE"
5. It just made more SENSE | to wait.
6. I was TORN between the TWO, | honestly.
7. The deciding FACtor was || the TEAM.
8. We decided aGAINST it | because it was too RISKy.
9. Looking BACK, | I'd do it exACTly the same.
10. It was a TOUGH call, | but I think it was the RIGHT one.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search for "why I quit my job vlog", "why I moved to another city", "why we chose this house vlog" or "decision explained podcast". Choose someone explaining a real choice in a relaxed way.

1. Find a 30–40 second part where they explain why.
2. Listen twice. Where's the pause before the reason?
3. Speak along 5 times.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the "because…" sentences, 5 times each.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Explaining decisions

### Explanation

"Why did you move here?" "Why did you choose that job?" "Why did your team go with that design?" These questions come up all the time, in small talk and at work. A one-word answer ("Money") sounds rude or flat. A ten-minute answer loses people.

A clear decision explanation has five parts, and you can make it short or long:

1. **The decision:** "We went with the cheaper apartment."
2. **The options:** "We were looking at two places, one downtown and one a bit further out."
3. **What it came down to:** "In the end, it came down to space."
4. **The reasons** (with an example): "The downtown one was tiny. Like, the bed was in the kitchen."
5. **How it turned out** (optional): "And honestly? Best decision we've made."

**Say what you gave up.** Real decisions have a downside. Saying it makes you sound honest and thoughtful: "The downside is the commute, but it's worth it." (You'll go deeper into trade-offs at work in Month 5. Tonight, one sentence is enough.)

**"We" or "I"?** At work, use "we" for team decisions: "We went with option B." Use "I" when it was your personal call: "I pushed for option B."

**Looking back.** Often people ask "Do you regret it?" or "Was it the right call?". Use "Looking back…" + your reflection: "Looking back, I'd probably do it again." / "Looking back, I should've waited." (Natural contractions like "should've" come in [Week 8](../week-08-automatic-answers/day-4-contractions-in-your-own-speech.md).)

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| We went with (X) because… | you say what you chose, casually | "We went with the blue one because it was easier to read." |
| We ended up going with… | the choice came after some thinking or a change of mind | "We ended up going with the smaller venue." |
| We were looking at (a few options)… | you list the options | "We were looking at three apartments, all pretty similar." |
| I was torn between (X) and (Y). | you found it hard to choose | "I was torn between Lisbon and Barcelona." |
| It came down to… | the one thing that decided it | "In the end, it came down to money." |
| What tipped it was… | the final reason that made you choose | "What tipped it was the garden." |
| The deciding factor was… | a bit more formal "what tipped it" | "The deciding factor was the team. I loved them." |
| It just made more sense to… | the choice was logical | "It just made more sense to fix the old one." |
| We decided against (X) because… | you explain what you didn't choose | "We decided against the app because it was too expensive." |
| The downside is…, but… | you admit what you gave up | "The downside is the commute, but the rent's half." |
| It was a tough call. | the decision was hard | "It was a tough call, but I think we got it right." |
| Looking back, I'd… / I should've… | you reflect on a past decision | "Looking back, I'd do it exactly the same." |

### Examples

**Example 1: personal decision, full shape**

> A: Why did you move to this neighbourhood?
> B: Honestly, it was a tough call. We were looking at two places, one right downtown and one here. The downtown one was super cool, but tiny. In the end, it came down to space. Like, here we have a second bedroom, so I can actually work from home without being in the kitchen. The downside is the commute, it's like forty minutes, but looking back, I'd totally do it again.

**Example 2: a team decision at work**

> A: Why did you guys drop the dark mode feature?
> B: So we were torn between dark mode and a better search. And what tipped it was the data. Like, people were searching like ten times a day, and nobody was asking for dark mode except the designers. Ha. So it just made more sense to fix search first. We'll probably come back to dark mode next year.

**Example 3: short version in small talk**

> A: Why'd you pick Spain for your holiday?
> B: Mainly the weather, honestly. We were thinking about Scotland, but I wanted sun. What tipped it was a cheap flight. Like, ninety bucks.

**Example 4: a decision you regret (with "looking back")**

> "I ended up going with the cheapest laptop, because I thought, OK, I just need it for email. Looking back, I should've spent a bit more. It's so slow I make coffee while it opens."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ We chose for the blue one. | ✅ We chose the blue one. / We went with the blue one. | "Choose" has no preposition. "Go with" is more casual. |
| ❌ At the end, we decided to stay. | ✅ In the end, we decided to stay. | "In the end" = finally. "At the end" = at the end *of* something (the film, the street). |
| ❌ It came down on price. | ✅ It came down to price. | "Come down to". |
| ❌ We decided to not go. | ✅ We decided not to go. / We decided against going. | "Decide not to" is the normal order. |
| ❌ The reason of our decision was… | ✅ The reason for our decision was… / The main reason was… | "Reason for", not "reason of". |
| ❌ Because money. (and stop) | ✅ Mainly because of money. Like, the other place was twice the price. | Add a reason chunk and an example. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "A big decision you made"** (moving city or country, changing job, choosing what to study, buying something big, starting English classes).

**Rules for tonight:** all five parts (decision, options, what it came down to, reasons with an example, how it turned out), at least **5 decision chunks**, one "The downside is…, but…", and a "Looking back…" at the end.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords, one per part.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** You can tell the story of the moment you decided (Week 5 skills).
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you say what you *didn't* choose?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** All five parts, short.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "A decision your team made at work"** (a tool, a design direction, a launch date, a hire). Use "we". 2 minutes, 1 minute, 30 seconds.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "We ended up going with…", "It came down to…", "What tipped it was…", "The downside is…, but…", "Looking back, I'd…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each from memory about a different decision (big or tiny: breakfast counts).
3. **Say Day 1's 5 phrases from memory**, explaining how something in your home works.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
