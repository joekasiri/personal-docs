## Learning objectives

- Open any story in one or two sentences that answer *when, where, who*.
- Use 8+ casual story openers without thinking ("So this was last summer…", "OK, so you know how…").
- Switch to the present tense to make a scene feel alive ("So I'm standing in line, right…").
- Cut background details that slow a story down.
- Try speak-along shadowing for the first time.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: fast, light story openings.** When Americans start a story, the opening words are quick and quiet, and the stress lands on the *time* or the *place*. The small words get squashed: "So this was" sounds like "so this w'z", "you know how" sounds like "y'know how", "I'm at" sounds like "I'm 'at".

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each sentence 3 times. Capitals = stressed. Keep everything else fast and light.

1. So this was LAST SUMmer. → "so this w'z LAST SUMmer"
2. OK, so you know how I was in CHIcago? → "okay so y'know how I w'z in chiCAgo"
3. So I'm at the AIRport, right? → "so I'm 'at the AIRport, right?"
4. This one time, me and my BROther… → "this WUN time, me 'n my BROther"
5. Picture this: it's like SIX a.m. → "PICture this: it's like SIX ay-em"
6. So a couple weeks ago, I'm at WORK… → "so a coupla weeks 'go, I'm at WORK"
7. Okay, so there's this guy on my TEAM… → "okay so there's this guy on my TEAM"
8. So it's FRIday night, and we're like, starving. → "so it's FRIday night, 'n we're like, STARving"
9. You remember that place on Fifth STREET? → "y'remember that place on Fifth STREET?"
10. So we get to the hoTEL, and it's like, totally EMPty. → "so we ged to the hoTEL, 'n it's like, toddally EMPty"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Find a casual storyteller clip. Search YouTube for "storytime vlog", "craziest thing that happened to me storytime" or "two friends podcast funny story". Choose a speaker who sounds relaxed, not like a presenter.

1. Listen to the first 30 seconds twice, without speaking.
2. Play it again and talk **at the same time** as the speaker. Don't pause. If you lose a word, mumble the melody and jump back in.
3. Do the same 30 seconds 5 times. You will be bad at it the first two times. That's normal.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the first 2–3 sentences of the story, where the speaker sets the scene. Notice how fast they get to the action.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Setting the scene

### Explanation

When you start a story, the listener needs three things very quickly: **when** it happened, **where** you were, and **who** was there. Sometimes they also need **one** piece of background ("I'd just moved there", "it was my first week"). That's it. Then you go straight into the action.

Many learners start too slowly. They give the date, the weather, the reason they were there, the name of the restaurant and the history of their friendship. By the time the problem arrives, the listener has stopped listening. In a group, someone will interrupt you. A good scene takes **one or two sentences**, about 5–10 seconds.

Three tricks make your opening sound natural:

1. **Start with a story signal.** "So…", "OK, so…", "This one time…". These words tell people "a story is coming, give me the floor". You don't need "I want to tell you a story".
2. **Use "this" and "there's" to bring people in.** "There's this guy at work…", "We went to this tiny restaurant…". Here "this" doesn't mean the one I'm pointing at. It means "a, and he/it is important to the story".
3. **Try the present tense.** In Month 1 you practised [past tenses in stories](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md). That is still correct. But in casual speech Americans often switch to the present to make the scene feel like it's happening now: "So I'm sitting at my desk, and my boss walks in…". Choose one: past or present, and stay with it for the story.

A fast scene formula: **signal + when + where/who + one detail → action.**

> "So this was last summer. Me and my sister are driving to Vegas, it's like 110 degrees, and the AC just dies."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So this was (last summer / a few years back)… | you start with the time | "So this was a few years back, when I was still in college." |
| OK, so you know how… | the listener already knows part of the background | "OK, so you know how I've been looking for a new apartment?" |
| This one time… | it's a single, memorable event | "This one time, I got locked out of my own office." |
| So a couple (of) weeks ago… | it happened recently | "So a couple weeks ago, I'm at the grocery store…" |
| Picture this: … | you want the listener to imagine the scene | "Picture this: it's 6 a.m., it's pouring, and I've got no umbrella." |
| So I'm (at / in / on)… | you use the present tense to put us in the scene | "So I'm on the train, half asleep…" |
| There's this guy / girl / woman (at work / in my building)… | you introduce a new person | "There's this guy in my building who always takes the elevator one floor." |
| We were at this (tiny / fancy / sketchy) place… | you introduce a place | "We were at this tiny place by the beach." |
| Me and (my friend) were / are… | casual way to say who was there | "Me and my roommate were trying to fix the sink." |
| It was my first (week / day / time)… | one key background fact | "It was my first week at the new job, so I didn't know anyone." |
| Back when I (lived in… / worked at…)… | the story is from an older part of your life | "Back when I worked at the agency, we had this crazy client." |
| So it's (Friday night / my birthday)… | present-tense time setting | "So it's Friday night, and everyone's already left." |

### Examples

**Example 1: too slow vs fast**

Slow (35 seconds before anything happens):
> "OK, I want to tell you a story. It was in July of last year. I went to Lisbon with my friend Sara. We have been friends since university. We stayed in a hotel near the centre. The weather was very hot. On the second day, we went to a restaurant…"

Fast (6 seconds):
> "So this was last summer in Lisbon. Me and my friend Sara walk into this tiny restaurant, and the waiter just looks at us and goes, 'No.'"

**Example 2: a mini dialogue at work**

> A: How was your week?
> B: Honestly, kinda crazy. OK, so you know how we launched the new checkout page on Monday?
> A: Yeah?
> B: So I'm in a meeting with the whole leadership team, right, and my phone starts buzzing like nonstop.
> A: Uh-oh.
> B: Yeah. Turns out nobody could pay for anything.

**Example 3: introducing a person**

> A: Anything funny happen lately?
> B: Oh man, yes. There's this guy on the floor below us, right? Super quiet, always wears the same gray hoodie. So last Thursday, I'm in the elevator with him, and he just turns to me and goes, "Do you like magic?"
> A: No way. What did you say?

**Example 4: "back when"**

> "Back when I was in college, I had this roommate who only ate cereal. Like, breakfast, lunch, dinner. So one night, we run out of milk…"

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ I want to tell you a story about my trip. | ✅ So this one time on my trip… | Announcing the story sounds formal. A story signal is enough. |
| ❌ One day, there was a man in my office. | ✅ So there's this guy in my office… | "One day, there was" sounds like a children's book. |
| ❌ In the last summer, we went to Spain. | ✅ Last summer, we went to Spain. | No "in the" before last / next / this + time. |
| ❌ Me and my friend was at a bar. | ✅ Me and my friend were at a bar. | Two people = "were". ("My friend and I were" is also fine.) |
| ❌ So I was sitting there, and my boss walks in and he said… | ✅ So I'm sitting there, and my boss walks in and goes… | Pick one tense for the scene and stay with it. |
| ❌ It was hot, it was July, we were tired, the hotel was far, the food was… | ✅ It was like 100 degrees and we were starving. | One or two details only. The rest slows the story down. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Tonight is your first **3/2/1 drill**: tell the same story in 3 minutes, then 2, then 1, recording each round. Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "A trip that didn't go to plan."** Use a real trip if you can (a holiday, a work trip, a weekend away).

**Rule for tonight:** your opening must be **two sentences or fewer** and must include when, where and who.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Write a maximum of 5 keywords. Write your opening sentence in full, once, then cover it.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** Start with your opening. Use at least 3 of tonight's key phrases.
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Listen to the first 20 seconds only. How long did it take you to get to the problem? Write the time down.
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).** Same story. Shorter scene, same best details.
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Your scene must now take under 10 seconds.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min).** Count the 3 numbers: umms, pauses over 2 seconds, different connectors. See [The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md).

**Prompt B (if you have about 15 minutes left): "Your first day at a job or school."** Do 2 minutes, then 1 minute, then a 30-second "speed version" with only the scene and the most surprising moment.

## 4. Review (15 min)

From this month, you say your phrases **from memory**, not by reading them.

1. **Log 5 phrases** from tonight in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "So this was…", "OK, so you know how…", "Picture this…", "There's this guy…", "So I'm at…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say each of tonight's 5 phrases out loud in a new sentence about your life. If you can't remember one, look, then cover it again and try once more.
3. **Say 5 phrases from Week 4** from memory (for example a phrasal verb, a collocation, an idiom, an intensifier, a job phrase). Check afterwards.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers (from round 3) in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
