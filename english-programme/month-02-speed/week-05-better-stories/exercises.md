# Week 5 exercises: Better stories
[Week 5 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-setting-the-scene.md) · [Day 2](day-2-building-suspense.md) · [Day 3](day-3-casual-reported-speech.md) · [Day 4](day-4-endings-and-punchlines.md) · [Day 5](day-5-retelling-films-and-shows.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** do everything **out loud**. Reading silently doesn't count. When an exercise says "record", record on your phone and listen back once. Write only when the exercise tells you to, and then always say what you wrote. Model answers are in the answer key for each day. Yours don't have to match them. They show one natural way to say it.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: Opener 5 times
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. For each opener below, say **5 different openings** about your own life. Each opening is 1–2 sentences and ends just before the action.
2. Don't stop to think. If nothing comes, invent something.
3. Say each opening at a fast, light speed, like the Day 1 shadowing.

| # | Opener | Ideas to get you started |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | So this was (last summer / a few years back)… | a holiday, a move, a job interview, a wedding, a concert |
| 2 | OK, so you know how… | your new apartment, a project at work, your diet, your car, your neighbour |
| 3 | This one time… | locked out, lost a phone, wrong train, met someone famous-ish, got sick abroad |
| 4 | There's this (guy / woman) (at work / in my building / at the gym)… | a strange habit, a loud voice, always late, always happy |
| 5 | Picture this: … | early morning, heavy rain, a crowded bus, an empty office, a dead phone |
| 6 | So I'm (at / in / on)… | the airport, a meeting, the subway, my desk, a café |

Example for opener 3: "This one time, I got on the right train going the wrong way. It was my first week in Berlin, and I was already late."

### Exercise 1.2: Cut it down
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each opening below is too slow. Rewrite it as **one or two fast sentences** with a story signal, when, where, who, and maximum one extra detail.
2. Say your version out loud 3 times, faster each time.
3. Compare with the answer key.

1. "I want to tell you about something that happened. It was in March. It was a Tuesday. I was at work. I work in an office in the city centre. My colleague, his name is Tom, we have worked together for three years, came to my desk."
2. "OK, so the story is about my trip to Rome. I went there two years ago with my wife. We stayed for one week. The hotel was near the train station. It was quite cheap. One evening we went to dinner."
3. "There was a day when I was going to the gym. The gym is near my house, about ten minutes on foot. I go there three times a week usually. On that day there was a man at the gym."
4. "So, it was last year, I think in October or maybe November, I don't remember exactly, and I had a job interview at a big tech company, and I was very nervous because it was my first interview in English."
5. "This story is from when I was a student. I studied design at university. I lived in a small apartment with two other students. One of them was called Leo. One night Leo came home very late."
6. "I was on the subway. It was in the morning. It was very crowded, because it was rush hour. I was standing. I was holding my coffee. The train stopped suddenly."
7. "It was my birthday. I was turning thirty. My friends organised a party. The party was at a restaurant. The restaurant was Italian. I arrived at the restaurant at eight."
8. "Let me tell you about my neighbour. My neighbour is an old man. He lives next to me. He has lived there for a long time. He has a dog. One day the dog came into my apartment."

### Exercise 1.3: Past to present
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last 3

1. Each scene below is in the past tense. Retell it **in the present tense**, starting with "So…".
2. Say it twice. The second time, add one detail of your own.
3. Record scenes 4, 5 and 6 and listen. Did you stay in the present the whole time?

1. I was sitting at my desk, and my manager walked in and asked if I had a minute.
2. We were driving to the beach, and the GPS told us to turn left into a lake.
3. I was waiting for my coffee, and the barista called a name that sounded a bit like mine.
4. It was 2 a.m., and I heard someone knocking on my door.
5. I was presenting to the whole team, and I noticed my screen was showing my messages.
6. We got to the hotel, and the lady at the desk looked at us and started laughing.

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Add the suspense
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each story below is told flat, with the surprise too early. Retell it with **a fast scene, a suspense phrase, a silent pause (||), then the surprise**.
2. Use a different suspense phrase for each one (see the [Day 2 key phrases](day-2-building-suspense.md)).
3. Say each version twice: once slowly, once at real speed. Keep the pause silent.

1. My flight was cancelled when I got to the airport.
2. My friend who said she was sick was at the same concert as me.
3. I spent two hours on a design, and the client chose the first, simple version.
4. I found a hundred dollars in an old jacket.
5. The new intern was the CEO's son.
6. The restaurant we drove an hour to was closed.
7. I sent a message about my boss to my boss.
8. The quiet guy in accounting is a professional DJ.

### Exercise 2.2: Guess what? dialogues
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** gap-fill, then read both parts out loud

1. Fill each gap with a suspense phrase. Use each phrase once: *Guess what? · You're not gonna believe this. · So get this: · Next thing I know · And who do you think*
2. Read the dialogue out loud, both parts. Pause after each suspense phrase.

**Dialogue 1**
> A: How was the job interview?
> B: (1) ______ They offered me the job. In the interview!
> A: No way! Congrats!

**Dialogue 2**
> A: Did you get the tickets?
> B: OK, (2) ______ I'm online at 10 a.m. exactly, ready to click, and there are already 50,000 people in front of me.
> A: Oh no.

**Dialogue 3**
> A: How was the party?
> B: Weird. So I'm in the kitchen, getting a drink. (3) ______ is standing next to the fridge? My old high school teacher.
> A: Wait, really?

**Dialogue 4**
> A: Did you finish the report?
> B: Well, I sat down on the couch "for five minutes" after dinner. (4) ______, it's midnight.
> A: Ha! Classic.

**Dialogue 5**
> A: What's up? You look shocked.
> B: (5) ______ My landlord is selling the building. To my boss.
> A: Wait, what?

### Exercise 2.3: Speed control
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record

1. For each build-up below, say it **three ways**: (a) all fast, (b) all slow, (c) **fast build-up + slow key line + 1-second silent pause before the surprise**.
2. Version (c) is the goal. Record your version (c) for every line, then listen to all six in a row.
3. Pick your best one and say it 5 more times.

1. "We've been driving for six hours, everyone's tired, the kids are asleep, and then… || the car makes this noise."
2. "So I finally get the courage to ask her out, I walk over, I open my mouth, and… || her boyfriend walks in."
3. "The demo's going perfectly, the client's nodding, my boss is smiling, and that's when… || the app crashes."
4. "I'm thinking, OK, it's just a quick haircut, no big deal. Next thing I know… || I have no eyebrows."
5. "The house is super quiet, I'm alone, it's like midnight, and all of a sudden… || the doorbell rings."
6. "I check my bank app, just a normal Monday, and guess what? || I've been charged for twelve gym memberships."

### Answer key

**Exercise 2.1 (model answers):**

1. "So I get to the airport super early for once, I'm feeling great, and guess what? || Cancelled."
2. "So I'm at this concert, right, and who do you think is two rows in front of me? || My friend. The one who was 'too sick' to come."
3. "So I spend two whole hours on this design, all the details, perfect. And then… || the client goes, 'Actually, we like the first one.'"
4. "So I'm cleaning out my closet, and I pick up this old jacket, and you're not gonna believe this. || There's a hundred bucks in the pocket."
5. "So there's this new intern, super nice guy, right? And so get this: || he's the CEO's son."
6. "We drive like an hour to this amazing restaurant, we're starving, we park, and… || it's closed."
7. "So I write this message complaining about my boss, I hit send, and that's when I see the name. || I sent it to my boss."
8. "There's this quiet guy in accounting, never says a word. Little did I know… || he's a professional DJ on weekends."

**Exercise 2.2:** (1) Guess what? (2) So get this: (3) And who do you think (4) Next thing I know (5) You're not gonna believe this.

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: Formal to casual
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** write then say with the voice

1. Turn each formal reported sentence into a **casual direct quote** with *be like, go, be all* or *say*. Use at least three different verbs across the 10 sentences.
2. Say each one out loud, changing your voice for the quote.

1. She told me that she was too tired to come.
2. He asked me whether I had finished the slides.
3. I told him that it wasn't a problem.
4. My mum asked me why I hadn't called her.
5. The waiter informed us that the kitchen was closed.
6. My manager said that he had been doing this job for twenty years and knew best (and he said it in an annoying way).
7. I asked her if she was serious.
8. He told me that he would be there in five minutes.
9. I thought that it was going to be a disaster.
10. The airline told me that they couldn't help me.

### Exercise 3.2: Act the conversation
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** out loud, record the last one

1. Read each short story below out loud, **acting the quotes**: a different voice for each person.
2. Then tell it again **without reading**, in your own words. Keep at least 3 quotes.
3. Record your own-words version of Story C.

**Story A: The neighbour**
> So I'm carrying all my groceries up the stairs, right, and my neighbour opens her door and goes, "Oh, you're home! Can you help me move a couch?" And I'm like, "Uh… right now?" And she's all, "It'll take two minutes." Two minutes. It took two hours. And at the end she goes, "Thanks, sweetie, see you next week!" And I was like… [stares]. Next week?

**Story B: The feedback meeting**
> So my manager calls me into this meeting, and I'm thinking, "OK, this is it, I'm getting fired." And he sits down, and he goes, "So… about your presentation yesterday." And I'm like, "Yeah?" And he's like, "Everyone loved it. We want you to present it to the board." And I was just like… [silence]. I couldn't even say anything.

**Story C: The taxi**
> So I get in this taxi, and the driver goes, "Where to?" And I say, "The airport, please, I'm kinda late." And he's all, "Don't worry. I'm the fastest driver in this city." And I'm thinking, "Please don't be the fastest driver in this city." Then halfway there, he turns around and goes, "Wait, which airport?"

### Exercise 3.3: Thought or words?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, rapid

1. For each situation, react **out loud** with a quote. Use "And I'm like, '…'" for something you said, "And I'm thinking, '…'" for something you only thought.
2. For situation 8, use only a sound or a face after "like".
3. Go fast: about 30 seconds per situation. Do the whole list twice.

1. Your friend says she's moving to Australia next week. (you said something)
2. Your boss asks, "Can you stay late tonight?" You smile, but inside… (thought)
3. The waiter brings you a dish you didn't order. (you said something)
4. In a meeting, a colleague presents your idea as his own. (thought)
5. Your little brother says he crashed your car. (you said something)
6. You open your exam results and see a very low score. (thought)
7. A stranger on the street says, "I love your shoes!" (you said something)
8. Your roommate eats your last piece of pizza in front of you. (only a sound / face)

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.1 (model answers):**

1. "And she's like, 'I'm way too tired, I'm not coming.'"
2. "And he goes, 'Did you finish the slides?'"
3. "And I was like, 'No problem.'"
4. "And my mom's like, 'Why didn't you call me?'"
5. "And the waiter goes, 'Sorry, guys, kitchen's closed.'"
6. "And he was all, 'I've been doing this for twenty years, I know what I'm doing.'"
7. "And I was like, 'Are you serious?'"
8. "And he goes, 'I'll be there in five minutes.'"
9. "And I'm thinking, 'This is gonna be a disaster.'"
10. "And the airline was like, 'Sorry, we can't help you.'"

**Exercise 3.3 (model answers):**

1. "And I'm like, 'Wait, what? Next week?'"
2. "And I'm thinking, 'Please, not tonight.'"
3. "And I'm like, 'Sorry, I don't think this is mine.'"
4. "And I'm thinking, 'Excuse me? That was my idea.'"
5. "And I'm like, 'You did WHAT?'"
6. "And I'm thinking, 'OK, don't panic.'"
7. "And I'm like, 'Oh, thanks! They were on sale.'"
8. "And I'm just like… [open mouth, slow head shake]."

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: Give it an ending
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Each story below ends weakly ("…and yeah, that's it"). Add a **strong ending**: a result, a punchline, or a lesson, using a phrase from the [Day 4 key phrases](day-4-endings-and-punchlines.md). Use a different phrase each time.
2. Say the last sentence of the story + your ending out loud, with a pause before the ending and a falling voice.

1. "I tried to fix my own sink with a video tutorial, and water went everywhere, and the downstairs neighbour came up, and… yeah, that's it."
2. "I said yes to a blind date my friend organised, and the guy turned out to be my dentist, and… so, umm, yeah."
3. "We waited three hours for a table at this famous burger place, and the burger was… just a normal burger, so… that's the story."
4. "I accidentally called my new boss 'Mom' in my first meeting, and everyone heard, and… I don't know, that's it."
5. "I booked a 'sea view' room, and the view was a car park with a tiny blue corner in the distance, and… yeah."
6. "I practised my presentation fifty times, and on the day the projector didn't work, so I presented with no slides, and… it was OK, I think, so…"
7. "My friend told me the party was fancy dress, so I went as a banana, and nobody else was dressed up, and… yeah, so."
8. "I left my phone in a taxi, and I called it from my friend's phone, and the driver answered and drove back, and… that's all."

### Exercise 4.2: Punchline timing
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud, record the best 3

1. Say each set-up + punchline. Pause (||) before the punchline. Make the punchline short, with a falling voice (↘). Then **stop** for 2 seconds.
2. Say each one 3 times. Record your best 3 and listen.

1. "I finally got a reservation at the best restaurant in town. || For 2029." ↘
2. "My dad tried to send me a selfie. || I got 40 photos of his ear." ↘
3. "I spent all weekend building a bookshelf. || It's now a very expensive table." ↘
4. "The client loved the new logo. || The one I made as a joke." ↘
5. "I told my kid we were going somewhere fun. || It was the dentist." ↘
6. "We trained for the marathon for six months. || My friend took a cab for the last mile." ↘
7. "I finally learned to cook my mom's famous soup. || She uses a can." ↘
8. "I said I'd only watch one episode. || It's 4 a.m." ↘

### Exercise 4.3: Full story check
**Time:** 15 min · **Type:** record and score

1. Choose one story: **"The worst haircut, outfit or style decision you ever made"** (or use any of your own stories).
2. Tell it in about **90 seconds** and record it.
3. Listen and score yourself with the checklist (1 point each).
4. Tell it again, fixing your lowest point. Record and score again. This is the story you'll tell Claude first in tonight's voice session.

| ✔ | Checklist | Score |
|---|---|---|
| | Story signal at the start ("So…", "This one time…") | |
| | Scene in under 10 seconds (when, where, who) | |
| | At least one suspense phrase with a silent pause | |
| | At least two casual quotes (*be like, go, be all, say*) | |
| | A voice change for at least one quote | |
| | A clear ending phrase (result, punchline or lesson) | |
| | Stopped after the ending (no extra details) | |
| | Under 3 umms | |
| **Total** | | **/ 8** |

### Answer key

**Exercise 4.1 (model endings):**

1. "…and the downstairs neighbour comes up. || Needless to say, I called a plumber. Lesson learned."
2. "…and the guy turns out to be my dentist. || And the worst part? I had a check-up with him the next week."
3. "…and the burger was just a normal burger. Moral of the story: never wait three hours for anything."
4. "…and everyone heard. || To this day, half the team still calls me 'Junior'."
5. "…and a tiny blue corner in the distance. So now I always check the photos people post, not the hotel's."
6. "…so I presented with no slides. Long story short, it went better than with slides. I still can't believe it."
7. "…and nobody else was dressed up. || And that's how I ended up being 'the banana guy' for the rest of college."
8. "…and the driver actually drove back. I still can't believe it. So yeah, there are good people out there."

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Present-tense plots
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud

1. Each plot line is in the past tense. Say it in the **present simple**, as you would when retelling a film.
2. Say it again faster, starting with "So basically…".

1. A young teacher moved to a tiny island where nobody wanted children to go to school.
2. Two brothers who hated each other had to drive across the country together.
3. A robot that worked in a hospital started to have feelings.
4. A detective found out that the killer was her own neighbour.
5. A chef lost his sense of taste the week before the biggest competition of his life.
6. A family won the lottery, and then everything started to go wrong.
7. An old woman decided to run a marathon at the age of eighty.
8. A group of friends went camping, and one of them didn't come back.

### Exercise 5.2: Retell an invented show
**Time:** 20 min · **Type:** out loud, record each

1. Below are 4 invented shows and films (they don't exist, so there's nothing to look up). Read the notes, then **cover them** and retell each one in about **1 minute**.
2. Use the 4-part structure: **what it is → setup → hook → your take**. Use the present tense, one "I won't spoil it, but…" or similar, and end with a recommendation and a question.
3. Record each retelling. After all four, listen to the one you liked least and redo it.

**A. *Platform 9* (TV series, 8 episodes)**
- Type: mystery / thriller, slow burn
- Setup: a train driver in a small German town notices the same woman waiting on platform 9 every night, but the last train left years ago.
- Hook: he starts to find old newspaper photos of her… from 1975.
- Twist (spoiler, only if asked): she's his grandmother, and the town has been hiding what happened to her.
- Your take: amazing atmosphere, a bit slow in episodes 2–3, great ending.

**B. *Second Shift* (film, 2 hours)**
- Type: comedy / drama
- Setup: a stressed hospital nurse in Ohio accidentally swaps lives with her lazy twin sister for one week.
- Hook: the sister has to work night shifts in the ER; the nurse has to deal with the sister's chaotic family.
- Twist (spoiler): the lazy sister turns out to be really good at the job.
- Your take: really funny, a little predictable, perfect for a Friday night.

**C. *Base Camp* (reality show, 10 episodes)**
- Type: reality competition, like a survival show meets a cooking show
- Setup: twelve strangers live in tents on a mountain and have to cook a three-course meal every day with whatever they find.
- Hook: every episode, one person is sent down the mountain.
- Twist (spoiler): the winner is the person who said in episode 1 they couldn't cook.
- Your take: silly but super binge-able; you watched it all in two days.

**D. *The Quiet Office* (film, 1 hour 40 minutes)**
- Type: horror / workplace satire
- Setup: a young designer starts a dream job at a tech start-up where nobody ever talks, everyone just smiles and types.
- Hook: anyone who speaks in a meeting disappears the next day.
- Twist (spoiler): the "company" is actually an experiment run by the designer's own app.
- Your take: weird, a bit too long, but you can't stop thinking about it; you'd recommend it to people who work in tech.

### Exercise 5.3: Spoiler or no spoiler?
**Time:** 10 min · **Type:** out loud role-play (say both parts)

1. Read situation A's line, then answer as B. Decide each time: **spoil, ask first, or tease without spoiling**.
2. Use a different phrase each time: *I won't spoil it, but… · Without giving too much away… · Spoiler alert… · Wait, have you finished it? · No spoilers, I promise, but…*

1. A: "I'm about to start *Platform 9*. Is it good?"
2. A: "I watched the first episode of *Base Camp* last night. Who wins? Actually, no, don't tell me!"
3. A: "I finished *Second Shift*! What did you think of the ending?"
4. A: "My sister keeps telling me to watch *The Quiet Office*. What's it about?"
5. A: "I gave up on *Platform 9* after episode 2. It was so slow. Does it get better?"
6. A: "I don't care about spoilers. Just tell me what happens at the end of *The Quiet Office*."

## Day 6 materials

### Re-record test prompt

> **"Tell me about the most surprising thing that's happened to you in the last year."**

Unprepared. Start talking within 5 seconds. Record exactly 2 minutes.

### Story checklist (tick while you listen to your recording)

| ✔ | Did I… |
|---|---|
| | start with a story signal ("So…", "This one time…", "OK, so you know how…")? |
| | set the scene in under 10 seconds? |
| | use at least one suspense phrase ("Guess what?", "Next thing I know…")? |
| | make a silent pause before the surprise (no "umm")? |
| | report at least two lines with *be like / go / be all / say*? |
| | change my voice for a quote? |
| | finish with a clear ending phrase and stop? |
| | stay in one main tense (past or present)? |

Score: count the ticks (out of 8). Write it in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) next to your 3 numbers.
