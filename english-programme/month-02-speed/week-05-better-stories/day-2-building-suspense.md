## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: slowing down and pausing for suspense.** Good storytellers speed up for boring parts and **slow down** for the important line. Just before the surprise, they **stop for about one second**. That silence is not a mistake. It's the most powerful part of the story. In the sentences below, `|` = a short pause and `||` = a 1-second suspense pause. Capitals = stressed, and the stressed words get *longer*.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each sentence 3 times.

1. And then… || the LIGHTS go out.
2. So I open the door, | and guess WHO'S standing there?
3. Everything's FINE, | everything's FINE, || and then I hear this NOISE.
4. You're not gonna beLIEVE this. → "y'r not gonna b'LIEVE this"
5. So get THIS. || She already KNEW.
6. Next thing I KNOW, | I'm on the FLOOR.
7. And I'm thinking, | OK, no big DEAL, || right?
8. And that's when I SAW it.
9. Out of NOWHERE, || this DOG just runs into the office.
10. So I check my PHONE, | and it's my BOSS. || Fifteen missed CALLS.

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube for "scary storytime", "crazy story time" or "worst day ever storytime". Choose a clip where the speaker builds up to a surprise.

1. Find the 30–40 seconds just before the big moment of the story.
2. Listen twice. Mark (in your head) where the speaker slows down and where they stop.
3. Speak along 5 times. Copy the pauses exactly: if they stop, you stop.
4. Last 3 minutes: speak along with only the final build-up sentence and the surprise line, 10 times. Make your pause the same length as theirs.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Building suspense

### Explanation

Suspense is the feeling of "what happens next?". In a casual story you don't need a crime or a ghost to create it. You only need to **hold back the surprise for a few seconds** and give the listener small signals that something is coming.

There are four simple tools:

1. **Warning phrases.** Tell the listener that something big is coming: "You're not gonna believe this", "So get this", "And here's the thing".
2. **False calm.** Say that everything was normal, so the change feels bigger: "Everything's fine, everything's normal, and then…".
3. **Questions to the listener.** "Guess what?", "And who do you think walks in?". You don't wait for a real answer. Usually you pause for a second and then answer yourself. If they do guess, great, say "Close!" or "Nope!".
4. **Speed and silence.** Fast build-up, then slow down, then a one-second pause, then the surprise. This is where your shadowing practice goes.

Why this helps "talking without thinking": suspense phrases are **ready-made chunks**. While you say "And then, out of nowhere…", your brain has two or three free seconds to find the next words. Natives do this all the time. It sounds exciting, but it's also a hidden time-buyer (you learned more of those in [Week 3: time-buyers](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md)).

Don't use suspense for every sentence. **One or two suspense moments per story** is perfect. More than that, and it sounds like a TV advert.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| And then… (+ pause) | the simplest way to build up to something | "We're almost home, and then… the car just stops." |
| Guess what? | you want the listener to wonder for a second | "So I finally get to the front of the line, and guess what? They're sold out." |
| You're not gonna believe this. | the next part is surprising or crazy | "You're not gonna believe this. He's my new boss." |
| So get this: … | you're about to give the key surprising fact | "So get this: the 'small' meeting has like forty people in it." |
| Next thing I know… | something happens very fast, without warning | "I sit down for one second, and next thing I know, it's 3 a.m." |
| All of a sudden… | a sudden change | "All of a sudden, everyone's phone starts ringing." |
| And that's when… | you reach the key moment | "And that's when I realised I'd sent it to the whole company." |
| Everything's fine, and then… | false calm before the change | "Everything's going great, and then the client joins the call." |
| And who do you think (walks in / calls / is sitting there)? | a question that makes the listener guess | "And who do you think is sitting at the next table? My ex." |
| I'm thinking, OK, no big deal… | you show you didn't expect a problem (but it came) | "I'm thinking, OK, no big deal, it's just a small bug." |
| Little did I know… | slightly dramatic, often used as a joke | "Little did I know, that was the easy part." |
| Wait for it… | you're making the listener wait on purpose, usually funny | "He orders a salad, a steak, and… wait for it… a glass of milk." |

### Examples

**Example 1: flat vs with suspense**

Flat:
> "I was presenting to the client and my cat walked on the keyboard and ended the call."

With suspense:
> "So I'm presenting to the client, right? Big deal, our biggest account. Everything's going great, they love it. And then… || I see this little gray tail. And next thing I know, my cat walks right across the keyboard. And guess what? The call's gone. I ended it. For everyone."

**Example 2: a dialogue with a listener who guesses**

> A: So I finally get to the gate, totally out of breath. And guess what?
> B: Missed it?
> A: Nope! It's delayed. Four hours.
> B: Oh no.
> A: And that's not even the worst part. So get this: they move us to a different gate… in another terminal.
> B: No way.

**Example 3: "Who do you think…"**

> "So we're at this tiny café in the middle of nowhere, like, a three-hour drive from home. And who do you think walks in? || My manager. In shorts. With his whole family."

**Example 4: false calm at work**

> "Friday afternoon, everyone's relaxed, I'm thinking, OK, nice quiet weekend. Little did I know… || at 4:55 the client sends a message: 'Quick question.' And it was not a quick question."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Suddenly, suddenly, and then suddenly… | ✅ All of a sudden… / Next thing I know… / Out of nowhere… | Vary your suspense phrases. Repeating one word kills the effect. |
| ❌ Guess what? [waits 10 seconds for an answer] | ✅ Guess what? [1-second pause] They're sold out. | It's not a real quiz. Pause briefly, then answer yourself. |
| ❌ You will not believe it. | ✅ You're not gonna believe this. | The fixed chunk is "You're not gonna believe this". |
| ❌ And then… umm… uhh… the lights go out. | ✅ And then… [silent pause] the lights go out. | A suspense pause is *silent*. "Umm" sounds like you forgot. |
| ❌ The next thing I knew it, I was on the floor. | ✅ Next thing I know / knew, I'm / I was on the floor. | No "it" after "know". |
| ❌ Telling the surprise first: "My boss saw my messages. So I was in the kitchen…" | ✅ "So I'm in the kitchen… and that's when my boss sees my messages." | Keep the surprise for the end of the build-up. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "A time something went wrong at work"** (a bug, a missed deadline, a meeting that went badly, a message sent to the wrong person). If you've never had one, use school or a part-time job.

**Rules for tonight:** fast scene (from Day 1) + **at least two** suspense moments + **one** real silent pause before the problem.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords. Decide where your suspense moment goes and which phrase you'll use. Write only that phrase.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Find your suspense pause. Was it silent, or did you say "umm"?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).** Keep both suspense moments.
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Keep at least one suspense moment, even though time is short.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). Compare with yesterday's round 3.

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "A time you got lost, locked out, or stuck somewhere."** 2 minutes, then 1 minute, then a 30-second version that starts with "You're not gonna believe this."

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "Guess what?", "You're not gonna believe this", "So get this", "Next thing I know…", "And that's when…".
2. **Cover the log.** Say tonight's 5 phrases from memory, each in a new sentence.
3. **Say Day 1's 5 phrases from memory** ("So this was…", "OK, so you know how…" and the others), each starting a different mini-story.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
