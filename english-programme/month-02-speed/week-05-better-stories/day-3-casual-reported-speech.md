**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 20 min · Main skill 40 min · Speaking drill 45 min · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Do [Day 1](day-1-setting-the-scene.md) and [Day 2](day-2-building-suspense.md) first. Tonight's stories use a fast scene and one suspense moment.
- In Month 1 you met "I was like…" and "And he goes… / And she goes…" as two of [the 12 connectors](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md). Tonight you learn the whole system around them, so you can report a full conversation.
- Have a storyteller clip with lots of dialogue ready (search terms in section 1).

## Learning objectives

- Report what people said with *be like*, *go*, *be all* and *say*, in past or present.
- Report your own thoughts and reactions ("and I'm like, 'Oh no'").
- Use a sound, a face or a gesture after "like" when there are no words.
- Change your voice a little for each person, so the listener knows who's talking.

## 1. Shadowing (20 min)

**Tonight's feature: changing your voice for quotes.** When Americans report speech, they **act it**. The voice jumps up or down for the other person, gets slower for a sarcastic line, or gets louder for an angry one. There's often a tiny pause before the quote, like opening quotation marks. "I was like" is said very fast and low ("I w'z like"), then the quote is said in a new voice.

**Step 1: pause-and-repeat warm-up (5 min).** Say each sentence 3 times. `|` = tiny pause before the quote. Exaggerate the voice change. It will feel silly. That's correct.

1. And I was like, | "Are you SERIOUS?" → "an' I w'z like, are you SERious?" (voice goes up)
2. And she goes, | "Oh, I didn't see your EMAIL." (sweet, innocent voice)
3. He was ALL, | "I'm the MANager here." (slow, proud voice)
4. And I'm just like… | [big sigh] (no words, just a sound)
5. So I said, | "Look, it's FINE." (calm, flat)
6. And my mom's like, | "You're not EATing enough." (worried voice)
7. And the guy goes, | "Sir, this is a BAKery." (bored voice)
8. And I'm THINKing, | "OK, don't PANic." (quiet, fast)
9. And they were like, | "Nope. Not today." (firm, short)
10. And I was like, | "Wait, what?" → "an' I w'z like, wait, WHAT?"

**Step 2: speak-along (15 min).** Search YouTube for "storytime conversation with my mom", "funny customer storytime", "roommate drama storytime". Choose a clip where the speaker quotes other people a lot.

1. Find a 30–40 second part with at least 3 quotes.
2. Listen twice. Notice how the voice changes for each person.
3. Speak along 5 times. Copy the voice changes, not only the words.
4. Last 3 minutes: pause the clip after each quote and repeat **only the quote**, in the same voice, 3 times.

More on the method: [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Casual reported speech

### Explanation

In grammar books, reported speech looks like this: *She told me that she had already sent the email.* That's correct, and you'll still use it at work. But in a casual story, Americans almost always use **direct quotes** with special casual verbs. It's faster (no tense changes) and more alive (you act it out).

The four casual quote verbs:

| Verb | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **be like** | the most common. Reports words, thoughts, feelings or a sound. Doesn't have to be the exact words | "And I was like, 'No way.'" |
| **go** | reports words, often other people's. Very common in the present tense | "And she goes, 'Is that your car?'" |
| **be all** | reports words *and* attitude, often a bit annoying or dramatic | "He was all, 'I've been doing this for twenty years.'" |
| **say** | neutral, a bit more serious. Good for the important line | "So I said, 'I'm not doing it.'" |

**Tense:** match the tense of your story. If you're telling it in the present (Day 1), use *I'm like / she goes / he's all*. If you're telling it in the past, use *I was like / she went / he was all*. With "go", most people use the present even in a past story: "So I walked in, and she goes, 'You're late.'"

**Thoughts:** "be like" also reports what you **thought**, not only what you said. "And I'm like, 'Please don't call on me.'" The listener understands from your voice whether you said it or just thought it. If you need to be clear, use "And I'm thinking…".

**No words:** after "like", you can make a sound, a face or a gesture. "And he was just like… [shrugs]", "And I was like… [long sigh]". This is very natural and it saves you from finding words.

**Not exact:** "be like" can give the *general meaning*: "And she was like, she couldn't come because her kid was sick." This is half reported, half direct, and it's fine in casual speech.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| And I was like, "…" | you report your words or reaction in a past-tense story | "And I was like, 'Are you kidding me?'" |
| And I'm like, "…" | the same, in a present-tense story | "And I'm like, 'Dude, it's 2 a.m.'" |
| And she goes, "…" / And he goes, "…" | you report someone else's words | "And he goes, 'Can you keep a secret?'" |
| And I go, "…" | you report your own words with "go" (very casual) | "And I go, 'Sure, why not?'" |
| He was all, "…" / She's all, "…" | you report words + an attitude (proud, annoying, dramatic) | "She's all, 'I don't do mornings.'" |
| And I'm just like… [sound / face] | there are no words, just a reaction | "And I'm just like… [blank stare]" |
| And I'm thinking, "…" | you want to be clear it was a thought, not words | "And I'm thinking, 'This is not gonna end well.'" |
| So I said, "…" | the key line, said seriously | "So I said, 'OK, I quit.'" |
| And they were like, "No." | reporting a group or an organisation | "I asked the airline for a refund, and they were like, 'No.'" |
| She was like, she… (summary) | you give the general meaning, not exact words | "He was like, he'd already paid, so it wasn't his problem." |
| And I was like, "Wait, what?" | you didn't understand or couldn't believe it | "She said the meeting was cancelled, and I was like, 'Wait, what?'" |
| And then he's like, "Oh, by the way…" | someone adds a surprise at the end | "And then he's like, 'Oh, by the way, I'm moving to Japan.'" |

### Examples

**Example 1: a full story with quotes (past tense)**

> "So I get to the front desk, and I'm like, 'Hi, I have a reservation under Park.' And the guy goes, 'Hmm… no, I don't see anything.' And I was like, 'Wait, what? I have the email right here.' And he's all, 'Sir, that email is for next month.' And I'm just like… [stares]. Yeah. I booked the wrong month."

(Notice: real speakers mix "I was like" and "I'm like" a bit. That's OK. Just don't jump around in the main verbs of the story.)

**Example 2: at work**

> A: How'd the design review go?
> B: Ugh. So I show them the new home screen, right? And the product lead goes, "I love it." And I'm like, great! And then the CEO's like, "Can we make it more… purple?"
> A: More purple?
> B: Yep. And I'm thinking, "Please, anything but purple."

**Example 3: a sound instead of words**

> A: Did you tell your landlord about the leak?
> B: Yeah. And he was just like… [shrugs]. Like, "Not my problem."
> A: That's rough.

**Example 4: summary with "be like"**

> "I called my brother to ask if he wanted to come, and he was like, he's super busy, maybe next time, blah blah blah. Which basically means no."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ And I liked, "No way." | ✅ And I was like, "No way." | It's "be like", not the verb "like". |
| ❌ She told, "I'm tired." | ✅ She said, "I'm tired." / She goes, "I'm tired." | "Tell" needs a person: "She told me…". For quotes, use "say", "go" or "be like". |
| ❌ And he was like that he would come later. | ✅ And he was like, he'd come later. / He said he'd come later. | No "that" after "be like". |
| ❌ She goes to me, "Sit down." | ✅ She goes, "Sit down." / She's like, "Sit down." | "Go" for quotes doesn't need "to me". |
| ❌ (same flat voice for everyone) | ✅ (small voice change for each person) | Without a voice change, the listener can't follow who's talking. |
| ❌ I was like, I was like, I was like… (every line) | ✅ I was like… / she goes… / and I said… | Mix the verbs so the conversation has a rhythm. |

### Practise

Do these out loud, in order. Full content is in [this week's exercises](exercises.md#day-3-exercises).

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "An argument or misunderstanding"** (with a friend, a family member, a neighbour, a shop, a colleague). It can be small: who was supposed to buy milk, a wrong order, a confusing message.

**Rules for tonight:** fast scene + one suspense moment + **at least four quotes** using at least three different verbs (*be like, go, be all, say*). Change your voice for each person.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Max 5 keywords. Write down only the most important line someone said.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).**
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Count your quotes. Can you tell who is speaking from your voice alone?
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** Keep the two best quotes.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)). "I was like" and "she goes" count as connectors.

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "A funny conversation with a stranger"** (a taxi driver, a waiter, someone on a plane, a customer service person). 2 minutes, then 1 minute, then 30 seconds with only the scene and the funniest line.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "And I'm like…", "And she goes…", "He was all…", "And I'm thinking…", "And I'm just like… [sound]".
2. **Cover the log.** Say tonight's 5 phrases from memory, each with a different quote and a different voice.
3. **Say Day 2's 5 suspense phrases from memory**, each followed by a quote. Example: "And guess what? She goes, 'I'm not coming.'"
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
