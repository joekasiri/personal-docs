## Learning objectives

- Retell the plot of a film or episode in about 1 minute, in the present tense.
- Describe what kind of film or show it is, without long explanations.
- Avoid spoilers, or warn people before you give one.
- Give a quick opinion and recommend (or not) at the end.

## 2. Main skill (40 min): Retelling films and shows

### Explanation

"Have you seen…?" is one of the most common questions in casual conversation. People talk about films and series with friends, at work, on dates. But retelling a plot is surprisingly hard: there are many characters, things happen in a strange order, and you're scared of spoiling it. So learners either say "It's good, you should watch it" (too short) or tell every scene for five minutes (too long).

A good casual retelling takes about **one minute** and has four parts:

1. **What it is.** Type + a quick comparison. "It's a thriller, kind of a slow burn." "It's like a cooking show meets a reality show."
2. **The setup.** Who + where + the situation, in the **present tense**. "It's about this woman who inherits a hotel in the mountains. But the hotel's, like, falling apart."
3. **The hook.** The problem or mystery that makes people want to watch, with a little suspense from [Day 2](day-2-building-suspense.md). "And then guests start disappearing."
4. **Your take.** A short opinion + a recommendation + often a question. "It starts slow, but the last two episodes are insane. You should totally watch it. Have you seen it?"

**Present tense:** for plots, use the present simple ("she finds", "he decides"), not the past. This is standard in English for films, books and shows.

**Spoilers:** a *spoiler* is information about the ending or a big twist. Before you give one, warn people: "Spoiler alert…" or "OK, I'm gonna spoil it, is that OK?". If you don't want to spoil it: "I won't spoil it, but…" / "Without giving too much away…".

You're allowed to forget names. Natives forget them all the time: "the main guy", "his sister, I forget her name", "the bad guy".

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It's about this (guy / woman / family) who… | you start the setup | "It's about this guy who finds a door in his basement." |
| It's set in (place / time)… | you say where or when it happens | "It's set in a small town in Alaska in the eighties." |
| It's kind of like (X) meets (Y). | you describe it with a comparison | "It's kind of like a detective show meets a comedy." |
| It's one of those (shows / movies) where… | you describe a familiar type | "It's one of those movies where nothing happens, but you can't stop watching." |
| It starts off (slow / kinda weird), but… | you warn about the beginning | "It starts off kinda slow, but it gets really good." |
| I won't spoil it, but… | you want to tease without telling | "I won't spoil it, but the ending is wild." |
| Without giving too much away… | the same, slightly more careful | "Without giving too much away, someone's not who they say they are." |
| Spoiler alert. / OK, spoiler: … | you're about to tell a big secret | "Spoiler alert: the brother did it." |
| And then things get (really dark / crazy / weird). | you add a hook without details | "And then in season two, things get really dark." |
| The main guy / his sister, I forget her name… | you can't remember a name and you keep going | "The main guy's best friend, I forget his name, he's hilarious." |
| It's a slow burn. / It's super binge-able. | you describe the pace | "It's a slow burn, but trust me." |
| You should totally watch it. / I'd skip it, honestly. | you recommend or don't | "Honestly? I'd skip it. The first season's way better." |

### Examples

**Example 1: a one-minute retelling (invented show)**

> A: Watched anything good lately?
> B: Oh yeah, I just finished this show called *Low Tide*. It's kind of like a mystery meets a family drama. So it's about this woman, Nora, who goes back to her hometown after like fifteen years, because her dad's sick. And the town's tiny, everyone knows everyone. And then, the first night, she finds this old box in his garage, full of photos of people she's never seen. And that's when things get kinda weird. I won't spoil it, but… nobody in that town is who they say they are. It starts off slow, but the last three episodes, oh my god. You should totally watch it. Have you heard of it?

**Example 2: forgetting names and keeping going**

> "So there's this guy, the main guy, I forget his name… he's a chef in Chicago, and his restaurant's about to close. And his sister, she's the one who handles the money, she finds out he's been lying about everything. So the whole season is basically them trying to save the place in like thirty days."

**Example 3: a spoiler, with permission**

> A: Did you finish *The Long Winter*?
> B: Yeah, last night! Wait, have you finished it?
> A: No, I'm on episode six.
> B: OK, then I'm not saying anything. Without giving too much away… keep an eye on the neighbour.
> A: Ugh, now I'm scared.

**Example 4: not recommending**

> "Honestly? I'd skip it. It's one of those movies where the trailer is better than the actual movie. Like, it's about a heist in a museum, which sounds fun, right? But it's two and a half hours, and nothing happens for the first hour."

### Common mistakes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ The film talks about a woman who… | ✅ The film is about a woman who… / It's about… | Films don't "talk about". They're "about" something. |
| ❌ In the film, she found a box and she went to the police. | ✅ In the film, she finds a box and goes to the police. | Use the present simple for plots. |
| ❌ It's a film of horror. | ✅ It's a horror movie. | Type word goes before "movie / show / film". |
| ❌ I don't want to make spoiler. | ✅ I don't wanna spoil it. / No spoilers! | "Spoil" is the verb. "Spoiler" is the noun. |
| ❌ (stopping) The main character is called… umm… umm… | ✅ The main guy, I forget his name… | Never stop for a name. Describe and keep going. |
| ❌ (telling every scene for 5 minutes) | ✅ What it is + setup + hook + your take (about 1 minute) | Give people a taste, not the whole meal. |

## 3. Speaking drill (45 min)

Full method: [The 3/2/1 drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md).

**Prompt A: "Retell a film or series episode you know well."** In your own words, as if a friend just asked, "Watched anything good lately?"

**Rules for tonight:** the 4-part structure (what it is → setup → hook → your take), present tense, at least one "I won't spoil it, but…" or a spoiler warning, and end with a recommendation and a question.

1. **Plan (3 min).** Write 4 keywords only, one for each part.
2. **Round 1: 3 minutes (record).** You can include more of the plot here.
3. **Quick listen (5 min).** Did you stay in the present tense? Note every past verb you used.
4. **Round 2: 2 minutes (record).**
5. **Round 3: 1 minute (record).** This is your "real conversation" version.
6. **Listen to round 3 (5 min)** and count your 3 numbers ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)).

**Prompt B (about 15 minutes): "The worst film or show you've ever watched."** Same structure, but your take is negative ("I'd skip it, honestly"). 2 minutes, 1 minute, then a 30-second version.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log 5 phrases** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md). Suggested: "It's about this guy who…", "It's kind of like X meets Y", "I won't spoil it, but…", "It starts off slow, but…", "You should totally watch it."
2. **Cover the log.** Say tonight's 5 phrases from memory, about a different show each time.
3. **Choose the 10 best phrases from this whole week** and say each one from memory in a new sentence. Mark any you forgot. Those are your focus for Day 6.
4. Write tonight's 3 numbers in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

## Expected outcome

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] Retell a film or episode in about 1 minute, in the present tense.
- [ ] Describe a show with "It's kind of like X meets Y" or "It's one of those…".
