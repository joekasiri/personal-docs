## Learning objectives

- Understand that English uses stress (not word order) to show what's new, important or different.
- Move the stress in one sentence to give it 5–7 different meanings.
- Use **contrastive stress** to correct someone naturally ("No, I said THURSday").
- Stress noun/verb pairs correctly (a REcord / to reCORD).

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): contrastive stress

Tonight the shadowing **is** the lesson. In English, the most important word in a sentence is louder, longer and higher. When you correct or contrast something, that word gets **extra** stress, and the rest of the sentence goes quiet.

Pause-and-repeat first: say each line 3 times, really exaggerating the CAPITAL word (make it longer and higher). Then say it at normal speed.

1. "No, I said THURSday, not TUESday."
2. "I didn't say she was WRONG. I said she was LATE."
3. "It's not MY laptop. It's HERS."
4. "I DID send it! Check your spam."
5. "I like the BLUE one. The RED one's kinda loud."
6. "We're meeting at SIX. Not at SEVEN."
7. "I don't HATE it. I just don't LOVE it."
8. "Wait, YOU made this?"
9. "It's not that I don't WANT to. I just CAN'T."
10. "She's not my BOSS. She's my boss's BOSS."
11. "I'm not ANGRY. I'm DISappointed."
12. "THAT'S what I've been trying to tell you!"

**Find a clip:** look for two people disagreeing in a friendly way, or someone correcting a misunderstanding. Search for "friendly debate podcast", "co-hosts disagree podcast", "comedy podcast arguing about food". Use **pause-and-repeat**: stop after every correction you hear, and copy the stressed word exactly. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Stress changes meaning

### Explanation

In many languages, you change word order to show what's important. In English, word order is fixed, so we use **stress**: we make one word louder, longer and higher in pitch. Listeners hear the stressed word as "the important part" or "the new part".

**1. One sentence, many meanings.** Look at this sentence. The words are the same, but the meaning changes depending on which word you stress:

| You say… | It means… |
|---|---|
| "I didn't take your PHONE." | I took something else of yours. |
| "I didn't take YOUR phone." | I took someone else's phone. |
| "I didn't TAKE your phone." | I borrowed it / I moved it / I only looked at it. |
| "I DIDN'T take your phone." | I'm denying it strongly. |
| "I didn't take your phone." (stress on **I**) | Someone else did. |

**2. Contrastive stress for corrections.** When someone gets a detail wrong, don't repeat the whole sentence with flat stress. Stress only the **corrected** word: "Is the meeting on Tuesday?" → "No, THURSday." This is how native speakers correct each other quickly and politely.

**3. New information gets the stress.** In normal conversation, the stress moves to the new word. "What kind of coffee do you want?" → "A LARGE coffee." (Coffee isn't new: they already said it.) If you stress the old word ("a large COFFEE"), it sounds slightly strange.

**4. Stress inside words.** Some words change meaning (or type) with stress:
- **REcord** (noun: "a world record") vs **reCORD** (verb: "Can I record this?")
- **PREsent** (noun: a gift) vs **preSENT** (verb: to show something formally)
- **OBject** (noun: a thing) vs **obJECT** (verb: to disagree)
- **INcrease** (noun) vs **inCREASE** (verb)

The rule of thumb: **nouns stress the first part, verbs stress the second part.**

**5. Compound nouns.** "A GREENhouse" (a glass building for plants) vs "a green HOUSE" (a house that's green). In compound nouns, stress the **first** word.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No, I said ___. (stress the correct word) | correcting a detail | "No, I said FIFteen, not fifty." |
| I didn't say ___. I said ___. | correcting what someone thinks you said | "I didn't say it was BAD. I said it was LONG." |
| I DID… | insisting something happened | "I DID call you! Twice!" |
| I don't ___ it, I just don't ___ it. | a fine difference in feeling | "I don't HATE it, I just don't LOVE it." |
| It's not that I don't ___. I just ___. | explaining the real reason | "It's not that I don't WANT to come. I just have to WORK." |
| Wait, YOU…? | surprise about who | "Wait, YOU wrote this?" |
| THAT's the thing. | agreeing that something is the key point | "THAT's the thing. Nobody asked the users." |
| I meant the OTHER one. | clearing up which thing | "Sorry, I meant the OTHER file." |
| Not THIS week, NEXT week. | correcting a time | "The launch is not THIS week, it's NEXT week." |
| I'm not saying it's ___. I'm saying it's ___. | a precise opinion (recycles [Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md)) | "I'm not saying it's a bad idea. I'm saying it's EXpensive." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Quick correction**

> A: So the demo's on Tuesday at three?
> B: THURSday at three.
> A: Oh, OK. In the big room?
> B: In the SMALL room. The big one's booked.

Notice: B never repeats the whole sentence. Just the corrected word, with strong stress.

**Example 2: Opinions with precise stress**

> A: So you hated the new design?
> B: I didn't HATE it. I just think the COLORS are a little off.
> A: But the layout's good?
> B: The layout's GREAT. It's really just the colors.

**Example 3: Noun and verb**

> A: Can I reCORD this call? I wanna keep a REcord of what we decide.
> B: Sure. Oh, and are you gonna preSENT the results on Friday?
> A: Yeah, I'm presenting. And I got you a little PREsent, by the way. It's on your desk.

### Common mistakes

❌ "No, the meeting is on THURSDAY AT THREE IN THE ROOM." (everything stressed)
✅ "No, THURSday."
*If you stress everything, nothing stands out. Stress only the new or corrected word.*

❌ "I would like to reCORD a new REcord." (reversed)
✅ "I'd like to reCORD a new REcord."
*Nouns stress the first syllable; verbs stress the second.*

❌ "a large COFFEE" (when coffee was already mentioned)
✅ "a LARGE coffee"
*Stress the new information, not the word everyone already knows.*

❌ Saying "I didn't say that" with flat stress when you're upset.
✅ "I didn't SAY that." (or "I DIDN'T say that!")
*Flat stress can sound unsure or robotic. Choose one word to carry your meaning.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Mixed drills tonight, using **opinions** from [Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md) as the content and **stress** as the focus. Record everything. Hardest first.

**Part A: "Correct me" role-play (20 min)**
Play both people. Person A is a colleague, Chris, who keeps getting the details of your weekend plans wrong. You correct every mistake with contrastive stress, staying friendly. Chris's lines (read each one, then answer):

1. "So you're going to Chicago this weekend?" (You're going to Boston.)
2. "With your brother, right?" (With your sister.)
3. "And you're driving?" (You're taking the train.)
4. "Leaving Saturday morning?" (Friday night.)
5. "For a wedding?" (For a birthday party.)
6. "Your brother's birthday?" (Your sister's partner's birthday.)
7. "And you're staying for a week?" (Just two nights.)
8. "Cool, so I'll see you Monday?" (Tuesday: Monday's a day off.)

Then swap: you get the details wrong about Chris's (invented) vacation, and Chris corrects you. Do the whole thing twice, faster the second time.

**Part B: 3/2/1 opinion (25 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))
**Prompt:** "Something people say is 'the best', but you think is overrated. What is it? What do people say about it? What's your actual opinion, and what would you recommend instead?"
Use at least three contrast structures with strong stress: "I'm not saying it's BAD, I'm saying it's…", "It's not that I don't LIKE it…", "The FOOD is fine, it's the PRICE…". 3, 2, then 1 minute.

**Part C: Opinion random topics (20 min)** ([random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md))
Talk for 90 seconds on each, giving an opinion with at least one contrast ("not X, but Y"):
- Working from home vs the office
- Coffee vs tea
- Social media for kids
- Living alone vs with roommates
- Paper books vs e-books
- Morning people vs night people
- Tipping in restaurants
- Learning languages with apps

**Part D: Listen back (5 min)**
Play back Part C. For one 90-second talk, write down the 5 words you stressed most. Were they the important words? If you hear mostly flat stress, say the talk again with exaggerated stress.
