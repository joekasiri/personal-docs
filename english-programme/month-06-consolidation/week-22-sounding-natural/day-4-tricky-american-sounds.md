## Before you start

- You practised "th" in shadowing in [Week 9](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md) and the flap T in [Week 1 Day 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md). Tonight covers four sounds that cause the most misunderstandings for learners: **the American R**, **"ee" vs "i"**, **"th"**, and **V vs W**.
- You probably don't have trouble with all four. Different first languages find different sounds hard. During the shadowing, notice which ones feel hardest **for you**, and spend more of the main-skill time on those.
- Have a **mirror** (or your phone's front camera) ready. Watching your mouth helps a lot.
- Set up [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Important: voice mode turns your speech into text before Claude reads it, so Claude can't hear your accent the way a person does. But it's still a useful test: **if it writes "ship" when you said "sheep", a listener might hear "ship" too.** We'll use that tonight.

## Learning objectives

- Make the American R correctly, including after vowels ("car", "work", "world").
- Hear and say the difference between long "ee" and short "i" (sheep / ship).
- Say the voiced and unvoiced "th" without changing it to t, d, s, z or f.
- Separate V and W clearly (vest / west).
- Keep these sounds clear in fast, natural speech, not only in single words.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): the four sounds in phrases

Tonight, use **pause-and-repeat** with these lines, slowly first, then at natural speed. Look in the mirror while you do them. Each line is packed with one sound.

**American R** (tongue pulled back, not touching anything; lips slightly round):
1. "Are you sure it's the right report?" ("Are ya sure…")
2. "I'm working really hard this year."
3. "The world's first car was weird."

**"ee" vs "i"** (ee = long, smile, tense; i = short, relaxed):
4. "Please leave it, I'll fix it."
5. "We need three sheets, not six."

**"th"** (tongue tip between the teeth, gently):
6. "I think they thought it was the third one."
7. "Thanks, that's the thing I was thinking of."
8. "Both of them are with their mother."

**V vs W** (V = top teeth on bottom lip, buzz; W = round lips, no teeth):
9. "We've got a very wide view of the west."
10. "Will you wear the vest? It's very warm."

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Tricky American sounds

### Explanation

You don't need a perfect accent. But some sounds, if they're unclear, change the **word** the listener hears, and that's when people say "Sorry, what?". These four are the most common.

**1. The American R**

- **How:** pull your tongue **back** in your mouth, curl the tip up slightly, and don't let it touch the top of your mouth. Round your lips a little. The tongue never taps (like Spanish or Italian R) and never rolls.
- **After vowels, you say it.** This is the big American feature: "car" = "carr", "work" = "wrrk", "water" = "wadder". British English often drops this R. American English always says it.
- **Hardest words:** "world", "girl", "rural", "mirror", "squirrel", "February", "library". Slow them down: "w-rr-ld", "g-rr-l".

**2. "ee" /iː/ vs "i" /ɪ/**

- **"ee"** (sheep, leave, feel): long, with your lips spread like a smile, and your tongue tense and high.
- **"i"** (ship, live, fill): short and relaxed, jaw slightly lower, almost lazy.
- Many learners say both as "ee" (so "live" sounds like "leave"), or both as something in between. Some pairs are embarrassing if you get them wrong, so this one matters.

**3. "th"**

- Put the tip of your tongue **lightly between your teeth** (you should see it in the mirror) and blow air out.
- **Unvoiced** (no buzz): think, thanks, three, both, month.
- **Voiced** (buzz in your throat): the, this, they, mother, with.
- Don't replace it with t ("tink"), s ("sink"), f ("fink"), d ("dey") or z ("zey").
- **In fast speech**, Americans keep the "th" very short and light. You don't have to push your tongue far out. Just touch the teeth.

**4. V vs W**

- **V:** top teeth gently on your bottom lip, and **buzz**. Vest, very, voice.
- **W:** lips **round** like you're about to kiss, no teeth at all, then open. West, wine, way.
- Practise slowly in the mirror: if you see teeth, it's a V.

**The real test is fast speech.** Getting these right in single words is step one. Step two is keeping them clear in phrases: "I think we're very worried about the world." That's what the drill is for.

### Key phrases

These everyday phrases are packed with tonight's sounds. Make them automatic.

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example (sounds to watch) |
|---|---|---|
| I think so. / I don't think so. | agreeing or disagreeing | "Is it ready?" "I THINK so." (**th**) |
| Are you sure? | checking | "Are you sure? It looked broken." (**R**) |
| That's the thing. | pointing to the key issue | "That's the thing. Nobody knows." (**th**, voiced) |
| It's really weird. | something strange | "It's really weird, it worked yesterday." (**R**, **W**) |
| What do you want to work on? | planning | "So what do you want to work on first?" (**W**, **R**) |
| I'll leave it here. / I live here. | everyday, and a classic ee/i pair | "I'll leave it here for you." / "I live here." (**ee/i**) |
| Can you fill this in? / I feel bad. | another ee/i pair | "Can you fill this in? Sorry, I feel bad asking." (**i/ee**) |
| It's very worth it. | recommending | "The trip's expensive, but it's very worth it." (**V**, **W**, **th**) |
| Thanks for everything. | thanking warmly | "Thanks for everything this week." (**th**, **V**) |
| The weather's been weird. | small talk | "The weather's been weird this year." (**W**, **th**, **R**) |
| Three or four weeks. | numbers and time | "It'll take three or four weeks." (**th**, **R**, **W**, **ee**) |

### Examples

**Example 1: When sounds cause confusion**

> A: Where do you leave?
> B: Sorry, what do I leave?
> A: No, where do you LIVE? Like, your apartment.
> B: Oh! Live. In Brooklyn.

**Example 2: A sound-heavy work sentence**

> "So, I think there are three things we need to work on this week. First, the very first screen: users don't really understand it. Second, the word 'Save' is in the wrong place. And third, we've got to check everything with the research team."

Say it slowly once, then at normal speed. Watch for every R, th, V and W.

**Example 3: A friendly correction**

> A: I saw a really cool vine shop downtown.
> B: A vine shop? Like, plants?
> A: Ha, no, WINE. W, W. Wine shop.
> B: Oh, wine! OK, now I'm interested.

Note: if someone misunderstands a sound, just say it again with exaggeration, or spell the letter. That's a "never stop" move from [Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md).

### Common mistakes

❌ "I tink so."
✅ "I THink so." (tongue between the teeth)
*"Tink" and "sink" are the most common "th" replacements.*

❌ "I leave in Chicago." (sounds like "leave")
✅ "I live in Chicago." (short, relaxed "i")
*Keep "i" short and lazy, "ee" long and smiling.*

❌ "The vorld is wery big."
✅ "The world is very big."
*Swapping V and W. Remember: W = round lips, no teeth; V = teeth on lip.*

❌ "Cah" / "wok" for "car" / "work" (R dropped)
✅ "Carr" / "wrrk"
*American English says the R after vowels. Dropping it sounds British, or unclear.*

❌ A tapped or rolled R in "really", "right", "ready".
✅ Tongue pulled back, no touch.
*Rolled R is one of the strongest signs of a foreign accent in American English.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Voice mode with Claude

See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md). Record the session on your phone too, so you can hear yourself afterwards.

**Step 1 (2 min):** Open voice mode and paste this prompt:

```
Tonight I'm practising four American English sounds: the R, "ee" vs "i" (sheep/ship), "th", and V vs W. I know you receive my speech as text, so here's the game. We'll do three parts, about 20 minutes each. I'll say "next part" to move on.

Part 1, minimal-pair test: I'll say one word from a pair (like "sheep" or "ship"), one word at a time. You tell me exactly which word you received, nothing else. After every 10 words, tell me my score and which pairs went wrong.

Part 2, tongue-twister sentences: give me one short, original sentence full of one of the four sounds. I say it. You tell me if any words came through differently from what you expected, and then give me the next sentence. Mix all four sounds.

Part 3, talk on the spot: ask me quick, fun opinion and "would you rather" questions, like a friend. Choose questions whose natural answers use words like world, really, three, think, very, weather, work, live, leave. Keep it casual. After 15 minutes, list any words that you received that looked like a different word from the one I probably meant.

Use casual American English and keep your turns short.
```

**Step 2 (20 min): Part 1, minimal-pair test.** Use the pairs in [Day 4 exercises](exercises.md#day-4-exercises). Say a random word from each pair (don't tell Claude which one you mean). Keep score. If a pair fails twice, look in the mirror, say it slowly 5 times, and test it again.

**Step 3 (20 min): Part 2, tongue-twister sentences.** Say each sentence twice: once slowly and carefully, once at full speed. The goal is that both come through correctly.

**Step 4 (20 min): Part 3, talk on the spot.** This is the real test: can you keep the sounds clear when you're thinking about **what** to say? Also use your opinion and "would you rather" phrases from [Week 9 Day 5](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/day-5-would-you-rather.md) and use 3 of your stubborn ten.

**Step 5 (8 min): Listen back.** Play back 2 minutes of your phone recording from Part 3. Listen only for your hardest sound. Count how many times you said it clearly and how many times you didn't.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log Claude's results (5 min):** in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md), write the pairs that failed and the words that came through wrong. Say each correct word 5 times in the mirror.
2. **Spaced review out loud (7 min):** yesterday's emotion reactions (with tone), last week's (Week 21) and one page from last month (Week 20). Tonight, pay attention to the four sounds in every phrase.
3. **Stubborn ten and nightly log (3 min):** count ticks; write your minimal-pair score in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
