## Before you start

- You practised emotional tone (polite vs annoyed vs excited) in role-plays in [Week 12](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/README.md) and sarcasm in [Week 14 Day 1](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/day-1-sarcasm-and-tone.md). Tonight is about something more general: making your **normal** speaking voice expressive, so you don't sound flat when you speak English.
- Find your [Week 21 Day 6](../week-21-deep-conversations/day-6-weekly-check.md) re-record. You'll listen to it in the drill.
- This lesson feels silly at first. Exaggerate anyway. Most learners need to "overdo" emotion in practice to get a normal amount in real life.

## Learning objectives

- Understand the three tools of vocal emotion: **pitch** (high/low), **speed** and **volume**.
- Say the same sentence with 5+ different emotions and be understood.
- Hear how short words ("Really?", "Great.", "Sure.", "Wow.") change meaning with tone.
- Tell a story with 2–3 emotional shifts in your voice.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): one line, many emotions

Many learners speak English with a **narrow pitch range**: the voice stays in the middle, like a straight line. American English uses a **wide** range, especially when people are excited or surprised. Tonight, stretch your range.

Say each line in the emotion shown. ↗ = pitch goes up, ↘ = pitch goes down. Exaggerate the first time, then say it "normally".

1. **Excited** (high, fast, loud): "We got the CONtract! ↗"
2. **Disappointed** (low, slow, quiet): "Oh. We didn't get it. ↘"
3. **Surprised** (very high on the stressed word): "Wait, WHAT? ↗ ↗"
4. **Sympathetic** (low, soft, stretched): "Aw… that's so HARD. ↘"
5. **Annoyed** (flat, clipped, short): "Great. Just great. ↘"
6. **Doubtful** (rise-fall-rise, slower): "Really? ↘↗ You think so?"
7. **Bored** (flat, low, slow): "Yeah… sounds… fun. →"
8. **Curious** (rising, light): "Ooh, what kind of project? ↗"
9. **Relieved** (long breath, falling): "Oh, thank GOD. ↘"
10. **Proud** (warm, steady, stressed): "I actually DID it."

**Find a clip:** a vlog or comedy podcast where the speaker reacts a lot: surprise, laughter, frustration. Search for "reaction vlog storytime", "funny storytime vlog", "comedians telling stories podcast". Speak along, copying the **pitch jumps**, not just the words. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Emotion in your voice

### Explanation

When you speak a second language, your brain is busy with words and grammar, so it often forgets about melody. The result: you sound flat, even when you feel excited or sorry. Native listeners may think you're bored, unfriendly or not interested, even when your words are perfect. Fixing this is one of the fastest ways to sound natural.

Your voice has three controls:

| Control | Up | Down |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** (high/low) | excitement, surprise, curiosity, questions | sadness, sympathy, seriousness, finality |
| **Speed** | excitement, nerves, annoyance | sadness, sympathy, importance, doubt |
| **Volume** | excitement, anger, emphasis | sympathy, secrets, sadness, intimacy |

**Short words carry a lot.** In English, tiny words change meaning completely with tone:

| Word | Tone 1 | Tone 2 |
|---|---|---|
| "Really?" | high rise ↗ = interested, surprised | fall-rise ↘↗ = doubtful |
| "Great." | high and bright = happy | flat and low = sarcastic, annoyed |
| "Sure." | bright, quick = happy to help | slow, flat = reluctant |
| "Oh." | high, short = understanding | low, long = disappointed |
| "Wow." | high and long = impressed | flat and short = unimpressed / sarcastic |
| "Fine." | bright = it's fine | clipped, low = I'm not fine |

**In stories, emotion shows the shape.** When you tell a story, let your voice follow the feelings: calm at the start, faster and higher as things go wrong, a pause, then relief or a laugh at the end. You already know story structure from [Week 1 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-3-story-structure.md). Tonight, make the structure **audible**.

**A safety note:** you don't need to act like a movie star. If you normally speak calmly, that's fine. The aim is just **enough** variety that people can hear what you feel.

### Key phrases

These reactions only work with the right tone. Say each one with the tone described.

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example (tone) |
|---|---|---|
| No WAY! ↗ | surprise, excitement | "You got the job? No WAY!" (high, loud) |
| Oh no… ↘ | sympathy | "Oh no… are you OK?" (low, soft, slow) |
| That's AMAZING! | big excitement for someone | "You're engaged? That's AMAZING!" (high, warm) |
| Hmm, really? ↘↗ | polite doubt | "Hmm, really? I thought it was next week." (slow, rise at end) |
| Ugh, seriously? | mild annoyance | "Ugh, seriously? Another meeting?" (low, clipped) |
| Oh, thank God. ↘ | relief | "Oh, thank God. I thought I'd lost it." (long breath out) |
| Wait, what? ↗↗ | shock, didn't expect it | "Wait, what? She quit?" (very high) |
| Aw, that's so sweet. | something touching | "He made you breakfast? Aw, that's so sweet." (soft, warm, stretched) |
| I'm SO proud of you. | supporting someone | "You did it! I'm SO proud of you." (warm, strong stress) |
| Yeah… no. → ↘ | a funny, polite "no" | "Skydiving? Yeah… no." (pause, flat, then falling) |

### Examples

**Example 1: Same words, different meaning**

> A: I finished the whole project last night.
> B: Really? ↗ (high, impressed) That's awesome!

> A: I'll finish the whole project tonight.
> B: Really? ↘↗ (slow, doubtful) It's like forty screens.

**Example 2: A story with emotional shifts**

> "So I'm at the airport, totally relaxed, getting a coffee. *(calm, normal pitch)* And then I look at the board, and my flight is BOARDING. *(faster, higher)* Like, now. And I'm at the other end of the terminal. *(faster again)* So I'm running, with my coffee, like an idiot… *(laughing)* and I get to the gate, and… // the door's closed. *(pause, low, slow)* BUT. The guy looks at me and goes, 'Go on, quick.' *(rising again)* Oh my god. The relief. *(long, falling breath)*"

**Example 3: Flat vs expressive**

Flat: "I got promoted today. I am very happy." (all on one note)
Expressive: "Guess what? ↗ I got proMOted! ↗ I'm, like, SO happy right now."

### Common mistakes

❌ Saying "That's amazing!" on one flat, low note.
✅ "That's aMAZing!" with a high, bright stress.
*Flat excitement sounds sarcastic in English.*

❌ "Really?" with a falling tone when you're interested.
✅ "Really? ↗" with a high rise.
*A falling "Really?" can sound bored or doubtful.*

❌ Telling a whole story at the same speed and pitch.
✅ Faster and higher when it gets exciting, slower and lower when it's serious.
*Your voice should follow the story's shape.*

❌ Using a big, loud voice for sympathy.
✅ "Oh no… ↘" softly and slowly.
*Sympathy is quiet. Loud sympathy can sound like you're making fun.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Mixed drills tonight, using **hypotheticals** from [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md) as the content and **emotion** as the focus. Record everything.

**Part A: Listen to yourself (5 min)**
Play the first 60 seconds of your Week 21 Day 6 re-record. Does your voice go up and down, or is it mostly flat? Rate it: 1 (flat), 2 (some variety), 3 (expressive). Write it down.

**Part B: Emotion role-plays (25 min)**
Play both sides. In each one, your voice must clearly show the emotion in brackets. Exaggerate. Do each one twice.

1. Your friend tells you she's pregnant. **(surprise → big excitement)**
2. Your colleague tells you the launch is delayed again. **(annoyance, but polite)**
3. Your neighbor tells you his dog died. **(sympathy)**
4. Your manager says you can work from anywhere next month. **(disbelief → excitement)**
5. A friend says "I'm gonna quit my job and become a DJ." **(polite doubt → curiosity)**
6. You find your lost wallet in your coat pocket. **(panic → relief)**

**Part C: 3/2/1 story (25 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))
**Prompt:** "Tell me about a day that went from really bad to really good (or the other way around). What happened, step by step, and how did you feel at each point?"
Your voice must change at least **three times**: at the start, at the turning point and at the end. Use one power pause from [Day 2](day-2-thought-groups-and-pausing.md) before the turning point. 3, 2, then 1 minute.

**Part D: "What if" with feeling (15 min)** ([random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md))
Answer each "what if" for 60–90 seconds, letting your voice show how you'd feel. Start with an emotional reaction ("Oh my god, I'd be SO…", "Honestly? I'd be terrified.").
- What if you won a million dollars tomorrow?
- What if you could never use your phone again?
- What if your best friend moved to the other side of the world?
- What if you had to give a speech at a wedding tomorrow, in English?
- What if you woke up and could speak perfect English?
- What if you had to live in your office for a week?

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Spaced review out loud (8 min):** yesterday's thought-group phrases, last week's (Week 21: feeling words are perfect for tonight: say each with the right emotion) and one page from last month (Week 19). See [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).
2. **Stubborn ten check (2 min):** count today's ticks.
3. **Log tonight (5 min):** 5 reactions with their tone marked, for example "No WAY! ↗", "Oh no… ↘", "Hmm, really? ↘↗", "Oh, thank God ↘", "Yeah… no." Note your self-rating from Part A in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
