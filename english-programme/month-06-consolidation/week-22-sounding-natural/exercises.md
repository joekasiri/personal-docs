# Week 22 exercises: Sounding natural
[Week 22 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-stress-changes-meaning.md) · [Day 2](day-2-thought-groups-and-pausing.md) · [Day 3](day-3-emotion-in-your-voice.md) · [Day 4](day-4-tricky-american-sounds.md) · [Day 5](day-5-controlling-your-speed.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use these exercises:** this week is all about sound, so **everything is out loud**, and recording is extra useful. Exaggerate first (too much stress, too much emotion, too slow), then say it again normally. CAPITALS = stressed. / = short pause. // = longer pause. ↗ = rising tone. ↘ = falling tone.

## Day 1 exercises

### Exercise 1.1: One sentence, seven meanings

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud, then record

1. Say the sentence below 7 times, stressing a different word each time (the CAPITAL word).
2. After each one, say out loud what it means, starting with "…so what I mean is…".
3. Check the answer key.
4. Record yourself saying all 7 and listen: can you hear the difference?

**Sentence A:** "I didn't say you broke my laptop."

1. "I didn't say you broke my laptop." (stress on **I**)
2. "I DIDN'T say you broke my laptop."
3. "I didn't SAY you broke my laptop."
4. "I didn't say YOU broke my laptop."
5. "I didn't say you BROKE my laptop."
6. "I didn't say you broke MY laptop."
7. "I didn't say you broke my LAPTOP."

**Sentence B (do the same):** "She told me we'd finish the design today."

Stress in turn: SHE, TOLD, ME, WE'D, FINISH, DESIGN, TODAY.

### Exercise 1.2: Noun or verb?

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

Read each sentence. The bold words are spelled the same but stressed differently. Decide which is the noun (stress first) and which is the verb (stress second), then say the sentence. Check the key.

1. Can I **record** this? I want a **record** of the meeting.
2. I'll **present** the results, and then we'll give her a **present**.
3. Does anyone **object** to moving the meeting? / What's that weird **object** on your desk?
4. We expect an **increase** in users if we **increase** the budget.
5. Our **progress** is slow, but we'll **progress** faster next month.
6. They want to **contract** us for a year. Have you read the **contract**?
7. I need a **permit** to park here. They don't **permit** dogs inside.
8. This store sells fresh **produce**. Our team will **produce** a new video.
9. Can I get a **refund**? They said they'll **refund** me next week.
10. We need to **update** the app. Did you see the latest **update**?
11. The two plans **conflict**. There's a **conflict** in the team.
12. Please don't **insult** him. That was a real **insult**.

### Exercise 1.3: Correct me

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

Your friend gets each detail wrong. The real fact is in brackets. Correct them in the most natural way: usually just **one stressed word** or a short phrase. Then try a longer version: "No, not X, Y."

1. "So you're from Brazil?" (Portugal)
2. "Your birthday's in June, right?" (July)
3. "You work in marketing?" (product design)
4. "The party's on Saturday?" (Friday)
5. "You have two brothers?" (two sisters)
6. "The meeting's at three-thirty?" (two-thirty)
7. "You hated the movie?" (You just thought it was too long.)
8. "You're moving to a bigger apartment?" (smaller, but closer to work)
9. "So you're learning Spanish?" (English, obviously)
10. "Your boss's name is Mike?" (Mark)
11. "You went to Japan last year?" (two years ago)
12. "You're taking the bus?" (You're driving.)

### Answer key

**Exercise 1.1, Sentence A:**
1. Somebody else said it.
2. I'm strongly denying that I said it.
3. I didn't say it, but maybe I thought it or hinted it.
4. I said someone broke it, but not you.
5. I said you did something to it, but not break it (maybe you used it, or lost it).
6. I said you broke someone else's laptop.
7. I said you broke something of mine, but not my laptop.

**Exercise 1.1, Sentence B:** SHE = it was her, not someone else, who said it · TOLD = she said it clearly (or: she told me, but it may not be true) · ME = she told me, not other people · WE'D = we would finish it, not another team · FINISH = finish, not just start · DESIGN = the design, not the code or the copy · TODAY = today, not tomorrow.

**Exercise 1.2:** 1 reCORD (v) / REcord (n) · 2 preSENT (v) / PREsent (n) · 3 obJECT (v) / OBject (n) · 4 INcrease (n) / inCREASE (v) · 5 PROgress (n) / proGRESS (v) · 6 conTRACT (v) / CONtract (n) · 7 PERmit (n) / perMIT (v) · 8 PROduce (n) / proDUCE (v) · 9 REfund (n) / reFUND (v) · 10 upDATE (v) / UPdate (n) · 11 conFLICT (v) / CONflict (n) · 12 inSULT (v) / INsult (n).

**Exercise 1.3: model answers:** 1 "PorTUgal." 2 "JuLY." 3 "Product DEsign." 4 "FRIday." 5 "Two SISters." 6 "TWO-thirty." 7 "I didn't HATE it. It was just too LONG." 8 "SMALLer, actually. But it's CLOSer to work." 9 "ENGlish! That's kinda the whole point." 10 "MARK." 11 "Two years ago." (stress on TWO) 12 "No, I'm DRIVing."

## Day 2 exercises

### Exercise 2.1: Mark the thought groups

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** write then say

1. Copy each passage (or read it on screen).
2. Mark the thought-group boundaries with /, and any power pause with //.
3. Underline (or say louder) one stressed word per thought group.
4. Read it out loud, pausing at your marks. Then check the answer key and read the key version.

**Passage 1 (a story):**
So last week I was at the airport and my flight was delayed by like four hours so I went to get some food and when I came back the gate had changed and nobody told me.

**Passage 2 (an explanation, recycles [Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md)):**
The way our app works is pretty simple you take a photo of your receipt the app reads the total and the date and then it adds it to your monthly budget automatically so you don't have to type anything.

**Passage 3 (an opinion, recycles [Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md)):**
Honestly I think working from home is great for focus but the thing is you lose all the small conversations by the coffee machine and that's where a lot of good ideas actually come from.

**Passage 4 (a hypothetical, recycles [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md)):**
If I could live anywhere for a year I'd probably go to Portugal because the food is amazing the people are super friendly and I've always wanted to learn to surf.

### Exercise 2.2: Pause, don't umm

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** record

1. Answer each question for **45 seconds**.
2. Rule: whenever you need to think, **close your mouth** and pause silently at the end of a chunk. No "umm", no "uh".
3. Put a tally mark on paper every time an "umm" escapes. Don't stop, just mark it and continue.
4. Record questions 1, 4 and 7. Listen back: do your silent pauses sound OK? (They almost always sound better than you think.)

1. What's the most useful app on your phone, and why?
2. How would you explain your job to a ten-year-old?
3. What's a decision you made recently, and how did you make it?
4. What's the best way to learn a new skill?
5. What would you change about your company if you were the CEO?
6. How do you make your favorite meal?
7. What's something you believe that most people don't?
8. What would you do if you had an extra hour every day?

### Exercise 2.3: Power pauses

**Time:** 5 min · **Type:** out loud

In each sentence, decide where the power pause (//) goes: just **before** the surprising or important part. Say it with a one-second pause and strong stress after it. Then check the key.

1. And you know who was standing at the door? My old boss.
2. We worked on it for six months and the client said no.
3. I opened the box and it was completely empty.
4. And the best part? It was free.
5. After all that, the answer was really simple: ask the users.
6. Guess how much it cost. Three dollars.
7. I finally got to the front of the line and they closed.
8. The one thing I learned from all of it is this: always save your work.

### Answer key

**Exercise 2.1:** (small differences are fine)

1. So last WEEK / I was at the AIRport / and my flight was deLAYED / by like four HOURS / so I went to get some FOOD / and when I came BACK / the gate had CHANGED // and NObody told me.
2. The way our app WORKS / is pretty SIMple. // You take a photo of your reCEIPT / the app reads the TOtal / and the DATE / and then it adds it to your monthly BUDget / autoMATically / so you don't have to TYPE anything.
3. HONestly / I think working from HOME / is great for FOcus / but the thing IS / you lose all the small conVERsations / by the coffee maCHINE // and that's where a lot of good IDEas / actually come FROM.
4. If I could live ANYwhere / for a YEAR / I'd probably go to PORtugal / because the FOOD is amazing / the PEOple are super friendly / and I've always wanted / to learn to SURF.

**Exercise 2.3:** 1 "…at the door? // My old BOSS." 2 "…for six months // and the client said NO." 3 "…opened the box // and it was completely EMPty." 4 "And the best part? // It was FREE." 5 "…was really simple: // ask the USERS." 6 "Guess how much it cost. // Three DOLlars." 7 "…front of the line // and they CLOSED." 8 "…of all of it is this: // always SAVE your work."

## Day 3 exercises

### Exercise 3.1: One line, five feelings

**Time:** 8 min · **Type:** record

Say each line in **all five emotions** listed. Use the pitch/speed/volume table from [Day 3](day-3-emotion-in-your-voice.md). Record lines 1 and 5, then play them back to see if you can hear five different feelings.

| Line | Say it… |
|---|---|
| 1. "We're having pizza again." | excited · disappointed · surprised · annoyed · bored |
| 2. "He called me last night." | happy · worried · surprised · annoyed · secretive (whisper) |
| 3. "The meeting's been moved to Friday." | relieved · annoyed · surprised · neutral · doubtful |
| 4. "I got an email from the CEO." | excited · nervous · confused · proud · bored |
| 5. "You finished it already?" | impressed · doubtful · shocked · annoyed · sympathetic ("you poor thing, you worked all night") |
| 6. "It's raining." | happy (you love rain) · disappointed · annoyed · surprised · neutral |
| 7. "That's an interesting idea." | genuinely interested · politely doubtful · sarcastic · excited · thoughtful |
| 8. "My parents are coming to visit." | excited · stressed · surprised · nervous · happy and calm |

### Exercise 3.2: Tone detective

**Time:** 5 min · **Type:** out loud

Read each mini dialogue. The tone of B's answer is described in brackets. Decide what B **really** means, say it out loud ("B means…"), then say B's line in that tone. Check the key.

1. A: "I signed us up for the 6 a.m. yoga class." B: "Great." (flat, low, short)
2. A: "I've been promoted!" B: "Really? ↗" (high, bright, loud)
3. A: "I'll have the report ready in an hour." B: "Really? ↘↗" (slow, low then rising)
4. A: "Can you help me move on Saturday?" B: "Sure…" (slow, falling, stretched)
5. A: "We're going to Hawaii!" B: "Oh." (low, long, falling)
6. A: "I made dinner for everyone." B: "Wow." (high, long, warm)
7. A: "The client loved the new design." B: "Oh, thank God." (long, falling, breath out)
8. A: "How was the party?" B: "Fine." (clipped, low, very short)

### Exercise 3.3: Emotion story

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** record

Choose **two** of these story prompts. Each one has an **emotion map**: the feelings your voice should show, in order. Tell each story in about 90 seconds. Make the changes clearly audible. Record and listen.

1. **The lost passport:** calm → panic → frustration → huge relief → laughing at yourself
2. **The surprise party:** curious → confused → shocked → touched → happy
3. **The job interview:** nervous → confident → caught off guard → recovered → proud
4. **The new apartment:** excited → disappointed → annoyed → resigned ("it is what it is") → happy again
5. **The presentation:** calm → nervous → disaster moment → embarrassed → laughing about it now
6. **Meeting an old friend by accident:** bored → surprised → excited → nostalgic → warm

### Answer key

**Exercise 3.2:** 1 B is annoyed or sarcastic: they don't want to go. 2 B is genuinely surprised and happy for A. 3 B doubts it: they don't believe it'll be ready in an hour. 4 B is agreeing, but reluctantly: they don't really want to. 5 B is disappointed, maybe because they can't come, or jealous. 6 B is impressed and grateful. 7 B is relieved: they were worried. 8 B didn't enjoy it, or something happened, and they don't want to talk about it.

## Day 4 exercises

### Exercise 4.1: American R

**Time:** 5 min · **Type:** out loud (use a mirror)

Remember: tongue pulled **back**, tip slightly curled, **not touching** anything. Lips a little round. Say the R after vowels.

**Step 1: R at the start (say each twice):**
right · really · ready · red · run · room · reason · write · wrong · rule

**Step 2: R after a vowel (the American one):**
car · far · hard · park · work · word · first · bird · year · here · door · more · four · sure · better · water · teacher · never · over · after

**Step 3: the hardest words (slowly, then normal speed):**
world · girl · early · learn · rural · mirror · February · library · squirrel · particularly · career · rarely

**Step 4: phrases at normal speed:**
1. "I'm really sorry I'm late."
2. "Are you sure it's the right floor?"
3. "I learned a lot this year."
4. "It's the first time I've worked here."
5. "She's a really hard worker."
6. "Where are you from originally?"
7. "We're gonna be there around four."
8. "It's rarely this warm in February."

| heel | hill | | cheap | chip |

## Day 5 exercises

### Exercise 5.1: Measure your speed

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** record

1. Set a timer for **1 minute**. Record yourself talking about **Topic A** (easy).
2. Set a timer for **1 minute** again. Record yourself on **Topic B** (harder).
3. Listen back and count the words in each (or use your phone's transcription and count). Count "gonna" as one word, "you know" as two.
4. Fill in the table in your notebook.

- **Topic A (easy):** "What did you do last weekend?"
- **Topic B (harder):** "Explain the biggest problem in your industry right now and how you'd fix it."

| | Words in 1 minute | Felt… (rushed / OK / slow) | Umms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic A | | | |
| Topic B | | | |

**What the numbers mean:** under 100 = hesitant (more chunks, fewer pauses) · 100–130 = calm and clear · 130–170 = natural conversation · over 180 = probably rushing: check if your sounds are still clear.

### Exercise 5.2: Fast lane, slow lane

**Time:** 7 min · **Type:** out loud

**Part 1:** Read this passage out loud. **[fast]** parts: squash them, like one long word. **[slow]** parts: clear, stressed, with small pauses. Do it 3 times, getting more natural each time.

> [fast] So-I-was-at-this-conference-last-month, right, and-I-had-to-give-a-short-talk, like-ten-minutes, no-big-deal. [slow] And then… the projector… DIED. [fast] Like, totally-black-screen, nothing. And-I'm-standing-there-with-like-two-hundred-people-looking-at-me. [slow] So I just said… "OK. Let's do this… without slides." [fast] And-honestly? It-was-kinda-the-best-talk-I've-ever-given. [slow] Because I had to actually TALK to people.

**Part 2:** For each sentence, decide which part should be **fast** and which part **slow**. Say it. Then check the key.

1. "Anyway, long story short, the answer is no."
2. "I don't know, maybe I'm wrong, but I think we should wait."
3. "The flight leaves at 6:45 from gate B12."
4. "So, you know, it was kinda whatever, but the ending was incredible."
5. "Just to be clear, the budget is twenty thousand, not fifty."
6. "I mean, it's not a big deal, but please don't touch my coffee."

### Exercise 5.3: Brake phrases

**Time:** 6 min · **Type:** out loud

In each situation, you notice you're rushing. Say a **brake phrase**, take a breath, then say the main point slowly and clearly. Make up the main point. Use a different brake phrase each time.

1. You're explaining a bug to your manager, and words are tumbling out.
2. You're telling a friend about your exciting weekend, too fast.
3. You're giving someone directions to your apartment.
4. You're in an interview and you've been talking for a minute without a clear point.
5. You're telling your landlord about three problems in the apartment.
6. You're explaining a new process to your team, and they look confused.
7. You're leaving a voice message for a doctor's office (name, date, phone number).
8. You're pitching an idea in a meeting and you're nervous.

### Answer key

**Exercise 5.2, Part 2:**
1. [fast] "Anyway, long story short," [slow] "the answer is NO."
2. [fast] "I don't know, maybe I'm wrong, but" [slow] "I think we should WAIT."
3. Everything slows down on the numbers: "The flight leaves at… six forty-FIVE… from gate… B… TWELVE."
4. [fast] "So, you know, it was kinda whatever, but" [slow] "the ENDing was inCREDible."
5. [slow] "Just to be clear… the budget is TWENty thousand… not FIFty."
6. [fast] "I mean, it's not a big deal, but" [slow] "please don't touch my COFfee."

**Exercise 5.3: model answers:**
1. "OK, so… let me put it this way. The app crashes when you upload a big photo. That's the main problem."
3. "Bear with me, it's a little complicated. Take the train to… Fifth Street. Then walk… two blocks… north."
7. "Hi, this is… Sam… Rivera. R-I-V-E-R-A. I'd like to change my appointment on… Thursday the… twelfth. My number is… five-five-five… two-one-eight… four-four-three-oh."
