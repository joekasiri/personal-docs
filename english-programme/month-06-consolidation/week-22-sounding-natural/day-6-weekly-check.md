# Week 22 · Day 6: Weekly check
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 6](../README.md) › [Week 22](README.md) · [← Previous: Controlling your speed](day-5-controlling-your-speed.md) · [Next: Week 23 · Job interview answers →](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/day-1-job-interview-answers.md)

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Re-record test 15 min · Skill check 10 min · Live conversation 60 min · Review and report 20 min

## Before you start

- Have your recording app, the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), your stubborn-ten page, your **Week 21 Day 6 re-record** and your **Week 1 Day 1 baseline** ready.
- Open the [sound checklist](exercises.md#day-6-materials) in this week's exercises.
- **Live conversation #3 is tonight.**

## 1. Shadowing (free choice, 15 min)

Go back to the sound that was **hardest for you** this week. If it was one of the four sounds from [Day 4](day-4-tricky-american-sounds.md), do 5 minutes of minimal pairs in the mirror, then 10 minutes of speak-along with a clip full of that sound. If it was stress, pauses, emotion or speed, redo that night's shadowing lines and then speak along with the clip you found that night.

## 2. Re-record test (15 min)

**No preparation.** Read the prompt, press record, start talking straight away, and keep going for **2 full minutes**.

> **Prompt:** "Tell me about something you think most people get wrong, and explain what you'd change if you could change it tomorrow."

This mixes an opinion ([Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md)), an explanation ([Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md)) and a hypothetical ([Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md)). The content isn't the focus tonight. The sound is.

Then:

1. Count [the 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md): umms, pauses over 2 seconds, different connectors.
2. Count your **WPM** for the first minute (see [Day 5](day-5-controlling-your-speed.md)).
3. Fill in the [sound checklist](exercises.md#day-6-materials) while you listen.
4. Now play the first 30 seconds of your **Week 1 Day 1 baseline**, then the first 30 seconds of tonight. Don't judge. Just notice what's different.

Write everything in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).

## 3. Skill check

Rate yourself honestly. **3** = I do it naturally, without thinking. **2** = I can do it when I focus. **1** = not yet.

| Day | I can… | Rating (1–3) | If you scored 1… |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Day 1: Stress](day-1-stress-changes-meaning.md) | …move stress to change meaning, and correct someone with one stressed word | | Redo Exercise 1.1 and the "correct me" role-play from Day 1 |
| [Day 2: Thought groups](day-2-thought-groups-and-pausing.md) | …speak in clear chunks and pause silently instead of "umm" | | Redo Exercise 2.1 (passage 3) and Exercise 2.2 |
| [Day 3: Emotion](day-3-emotion-in-your-voice.md) | …let my voice show surprise, sympathy, excitement and doubt | | Redo Exercise 3.1 with a recording, and compare with the lines in the lesson |
| [Day 4: Sounds](day-4-tricky-american-sounds.md) | …say R, ee/i, th and V/W clearly, even when speaking fast | | Do 5 minutes of minimal pairs for your hardest sound every night next week (add it to your review) |
| [Day 5: Speed](day-5-controlling-your-speed.md) | …go fast on easy chunks, slow on key points, and brake when I rush | | Redo the speed ladder from Day 5 with a new story |

**Accent polish is slow.** Unlike phrases, sounds change over months, not days. A 2 here is a good result. Keep your hardest sound in your nightly shadowing for the rest of the month.

**Stubborn ten:** move phrases with 2+ ticks to your normal review; keep the rest for next week.

## 4. Live conversation (60 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: the feedback conversation.** Have a normal, relaxed conversation, and at a comfortable moment, ask your partner for honest feedback on how you sound. Most people are happy to help if you ask clearly.

Try these 5 things:

1. Tell one story with emotion in your voice (Day 3) and a slow key moment (Day 5).
2. Correct at least one small misunderstanding with contrastive stress (Day 1). If none happens naturally, create one in a playful way: "No, I said I went to BOSton, not Austin!"
3. Use 3 silent pauses instead of "umm" (Day 2).
4. Ask for feedback. Use this: "Can I ask you something? I've been working on my pronunciation. Is there any sound or word I say that's hard to understand? You can be totally honest."
5. Use at least 3 of your stubborn ten.

Write down exactly what they tell you. Real listener feedback is worth more than any app.

**Backup plan:** if the live conversation falls through, use [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) for 40 minutes:

```
Let's have a 40-minute casual voice conversation. You're Alex, a friend from my old job. Ask me about my week, a story from my life, my opinion on something, and what I'd change about my city if I could. Keep your turns short and casual. I've been practising stress, pauses, emotion, speed and the sounds R, ee/i, th and V/W this week. Since you get my speech as text, at the end tell me: (1) any words that came through as a different word than I probably meant, (2) any sentences that seemed rushed or confusing, and (3) three corrections.
```

3. **Carry into next week:** add Claude's 5-minute sound routine to the start of your shadowing block every night in Week 23 and Week 24. Week 23 is about **fluency under stress**: interviews, being put on the spot, negotiating, conflict and strangers. Stress makes accents and speed fall apart, so this week's control is exactly what you'll need.
