## Before you start

- Last night you learned that one word in each sentence carries the stress ([Day 1](day-1-stress-changes-meaning.md)). Tonight: each **thought group** has one of those stressed words, and the gaps between thought groups are where you pause.
- You shadowed thought groups in explainer videos in [Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md). Tonight you use them in your own speech, and you use the pause as a **replacement for "umm"**.
- **Live conversation #1 is tonight.** Have it booked.

## Learning objectives

- Split your speech into thought groups of about 3–7 words, each with one main stress.
- Pause **between** thought groups, never in the middle of one.
- Replace "umm" with a short, silent pause (and know that silence sounds confident).
- Use a slightly longer pause before an important word or point.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): thought groups

A **thought group** is a small chunk of words that belong together, like "in the morning", "I was gonna say", "the thing is". Native speakers say each chunk smoothly, as if it were one long word, and then pause very slightly before the next chunk. The / below marks a thought-group boundary (a tiny pause). // marks a longer pause.

First, pause-and-repeat: say each line slowly, pausing at every /. Then speak it at normal speed: the pauses get shorter, but they don't disappear.

1. "So basically / what happened was / we ran out of TIME."
2. "The thing IS / nobody actually ASKED the users."
3. "When I got HOME / I was so tired / I just went to BED."
4. "If you think about it / it's actually kind of GENius."
5. "My manager / who's super nice by the way / totally forgot the MEETing."
6. "I'm not saying it's BAD // I'm saying it's not READY."
7. "First you open the APP / then you tap SETtings / and then you scroll DOWN."
8. "Honestly / if I were YOU / I'd just call them."
9. "The reason I'm ASKing / is that we had the same problem LAST year."
10. "And then // out of NOwhere // she just QUITS."

**Find a clip:** an explainer or a calm storyteller who speaks in clear chunks. Search for "explainer video calm narrator", "how it works explained simply", "storytelling podcast slow narrator". Play 20 seconds, write down where you hear the pauses (use /), then speak along. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Thought groups and pausing

### Explanation

When learners are nervous, they often do one of two things: they **rush** (no pauses at all, everything joined into one long line), or they **break in the wrong places** ("I went to the… umm… shop and… bought a…"). Both are hard to understand. Native speakers do something different: they speak in **chunks**, with tiny pauses in between.

**Where do thought groups break?** Usually:
- after an opening phrase: "So basically /", "The thing is /", "Honestly /"
- at commas and between clauses: "When I got home / I was exhausted"
- before and after extra information: "My manager / who's super nice / forgot"
- before "and", "but", "because", "so" when they start a new idea
- before a key point you want people to notice: "And the answer was // NO."

**Where do they NOT break?** Inside a chunk:
- ❌ "in the / morning", "a cup of / coffee", "I'm gonna / go"
- ❌ between an adjective and its noun: "a really / good idea"
- ❌ between a verb and a short object: "I sent / it"

**The silent pause beats "umm".** Here's the most useful idea of the night: a **silent pause of half a second to one second sounds confident**. It makes you sound thoughtful, like a good speaker. "Umm" sounds unsure. So when your brain needs time, **close your mouth and pause**, at a thought-group boundary. You still get thinking time, but now it sounds deliberate. This directly lowers your "umm" number.

**The power pause.** Before your most important word or point, pause a little longer (about one second): "And you know what she said? // NOTHING." This is the same timing you used for punchlines in [Week 14](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md) and for effect in [Week 16](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md).

### Key phrases

These are phrases that naturally **end a thought group**, so a short pause after them sounds perfect. They give you a safe place to pause and think.

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So basically / | starting a summary or explanation | "So basically / we need more time." |
| The thing is / | introducing the key problem | "The thing is / nobody owns this project." |
| Here's the thing / | introducing an important point | "Here's the thing / it already works." |
| What happened was / | starting to explain a story | "What happened was / the server went down." |
| If you think about it / | introducing a new way of seeing something | "If you think about it / it's kind of funny." |
| The way I see it / | introducing your opinion | "The way I see it / we have two options." |
| To be honest / | before an honest opinion | "To be honest / I'm not sure it's worth it." |
| The reason I ask is / | explaining why you're asking | "The reason I ask is / we had the same issue." |
| And the best part? // | before a surprising or fun point (power pause) | "And the best part? // It was free." |
| Long story short / | jumping to the end | "Long story short / we got the contract." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Rushed vs chunked**

Rushed (hard to follow): "Sowhathappenedwasweweregonnalaunchonmondaybutthenthedesignteamfoundabugsowehadtowait."

Chunked: "So what happened was / we were gonna launch on MONday / but then the design team found a BUG // so we had to WAIT."

**Example 2: Silent pause instead of "umm"**

> A: What did you think of the new office?
> B: Hmm. / Honestly / it's nice. / But it's kinda LOUD. / Like / you can hear every phone call.

B pauses four times, but never says "umm". It sounds calm and thoughtful.

**Example 3: Explaining with clear chunks** (recycles [Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md))

> "OK, so the way it works is / you upload a PHOTO / the app finds the main COLORS / and then it suggests a PALette. // The cool part is / it also checks if the colors are ACCESSible / like, if people with color blindness can see the difference."

### Common mistakes

❌ "I went to the… umm… supermarket and… umm… I bought…"
✅ "I went to the supermarket / and I bought…" (silent pauses, at boundaries)
*Pause at the end of a chunk, not inside it, and pause silently.*

❌ "a really / good / idea"
✅ "a really good IDEA"
*Adjective + noun is one chunk. Breaking it sounds like you've lost the word.*

❌ Speaking for 30 seconds with no pause at all.
✅ Short pauses every 3–7 words.
*Without pauses, listeners can't process what you said, even if your grammar is perfect.*

❌ Filling every pause because silence feels scary.
✅ Letting a half-second of silence happen.
*In English, a short silence sounds confident. "Umm" doesn't.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #1 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: the calm-pause challenge.** Have a normal conversation, but every time you need to think, use a **silent pause** at a thought-group boundary instead of "umm". You won't be perfect. The goal is to notice the moments.

Try these 4 things:
1. Start at least 3 answers with a thought-group opener and a tiny pause ("Honestly / …", "The thing is / …").
2. When you explain something (your job, a plan, how something works), deliberately chunk it.
3. Use one power pause before a surprising point in a story ("And the best part? // …").
4. Near the end, ask your partner: "Quick question. Is my English easy to follow? Is there anything that's hard to understand, like a sound or the speed?" Write down what they say.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's have a 30-minute casual voice conversation. You're Jamie, a friendly coworker. Ask me to explain things: my job, how something in my life works, a plan I have, a decision I made. Ask follow-up questions. I'm practising speaking in clear chunks with short silent pauses instead of saying "umm". Every 10 minutes, tell me roughly how often I said "umm" or "uh", and quote one sentence that was hard to follow, then show me how I could split it into clearer chunks.
```

### Part B: 3/2/1 explaining (30 min)

([How to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))

**Prompt:** "Explain how something works that you know really well, to someone who has never seen it: for example, a tool you use at work, a recipe, a sport, or how your company makes money."

Focus: clear thought groups, one stress per chunk, **zero "umm"s** (replace them with silent pauses). Use 3 key phrases from tonight's table. Tell it in 3, 2, then 1 minute. In the 1-minute version, keep the pauses: cut words, not pauses.

After the drill, listen to the 3-minute version and count the umms. Write the number down. Then compare it with your Week 21 re-record.
