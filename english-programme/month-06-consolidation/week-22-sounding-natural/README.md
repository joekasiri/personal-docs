# Week 22: Sounding natural
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 6: Consolidation](../README.md) · [← Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/README.md) · [Week 23 →](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/README.md)

## This week's goal

You can talk now. This week is about **how you sound** while you talk: the accent polish that makes people stop noticing your English and start listening to your ideas. We won't try to make you sound like a news reader. The goal is to be **easy to understand at full speed**, and to sound relaxed and expressive instead of flat or rushed. Five features make the biggest difference: putting stress on the right word, pausing in the right places, letting emotion into your voice, fixing the four sounds that most often cause confusion (American R, "ee" vs "i", "th", and V vs W), and controlling your speed. This week, **the shadowing block is part of the lesson**: each night you shadow the exact sound you're learning.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/README.md) you worked on *what* to say in deeper conversations. This week you work on *how* it sounds. You've met many of these features in shadowing before: sentence stress in [Week 1 Day 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-4-past-tenses-in-stories.md), stress for emphasis in [Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md), thought groups in [Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md), "th" in [Week 9](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md), emotional tone in [Week 12](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/README.md) and sarcasm in [Week 14](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md). Back then they were warm-ups. This week each one is the main lesson, practised in your **own** unscripted speech.

The source plan says Week 22 recycles Months 2 and 3: opinions, explaining, hypotheticals and talking on the spot. So every speaking drill this week uses those skills as the content, while you focus on the sound.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Find a podcast with one very clear, relaxed American host, for example a storytelling or interview show. Search for "storytelling podcast American host", "calm interview podcast" or "radio storytelling show". Listen while you walk.

Your one task: **listen for the music, not the words.** For 5 minutes, try to notice only three things: which word in each sentence is the loudest (stress), where the speaker pauses (thought groups), and when they speed up or slow down. Then, for one minute, hum the melody of a sentence you just heard, without words. It sounds silly, but it trains your ear for intonation. Then stop. No recording, no drills. Rest.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Move the stress in a sentence to change its meaning, and correct people with contrastive stress.
- [ ] Speak in clear thought groups, with short silent pauses instead of "umm".
- [ ] Say the same sentence with 5 different emotions, and let real emotion into your stories.
- [ ] Say the American R, "ee" vs "i", "th" and V vs W clearly in minimal pairs and in fast speech.
- [ ] Slow down for important points and speed up on easy chunks, without rushing when you're nervous.
- [ ] Get feedback on your pronunciation from at least one real person.
