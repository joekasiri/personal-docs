## Before you start

- Tonight is the top rung of the [depth ladder](day-1-from-small-talk-to-real-talk.md): values, what matters to you and why.
- You already know how to give opinions ([Week 6 Day 1](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-1-giving-opinions.md)), disagree politely ([Week 6 Day 3](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/day-3-disagreeing-politely.md)), compare cultures ([Week 19 Day 3](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-19-complex-topics/day-3-culture-and-differences.md)) and give balanced views ([Week 19 Day 5](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-19-complex-topics/day-5-balanced-views.md)). Opinions are about *topics*. Values are about *you*: what you care about, where it comes from, and how it changed.
- **Live conversation #2 is tonight.** Ideally with someone you've spoken to before.
- Values talk can touch sensitive areas (religion, politics). You don't need them tonight. Stick to everyday values like family, freedom, honesty, work, money and time.

## Learning objectives

- Say what you value and **where it comes from** (family, culture, an experience).
- Talk about how your views have **changed** ("I used to think…, but now…").
- Respond respectfully when someone has different values, without arguing.
- Ask others about their values with natural, non-awkward questions.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: thoughtful hesitation.** When people talk about values, they often search for the right words, and native speakers make that searching sound smooth: "I think… for me, it's… yeah, it's freedom." The voice stays calm, the pauses are short, and fillers like "I think", "for me" and "I guess" hold the space instead of "umm". This is the "never stopping" skill from [Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md) in a slower, warmer style.

Practise each line 3 times. **…** = short, calm pause. CAPITALS = stress.

1. "I think… for ME, it's… yeah, it's FREEdom."
2. "I guess what really MATTERS to me is… TIME."
3. "That's a big one. Hmm… probably HONesty."
4. "I used to think MONey was everything. Now? Not so MUCH."
5. "It's kind of… a big part of who I AM."
6. "Where I grew UP… family came FIRST. Always."
7. "I can see WHY someone would think that."
8. "We just see it DIFFerently… and that's OK."
9. "What SHAPED that for you, do you think?" ("d'you think")
10. "Deep down? I think I just want to be USEful."

**Find a clip:** a long interview where someone talks about their life lessons or what they've learned. Search for "life lessons interview podcast", "what I've learned at 50 interview", "values and purpose conversation podcast". Speak along with the slow, thoughtful answers, copying the pauses exactly. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Values and beliefs

### Explanation

A value is something you think is important in how you live: honesty, family, freedom, security, fun, fairness, learning. When people talk about values in a friendly conversation, three things make it interesting:

1. **A value + a reason or story.** "Family matters to me" is a fact. "Family matters to me because my grandparents raised me, and they gave up a lot" is a conversation.
2. **Change over time.** The most honest and interesting thing you can say is how a value changed: "I used to think a good job meant a big salary. Now I think it means good people." This uses "used to", which you know well.
3. **Respect for difference.** When someone values something else, you don't need to agree or argue. You can be curious: "That's interesting. What shaped that for you?" Or accept it: "We just see it differently, and that's OK."

Remember: values are personal, so **"for me"** and **"I guess"** are your best friends. They show you're talking about yourself, not telling other people how to live.

Good questions to ask others (none of them are too heavy for a friendly chat):
- "What's something you really care about?"
- "What would you never compromise on?"
- "Has anything changed the way you see things?"
- "What did your parents teach you that you still believe?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| For me, what really matters is… | naming a key value | "For me, what really matters is having time with people I love." |
| I've always believed that… | a value you've held a long time | "I've always believed that you should treat everyone the same." |
| I value… more than… | comparing two values | "I value freedom more than security, I guess." |
| That's something I care about a lot. | showing a value is important | "Being fair to my team is something I care about a lot." |
| I used to think…, but now… | describing a change | "I used to think working late was cool, but now I think it's kinda sad." |
| Where I grew up, people… | explaining where a value comes from | "Where I grew up, people helped their neighbors, no question." |
| It's a big part of who I am. | a value that defines you | "Learning new things is a big part of who I am." |
| I'd never compromise on… | a value you won't give up | "I'd never compromise on honesty, even at work." |
| What shaped that for you? | asking where someone's value comes from | "You really care about education. What shaped that for you?" |
| I can see why someone would think that. | respecting a view you don't share | "I can see why someone would think that, but for me it's different." |
| We just see it differently, and that's OK. | ending a difference peacefully | "Yeah, we just see it differently, and that's OK." |
| Deep down, I think… | sharing something very honest | "Deep down, I think I just want people to like my work." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Value + story + change**

> A: What's something you really care about? Like, a value thing.
> B: Ooh, deep question. I think… for me it's time. Like, having control of my own time.
> A: How come?
> B: My dad worked six days a week my whole childhood. I barely saw him. So I used to think working hard meant working long hours. Now I think it means doing good work and then going home.
> A: That makes so much sense. Does your job let you do that?
> B: Mostly, yeah. I'd never compromise on that now, honestly.

**Example 2: Respecting a difference**

> A: I don't know, I think stability is the most important thing. A good, safe job, a house…
> B: Yeah, I can see why someone would think that. For me, it's kinda the opposite. I value freedom more than security, I guess. I'd rather rent and travel.
> A: Really? Doesn't that stress you out?
> B: Sometimes! What shaped that for you, the stability thing?
> A: My parents lost their business when I was a kid. It was scary.
> B: Oh, wow. Yeah, that would definitely shape you. We just see it differently, and that's totally OK.

**Example 3: Where you grew up**

> "Where I grew up, family came first, always. Like, every Friday, the whole family had dinner together, no excuses. When I moved abroad, I didn't realize how much I'd miss that. So now I call my mom every Friday at the same time. It's a big part of who I am, I guess."

### Common mistakes

❌ "My values are honesty, family and freedom." (and stop)
✅ "Honesty matters a lot to me, probably because my grandma was super direct."
*A list is a form. A value + a reason is a conversation.*

❌ "People must value family. It's the most important."
✅ "For me, family is the most important thing."
*"Must" tells others how to live. "For me" talks about yourself.*

❌ "I am used to think money is important."
✅ "I used to think money was really important."
*"Used to + verb" = a past habit or belief. "Be used to + -ing" = being familiar with something.*

❌ "You are wrong, family is more important than freedom."
✅ "Interesting! I see it a bit differently."
*With values there is no right or wrong. Curiosity keeps the conversation warm.*

❌ "Which is your values?"
✅ "What do you really care about?" / "What matters most to you?"
*"Values" can sound formal. The casual question is more natural.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #2 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission:** have a conversation that reaches **rung 4** (values) at least once. Start with normal small talk, climb the ladder from [Day 1](day-1-from-small-talk-to-real-talk.md), and see how far it goes naturally.

Try these 5 things:
1. Use at least 3 bridge phrases ("How'd you end up…?", "What was that like?", "Can I ask you something kinda random?").
2. Share a value of yours with a reason or story.
3. Ask one values question from tonight's list ("What's something you really care about?").
4. Ask "What shaped that for you?" when they share something.
5. If they say something you see differently, respond with respect, not argument.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's have a relaxed 30-minute voice conversation. You're Morgan, someone I met at a language exchange a few weeks ago, and we're having coffee. Start with small talk, then let the conversation go deeper naturally, toward what we each care about in life and why. Share your own made-up values and stories, and gently disagree with me once on something everyday (for example, money vs time, or city vs country life), so I can practise responding respectfully. Keep your turns short and casual. At the end, tell me which rung of the conversation (facts, experiences, feelings, values) we reached and give me 3 corrections.
```

### Part B: 3/2/1 drill (30 min)

([How to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))

**Prompt:** "Tell me about something you changed your mind about. What did you use to think? What happened that changed it? What do you think now, and how does it affect your life?"

Use "I used to think…, but now…", at least one phrase from [Week 6 opinions](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md), and at least 4 storytelling connectors. 3 minutes, 2 minutes, 1 minute. In the 1-minute version, the "used to → now" contrast must still be clear.
