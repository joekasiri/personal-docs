## Before you start

- You already own the grammar for tonight: future forms ("gonna", "might", "I'm thinking of…") from [Week 10 Day 5](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/day-5-future-plans-and-predictions.md), "If I could…" from [Week 10 Day 1](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/day-1-if-i-could.md) and "I wish…" from [Week 10 Day 3](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/day-3-wishes-and-regrets.md). Tonight is not about grammar. It's about **talking about what you want from life** in a way that's honest, specific and interesting to listen to.
- Spend 2 minutes before you start thinking of **one real goal** (big or small) and **one dream** (something you'd do if nothing stopped you). You'll talk about both tonight.
- Set up [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and your recording app. Use headphones if you can.

## Learning objectives

- Talk about goals at three distances: **now** (this year), **soon** (next few years) and **someday** (the dream).
- Make goals sound real and specific, with steps and obstacles, not just "I want to be successful".
- Talk about obstacles and doubts in a natural, light way ("It's kind of a long shot, but…").
- Ask other people about their goals with curiosity, and follow up.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: curious, rising tone.** When people ask about dreams, their voice goes **up** with real interest, and the key word gets extra stress: "So what's the BIG dream?" ↗. When they answer, they often start lower and build energy as they get excited. Copy both: curious questions, and answers that warm up.

Practise each line 3 times. ↗ = rising tone. CAPITALS = stress.

1. "So what's the BIG dream? ↗"
2. "Wait, you wanna open a CAFÉ? ↗"
3. "What's stopping you? ↗"
4. "Honestly… someday I'd LOVE to live by the ocean." ("I'd love ta")
5. "It's kind of a long SHOT, but…"
6. "I'm not there YET, but I'm getting CLOSER."
7. "Five years from now? Hmm… GOOD question."
8. "I'm taking baby STEPS." ("takin'")
9. "If everything goes RIGHT, I'll be there by next SUMMER."
10. "That's AMAZING. How'd you even get INTO that?" ("howdja")

**Find a clip:** an interview where someone talks about their career dream or a big life change. Search for "how I quit my job to follow my dream interview", "career change story podcast", "founder origin story interview". Speak along with the guest as they get more excited: notice the energy rising. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Goals and dreams

### Explanation

"What are your goals?" can feel like a job-interview question. In a deep conversation, it's different: people want to hear **what you care about and why**. The best answers have four parts:

1. **The goal**, said casually: "My big goal right now is to lead a design team."
2. **Why** it matters to you: "…because I love helping other designers get better."
3. **Where you are now**, the steps: "I'm already mentoring two juniors, so I'm kind of working toward it."
4. **The honest obstacle**, said lightly: "The hard part is I've never managed anyone, so it's a little scary."

Talk about goals at three distances. Each one has its own natural language:

| Distance | Natural language |
|---|---|
| **Now** (this year) | "Right now I'm focused on…", "This year I really wanna…", "I'm working on…" |
| **Soon** (1–5 years) | "In a few years, I'd like to…", "I'm hoping to…", "The plan is to…" |
| **Someday** (the dream) | "Someday I'd love to…", "The dream is…", "If money weren't an issue, I'd…" |

When you **ask** about other people's goals, avoid the job-interview version. Ask softly and personally: "What's the dream?", "Is there something you've always wanted to do?", "What would you do if you didn't have to work?" Then follow up: "What's stopping you?", "How'd you get into that?", "What would the first step be?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| My big goal right now is… | stating your main current goal | "My big goal right now is to run a half marathon." |
| I'm working toward… | showing you're taking steps | "I'm working toward a senior role." |
| I've always wanted to… | a long-held wish | "I've always wanted to learn to surf." |
| Someday I'd love to… | a dream, far away | "Someday I'd love to live in Lisbon for a year." |
| The dream is… | your ideal outcome, said casually | "The dream is to have my own little studio." |
| It's kind of a long shot, but… | a goal that might not happen | "It's kind of a long shot, but I'd love to speak at a big design conference." |
| I'm taking baby steps. | you're progressing slowly | "I haven't quit my job or anything. I'm taking baby steps." |
| What's stopping me is… | naming an obstacle honestly | "What's stopping me is money, honestly." |
| If everything goes right… | a hopeful timeline | "If everything goes right, I'll move next spring." |
| It's on my bucket list. | something you want to do before you die (casual) | "Seeing the northern lights is on my bucket list." |
| What's the dream? | asking someone about their dream | "OK, forget your job for a second. What's the dream?" |
| What would the first step be? | helping someone make a dream real | "That sounds amazing. What would the first step be?" |

### Examples

**Example 1: A full goal answer**

> A: So what are you working on these days? Like, outside of work?
> B: Honestly, my big goal right now is to get way more confident speaking English. Like, in front of people.
> A: Oh, nice. How come?
> B: Because at work I have all these ideas, but in meetings I kinda freeze. So I've been practicing every night for, like, five months.
> A: Every night? That's commitment.
> B: Ha, yeah. I'm not there yet, but I'm getting closer. The hard part is the nerves, not the words.

**Example 2: Asking about someone's dream**

> A: OK, random question. If money wasn't an issue, what would you do?
> B: Oh man. Probably open a little bakery. Like, a tiny one, with three tables.
> A: Wait, really? I didn't know you baked!
> B: Every weekend. It's kind of my therapy.
> A: That's so cool. What's stopping you?
> B: Fear, mostly. And rent prices. It's kind of a long shot.
> A: What would the first step be, though? Like, a small one?
> B: Hmm… maybe selling at a farmers' market?
> A: See? That's not a long shot. That's a Saturday.

**Example 3: Three distances**

> "Right now I'm focused on finishing this big project at work. In a couple of years, I'm hoping to move into a lead role. And someday? The dream is to work from somewhere by the ocean, maybe Portugal. It's on my bucket list to live abroad at least once."

### Common mistakes

❌ "My dream is become a manager."
✅ "My dream is to become a manager." / "The dream is becoming a manager."
*After "is", use "to + verb" or "-ing".*

❌ "I want to be successful and happy."
✅ "I want to lead a small team and still have time to travel."
*General goals are boring. Specific goals start conversations.*

❌ "In the future I will open a café."
✅ "Someday I'd love to open a café." / "I'm hoping to open a café."
*"I will" sounds like a fixed fact. Dreams use softer forms.*

❌ "What is your goal in life?" (to someone you just met)
✅ "Is there something you've always wanted to do?"
*The first one sounds like an exam. The second feels like a chat.*

❌ "If I would have money, I would travel."
✅ "If I had the money, I'd travel."
*No "would" in the "if" part. You know this rule from [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md).*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Voice mode with Claude

See [Voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) for setup. Record the whole session with your phone if possible, so you can listen back.

**Step 1 (2 min):** Open voice mode and paste this prompt:

```
Let's have a long, relaxed voice conversation, like two friends at dinner. You're my friend Riley. We're going to talk about goals and dreams, in four rounds of about 12 minutes each. I'll say "next round" when I want to move on.

Round 1: Ask me what I'm working on right now in my life, and keep asking follow-up questions ("how come?", "what's that like?", "what's the hard part?").
Round 2: Ask me where I want to be in five years. Push me gently to be specific.
Round 3: Ask me about a big dream, the "if nothing stopped you" kind. Ask what's stopping me and what a first step could be.
Round 4: Now it's my turn to ask. Tell me about your own (made-up) goals and dream, and let me ask you follow-up questions.

Rules: talk like a real friend, short turns, casual American English. Share your own opinions and small stories too, so it doesn't feel like an interview. Don't correct me during the rounds. At the end of each round, give me quick feedback: one phrase I used well, one mistake with the corrected version, and one more natural way to say something I said.
```

**Step 2 (50 min): the four rounds.** Focus on:
- the four parts of a goal answer: goal, why, where you are now, obstacle
- the three distances: "Right now…", "In a few years…", "Someday…"
- in round 4, asking with curiosity and following up at least 3 times before changing topic
- your stubborn ten: try to use 4 of them tonight

**Step 3 (10 min): replay round 3.** Say to Claude: "Let's do round 3 again, but this time ask me harder follow-up questions about my dream." Answer again, with no long pauses: if you need time, use a time-buyer from [Week 3 Day 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-4-time-buyers.md) ("Ooh, good question…").

**Step 4 (8 min): feedback.** Ask Claude:

```
Thanks, Riley. Now please step out of the role. Give me: (1) the 5 most useful corrections from tonight, with my sentence and the natural version, (2) 3 phrases I could use next time I talk about my goals, and (3) one thing I did well.
```

Write the corrections in your phrase log.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log Claude's corrections (5 min):** write the 5 corrections in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md), and say each correct version out loud 3 times.
2. **Spaced review out loud (7 min):** yesterday's advice phrases, last week's phrases (Week 20) and one page from last month (Week 19: complex topics). From memory, full sentences.
3. **Stubborn ten check and nightly log (3 min):** count ticks and fill in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md).
