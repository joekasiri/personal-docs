# Week 21: Deep conversations
[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 6: Consolidation](../README.md) · [← Week 20](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/README.md) · [Week 22 →](../week-22-sounding-natural/README.md)

## This week's goal

Most of your practice so far has been about getting the words out: stories, small talk, opinions, meetings, tough questions. This week is about **what happens after the small talk**. Real friendships and good work relationships are built in the conversations that go a bit deeper: how someone is *really* doing, what they want from life, what they care about. These conversations are slower and warmer, but they are also scary for a learner, because you can't hide behind a script. By the end of the week you should be able to move a chat from "So what do you do?" to a real, personal conversation, talk honestly about everyday feelings, support someone who is having a hard time, talk about your goals and values, and keep all of it relaxed and natural.

## How this builds on last week

In [Week 20](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/README.md) you handled pressure from the outside: tough questions, a tough boss, hard feedback. This week the pressure is quieter and more personal. Nobody is attacking you; the hard part is being open in a second language. You won't learn new grammar. Instead you'll **recycle** what you already own and use it at a deeper level:

- your small-talk openers and **answer, add, ask back** from [Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md), as the starting point you climb up from
- your follow-up questions from [Week 2 Day 2](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/day-2-follow-up-questions.md), which are how you go deeper
- your storytelling connectors from [Week 1 Day 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) and your group skills from [Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md)
- your hypotheticals and wishes from [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md), which come back on Day 4 (goals and dreams)

The source plan says Week 21 recycles Months 1 and 4: stories, small talk, groups and humour. Deep conversations are where all of those meet.

## Before you start

- Finish the [Week 20 weekly check and Month 5 check](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/day-6-weekly-check.md) and send your numbers to Claude.
- Read the [Month 6 overview](../README.md), especially **"Your stubborn ten"**: every Day 1 this month you pick the 10 phrases from your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md) that you still don't use naturally.
- Plan your **3 live conversations** (Days 2, 5 and 6). This week, the best partner is someone you already know a little: a colleague you get on with, a language-exchange partner you've met before, or a regular at a meetup. Deep conversations are easier with a bit of trust. See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- A note on tone: this week is about **everyday feelings in friendly conversation**: stress before a deadline, being tired, missing home, being excited about a trip. It is not therapy and you never have to share anything you don't want to. Use real but small things from your life, or make them up. Practising the language is what matters.
- Have your recording app, your phrase log and the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) ready.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Find a long, relaxed conversation podcast where two people really talk, not an interview with a celebrity promoting something. Search for "deep conversation podcast", "two friends honest conversation", "long form interview life lessons" or "slow conversation podcast". Listen while you walk.

Your one task: **listen for the moment it gets deeper.** Every conversation has a point where it moves from facts to feelings. It often starts with a phrase like "Can I ask you something?", "What was that like?" or "Honestly…". Each time you hear one, pause and say it out loud twice, copying the warm tone. Write the best two into your phrase log. Then stop. No recording, no drills. Rest is part of the plan.

## By the end of this week you can…

- [ ] Move a conversation from small talk to a real, personal topic in 3–4 natural steps, without it feeling like an interview.
- [ ] Describe everyday feelings precisely ("drained", "torn", "relieved", "on edge") and say how strong they are.
- [ ] Listen, show you understand, and then offer gentle advice or support, without lecturing.
- [ ] Talk about your goals and dreams for 3+ minutes, and ask others about theirs with real curiosity.
- [ ] Explain what matters to you and why, and respond calmly when someone sees things differently.
- [ ] Have at least one live conversation this week that goes beyond small talk.
