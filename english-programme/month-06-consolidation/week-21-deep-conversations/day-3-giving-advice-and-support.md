## Learning objectives

- Follow the **L-V-A** order: Listen, Validate, then Advise (only if they want it).
- Ask "Do you want advice, or do you just need to vent?" naturally.
- Give advice softly, with 8+ gentle advice phrases, instead of "You should…" or "You must…".
- Offer support at the end of the conversation in a warm, natural way.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: the soft pause before advice.** Listen to a kind person giving advice and you'll notice they don't jump in. They pause, breathe, and start with a soft, lower voice: "Hmm… well… for what it's worth…". The pause shows that they've really listened. Copy the pause and the lower start.

Practise each line 3 times. **…** = soft pause. CAPITALS = stress.

1. "Hmm… that's a TOUGH one."
2. "Well… for what it's WORTH… I think you did the right thing."
3. "Can I be HONEST with you?"
4. "Do you want ADvice, or do you just wanna VENT?" ("wanna")
5. "That makes total SENSE."
6. "If it were ME… I'd probably just TALK to her." ("I'd prob'ly")
7. "Have you thought about… taking a few days OFF?"
8. "One thing that helped ME was… writing it all DOWN."
9. "You don't have to figure it all out TONIGHT."
10. "I'm here if you need ANYthing. Seriously."

**Find a clip:** a podcast where a host gives advice to callers or answers listener questions in a kind, relaxed way. Search for "advice podcast listener questions", "friendly advice call-in podcast" or "life advice podcast two hosts". Speak along with the host's **first sentence** after each question: that's where the soft pause lives. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Giving advice and support

### Explanation

When someone shares a problem, many people (in every language) jump straight to fixing it: "You should just quit!" It feels helpful, but often the other person feels unheard. In English-speaking cultures, and especially in American conversation, the friendly order is:

1. **Listen.** Let them finish. Use back-channels: "Mm-hm", "Oh no", "Wait, really?" (you know these from [Week 3 Day 2](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-2-echo-questions-and-showing-interest.md)). Ask one or two follow-ups: "What did he say?", "How long has this been going on?".
2. **Validate.** Show that their feeling makes sense: "That makes total sense." "Anyone would be upset about that." "No wonder you're stressed." This is the most important step, and the one learners skip.
3. **Advise, if they want it.** Not everyone wants advice. Sometimes people just want to **vent** (let the feeling out). If you're not sure, ask: "Do you want advice, or do you just need to vent?" It sounds direct, but Americans find it caring.

When you do give advice, make it **soft**. Strong advice ("You must…", "You have to…", "You should definitely…") can sound bossy. Soft advice gives them the choice:

- a question: "Have you thought about…?", "What if you…?"
- your own experience: "One thing that helped me was…"
- a hypothetical: "If it were me, I'd probably…"
- a humble opener: "For what it's worth…", "I don't know if this helps, but…"

Finish with **support**: "I'm here if you need to talk." "Let me know how it goes." "You got this." It closes the conversation warmly.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| That makes total sense. | validating a feeling | "You're upset he forgot? That makes total sense." |
| No wonder you're… | validating by connecting cause and feeling | "You worked all weekend? No wonder you're wiped." |
| Anyone would feel that way. | telling them their reaction is normal | "Honestly, anyone would feel that way after a week like that." |
| Do you want advice, or do you just need to vent? | checking what they need | "Ugh, that sucks. Do you want advice, or do you just need to vent?" |
| Have you thought about…? | suggesting gently, as a question | "Have you thought about talking to your manager?" |
| What if you…? | suggesting a small, specific action | "What if you just sent her a short message?" |
| If it were me, I'd probably… | giving your view without pressure | "If it were me, I'd probably wait until Monday." |
| One thing that helped me was… | sharing your experience instead of an order | "One thing that helped me was going for a walk before calls." |
| For what it's worth… | giving an opinion humbly | "For what it's worth, I think you handled it really well." |
| You don't have to decide right now. | reducing pressure | "You don't have to decide right now. Sleep on it." |
| I'm here if you need to talk. | offering support at the end | "Anyway, I'm here if you need to talk, OK?" |
| Let me know how it goes. | showing you care about what happens next | "Good luck tomorrow. Let me know how it goes!" |
| You got this. | encouraging before something hard (casual) | "You've prepared so much. You got this." |

### Examples

**Example 1: The full L-V-A**

> A: Ugh, I just had the worst one-on-one with my manager.
> B: Oh no. What happened? *(Listen)*
> A: She said my designs are "too safe". In front of the whole team, basically, because it was in a shared doc.
> B: Wait, in a shared doc? That's rough. No wonder you're upset. *(Validate)*
> A: Yeah. I don't know. Maybe she's right.
> B: Do you want advice, or do you just need to vent?
> A: Advice, I guess.
> B: OK. For what it's worth, I think "too safe" is actually fixable. What if you asked her for one example of a design she thinks is braver? Then you know what she means. *(Advise)*
> A: Huh. Yeah, that's not a bad idea.
> B: And honestly, you're a great designer. Let me know how it goes, OK? *(Support)*

**Example 2: When they just want to vent**

> A: My landlord still hasn't fixed the heating. It's been three weeks.
> B: Three weeks? That's insane.
> A: I know! I've emailed him like five times.
> B: Do you want ideas, or do you just need to vent?
> A: Honestly, just vent. I'm gonna call the city tomorrow anyway.
> B: OK, then vent away. Three weeks with no heat? I'd be furious.
> A: Thank you! That's all I needed.

**Example 3: Sharing your own experience**

> A: I've got my first conference talk next week and I'm kinda freaking out.
> B: That makes total sense. It's a big deal. One thing that helped me before my first talk was practicing it out loud to, like, an empty room. Like five times. By the fifth time it was almost boring.
> A: Huh, OK.
> B: And you don't have to be perfect. People are way more forgiving than you think. You got this.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Don't be sad."
✅ "That sounds really hard."
*Telling someone not to feel something doesn't help. Validate first.*

❌ "You must talk to your boss."
✅ "Maybe it's worth talking to your boss?" / "Have you thought about talking to your boss?"
*"Must" sounds like an order. A question leaves the choice with them.*

❌ "If I were you, I will quit."
✅ "If I were you, I'd probably quit."
*Hypotheticals use "would" ("I'd"). You learned this in [Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md).*

❌ "I know exactly how you feel."
✅ "I went through something kinda similar."
*You can't know exactly. Sharing a similar experience is warmer and more honest.*

❌ Jumping to advice after the first sentence.
✅ "Oh no. What happened?" … then advice later.
*Listen and validate first, or your advice won't land.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Mixed drills tonight. Record everything.

**Part A: Six support role-plays (30 min)**
Use the six situations in [Exercise 3.3](exercises.md#day-3-exercises). For each one, play your friend's first line out loud, then respond as yourself for about 3–4 minutes, playing both sides. Follow L-V-A: at least one follow-up question, one validation phrase, the "advice or vent?" check, one soft advice phrase, and a support ending. Hardest first: start with the situation that feels most awkward to you.

**Part B: 3/2/1 (25 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))
**Prompt:** "Tell me about the best piece of advice anyone ever gave you. Who gave it to you? What was going on in your life? Did you follow it straight away? How has it helped you since?"
Tell it in 3, 2, then 1 minute. Include at least one feeling word from [Day 2](day-2-talking-about-feelings.md), one phrase from your stubborn ten, and end with a question for the listener ("What's the best advice you ever got?").

**Part C: Advice random topics (10 min)** ([random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md))
A friend asks you each of these. Answer straight away, 60–90 seconds each, using soft advice phrases:
- "How do I make friends in a new city?"
- "Should I tell my friend her boyfriend is kind of rude?"
- "How do I stop checking my phone at night?"
- "My manager never says anything nice. Should I care?"
- "I wanna learn to cook. Where do I start?"

**Part D: Listen back (5 min)**
Play back one role-play. Did you validate **before** you advised? Count your "you should"s. If there are more than two, re-do that role-play with soft phrases only.
