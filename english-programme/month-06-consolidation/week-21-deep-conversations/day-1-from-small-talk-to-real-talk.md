## Learning objectives

- Understand the **depth ladder**: facts → experiences → feelings → values.
- Use 10+ "bridge" phrases that move a conversation one step deeper, naturally.
- Share something about yourself first, so the other person feels safe to go deeper too.
- Notice when someone doesn't want to go deeper, and step back down politely.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**This week's feature: the warm, unhurried voice of long-form interviews.** When people talk about real things, they slow down a little, the voice gets softer, and there are short, comfortable pauses. The pause is not "umm". It's a calm silence that says "I'm thinking about what you said". Tonight, copy that pace: slightly slower than your normal speed, relaxed jaw, and let the last word of each sentence fall gently.

Practise each sentence 3 times. First slowly, then at the speaker's speed. The **…** marks a short, warm pause. CAPITALS show stress.

1. "So how'd you END up… doing what you DO?" ("howdja end up")
2. "That's… actually really INteresting."
3. "Can I ask you something kinda RANdom?" ("kinda")
4. "What's that been LIKE for you?" ("what's that bin like")
5. "Yeah… I totally get THAT."
6. "Honestly? I don't think I've ever told anyone THAT."
7. "Wait, so what made you deCIDE to move?"
8. "Huh… I never thought about it THAT way."
9. "Do you ever MISS it?" ("d'you ever miss it")
10. "I feel like… that says a LOT about you."

**Find a clip:** a long-form, relaxed interview where the host asks personal questions slowly. Search YouTube or a podcast app for "long form interview life story", "deep conversation podcast", "slow interview podcast" or "honest conversation two friends". Choose a 2-minute section where the guest tells a personal story. Use the **speak-along** method from [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md): first listen once, then speak along twice, matching the pace and the warmth, not just the words.

## 2. Main skill (20 min): From small talk to real talk

### Explanation

Small talk is the door. Most learners get good at the door and then stay there all night: "What do you do?", "How long have you been here?", "Do you like it?". The conversation never becomes personal, so it ends after five minutes and nobody remembers it.

Native speakers move deeper **in small steps**. Think of a ladder with four rungs:

| Rung | What you talk about | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Facts** | Job, home, plans, what happened | "What do you do?" |
| **2. Experiences** | Stories, what it was like | "How'd you end up doing that?" |
| **3. Feelings** | How it felt, what's hard, what's exciting | "What's the best part of it, honestly?" |
| **4. Values** | What matters, what you've learned, who you are | "Is that something you've always cared about?" |

Three rules make the climb feel natural:

1. **One rung at a time.** Jumping from rung 1 to rung 4 ("What do you do?" → "What's your biggest fear?") feels strange. Go 1 → 2 → 3, and only go to 4 if the other person is enjoying it.
2. **Share first, then ask.** If you want someone to talk about feelings, give a small, honest piece of yours first: "Honestly, I found my first year here pretty lonely. Was it like that for you?" This is your old **answer, add, ask back**, but the "add" is now something personal.
3. **Watch the answer length.** Long, detailed answers mean "keep going". Short answers ("Yeah, it's fine.") mean "not today". When you get a short answer, step back down a rung with a lighter question. That's not a failure; it's good social skill.

The magic words are usually **"what was that like?"**, **"how come?"** and **"honestly"**. They signal: "I'm not just being polite, I actually want to know."

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How'd you end up…? | moving from a fact (rung 1) to a story (rung 2) | "How'd you end up in product design?" |
| What was that like? | inviting the feeling behind a story | "You moved here alone? What was that like?" |
| Can I ask you something kinda random? | you want to change to a deeper topic politely | "Can I ask you something kinda random? Do you ever think about moving back?" |
| What's the best part of it, honestly? | going from facts to feelings about something positive | "What's the best part of being a nurse, honestly?" |
| What's the hardest part? | inviting a more honest answer (still safe) | "What's the hardest part of working remotely?" |
| Honestly, for me… | sharing first, to make it safe for them | "Honestly, for me the first year was kinda lonely." |
| Is that something you always wanted? | climbing towards values | "Is that something you always wanted to do?" |
| What's been on your mind lately? | opening a deeper chat with a friend | "So what's been on your mind lately? Like, actually." |
| I've been thinking a lot about… | starting a real-talk topic yourself | "I've been thinking a lot about what I wanna do next." |
| That's a big question! / That's deep. | reacting when someone asks *you* something deep | "Ooh, that's a big question. Let me think." |
| No pressure, by the way. | giving them an easy way out | "What made you leave? No pressure, by the way." |
| Anyway, enough about the deep stuff… | stepping back down when the other person seems uncomfortable | "Anyway, enough about the deep stuff. Did you try the tacos?" |

### Examples

**Example 1: A meetup, climbing the ladder**

> A: So what do you do? *(rung 1)*
> B: I'm a nurse, at a hospital downtown.
> A: Oh, nice. How'd you end up doing that? *(rung 2)*
> B: Honestly, kind of by accident. My mom got sick when I was in college, and I spent a lot of time in hospitals.
> A: Oh wow. What was that like? *(rung 3)*
> B: It was rough. But the nurses were amazing, and I was like, OK, that's what I wanna do.
> A: That's really cool. So is it what you expected? Like, the best part of it, honestly?
> B: The best part is when someone goes home. That never gets old.
> A: I love that. I feel like that says a lot about why you do it. *(rung 4)*

Notice: A never asks a "big" question out of nowhere. Every question grows out of the last answer.

**Example 2: Share first**

> A: How long have you lived here?
> B: Like, two years.
> A: Same, kind of. Honestly, for me the first few months were pretty lonely. I didn't really know anyone. Was it like that for you?
> B: Oh my god, yes. I think I talked to my cat more than to people.
> A: Ha! So what changed?
> B: I joined a climbing gym. That's where I met basically all my friends.

**Example 3: Reading a short answer and stepping back down**

> A: So do you think you'll stay at your company long-term?
> B: Uh, I don't know. Maybe.
> A: Fair enough, no pressure. Anyway, what are you up to this weekend?
> B: Oh, I'm going hiking, actually! I'm kinda excited.

That's good conversation. A noticed the short answer and changed direction without making it awkward.

### Common mistakes

❌ "What is your biggest fear in life?" (two minutes after meeting)
✅ "What's the hardest part of moving to a new city?"
*Jumping to rung 4 too fast feels like a psychology test. Climb one rung at a time.*

❌ "Why are you sad?"
✅ "You seem a little quiet today. Everything OK?"
*Don't name someone's feeling for them. Describe what you notice and ask softly.*

❌ Asking five deep questions in a row without sharing anything.
✅ "Honestly, for me it was hard at first. How about you?"
*Too many questions feels like an interview. Give a little, then ask.*

❌ "Please tell me about your feelings about your job."
✅ "Do you actually like it? Like, honestly?"
*Deep talk in English is casual and short. "Honestly" and "like, actually" do the work.*

❌ Continuing after "Yeah, I don't really wanna get into it."
✅ "Totally, no worries. So, what else is new?"
*Respect the signal and change topic lightly. They'll trust you more next time.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Mixed drills tonight. **Do the hardest part first.** For most people that's the role-play, so the order below starts there. Record everything.

**Part A: "Climb the ladder" role-plays (25 min)**
Play both people out loud. Change your voice a little for the other person if it helps. For each situation, start at rung 1 and climb to rung 3 (or 4) in about 3 minutes, using at least 3 bridge phrases from the table. Do all three, then re-do the one that felt most awkward.

1. **The office kitchen.** You and a colleague from another team, Jordan, are waiting for the coffee machine. Start: "Ugh, this machine is so slow." Climb to: what Jordan really thinks about their job.
2. **A friend's barbecue.** You meet Priya, a friend of a friend, who just moved from Toronto. Start: "So how do you know Alex?" Climb to: what she misses about home.
3. **A long flight.** The person next to you, Dan, is reading a book about starting a business. Start: "Is that any good? I've been wanting to read it." Climb to: his dream.

**Part B: 3/2/1 (25 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))
**Prompt:** "Tell the story of a conversation that went deeper than you expected: with a stranger, a colleague or a friend. Where were you? How did it start? What did you end up talking about? How did you feel afterwards?"
Tell it in 3 minutes, then 2, then 1. Use at least four connectors from [Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md) ("So basically…", "The funny part is…", "At that point…", "In the end…") and at least one phrase from your stubborn ten.

**Part C: Deep random topics (15 min)** ([random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md))
Shuffle these and talk for 90 seconds each, no preparation. End each one with a question you'd ask a listener (that's your "ask back").
- A place that feels like home
- A risk you're glad you took
- Something you were wrong about
- A friend you lost touch with
- The best year of your life so far
- Something you're secretly proud of

**Part D: Listen back (5 min)**
Play back Part A, situation 3. Count: how many bridge phrases did you use? Which rung did you reach? Did you share something before asking a deep question? Write one line in your nightly log.
