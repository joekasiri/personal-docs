## Learning objectives

- Use 20+ precise, natural feeling words instead of "good", "bad", "happy" and "sad".
- Say **how strong** a feeling is, and that you feel two things at once.
- Answer "How are you?" honestly when you want to, in a way that invites a real conversation.
- Talk about a feeling without making it heavy or awkward.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: sympathetic intonation.** When someone shares something hard, Americans often answer with a **long, falling** tone and a softer voice: "Oh no…" goes down slowly, like a sigh. "That's so hard" stretches the stressed word. If you say these fast and flat, they sound fake. Stretch and fall.

Practise each line 3 times. CAPITALS = stressed and stretched. ↘ = falling tone.

1. "Oh NO… ↘"
2. "That's so HARD. ↘"
3. "Aw, I'm SO sorry. ↘"
4. "That sounds really exHAUSTing."
5. "Ugh, I HATE that feeling." ("I hate that feelin'")
6. "No wonder you're TIRED."
7. "Yeah… that'd stress me OUT too." ("that'd" = "thad")
8. "Wait, are you OK, though?"
9. "Honestly, I've been kinda all over the PLACE." ("kinda")
10. "I'm just… really reLIEVED it's over."

**Find a clip:** an interview or podcast where someone talks about a tough time in their life and the host responds kindly. Search for "podcast talking about burnout honest", "interview overcoming a hard year", "vulnerable conversation podcast". Pick 2 minutes where the host reacts with sympathy. Speak along with the **host's reactions** especially. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Talking about feelings

### Explanation

When a B2 speaker is asked "How are you?", the answer is usually "Good" or "Fine" or "A little tired". That's correct, but it closes the door. When you want to have a real conversation, you need two things:

1. **Precise words.** "Bad" can mean tired, worried, sad, angry or bored. Native speakers pick a more exact word: *drained*, *on edge*, *bummed*, *fed up*. The exact word makes the other person understand you instantly, and it sounds fluent.
2. **A size.** Feelings have a volume. "A little nervous" and "freaking out" are very different. Use the softeners and intensifiers you already know to set the volume.

Here are the most useful everyday feeling words, from mild to strong:

| Family | Mild | Medium | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tired** | a little tired | drained, worn out | exhausted, completely wiped |
| **Worried** | a little nervous | anxious, on edge | stressed out of my mind, freaking out |
| **Sad** | a bit down | bummed (out) | heartbroken, really low |
| **Annoyed** | a little annoyed | frustrated, fed up | furious, so done |
| **Happy** | pretty good | excited, stoked | over the moon, thrilled |
| **Calm** | OK | relieved | totally at peace |
| **Unsure** | not sure | torn, confused | completely lost |

Two more tools:

- **Mixed feelings.** Real life is often two feelings at once: "I'm excited but also kinda terrified." "It's bittersweet." "I have mixed feelings about it." "Part of me wants to…, but another part…"
- **Keeping it light.** You can share a feeling and then make it smaller, so the other person doesn't feel pressure: "…but it's fine, it is what it is." "…but I'll survive." "…anyway, that's my drama for the week!" This is very American, and it's useful when you want to be honest without being heavy.

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Honestly? It's been a lot. | someone asks how you are and you want to be real | "Honestly? It's been a lot. We have a big launch on Friday." |
| I've been feeling kinda… | describing a mood that has lasted a while | "I've been feeling kinda drained lately." |
| I'm a little on edge. | you're nervous and tense | "I'm a little on edge about the presentation tomorrow." |
| I'm kinda bummed (about…). | something disappointing happened (casual) | "I'm kinda bummed I missed the concert." |
| I'm so fed up with… | you've been annoyed for a long time | "I'm so fed up with this commute." |
| I'm stoked! | you're very excited (very casual) | "I'm stoked, we're going to Mexico in May!" |
| I'm torn. | you can't decide between two options | "I'm torn. The new job pays more, but I love my team." |
| I have mixed feelings about it. | you feel good and bad about the same thing | "I have mixed feelings about moving. It's exciting, but I'll miss everyone." |
| It kinda got to me. | something affected you more than you expected | "She didn't mean it badly, but it kinda got to me." |
| I'm just relieved it's over. | after a stressful thing ends | "The interview went OK. I'm just relieved it's over." |
| Part of me…, but part of me… | explaining two feelings at once | "Part of me wants to quit, but part of me wants to prove I can do it." |
| It is what it is. | accepting something you can't change, keeping it light | "My flight got canceled. It is what it is." |
| How are you doing, like, really? | asking a friend for an honest answer | "How are you doing, like, really? You've had a crazy month." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Beyond "fine"**

> A: Hey! How's it going?
> B: Honestly? It's been a lot. We're launching the new app on Friday and I've been working till like ten every night.
> A: Oh no. You must be exhausted.
> B: Yeah, I'm pretty wiped. But I'm also kinda excited, you know? It's been a year of work.
> A: That's huge. So it's like, stressed but excited?
> B: Exactly. Mixed feelings. Ask me again on Saturday!

**Example 2: Saying the size of a feeling**

> A: Are you nervous about the move?
> B: A little, yeah. Not freaking out or anything, just a little on edge. I've never lived alone before.
> A: That makes sense. What part worries you most?
> B: Probably the first few weeks, when I don't know anyone. But I'm stoked about the apartment. It has this huge window.

**Example 3: Keeping it light**

> A: How was the job interview?
> B: Ugh. I think it went badly. They asked me a question about budgets and I just blanked. It kinda got to me, actually. I was in a weird mood all evening.
> A: Aw, that's so frustrating.
> B: Yeah. But it is what it is. If it's meant to be, it's meant to be. Anyway, that's my drama for the week! How about you?

### Common mistakes

❌ "I'm very boring today."
✅ "I'm so bored today."
*"-ed" is how you feel (bored, tired, excited). "-ing" is what causes it (a boring meeting).*

❌ "I feel myself tired."
✅ "I feel tired." / "I'm feeling tired."
*In English we don't use "myself" after "feel".*

❌ "I'm depressed because the café was closed."
✅ "I'm so bummed the café was closed."
*"Depressed" can sound like a medical condition. For everyday disappointment, use "bummed", "a bit down" or "disappointed".*

❌ "I am in stress."
✅ "I'm stressed (out)." / "I'm under a lot of stress."
*"In stress" is a direct translation. Use the adjective, or "under a lot of stress".*

❌ "Fine." (and nothing else, when a friend really wants to know)
✅ "Honestly? Kinda tired. It's been a long week. But good, mostly."
*One honest detail turns a closed answer into an open one. It's your old answer, add, ask back.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #1 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md) for how to prepare and handle nerves.

**Your mission:** have a normal, friendly conversation, and at some point move it to rung 3 (feelings) about something **everyday**: work stress, a trip, a change in your life, a hobby that's going well or badly.

Try these 4 things:

1. When they ask "How are you?", don't say "fine". Give an honest answer with a precise word and a size: "Pretty good, but a little drained. Long week."
2. Ask them "How've you been, like, really?" or "What's that been like for you?" at least once.
3. Use **one mixed-feelings phrase** ("I'm torn", "I have mixed feelings", "Part of me…").
4. React to their feelings with a sympathetic, falling tone from tonight's shadowing ("Oh no…", "No wonder you're tired").

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's have a casual voice conversation for 30 minutes. You're my friend Sam, and we're catching up after a few weeks. Start by asking how I've been. Ask me follow-up questions about how I really feel about things in my life, and share your own feelings too, like a real friend would. Keep it everyday and light, not therapy. Every 10 minutes, tell me one feeling word I used well and one place where a more precise feeling word would sound more natural.
```

### Part B: 3/2/1 drill (30 min)

([How to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))

**Prompt:** "Tell the story of a week when everything seemed to go wrong. What happened, one thing after another? How did you feel at each stage? How did it end, and how do you feel about it now?"

Rules: use at least **6 different feeling words** from tonight's table, at least one "size" (a little…, kinda…, completely…), and one "keeping it light" ending ("It is what it is", "I'll survive"). Tell it in 3 minutes, 2 minutes, then 1 minute. Record all three. In the 1-minute version, keep the feeling words. Cut details, not feelings.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Spaced review out loud (8 min):** yesterday's phrases (bridges like "How'd you end up…?", "What was that like?"), last week's (Week 20) and one page from last month (Week 17: meeting phrases). Full sentences, from memory. See [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).
2. **Stubborn ten check (2 min):** how many ticks did your stubborn ten get today? Say any with zero ticks out loud, twice, in a sentence about your day.
3. **Log tonight (5 min):** 5 new phrases, for example "It's been a lot", "I'm a little on edge", "I'm torn", "It kinda got to me", "It is what it is". Write one line about the live conversation in your [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md): what went well, and one moment you had to think.
