## Before you start

- You know conversation openers ([Week 3 Day 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-1-conversation-openers.md)), how to end a chat ([Week 3 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-5-changing-topic-and-ending.md)) and how to meet new people at a party ([Week 12 Day 3](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-3-meeting-new-people.md)). At a party, people expect to meet strangers. Tonight's angle is harder: **starting a conversation where nobody expects it** (a café, a queue, a co-working space, an event where you know no one) and keeping it going for 5 minutes.
- **Live conversation #2 is tonight, and it's with strangers.** Go to the place you picked at the start of the week. Plan to be there for at least 40 minutes.
- Safety and comfort: choose public, friendly places. You never have to continue a conversation that feels uncomfortable. If someone doesn't want to chat, that's about them, not your English.

## Learning objectives

- Open a conversation with a stranger using a **situational** comment (about the place, the moment or something you share).
- Move from the opener to a real chat with 2–3 natural follow-ups.
- Handle "Where's your accent from?" confidently and use it to keep talking.
- Exit a stranger conversation politely, and sometimes swap contact details.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: friendly, upbeat openers.** Listen to a street interviewer or a friendly vlogger who talks to people in public. Their openers are short, light, a little higher in pitch, with a smile you can hear: "Hey, sorry, quick question…". Energy up, speed relaxed.

Start with your 5-minute sound routine. Then say each line 3 times, smiling as you speak (it really changes the sound).

1. "Hey, sorry, quick QUEStion…"
2. "Is anyone SITting here?"
3. "Is this the line for COFfee?"
4. "Sorry, I couldn't help overHEARing… are you a DEsigner?"
5. "That looks AMAZing. What did you GET?"
6. "First time HERE?"
7. "How do you know the HOST?"
8. "Oh, I love your JACKet. Where'd you GET it?" ("where'dja")
9. "It's been SO nice chatting with you."
10. "Anyway, I'll let you get back to your BOOK."

**Find a clip:** street interviews or "talking to strangers" vlogs where someone chats with people in public. Search for "street interview asking strangers", "talking to strangers vlog", "man on the street interview". Speak along with the **interviewer's** openers and follow-ups. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Talking to strangers

### Explanation

Talking to strangers is scary in any language. In a second language, there's an extra fear: "What if they can't understand me?" The good news: in most of the US and many other places, short, friendly chats with strangers are completely normal: in queues, at coffee shops, at the gym, at events. People are usually happy to talk for a few minutes.

**1. The situational opener.** The easiest opener is about **something you both can see or share right now**. It doesn't feel random, and it's easy to answer:
- the place: "Have you been here before? Is the food good?"
- the moment: "Is this line always this long?"
- something they have: "Is that book any good? I've been wanting to read it."
- the event: "How do you know the host?" / "First time at one of these?"

Avoid: personal questions straight away ("Where are you from? Are you married?") and compliments about someone's body or face.

**2. From opener to chat.** After their first answer, you have 3 seconds to keep it going. Use **answer, add, ask back** (from [Week 3 Day 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/day-3-answer-add-ask.md)): react to their answer, add something about you, ask a follow-up. After 2–3 exchanges, you can introduce yourself: "I'm Yuki, by the way."

**3. Reading the signals.** Good signs: they turn their body towards you, ask you a question back, give long answers. Bad signs: one-word answers, looking at their phone, headphones going back in. If you see bad signs, exit politely. No problem.

**4. "Where's your accent from?"** People often ask this. It's usually friendly curiosity, and it's a **gift**: an easy topic. Answer it confidently and turn it into conversation: "I'm from Brazil originally. I've been here three years. Have you ever been to South America?" Don't apologise for your accent.

**5. The exit.** Always end warmly. "It was so nice chatting with you." "Anyway, I'll let you get back to your coffee." If you connected: "Hey, are you on LinkedIn? We should stay in touch." or "Are you coming to the next one?"

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sorry, quick question… | starting a chat with a stranger politely | "Sorry, quick question. Is this the line for tickets?" |
| Is anyone sitting here? | sitting next to someone (an easy first step) | "Is anyone sitting here? … Cool, thanks." |
| Have you been here before? | a place-based opener | "Have you been here before? What's good?" |
| Is that any good? | asking about something they have | "Is that any good? I've been wanting to try it." |
| First time at one of these? | an event opener | "First time at one of these? It's mine too." |
| How do you know [name]? | at a party or event | "How do you know Alex?" |
| I couldn't help overhearing… | joining a conversation politely | "Sorry, I couldn't help overhearing, are you guys designers?" |
| I'm [name], by the way. | introducing yourself after a few exchanges | "I'm Sam, by the way." "Oh, I'm Kate!" |
| I'm from ___ originally. | answering the accent question confidently | "I'm from Turkey originally. Have you ever been?" |
| It was so nice chatting with you. | ending warmly | "Well, it was so nice chatting with you!" |
| I'll let you get back to… | a polite exit | "Anyway, I'll let you get back to your work." |
| We should stay in touch. / Are you on…? | swapping contacts | "This was fun. Are you on LinkedIn? We should stay in touch." |

### Examples

**Example 1: The coffee shop**

> You: Sorry, is anyone sitting here?
> Stranger: No, go ahead.
> You: Thanks. Ooh, that looks good. What is it?
> Stranger: It's a cardamom latte. It's kinda their thing.
> You: Oh, nice. I always just get a regular coffee, I'm so boring. Is it sweet?
> Stranger: A little. You should try it.
> You: Next time, for sure. Do you come here a lot?
> Stranger: Like every day. I work from here.
> You: Oh, same! Well, I work from home, but I'm trying to get out more. What do you do?

**Example 2: The accent question**

> Stranger: I'm sorry, where's your accent from?
> You: Oh, I'm from Brazil originally. I've been here about three years.
> Stranger: Oh, cool! I've always wanted to go there.
> You: You totally should. The food alone is worth it. Have you traveled much?
> Stranger: A little. I did Morocco last year.
> You: No way! How was it?

**Example 3: A tech meetup, joining a group**

> You: Hey, sorry, I couldn't help overhearing, you guys work on design systems?
> Person A: Yeah! Well, he does, I just complain about it.
> You: Ha! I'm a product designer, so I'm kinda in the middle. I'm Sam, by the way.
> Person B: Hey, I'm Leo. Where do you work?
> …
> You: OK, I'm gonna grab another drink. It was really nice meeting you guys. Are you coming to the next one?

### Common mistakes

❌ "Excuse me, can I speak English with you?"
✅ "Sorry, quick question… Is this place any good?"
*Asking to practise makes it a lesson, not a chat. Just start a normal conversation.*

❌ "Where are you from? What's your job? Are you married?"
✅ "Have you been here before?"
*Personal questions first feel like an interview. Start with the situation.*

❌ "Sorry for my bad English."
✅ (nothing, just talk) or "I'm from ___ originally."
*Don't apologise. Most people find accents interesting, not a problem.*

❌ Walking away suddenly when you run out of things to say.
✅ "Anyway, it was so nice chatting with you!"
*A warm exit makes the whole conversation feel good, even a short one.*

❌ Continuing when they keep looking at their phone.
✅ "I'll let you get back to it. Have a good one!"
*Reading the signals is part of the skill.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #2: strangers (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md). Go to your chosen place: a meetup, a co-working space, a café with shared tables, a community event, a class, a bookshop event.

**Your mission: three strangers.** Start conversations with **three different people**. Aim for at least one that lasts 5 minutes or more.

Try these 5 things:
1. Use a situational opener (not a personal question).
2. After their first answer, use answer, add, ask back.
3. Introduce yourself by name after a few exchanges.
4. If someone asks about your accent, answer confidently and ask them something back.
5. End every conversation warmly, even the short ones.

Score yourself honestly afterwards: how many conversations did you start? How long was the longest? Short conversations still count. Starting is the skill.

**Backup plan:** if you truly can't get out tonight, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's do a 30-minute voice role-play. I'm going to practise talking to strangers. You'll play five different strangers in five different places, one at a time: someone in a coffee shop line, a person at a tech meetup, someone next to me on a train, a dog owner at a park, and someone at a friend's barbecue who doesn't know anyone. I start each conversation. Be realistic: some strangers are chatty, one is busy and gives short answers (I should notice and exit politely), and one asks me where my accent is from. I'll say "next" to move on. At the end, tell me which openers worked best and give me 3 corrections.
```

### Part B: Drill (30 min)

**Round 1: Opener sprint (10 min).** Use the 12 places in [Exercise 5.1](exercises.md#day-5-exercises). For each one, say an opener, imagine the stranger's answer, and continue for 45 seconds. Out loud, no stopping.

**Round 2: 3/2/1 (15 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md)). **Prompt:** "Tell me about tonight: who did you talk to, how did you start, what did you talk about, what went well and what felt awkward?" (If you did the backup, tell it about the role-plays.) 3, 2, then 1 minute. Use at least one feeling word from [Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/day-2-talking-about-feelings.md).

**Round 3: Listen back (5 min).** Listen to your 1-minute version. Count the 3 numbers quickly. Is the story clear?
