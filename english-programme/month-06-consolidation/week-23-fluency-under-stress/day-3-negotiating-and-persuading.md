## Learning objectives

- Ask for what you want clearly and politely, without apologising too much.
- Use **conditional offers** ("I could do X if you could do Y") to trade.
- Respond to a "no" or a low offer without losing the conversation.
- Persuade with a reason that matters to the **other** person, not only to you.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: persuasive stress and calm push-back.** In a debate or a negotiation, good speakers stay calm and slightly slow, and they put strong stress on the **key word** of their offer: "I could do it by FRIday, IF we cut the second feature." They don't shout; they stress.

Start with your 5-minute sound routine. Then say each line 3 times.

1. "I totally GET that. And… here's what I CAN do."
2. "What if we met in the MIDdle?"
3. "I could do FRIday… IF we drop the export feature."
4. "That's a little more than I was HOPing to pay."
5. "Is there any FLEXibility on the price?"
6. "Would you be OPen to a three-month trial?"
7. "The way I see it… this is GOOD for both of us."
8. "Let me SLEEP on it." ("lemme")
9. "That's kind of a DEAL-breaker for me."
10. "OK… I think we have a DEAL."

**Find a clip:** a friendly debate, a "shark tank" style pitch show, or a negotiation scene in a business podcast. Search for "business pitch negotiation show", "friendly debate podcast", "negotiation role play example". Speak along with the calm, stressed offers. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Negotiating and persuading

### Explanation

Many learners avoid negotiating in English because it feels rude or risky. But in American culture, polite negotiation is normal and expected: for salaries, freelance rates, deadlines, used cars, apartment rent and even weekend plans. The trick is to be **friendly and firm at the same time**.

A simple negotiation has five moves:

1. **Ask clearly.** Say what you want, with a reason: "I was hoping for a bit more flexibility on the deadline, because we lost a week to the bug." Don't over-apologise ("Sorry, I'm so sorry, maybe if possible…").
2. **Listen to their side.** "What would make this work for you?" Find out what matters to **them**.
3. **Trade, don't just ask.** Use "if…": "I could do Friday **if** we drop the second feature." This turns a "no" into a choice.
4. **Handle the no.** If they say no, don't give up straight away and don't argue: "I totally get that. Is there any flexibility on…?" or "What if we…?"
5. **Close or pause.** "OK, so we're agreed: Friday, one feature." Or: "Let me sleep on it." It's always OK to not decide immediately.

**Persuading** works best when your reason matters to the other person. Compare:
- "I want to work from home on Fridays because I like it." (your benefit)
- "If I work from home on Fridays, I can focus on the design work without interruptions, so you'd get the screens faster." (their benefit)

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I was hoping for… | asking for something politely but clearly | "I was hoping for something closer to ninety thousand." |
| Would you be open to…? | suggesting an option | "Would you be open to a three-month trial?" |
| What if we…? | offering a creative solution | "What if we launched with just the core feature?" |
| I could do X if you could do Y. | trading | "I could do Thursday if you could send the content by Monday." |
| Is there any flexibility on…? | responding to a fixed offer | "Is there any flexibility on the price?" |
| That's a bit more than I was hoping to pay. | a polite "too expensive" | "Twelve hundred? That's a bit more than I was hoping to pay." |
| What would make this work for you? | finding out what they need | "OK, so what would make this work for you?" |
| Let's meet in the middle. / Let's split the difference. | proposing a compromise | "You said 400, I said 300. Let's split the difference: 350?" |
| I totally get that, and… | accepting their point before continuing | "I totally get that, and I still think the timeline's too tight." |
| That's kind of a deal-breaker for me. | something you can't accept | "No remote work at all is kind of a deal-breaker for me." |
| Let me sleep on it. | asking for time to decide | "It sounds good. Let me sleep on it and get back to you tomorrow." |
| So, just to confirm… | closing the deal clearly | "So, just to confirm: 350 a month, starting in June." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Deadline at work**

> PM: We need the full redesign by Friday.
> You: I totally get that Friday's important. Honestly, the full redesign by Friday isn't realistic, though. What if we did the checkout screens by Friday, and the rest by next Wednesday?
> PM: Hmm. The checkout's the important part, I guess.
> You: Exactly. And I could even do Thursday for the checkout if you can get me the final copy by tomorrow.
> PM: OK, deal. Checkout Thursday, copy from me tomorrow.
> You: Perfect. So, just to confirm: checkout Thursday, everything else Wednesday.

**Example 2: Buying a used bike**

> Seller: It's three hundred.
> You: It's a really nice bike. Honestly, three hundred's a bit more than I was hoping to pay, though. Is there any flexibility?
> Seller: Maybe a little. What were you thinking?
> You: Would you be open to two-twenty? I can pay cash today.
> Seller: Two-twenty's low. Two-seventy.
> You: Let's meet in the middle. Two-fifty, cash, and I'll pick it up right now.
> Seller: …OK, fine. Two-fifty.

**Example 3: Persuading a friend group**

> Friend: Let's just do the beach again this year.
> You: I love the beach, I do. But what if we tried the mountains this time? It's cheaper, it's not as crowded, and Jess has been wanting to hike forever. If we hate it, I'll plan the beach trip next year myself.
> Friend: Ha, OK, that's actually a pretty good pitch.

### Common mistakes

❌ "Sorry, sorry, I know it's maybe too much, but if possible, maybe…"
✅ "I was hoping for a bit more flexibility on the deadline."
*Too much apologising makes your request sound weak. Polite and clear is enough.*

❌ "No. That's too expensive."
✅ "That's a bit more than I was hoping to pay. Is there any flexibility?"
*A flat "no" ends the conversation. A soft "no" keeps it open.*

❌ "I want you to give me Friday off."
✅ "Would you be open to me taking Friday off? I could finish the report by Thursday."
*Questions and trades sound collaborative. Demands sound aggressive.*

❌ Agreeing to everything immediately because you're nervous.
✅ "Let me sleep on it."
*You're allowed to take time. Native speakers do it all the time.*

❌ "If you will send the content, I will finish."
✅ "If you send the content, I'll finish it." / "I could finish it if you sent the content."
*No "will" in the "if" part.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

Mixed drills tonight. Record everything.

**Part A: Negotiation role-plays (35 min)**
Use the 8 scenarios in [Exercise 3.2](exercises.md#day-3-exercises). For each one, play **both sides**, out loud, for about 4 minutes. You must reach a deal (or a clear "let me sleep on it"). In every negotiation, use at least: one "I was hoping for…", one "if" trade, one response to a "no", and one closing line. Start with the scenario that feels **hardest** for you (for most people that's the salary one). If you finish early, replay one scenario where the other side is much tougher.

**Part B: 3/2/1 persuasive pitch (20 min)** ([how to do the drill](../../toolkit/drill-3-2-1.md))
**Prompt:** "Convince me to try something you love: a hobby, a place, an app, a food or a habit. I'm a little skeptical. Tell me what it is, why it would be good for *me*, deal with my doubts, and make me a small offer at the end (like 'Try it once with me, and if you hate it, I'll buy you dinner')."
Use "their benefit" reasons and stress your key words. 3, 2, then 1 minute.

**Part C: Persuasion random topics (10 min)** ([random-topic drill](../../toolkit/random-topic-drill.md))
60 seconds each. Persuade a listener who disagrees:
- "Convince your manager to let the team have a four-day week."
- "Convince a friend to come to a language exchange meetup with you."
- "Convince your landlord to let you have a cat."
- "Convince your team to test the design with users before building it."
- "Convince your partner (or a friend) to take a vacation with no phones."

**Part D: Listen back (5 min)**
Listen to your hardest role-play. Did you apologise too much? Did you trade with "if"? Rewrite (out loud) the one sentence you'd most like to say differently.
