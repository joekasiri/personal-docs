Tonight's angle is different: **social** spotlight moments, where everyone suddenly looks at you. "Say a few words!", "What do *you* think?", "Tell everyone that funny story!", "Go on, introduce yourself." Same nerves, different setting, and often with people watching.
- You also know group skills from [Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md) and speaking in front of people from [Week 16](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md). Tonight is about the **first 5 seconds** of those moments.
- **Live conversation #1 is tonight.**

## Learning objectives

- Follow the **2-second rule**: say a useful first sentence within 2 seconds, every time.
- React to being put on the spot with humour or honesty, instead of freezing.
- Give a **short version** answer (3 sentences) when everyone is waiting.
- Turn the spotlight back to someone else smoothly when you're done.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Tonight's feature: quick reaction, then answer.** Watch talk-show guests or panelists when the host surprises them. They react first, often with a laugh or an "Ooh!", and then answer, with almost no gap. The reaction is high and quick, the answer starts lower and steadier.

Start with your 5-minute sound routine. Then say each line 3 times, the last time fast.

1. "Ooh, putting me on the SPOT! OK, um, so…"
2. "Ha! Oh man. OK… off the top of my HEAD…"
3. "Wow, no pressure, RIGHT? So, basically…"
4. "Good question. My gut reaction is… YES."
5. "Honestly? I have no IDEA. But if I had to GUESS…"
6. "OK, short version… we MET at a wedding."
7. "Let me think… TWO things, really."
8. "Oh gosh, OK. I'll keep it SHORT."
9. "That's a tough one. Can I come BACK to that?"
10. "Anyway, that's me. What about YOU guys?"

**Find a clip:** a talk show or a panel where guests get surprise questions, or a rapid-fire interview game. Search for "rapid fire questions celebrity interview", "talk show surprise question", "podcast lightning round". Speak along at full speed, especially with the reactions. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Being put on the spot

### Explanation

Being put on the spot means someone suddenly asks you to speak, and everyone's waiting. The panic usually comes from one thought: "I need to say something good." That's too much pressure. The real job is simpler: **say something useful within 2 seconds, then keep going.**

**The 2-second rule.** Your first sentence must come out within 2 seconds, even if it's not your answer yet. It can be:

1. **A reaction** that buys time and shows you're relaxed: "Ooh, putting me on the spot!", "Ha, no pressure, right?"
2. **A frame** that tells people how long and what kind of answer is coming: "OK, short version…", "Two things, really…", "Off the top of my head…"
3. **An honest "I don't know, but…"**: "Honestly, I have no idea, but if I had to guess…"

Then give the **short version**: 2–4 sentences. In a social spotlight, short is always better. People love a quick, confident answer. Nobody wants a five-minute speech at a dinner table.

Finally, **pass the spotlight back**: "What about you?", "Anyway, enough about me. Sam, you've done this, right?" This shows confidence and makes the moment end naturally.

**Humour helps.** A small joke about the pressure ("I knew this would happen", "OK, I wasn't ready for this") is very natural in American English, and it relaxes everyone, including you. You know these from [Week 14](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md).

### Key phrases

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ooh, putting me on the spot! | someone surprises you with a question in front of others | "Ooh, putting me on the spot! OK, so…" |
| No pressure, right? (ha) | making a small joke about the pressure | "Say a few words? Ha, no pressure, right?" |
| Off the top of my head… | giving a quick answer without preparation | "Off the top of my head, probably Lisbon." |
| My gut reaction is… | giving your first, instinctive opinion | "My gut reaction is no, but I'd wanna think about it." |
| OK, short version… | promising a short answer | "OK, short version: I moved here for a job and stayed for the tacos." |
| Two things, really. | framing a quick structured answer | "Two things, really. The people and the food." |
| Honestly, I have no idea, but if I had to guess… | you don't know but still answer | "Honestly, I have no idea, but if I had to guess, I'd say three hundred?" |
| I'll keep it short. | starting an unexpected speech or toast | "Oh gosh, OK, I'll keep it short." |
| Can I come back to that? | you need more time (use rarely) | "That's a tough one. Can I come back to that?" |
| I wasn't ready for that one! | laughing at a hard surprise question | "Ha, I wasn't ready for that one! OK…" |
| Anyway, that's me. What about you? | handing the spotlight back | "…so yeah, anyway, that's me. What about you?" |
| I'll let [name] tell it, they tell it better. | passing a story to someone else | "Oh, I'll let Jess tell it, she tells it better." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Dinner table**

> Friend: OK, everyone, quick, best trip you've ever taken. You first!
> You: Ooh, putting me on the spot! OK, off the top of my head… Japan. Two weeks, just trains and noodles. Best food of my life. What about you, Chris?

**Example 2: "Say a few words"**

> Host: And now… let's hear from the person who helped plan all this! Come on, say a few words!
> You: Ha, no pressure, right? OK, I'll keep it short. Thank you all so much for coming. Planning this was chaos, but seeing everyone here makes it totally worth it. So, enjoy, eat a lot, and… yeah! Cheers!

**Example 3: A question you can't answer**

> Colleague (in front of a group): Hey, you know about this stuff. How many people use our app every day?
> You: Honestly, I have no idea about the exact number, but if I had to guess, I'd say around fifty thousand? Maria would know for sure. Maria?

### Common mistakes

❌ (Silence for 5 seconds) "Umm… umm… I don't know…"
✅ "Ooh, putting me on the spot! OK, so…"
*A reaction in the first 2 seconds stops the freeze.*

❌ "Sorry, my English is not good, I can't explain."
✅ "OK, short version…"
*Apologising for your English draws attention to it. Just give a short answer.*

❌ A three-minute answer at a dinner party.
✅ 2–4 sentences, then "What about you?"
*In social spotlights, short and confident beats long and complete.*

❌ "I can come back to that?" (for everything)
✅ Use it once, maybe. Otherwise, give your gut reaction.
*Delaying every question sounds evasive. A quick, honest guess is usually better.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #1 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md). A group is ideal tonight (3+ people), but one-on-one works too.

**Your mission: never freeze.** Tonight, every time someone asks you something, your first words come within 2 seconds.

Try these 5 things:
1. Use at least two reactions or frames from tonight ("Off the top of my head…", "Short version…").
2. Put **someone else** on the spot in a friendly way ("OK, your turn, best trip ever?"), and see how they handle it. Notice what native speakers do.
3. When you get a question you can't answer, use "Honestly, I have no idea, but if I had to guess…".
4. Pass the spotlight back at least twice ("What about you?").
5. If you're in a group, volunteer to answer first at least once. Being first is a small, controlled way to practise being on the spot.

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's do a 30-minute voice role-play. You're the host of a dinner party with four guests (you play all of them: Sam, Jess, Chris and Maria, each with a different personality). Every few minutes, one of you suddenly puts me on the spot in front of everyone: ask me for a quick opinion, a story, a toast, an introduction, or a fact I probably don't know. Keep the conversation flowing between the guests in between. If I take more than 2 seconds to start answering, say "Tick tock!" playfully. At the end, tell me which moments I handled best and give me 3 corrections.
```

### Part B: Hot-seat drill (30 min)

Use the 40 questions in [Exercise 2.1](exercises.md#day-2-exercises).

1. **Round 1 (10 min):** shuffle them. For each one, start answering within 2 seconds and speak for 20–30 seconds. Use a different opener each time.
2. **Round 2 (10 min):** go again with the questions you found hardest, but now imagine 10 people are watching. Stand up. Speak a little louder (your projection from [Week 16](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md)).
3. **Round 3 (10 min):** record 5 answers and listen. Count how many started within 2 seconds. Goal: 5 out of 5.
