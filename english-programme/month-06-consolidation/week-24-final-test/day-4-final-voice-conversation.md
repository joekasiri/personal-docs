# Week 24 · Day 4: Final voice conversation

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Main skill 20 min · Speaking drill 70 min (voice mode with Claude: the 30-minute conversation) · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Tonight is step 2 of the final test from your original plan: **record a 30-minute unscripted conversation**. It's the longest single conversation in the programme. In Week 1, 20 minutes of voice mode felt long. Tonight, 30 minutes, no script, no topic list.
- Set up [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and a **second device to record** the whole conversation (your phone's voice recorder is perfect). Check that the battery and storage are OK for 30+ minutes.
- Find a quiet place where you won't be interrupted for 45 minutes. Have water nearby.
- Open the **conversation analysis sheet** in [Day 4 exercises](exercises.md#day-4-exercises).

## Learning objectives

- Keep an unscripted conversation going for 30 minutes without long silences.
- Move between many kinds of talk (stories, opinions, hypotheticals, deep topics, humour) naturally.
- Recover from moments of tiredness or blankness, and keep going.
- Analyse a long recording and find the moments where you still had to think.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min)

**Warm up your mouth and your stamina.**

1. **Your hardest sound (5 min):** do Claude's 5-minute sound routine from [Week 22](../week-22-sounding-natural/day-6-weekly-check.md).
2. **A full 2-minute clip (10 min):** pick a relaxed, long-form conversation podcast (search for "two friends podcast chat" or "long form conversation podcast"). Speak along for 2 minutes, twice. Then listen to the next 3 minutes without speaking, just to get the rhythm of a long, relaxed conversation into your head.

Warm-up lines (full speed, relaxed):

1. "Oh man, where do I even START?"
2. "OK, so that's a LONG story, but basically…"
3. "Wait, that reminds me of something…"
4. "Sorry, I totally lost my train of thought. Where WAS I?"
5. "Honestly? I've never really thought about it THAT way."
6. "OK, your turn. What about YOU?"
7. "Ha, no, no, that's not what I MEANT."
8. "Anyway, we're getting off topic. So…"

See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): How to last 30 minutes

### Explanation

A 30-minute conversation is a different challenge from a 2-minute talk. Three things happen around minute 15–20:

1. **You get tired.** Speaking a second language uses a lot of energy. Your sentences get shorter and the "umm"s come back.
2. **You run out of the topics you expected.** The conversation goes somewhere new, and you have to improvise.
3. **You notice yourself.** You start thinking "Am I doing OK?", which pulls your attention away from the conversation.

Here's how to handle each one:

- **Tiredness:** use your **automatic chunks**. They cost almost no energy. "That's the thing…", "Honestly…", "I feel like…". Slow down a little (brake phrases from [Week 22 Day 5](../week-22-sounding-natural/day-5-controlling-your-speed.md)). It's fine to say "Wow, my brain's getting tired, haha," and keep going.
- **New topics:** you've practised random topics since [Week 9](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md). New is normal. Use a time-buyer, then start: "Ooh, good question. I guess…".
- **Noticing yourself:** focus on the **other person**. Ask questions, react, follow up. Curiosity is the best cure for self-consciousness. That's what [Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/README.md) trained.

**Don't plan the content.** You may choose a starting topic (the analysis sheet has a menu), but after that, let it go where it goes. The test is whether you can follow a real conversation, not a plan.

### Key phrases

The "keep going" phrases from across the programme. These are your stamina tools tonight.

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| That reminds me… | moving to a new topic naturally ([Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md)) | "That reminds me, did I tell you about my trip?" |
| Where was I? | getting back after a side story ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "Sorry, where was I? Oh right, the interview." |
| What's the word… anyway, it's basically… | a missing word ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "It's a… what's the word… anyway, it's basically a tiny house." |
| I'm not sure, but I think… | a topic you don't know well ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "I'm not sure, but I think it started in Japan." |
| So basically what I'm saying is… | summarising when you've rambled ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "So basically what I'm saying is, I need a vacation." |
| Ooh, good question. | buying time for a new question ([Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md)) | "Ooh, good question. Let me think." |
| What about you? | handing the turn back when you're tired ([Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md)) | "…so that's my take. What about you?" |
| Wait, can we go back to…? | returning to an interesting earlier topic | "Wait, can we go back to the thing about your sister?" |
| Anyway, we're getting off topic. | steering back | "Ha, anyway, we're getting off topic." |
| My brain's getting tired, haha. | being honest about fatigue without stopping | "Sorry, my brain's getting tired, haha. OK, so…" |

### Examples

**Example 1: Recovering at minute 20**

> Claude: So what do you think about cities banning cars from downtown?
> You: Ooh, good question. Honestly, my brain's getting a little tired, haha. But… I think I'm mostly for it? Like, on one hand, it's way nicer to walk around. On the other hand, what about people who can't walk far? So I guess it depends how they do it. What about you?

**Example 2: Following a new topic**

> Claude: Wait, you mentioned your grandma earlier. What was she like?
> You: Oh, she was amazing. That's actually a long story, but basically, she ran a small shop for like forty years…

**Example 3: Going back**

> You: …so yeah, that's why I don't like flying. Anyway, wait, can we go back to the thing you said about learning to cook? Because I'm kinda trying to do that right now.

### Common mistakes

❌ Stopping the conversation at minute 15 because "it's going badly".
✅ Keep going. Minutes 20–30 are where the real data is.
*The test is stamina. Your best moments often come after you've been tired.*

❌ Answering every question in one sentence when you're tired.
✅ Answer, add, ask back, even if the sentences are simple.
*Short answers kill a long conversation. Simple-but-complete keeps it alive.*

❌ Asking Claude to stop and correct you in the middle.
✅ Save the feedback for the end.
*Stopping for corrections breaks the "unscripted" test.*

❌ Preparing a list of things to say.
✅ Choose a starting topic only, then follow the conversation.
*Unscripted means unscripted.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Voice mode with Claude

**Step 1 (5 min): set up.** Start your second-device recording. Open voice mode and paste:

```
Tonight is my final test after six months of English speaking practice. Let's have a completely unscripted, casual voice conversation for 30 minutes, like two friends catching up. Start with a normal "How's it going?". Let the conversation go wherever it naturally goes. During the 30 minutes, at some point, naturally bring in: a story (ask me to tell you about something that happened), an opinion question, a "what if" question, something a bit deeper (feelings, goals or values), something funny, and one tough or unexpected question. Share your own opinions and stories too, and sometimes disagree with me. Use natural, casual American English at normal speed. Do not correct me or give feedback during the conversation. I'll say "OK, that's thirty minutes" when we're done.
```

**Step 2 (30 min): the conversation.** Talk. Don't watch the clock. If you get tired, use the stamina phrases. If you blank, use "What's the word… anyway…". Keep going until 30 minutes.

**Step 3 (5 min): feedback from Claude.** When you finish, paste or say:

```
OK, that's thirty minutes. Thank you! Now please step out of the conversation and give me honest feedback: (1) the 5 most important corrections, with my sentence and the natural version, (2) the moments where I seemed to struggle or hesitate most, and what kind of situation they were (a topic, a type of question, a grammar point), (3) the 5 phrases I used most naturally, and (4) your overall impression of my fluency compared to a typical B2 speaker.
```

**Step 4 (25 min): analyse your recording.** Use the **conversation analysis sheet** (Exercise 4.4) in [Day 4 exercises](exercises.md#day-4-exercises). You don't need to listen to all 30 minutes: listen to **three 2-minute samples** (minutes 2–4, 14–16 and 26–28), count the 3 numbers in each, and note the moments where you had to think.

**Step 5 (5 min): compare the samples.** Was minute 26 worse than minute 2? Better? Most people expect it to be much worse, and find it isn't.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Log Claude's feedback (7 min):** write the 5 corrections in your [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md) and say each correct version 3 times. Copy Claude's "struggle moments" into your "still had to think" list (you'll finish it tomorrow).
2. **Spaced review out loud (5 min):** a quick round of last week's phrases (Week 23) and last month's (Week 17).
3. **Nightly log (3 min):** write the 3 numbers for each sample in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), plus: "I talked for 30 minutes without stopping: yes / no".

By the end of tonight you can…

- [ ] Have a 30-minute unscripted conversation in English, recorded.
- [ ] Recover from tiredness or a blank moment and keep going.
- [ ] Count the 3 numbers in three samples from a long recording.
- [ ] List the moments in a real conversation where you still had to think.
