## 1. Shadowing (15 min): redo Week 1, the full storytime clip

On [Week 1 Day 5](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-5-describing-people-and-places.md) you shadowed **one full 2-minute storytime clip** and tried to copy everything. Tonight, do it again.

1. Find the same clip, or a similar one: search for "casual vlog storytime" or "storytime funny thing happened".
2. Listen once. Then **speak along** with the whole 2 minutes, twice. No pausing.
3. Record the second time.
4. If you have your Week 1 Day 5 recording, play 30 seconds of it, then 30 seconds of tonight's.

Before you start, warm up with these lines, at full speed with full emotion (your [Week 22](../week-22-sounding-natural/README.md) skills):

1. "OK, so you guys are NOT gonna believe what happened to me today."
2. "So I'm just standing there, minding my own business, right?"
3. "And out of NOwhere, this guy comes up to me and goes…"
4. "I was like, 'Um, excuse me?'"
5. "And he's like, 'Oh my god, I'm SO sorry, I thought you were someone else.'"
6. "Which was weird, because… we were wearing the exact same jacket."
7. "Long story short, we ended up getting coffee."
8. "And now? He's literally one of my best friends."

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Rapid-fire test, Weeks 17–23

### Explanation

Same format as Days 1 and 2: 6 prompts per week, 42 in total, answered out loud in 10–30 seconds each (some of tonight's prompts need a little longer), recorded. Score each week 1–3.

These weeks are about high-pressure speaking: meetings, explaining your work, complex topics, tough questions, deep conversations, accent and fluency under stress. Many of the prompts put you in a role: a meeting, an interview, a conflict. **Answer as if it's real.**

### Key phrases

Core chunks from Months 5 and 6. Are they automatic?

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| So, quick update… | giving an update in a meeting ([Week 17](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-17-meetings/README.md)) | "So, quick update: the designs are done." |
| I hear you, but… | pushing back politely ([Week 17](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-17-meetings/README.md)) | "I hear you, but I don't think we're ready." |
| The downside is…, but… | explaining a trade-off ([Week 18](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-18-explaining-your-work/README.md)) | "The downside is it's slower, but it's way safer." |
| On one hand…, on the other… | a balanced view ([Week 19](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-19-complex-topics/README.md)) | "On one hand, AI saves time. On the other, it makes mistakes." |
| That's a fair question. | a tough question ([Week 20](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/README.md)) | "That's a fair question. Here's how I see it." |
| What was that like? | going deeper ([Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/README.md)) | "You moved alone? What was that like?" |
| So here's the thing… | braking before a key point ([Week 22](../week-22-sounding-natural/README.md)) | "So here's the thing… we're out of budget." |
| I could do X if you could do Y. | negotiating ([Week 23](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/README.md)) | "I could do Thursday if you could send the copy today." |
| I can tell you're really frustrated. | handling conflict ([Week 23](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/README.md)) | "I can tell you're really frustrated. Let's figure it out." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Week 17, score 3** (Prompt: "Your manager says 'Let's launch tomorrow.' Push back politely.")

> "I hear you, and I'd love to launch tomorrow too. But we still have two bugs in checkout. What if we launched on Thursday, once they're fixed?"

**Example 2: Week 22, score 2** (Prompt: "Say 'Really?' twice: interested, then doubtful.")

> "Really? ↗ … Really? ↘↗" The doubtful one sounded almost the same. That's a 2: practise the rise-fall-rise.

**Example 3: Week 23, score 3** (Prompt: "A stranger asks: 'Where's your accent from?'")

> "Oh, I'm from Brazil originally. I've been here about three years. Have you ever been?"

### Common mistakes

❌ Answering meeting and interview prompts as if they're casual chats.
✅ Use the work register: "So, quick update…", "Just to make sure I get it…".
*Part of the skill is choosing the right style for the situation.*

❌ Giving one-word answers to the deep-conversation prompts.
✅ A precise feeling, a reason, and a question back.
*Week 21 is tested on depth, not speed.*

❌ Forgetting the voice in the Week 22 prompts.
✅ Exaggerate stress, pauses and emotion. That's what's being tested.
*Sound is the content of those prompts.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Long test tasks

Use **Test 3.2: Long tasks** in [Day 3 exercises](exercises.md#day-3-exercises). There are 7 tasks, one for each week, about 6 minutes each. Record every task.

1. **Tasks 1–7 (45 min):** in order, no preparation. Stand up for Tasks 4 and 7.
2. **Listen and score (15 min):** use each task's success criteria. Fill in column 2, then the final score for each week.
3. **Complete the full score sheet (10 min):** add your totals for all six months in **Test 3.3**. Look at the pattern: which month is strongest? Which weeks scored 1? Write the list of weeks to revisit, with links, for Day 6.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Spaced review out loud (8 min):** last night's phrases, last week's (Week 23) and one page from last month (Week 18). See [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).
2. **Stubborn ten (2 min):** look at your stubborn ten. Did any of them come out by themselves during the tests? Tick them.
3. **Nightly log (5 min):** write your test totals for all six months in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), plus one sentence: "Across all three tests, the thing that surprised me most was…".
