# Week 24 · Day 2: Review of Months 3 and 4

**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Main skill 20 min (rapid-fire test) · Speaking drill 70 min (40 min live conversation + 30 min test tasks) · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Tonight tests **Weeks 9–16**: [Month 3: Spontaneity](../../month-03-spontaneity/README.md) and [Month 4: Real-life pressure](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/README.md).
- Two of those weeks, **group conversations** ([Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md)) and **humour and banter** ([Week 14](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md)), can only really be tested with real people. So tonight's **live conversation** is part of the test. A group of 3+ is ideal.
- Have [Day 2 exercises](exercises.md#day-2-exercises) open, your recording app ready, and your score sheet from last night.
- Same rules as last night: no preparation, start within 2 seconds, never restart.

## Learning objectives

- Find out which Month 3 and Month 4 skills are now automatic.
- Test group and humour skills in a real conversation.
- Find the 2–3 skills from these months that still need work.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): redo Week 1, linking and rhythm

In Week 1 you practised **linking** ("pick it up" → "pi-ki-dup") and **sentence rhythm** (stressed words long, the rest squashed). Tonight, go back to them.

1. If you kept your Week 1 recordings, play one, then record the same lines now and compare.
2. Otherwise, do these lines with pause-and-repeat, then find a casual storytime clip (search for "casual vlog storytime") and speak along for 5 minutes.

Practise each line twice. Link the words joined with ‿, and stress the CAPITALS.

1. "Pick‿it‿UP and put‿it‿aWAY." ("pi-ki-dup", "pu-di-daway")
2. "I'll call‿you‿in‿an HOUR."
3. "Turn‿it‿OFF, it's‿all‿over the PLACE."
4. "Not‿at‿ALL. It's‿an‿OLD one."
5. "I KNEW‿it was gonna RAIN."
6. "We MET‿at a PARty in BROOKlyn."
7. "Can‿I get‿a large COFfee‿and‿a MUFfin?"
8. "Check‿it‿OUT, it's‿amAZing."
9. "She LOOKED‿at me like I was CRAzy."
10. "GIVE‿it a TRY. You'll LOVE‿it."

Notice: in Week 1 you had to think about every link. Now they should just happen. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Rapid-fire test, Weeks 9–16

### Explanation

Same format as [Day 1](day-1-review-months-1-and-2.md): 48 prompts, 6 per week, answered out loud in 10–20 seconds each, recorded. Months 3 and 4 were about **no preparation** and **real-life pressure**, so these prompts are harder: talking on the spot, hypotheticals, paraphrasing, role-plays, groups, humour, calls and speaking in front of people.

Score each week **3** (automatic), **2** (had to think) or **1** (couldn't do it yet), using the same table as last night. For Weeks 13 and 14, give a first score now and a second score after the live conversation.

### Key phrases

Core chunks from Months 3 and 4. Are they automatic?

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Would you rather…? | a fun on-the-spot question ([Week 9](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md)) | "Would you rather live by the beach or in the mountains?" |
| If I'd known…, I would've… | a past hypothetical ([Week 10](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md)) | "If I'd known it was that cold, I would've brought a jacket." |
| It's like a… kind of a… | paraphrasing a missing word ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "It's like a… kind of a pan for making waffles." |
| Where was I? | getting back on track ([Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)) | "Sorry, where was I? Oh yeah, the hotel." |
| Would you mind…? | asking a favour ([Week 12](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/README.md)) | "Would you mind helping me move on Saturday?" |
| Can I just add something? | jumping into a group ([Week 13](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md)) | "Sorry, can I just add something quick?" |
| It took, like, a million years. | exaggeration for humour ([Week 14](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md)) | "The line took, like, a million years." |
| You're breaking up. | a bad call ([Week 15](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-15-calls/README.md)) | "Sorry, you're breaking up. Can you say that again?" |
| That's a great question. | answering in front of people ([Week 16](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md)) | "That's a great question. So, the short answer is yes." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Week 11 paraphrase, score 3**

> Prompt: "Describe 'umbrella' without saying it."
> "OK, so it's the thing you open over your head when it's raining, so you don't get wet. It folds up, and you always forget it on the train."

**Example 2: Week 10 hypothetical, score 2**

> Prompt: "Finish: 'If I hadn't started learning English…'"
> "If I hadn't started learning English, I… would… I wouldn't have got this job, I think. Or I would've stayed in my old job."

Correct, but slow to start. That's a 2.

**Example 3: Week 15 numbers, score 3**

> Prompt: "Say clearly: 15, 50, 2:45, $19.99."
> "FifTEEN. FIFty. Two forty-five. Nineteen ninety-nine."

### Common mistakes

❌ Preparing your answers to the would-you-rather questions before recording.
✅ Read one prompt, answer immediately, then read the next.
*Month 3's whole point was zero preparation.*

❌ Saying "I don't know this word" and stopping during the paraphrase prompts.
✅ "It's like a… you know, the thing you use to…"
*Paraphrasing is the skill being tested.*

❌ Doing the humour prompts in a flat, serious voice.
✅ Use the tone: sarcasm low and flat, exaggeration big and stretched.
*Humour lives in the voice ([Week 22 Day 3](../week-22-sounding-natural/day-3-emotion-in-your-voice.md)).*

1. **Test 2.1: Rapid-fire, Weeks 9–16** (18 min): 48 prompts, recorded.
2. **Score sheet, column 1** (2 min).

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #1, the group and humour test (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md). A group (3+ people) is best: a meetup, dinner with friends, a group call.

**Your mission:** have a normal, fun conversation, and quietly tick the group and humour skills from Weeks 13 and 14 as you use them. Use the **Test 2.3 live checklist** in [Day 2 exercises](exercises.md#day-2-exercises). You don't need to tick everything. Aim for 6 out of 10.

Try these 4 things especially:
1. Jump in at least twice ("Oh, that reminds me…", "Can I just add…").
2. Bring a quiet person in ("What about you, Sam?").
3. Use exaggeration or gentle sarcasm at least once.
4. Ask someone to repeat or explain, naturally ("Wait, you lost me").

**Backup plan:** if your group falls through, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's do a 30-minute group conversation role-play. You play three friends at a bar: Mia (talks a lot and interrupts), Ben (quiet, you have to bring him in) and Carlos (makes sarcastic jokes and teases people a little). Talk to each other as well as to me, so I have to jump in. Use casual American English, jokes and some fast talking. Sometimes say something I might not understand, so I can practise asking for repetition. At the end, tell me how often I jumped in, whether I brought Ben in, and give me 3 corrections.
```

After the conversation, give Weeks 13 and 14 their **column 2** scores on the score sheet.

### Part B: Long test tasks (30 min)

Use **Test 2.2: Long tasks** in [Day 2 exercises](exercises.md#day-2-exercises): 6 tasks for Weeks 9, 10, 11, 12, 15 and 16, about 4 minutes each, recorded. Then listen and score (column 2) using each task's success criteria (6 min).

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Complete the score sheet (5 min):** final scores for Weeks 9–16, and links for any 1s.
2. **Spaced review out loud (7 min):** last night's test phrases, last week's (Week 23) and one page from last month (Week 20). See [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).
3. **Nightly log (3 min):** totals for Months 3 and 4 in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), plus: "In the live conversation, the skill that came out most naturally was…".
