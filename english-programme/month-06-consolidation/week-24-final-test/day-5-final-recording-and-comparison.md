## 2. Main skill (20 min): The final baseline

### Explanation

Your original plan's final test has four steps. You did step 2 (the 30-minute conversation) last night. Tonight is **step 1 and step 3**:

1. **Redo the Day 1 baseline**: 2 minutes, unprepared, the same prompt. Count your 3 numbers.
2. ~~Record a 30-minute unscripted conversation~~ ✔ (Day 4)
3. **Compare both with your Day 1 recording.**
4. Write down where you still had to think, and send it all to Claude (Day 6).

**Same conditions = fair comparison.** For the numbers to mean something, the conditions must be the same as in Week 1:
- **The same prompt.** Use exactly the baseline prompt from [Week 1 Day 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md), the one every monthly check has used: **"Tell me about a day that didn't go as planned."** Choose a **different day** from your life, one you haven't told in this programme, so you can't rely on a story you've already practised.
- **No preparation.** Read the prompt, press record, start talking.
- **Exactly 2 minutes.** Use a timer.
- **Same counting rules** ([The 3 numbers](../../toolkit/the-3-numbers.md)): umms/uhhs; pauses longer than 2 seconds; different connectors (not "and" and "so").

**The targets** (from the [Month 6 overview](../README.md)):
- **Umms and long pauses:** about **half** of your Week 1 numbers (a 50% drop).
- **Connectors:** at least **8 different** connectors in 2 minutes.

**How to calculate your drop:** (baseline − final) ÷ baseline × 100. For example, baseline 24 umms, final 11: (24 − 11) ÷ 24 × 100 = 54% fewer. Target hit.

**Numbers aren't everything.** You might have more umms than you hoped but talk twice as fast, tell a much better story, or sound much more relaxed. That's why tonight's comparison also includes **listening**, not just counting.

### Key phrases

Tonight you'll **talk about your progress**, in your live conversation and on Day 6. Here's the language for it.

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Compared to six months ago… | comparing then and now | "Compared to six months ago, I'm way more relaxed." |
| I used to…, but now I… | describing a change | "I used to freeze in meetings, but now I just start talking." |
| …went down by about half. | describing a number that dropped | "My umms went down by about half." |
| Way fewer… / way more… | casual comparison | "I use way fewer fillers now." |
| It's gotten a lot easier to… | describing something that improved over time | "It's gotten a lot easier to talk to strangers." |
| I still have to think when… | naming what's still hard | "I still have to think when people talk really fast in a group." |
| The biggest difference is… | the main change | "The biggest difference is I don't translate in my head anymore." |
| I didn't expect… | a surprise about your progress | "I didn't expect my accent to change that much." |
| Honestly, I'm kinda proud of… | being proud, casually | "Honestly, I'm kinda proud of sticking with it." |
| The next step for me is… | what comes next | "The next step for me is presenting at a conference." |

### Examples

**Example 1: Talking about progress**

> A: So you've been doing this English thing for six months?
> B: Yeah, every night. It's kind of crazy.
> A: Has it helped?
> B: Honestly, a lot. Compared to six months ago, I'm way more relaxed. I used to freeze when someone asked me something in a meeting. Now I just start talking. The biggest difference is I don't translate in my head anymore. Like, most of the time.
> A: That's awesome.
> B: I still have to think when people talk really fast in a group, though. That's the next step.

**Example 2: Talking about the numbers**

> "So in my first recording, I said 'umm' like twenty-six times in two minutes. Today it was twelve. So it went down by more than half. And I used nine different connectors. In Week 1, I used three."

### Common mistakes

❌ Listening to your Week 1 baseline before you record tonight.
✅ Record first, listen after.
*Hearing the old version first changes how you speak tonight.*

❌ Choosing an easy prompt because you want good numbers.
✅ Use the same prompt as Week 1 ("Tell me about a day that didn't go as planned"), with a different day.
*The same prompt makes the comparison fair. An honest comparison is more useful than a flattering one.*

❌ "My English got more better."
✅ "My English got a lot better." / "My English is way better."
*No "more" before "-er" comparatives.*

❌ Recording three times and keeping the best one.
✅ One take, like Week 1.
*The baseline was one take. Be fair to your own data.*

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min)

### Part A: Live conversation #2 (40 min)

See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).

**Your mission: talk about the journey.** Have a normal conversation, and at some point tell your partner about the last six months: what you did, what changed, what was hard, what's still hard. Use tonight's comparison phrases. If they're also a learner, ask about their journey.

Try these 4 things:
1. Use at least 4 phrases from tonight's table.
2. Tell one short story from the programme (a live conversation that went well or badly, a funny voice-mode moment, the stranger challenge).
3. Ask them: "Have you ever learned something that took a long time? What was that like?" (a [Week 21](../week-21-deep-conversations/README.md) bridge question).
4. Notice: is this conversation easier than your first live conversation in Week 1?

**Backup plan:** if your partner cancels, open [voice mode with Claude](../../toolkit/voice-mode-with-claude.md) and paste:

```
Let's have a 30-minute casual voice conversation. You're Jordan, a friend I haven't seen for six months. You know I've been practising English every night. Ask me how it went, what changed, what was hard, what was funny, and what I want to do next. Ask lots of follow-up questions and share your own story about learning something difficult. Casual American English. At the end, give me 3 corrections and tell me one thing that sounded really natural.
```

### Part B: The comparison (30 min)

Use **Exercises 5.3 and 5.4** in [Day 5 exercises](exercises.md#day-5-exercises).

1. **Listen back to back (12 min):** play your Week 1 baseline in full. Then tonight's final recording in full. Then the first 30 seconds of each again.
2. **Fill in the listening comparison (8 min):** Exercise 5.3: speed, sounds, chunks, stories, confidence.
3. **Write your "still had to think" list (10 min):** Exercise 5.4. Use last night's analysis, Claude's struggle moments, your test scores from Days 1–3, and tonight. This list is the most important input for your next plan.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Finish the comparison table (5 min):** add the percentages and tick the targets you hit.
2. **Say it out loud (5 min):** record a 1-minute voice note to yourself: "Compared to Week 1…". You'll keep this one.
3. **Nightly log (5 min):** write tonight's numbers in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md) next to your Week 1 numbers, one last time. Then look at the whole log. That's six months of work.
