# Week 24 exercises: Final test
[Week 24 overview](README.md) · [Day 1](day-1-review-months-1-and-2.md) · [Day 2](day-2-review-months-3-and-4.md) · [Day 3](day-3-review-months-5-and-6.md) · [Day 4](day-4-final-voice-conversation.md) · [Day 5](day-5-final-recording-and-comparison.md) · [Day 6](day-6-weekly-check.md)

**How to use this week's tests:** everything is **out loud and recorded**. Don't read ahead, and don't prepare. Read one prompt, start talking within 2 seconds, and never restart: if you make a mistake, correct it smoothly and keep going. Each section tells you which week it tests and links to that week, so you know exactly where to go back if a skill isn't automatic yet. Score from your **recordings**, not from how you felt.

**Scoring for every week:**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3** | Automatic: straight away, natural, no real thinking |
| **2** | Got there, but had to think, paused, or it sounded a bit unnatural |
| **1** | Couldn't do it yet, froze, or had to stop and restart |

## Day 1 exercises

### Test 1.1: Rapid-fire, Weeks 1–8

**Time:** 18 min · **Type:** record

1. Start recording. Keep the recording running for the whole test.
2. Read each prompt and answer out loud **straight away**: 10–20 seconds for most prompts, up to 30 seconds where it says so.
3. After each week's 6 prompts, write a quick score (1–3) in **column 1** of the score sheet (Test 1.3). Don't check the answer key until you've finished all 48.

#### Week 1: Storytelling

Tests [Week 1: Storytelling](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/README.md).

1. Say **six** different storytelling connectors in 20 seconds, each in a short sentence.
2. React with two phrases: "I locked myself out of my apartment in my pajamas."
3. Finish the sentence in two ways: "I was walking home when…"
4. In 30 seconds, tell a story with a setting, a problem, a turning point and an ending: "the worst haircut of my life".
5. Describe the most crowded place you've ever been, using 3 casual, vivid phrases.
6. Correct this sentence: "When I arrived, the movie already started."

#### Week 2: Questions

Tests [Week 2: Questions](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/README.md).

1. Ask **five** different past-tense questions about someone's last vacation, in 20 seconds (what, where, who, how, why).
2. Your friend says, "I quit my job." Ask two follow-up questions.
3. Make it indirect, starting with "Do you know…": "Where is the station?"
4. Make it indirect, starting with "Can you tell me…": "What time does it start?"
5. Add question tags: "You're coming tonight, ___?" / "She didn't call, ___?" / "You've met Sam, ___?"
6. Give natural short answers: "Have you been to Mexico?" (yes) / "Do you like sushi?" (no) / "Did you finish it?" (yes)

#### Week 3: Small talk

Tests [Week 3: Small talk](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md).

1. Answer "What've you been up to?" with something better than "Not much".
2. Use an echo question and a reaction: "I went skydiving last weekend."
3. Answer, add, ask back: "Do you like your job?"
4. Use **three** different time-buyers in one answer: "What's the meaning of life?" (30 seconds)
5. You're talking about the weather. Change the topic to food using "Speaking of…" or "That reminds me…".
6. End a conversation politely in one or two sentences.

#### Week 4: Vocabulary for everyday talk

Tests [Week 4: Vocabulary](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/README.md).

1. Tell a 20-second story using "end up", "figure out" and "run into".
2. Say the right verb (make, do, take, have) for each: ___ a decision, ___ a break, ___ a favor, ___ a chat, ___ your best, ___ a mistake.
3. Soften this: "Your presentation was bad."
4. Use **three** everyday idioms in sentences, in 20 seconds.
5. Explain what you do for work to a stranger in 20 seconds.
6. Describe a recent problem at work in 3 sentences, using "came up" and "sort out".

#### Week 5: Better stories

Tests [Week 5: Better stories](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/README.md).

1. Set the scene for a story in one sentence, starting with "So this was…".
2. Say three suspense phrases, each in a sentence.
3. Tell a mini exchange using casual reported speech ("I was like…", "and she goes…").
4. Give this story a punchline ending: "We drove four hours to the concert, and when we got there…"
5. Retell the plot of a film or show you love in 30 seconds.
6. Correct this, in two ways (casual and neutral): "She said me that she is tired."

#### Week 6: Opinions

Tests [Week 6: Opinions](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md).

1. Give your opinion on remote work, starting with "I feel like…".
2. Agree enthusiastically in **three** different ways.
3. Disagree politely: "Cats are way better than dogs."
4. Give a reason and an example: "Is social media bad for kids?"
5. Hedge: "Is it better to rent or buy a home?"
6. Say this with strong emphasis, then explain why: "I really don't think that's a good idea."

#### Week 7: Explaining

Tests [Week 7: Explaining](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md).

1. Explain how to make your favorite coffee or tea in 4 steps.
2. Explain a decision: "Why did you choose the phone you have?"
3. Compare: living in a city vs the countryside, using "way better for…, but…".
4. Explain a problem in your home and how you'd solve it.
5. Check understanding with **two** different phrases.
6. Explain a term from your job (for example "wireframe" or "user flow") to a ten-year-old.

#### Week 8: Automatic answers

Tests [Week 8: Automatic answers](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/README.md).

1. "Tell me about yourself." (30 seconds)
2. Answer each in under 5 seconds: "Do you like spicy food?" / "Are you a morning person?" / "Seen any good movies lately?"
3. Start three sentences with three different sentence starters, about your week.
4. Say these as natural contractions: "I would have gone" / "It has been ages" / "I will call you" / "You should have told me" / "I would not do that".
5. In 30 seconds, combine a story, an opinion and a question: "your last weekend".
6. "What do you do for fun?" (20 seconds)

### Test 1.2: Long tasks

**Time:** 45 min (about 5 min each) · **Type:** record

Do the tasks in order. Read the task, start within 2 seconds. After all 8, listen back and score each one using its success criteria: **3** = all criteria, **2** = most, **1** = few. Write the score in **column 2** of the score sheet.

**Task 1 (Week 1: [Storytelling](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/README.md)).** Tell a 2-minute story: "a time something went completely wrong while you were traveling".
*Success criteria:* 6+ different connectors · past simple and past continuous used correctly · clear setting, problem, turning point and ending · at least one vivid description.

**Task 2 (Week 2: [Questions](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/README.md)).** Imagine you've just met someone who's a professional chef. Ask them 15 questions in 2 minutes, out loud, imagining short answers.
*Success criteria:* at least 6 different question words · 2+ follow-up questions · 2+ indirect questions with correct word order · 2+ question tags.

**Task 3 (Week 3: [Small talk](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md)).** Play both sides: you meet a neighbor in the elevator. 3 minutes: opener, small talk, a topic change, a polite ending.
*Success criteria:* answers go beyond "fine" · answer, add, ask back at least 3 times · echo questions or back-channels · a smooth topic change · a natural ending.

**Task 4 (Week 4: [Vocabulary](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/README.md)).** Talk for 2 minutes about "a normal week at your job, and one problem you solved recently".
*Success criteria:* 4+ phrasal verbs · 3+ make/do/take/have collocations · 2+ softeners or intensifiers · at least one idiom · clear description of the problem and solution.

**Task 5 (Week 5: [Better stories](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/README.md)).** Retell a film, series episode or a story a friend told you, in 2 minutes, as if it happened to someone you know.
*Success criteria:* a scene-setting opening · 2+ suspense phrases · 3+ casual reported speech lines ("I was like…", "she goes…") · an ending with a punchline or twist.

**Task 6 (Week 6: [Opinions](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md)).** Debate with yourself for 3 minutes: "Should everyone learn to cook?" Give your opinion, then argue with an imaginary friend who disagrees, then find a middle ground.
*Success criteria:* 3+ opinion starters · agree and disagree phrases · reasons and examples · at least one hedge ("I guess it depends…") · stress for emphasis.

**Task 7 (Week 7: [Explaining](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md)).** Explain in 3 minutes how a product you use every day works, why you chose it over alternatives, and compare it with one competitor.
*Success criteria:* clear sequence (first, then…) · a decision with reasons · a comparison with "better for…, but…" · 2+ checking-understanding phrases.

**Task 8 (Week 8: [Automatic answers](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/README.md)).** Ask yourself these 10 questions out loud (or have someone read them) and answer each in under 10 seconds: Where are you from? What do you do? Do you like it? What do you do for fun? Coffee or tea? Seen anything good lately? Where did you go on your last vacation? Are you a city person? What's your favorite food? What are you doing this weekend?
*Success criteria:* every answer starts within 2 seconds · natural short forms ("Yeah, totally", "Not really") · contractions throughout · at least 3 answers include a small extra detail.

### Answer key

Model answers for prompts with a clear right answer. For open prompts, any natural answer that uses the week's skill is correct.

- **W1.1:** any six of: So basically… · The thing is… · Anyway… · And then out of nowhere… · Turns out… · Which was weird, because… · I was like… · And he goes… / she goes… · Long story short… · The funny part is… · At that point… · In the end…
- **W1.2:** "No way!" / "Wait, really?" / "Oh no, that's rough!" / "Ha! I bet the neighbors loved that."
- **W1.3:** "I was walking home when it started pouring." / "I was walking home when I ran into my old boss."
- **W1.6:** "When I arrived, the movie had already started."
- **W2.3:** "Do you know where the station is?"
- **W2.4:** "Can you tell me what time it starts?"
- **W2.5:** "…aren't you?" / "…did she?" / "…haven't you?"
- **W2.6:** "Yeah, I have." / "Not really." or "No, I don't." / "Yeah, I did."
- **W3.6:** "Anyway, I'll let you go. It was great catching up!" / "I should probably get going, but let's grab coffee soon."
- **W4.2:** make a decision · take a break · do a favor · have a chat · do your best · make a mistake
- **W4.3:** "It was pretty good, but maybe a little long?" / "I think it could be even better if it were a bit shorter."
- **W5.6:** casual: "She was like, 'I'm so tired.'" · neutral: "She told me she was tired." (or "She said she was tired.")
- **W7.5:** "Does that make sense?" / "You know what I mean?" / "Am I explaining that OK?"
- **W8.4:** "I would've gone" ("I'd've gone" in fast speech) · "It's been ages" · "I'll call you" · "You should've told me" · "I wouldn't do that"

Tests [Week 9: Talking on the spot](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md).

3. Talk for 30 seconds about a relative you get along with.
4. Talk for 30 seconds about your first job.
5. Would you rather live by the beach or in the mountains? Why?

#### Week 10: Hypotheticals

Tests [Week 10: Hypotheticals](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md).

1. Finish: "If I could live anywhere in the world, I'd…"
2. Finish: "If I hadn't started learning English, I would've…"
3. Say one "I wish…" about now and one about the past.
4. What if animals could talk? (30 seconds)
5. Tell me your plans for next year, using "gonna", "might" and "I'm thinking of…".
6. Correct this: "If I would have more time, I will travel more."

#### Week 11: Never stopping

Tests [Week 11: Never stopping](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md).

1. Describe "umbrella" without saying the word.
2. Describe "dentist" without saying the word.
3. Start a sentence with a mistake and correct it smoothly: "I went there on Tuesday…" (it was Wednesday).
4. "How do airplanes stay in the air?" Answer without stopping, even if you're not sure.
5. Start a story, go off on a side detail, then get back: use "Where was I?" or "Anyway, back to…".

#### Week 12: Role-plays

Tests [Week 12: Role-plays](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/README.md).

3. A friend suggests dinner on Friday. You can't. Suggest another plan and agree on it.
4. You meet someone new at a party: open, then ask two questions.
5. Ask a friend a big favour: help you move apartments.

#### Week 13: Group conversations

Tests [Week 13: Group conversations](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-13-group-conversations/README.md).

1. Imagine two friends are talking about travel. Jump in with something related.

3. Someone interrupts you. Keep your turn politely.
4. Ask someone to repeat in three different natural ways.
5. Build on someone's idea: "We should do a team dinner."

#### Week 14: Humour and banter

Tests [Week 14: Humour and banter](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-14-humour-and-banter/README.md).

1. Your train is canceled, it's raining, and your phone is at 2%. React with sarcasm.
2. Describe a really long line at the airport using exaggeration.
3. Tell a 20-second funny moment, with a pause before the punchline.
4. Gently tease a friend who's always late.
5. React to a friend's joke in three ways.
6. A friend teases you about your accent. Respond lightly and with humour.

#### Week 15: Phone and video calls

Tests [Week 15: Calls](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-15-calls/README.md).

1. On the phone, make a plan with a friend: day, time and place.
2. Leave a 20-second voice message for a colleague asking them to call you back.
3. The connection is bad. Use three phrases.
4. Call a restaurant: book a table for four on Friday at 7:30.
5. Spell your name and your email address using "B as in boy" style.

Tests [Week 16: Speaking in front of people](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md).

1. Stand up. Introduce yourself to a group of 20 in 20 seconds.
2. Start a story for a group with a hook that makes people want to listen.
3. Give a 30-second toast for a colleague who's leaving.
4. Someone in the audience asks: "What's the hardest part of your job?" Repeat or rephrase, then answer.
5. In the middle of a sentence to a group, you forget a word. Recover and continue.
6. End a short talk clearly and invite questions.

### Test 2.2: Long tasks

**Time:** 24 min (about 4 min each), plus 6 min to score · **Type:** record

Score each with its success criteria (**3** = all, **2** = most, **1** = few) in **column 2**.

**Task 1 (Week 9: [Talking on the spot](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-09-talking-on-the-spot/README.md)).** Pick a random card from the [topic cards](../../toolkit/random-topics.md) (without looking) and talk for 2 minutes. Then do a second card.
*Success criteria:* started within 2 seconds · talked for the full 2 minutes both times · no pause longer than 2 seconds · a clear point or example.

**Task 2 (Week 10: [Hypotheticals](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-10-hypotheticals/README.md)).** 2 minutes: "If you could go back and change one decision in your life, what would it be, and how would your life be different now?"
*Success criteria:* correct third conditional ("If I had…, I would've…") · a mixed conditional ("…I'd be… now") · "I wish…" used correctly · natural reduced forms (woulda, coulda, shoulda).

**Task 3 (Week 11: [Never stopping](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md)).** Part A: describe these 8 words without saying them, about 10 seconds each: stapler, elevator, passport, sunscreen, landlord, receipt, jet lag, a ladder. Part B: talk for 90 seconds about "how the internet works" without stopping, even if you don't know.
*Success criteria:* every word described without freezing · paraphrase phrases used ("It's like a…", "the thing you use to…") · smooth self-corrections · "I'm not sure, but…" when needed · a summary at the end.

**Task 4 (Week 12: [Role-plays](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/README.md)).** Play both sides for 3 minutes: you bought a jacket online, it arrived in the wrong size, and the store says it's too late to return it. Then make a plan with a friend to go shopping together instead.
*Success criteria:* a polite complaint · a clear request · a natural reaction to "no" · a smooth switch to the second role-play · plans made with suggestions and agreement.

**Task 5 (Week 15: [Calls](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-15-calls/README.md)).** Close your eyes. Do a 3-minute "phone call" (both sides): call a hotel to change a booking (dates, number of people, a special request), with a bad connection in the middle. Then leave a 30-second voice message for a friend about it.
*Success criteria:* clear numbers and dates · spelling a name · bad-connection phrases · confirming details at the end · a clear, natural voice message.

**Task 6 (Week 16: [Speaking in front of people](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/README.md)).** Stand up. Give a 2-minute toast at a friend's wedding (invent the details), then answer one "audience" question: "How did you two meet?"
*Success criteria:* a strong opening · a short story · pauses for effect · projection (your voice fills the room) · a clear ending ("So please raise your glasses…") · a confident answer to the question.
