**Tonight (2 hours):** Shadowing 15 min · Main skill 20 min (rapid-fire test) · Speaking drill 70 min (long test tasks) · Review 15 min

## Before you start

- Tonight tests **Weeks 1–8**: [Month 1: Foundations](../../month-01-foundations/README.md) and [Month 2: Speed](../../month-02-speed/README.md).
- **Don't reread those lessons first.** The test shows what's automatic. If you prepare, you're testing your memory, not your fluency.
- Have [Day 1 exercises](exercises.md#day-1-exercises) open, your recording app ready, and a pen for the score sheet.
- Record **everything** tonight. You'll score from the recordings, not from how you felt.

## Learning objectives

- Find out which Month 1 and Month 2 skills are now automatic.
- Find the 2–3 skills that still need work, and know exactly which lesson to go back to.
- Use the "keep going" habit under test conditions: no stopping, no restarting.

## 1. Shadowing (15 min): redo Week 1

In [Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/README.md) you started with the **flap T** ("water" → "wadder") and **reductions** (gonna, wanna, kinda). Tonight, go back and hear the difference.

1. **If you kept your Week 1 shadowing recordings:** play one (30 seconds). Then record yourself doing the same sentences now. Listen to both, back to back.
2. **If you didn't keep them:** find a similar clip (search for "casual vlog storytime" or "two friends podcast chat"), and do the lines below with pause-and-repeat, then speak-along.

Practise each line twice: first slowly, then at full speed. Notice how easy they feel now.

1. "Wanna grab a coffee later?" ("wanna")
2. "I'm gonna get a bottle of water." ("gonna", "bottle" = "boddle", "water" = "wadder")
3. "Get it? It's kinda funny." ("ged it", "kinda")
4. "What are you gonna do about it?" ("whaddaya gonna do aboudit")
5. "I gotta admit, it was a little better." ("gotta", "liddle", "bedder")
6. "Put it on the table, I'll get to it later." ("pudit", "geddoit", "layder")
7. "Whaddya wanna eat tonight?"
8. "I've gotta get outta here." ("godda", "outta")
9. "That's a lot of city traffic." ("a lodda", "ciddy")
10. "I was kinda waiting for a better time." ("kinda", "waiding", "bedder")

Ask yourself: are these automatic now? Most learners find that sounds which took 20 minutes of effort in Week 1 now come out without thinking. That's what six months does. See [Shadowing](../../toolkit/shadowing.md).

## 2. Main skill (20 min): Rapid-fire test, Weeks 1–8

### Explanation

Tonight's main skill is a **test**, not a lesson. It has two parts:

1. **The rapid-fire test (now, 20 min):** 48 short prompts, 6 for each week. You answer each one out loud in about 10–20 seconds, straight away. It shows whether the **chunks** from each week come out automatically.
2. **The long tasks (in the speaking drill, 70 min):** one longer task per week, 4–5 minutes each. These show whether you can **use** each skill in real, longer speech.

**How to score.** For each week, give yourself a score:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3** | Automatic. I answered straight away, naturally, with no real thinking. |
| **2** | I got there, but I had to think, or I paused, or it came out a bit unnatural. |
| **1** | I couldn't do it, or I froze, or I had to stop and restart. |

A **1** isn't a failure. It's information: it tells you exactly which week to revisit. Every week in the test has a link to its lesson.

**Test rules:**
- Start each answer **within 2 seconds** ([Week 23 Day 2](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/day-2-being-put-on-the-spot.md)).
- **Never restart.** If you make a mistake, correct it smoothly ("I mean…") and keep going, like [Week 11](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-11-never-stopping/README.md) taught you.
- Don't look at the answer key until you've finished a whole week's prompts.

### Key phrases

These are some of the core chunks from Months 1 and 2. You'll need them tonight. Check: are they automatic?

| Phrase | Use it when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Turns out… | a surprise in a story ([Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/README.md)) | "Turns out, the restaurant was closed." |
| Wait, really? | reacting to a story ([Week 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/README.md)) | "Wait, really? He just left?" |
| Do you know where…? | an indirect question ([Week 2](../../month-01-foundations/week-02-questions/README.md)) | "Do you know where the meeting is?" |
| What've you been up to? | an opener ([Week 3](../../month-01-foundations/week-03-small-talk/README.md)) | "Hey! What've you been up to?" |
| I ended up… | an outcome, with a phrasal verb ([Week 4](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/README.md)) | "I ended up staying till midnight." |
| So this was last summer… | setting the scene ([Week 5](../../month-02-speed/week-05-better-stories/README.md)) | "So this was last summer, right…" |
| I see what you mean, but… | polite disagreement ([Week 6](../../month-02-speed/week-06-opinions/README.md)) | "I see what you mean, but it's really expensive." |
| Does that make sense? | checking understanding ([Week 7](../../month-02-speed/week-07-explaining/README.md)) | "So that's how it works. Does that make sense?" |
| I would've… / It's been… | contractions ([Week 8](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/README.md)) | "I would've called, but it's been crazy." |

### Examples

**Example 1: A score-3 answer** (Week 3 prompt: "Answer, add, ask back: 'Do you like your job?'")

> "Yeah, I do, actually! I'm a designer, and this month we're working on a really fun project for a travel app. What about you? Do you like yours?"

Straight away, natural, all three parts. That's a 3.

**Example 2: A score-2 answer** (Week 2 prompt: "Make it indirect: 'Where is the station?'")

> "Do you know where is… sorry, where the station is?"

The rule is there, but it needed a correction. That's a 2. Totally fine, and worth 5 minutes of review.

**Example 3: Keeping going under test conditions**

> "So basically, last week I was… I mean, two weeks ago, I was at the airport, and out of nowhere my flight got canceled…"

A self-correction without stopping. That's exactly what the test wants to see.

### Common mistakes

❌ Reading the prompt, thinking for 10 seconds, then answering perfectly.
✅ Starting within 2 seconds, even if the answer is imperfect.
*The test measures automatic speech. Thinking time hides the real result.*

❌ Stopping and starting again when you make a mistake.
✅ "…I mean…" and keep going.
*Restarting is the habit you've spent six months breaking.*

❌ Not recording, and scoring from memory.
✅ Record everything, then score while you listen.
*Memory is too kind (or too harsh). Recordings are honest.*

❌ Scoring yourself 1 because one answer was imperfect.
✅ Score the **week**, based on all its prompts.
*One bad answer doesn't mean the skill is gone.*

### Practise

Do the test now ([Day 1 exercises](exercises.md#day-1-exercises)):

1. **Test 1.1: Rapid-fire, Weeks 1–8** (18 min): 48 prompts, recorded.
2. **Score sheet, column 1** (2 min): give each week a quick first score.

## 3. Speaking drill (70 min): Long test tasks

Use **Test 1.2: Long tasks** in [Day 1 exercises](exercises.md#day-1-exercises). There are 8 tasks, one for each week, each about 5 minutes. Record every task.

1. **Tasks 1–8 (45 min):** do them in order. Start each one within 2 seconds of reading it. Don't stop between tasks for more than 30 seconds.
2. **Listen and score (20 min):** listen to each recording. For each task, check the success criteria in the exercise. Write a score (1–3) in **column 2** of the score sheet (Test 1.3). Then write a **final score** for each week, combining both columns.
3. **Redo your weakest task (5 min):** choose the task with the lowest score and do it once more, right now. Usually the second attempt is much better. That shows the skill is there, it just needs a warm-up.

## 4. Review (15 min)

1. **Complete the score sheet (5 min).** Add up your Month 1 and Month 2 scores. For every week that scored **1**, write the link to that week's README in your notes for Day 6.
2. **Spaced review out loud (7 min):** last night's phrases (Week 23 Day 6), last week's (Week 23: interview, negotiation, conflict, strangers) and one page from last month (Week 19). See [phrase log](../../toolkit/phrase-log.md).
3. **Nightly log (3 min):** write your Month 1 and Month 2 totals in the [nightly log](../../toolkit/nightly-log.md), plus one line: "The Month 1–2 skill that's most automatic now is… The one that still needs work is…".
