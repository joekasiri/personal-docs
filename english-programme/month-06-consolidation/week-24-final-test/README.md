[Programme home](../../README.md) › [Month 6: Consolidation](../README.md) · [← Week 23](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/README.md) · [Back to programme home →](../../README.md)

## This week's goal

This is it: the last week of six months. There's no new material. This week **tests** everything you've practised and **measures** how far you've come. Days 1–3 are real tests: fast, unprepared prompts and tasks that sample every week of the programme, Months 1 to 6, each with a link back to the week it tests. Day 4 is a 30-minute unscripted conversation with Claude, your longest ever. Day 5 is the moment you've been waiting for: you redo your Week 1 Day 1 baseline and compare your numbers. Day 6 brings it all together in the final check and the plan for your next six months.

Remember the goal you started with: **talk without thinking**. This week you'll find out where that's already true, and where you still have to think. Both answers are useful.

## How this builds on last week

[Week 23](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/README.md) was the hardest week of the programme: interviews, being put on the spot, negotiating, conflict and strangers. If you got through that, you're ready. This week the pressure is different: it's not about a new skill, it's about **range**. The tests jump from storytelling (Week 1) to hypotheticals (Week 10) to negotiating (Week 23) in a few minutes, just like real conversation does.

The shadowing this week also goes full circle: every night you **redo your Week 1 clips** (or clips like them), and on Day 5 you listen to the difference.

## Before you start

- Finish the [Week 23 weekly check](../week-23-fluency-under-stress/day-6-weekly-check.md) and send your report to Claude.
- **Put everything in one folder** (on your phone or computer) before Day 1:
  - your **Week 1 Day 1 baseline** recording ([Week 1 Day 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-01-storytelling/day-1-storytelling-connectors.md))
  - your Week 1 shadowing recordings, if you kept them
  - your numbers from every monthly check: [Month 1](../../month-01-foundations/week-04-vocabulary/day-6-weekly-check.md), [Month 2](../../month-02-speed/week-08-automatic-answers/day-6-weekly-check.md), [Month 3](../../month-03-spontaneity/week-12-role-plays/day-6-weekly-check.md), [Month 4](../../month-04-real-life-pressure/week-16-speaking-in-front-of-people/day-6-weekly-check.md), [Month 5](../../month-05-work-and-complex-topics/week-20-thinking-on-your-feet/day-6-weekly-check.md)

- **Don't prepare for the tests.** Don't reread old lessons this week. The tests are supposed to show what's automatic, not what you can remember with effort.
- Book your **3 live conversations** (Days 2, 5 and 6). For Day 6, if possible, choose someone who has heard your English before: a language partner from earlier months, a colleague, a tutor. They can tell you what's changed. See [Live conversations](../../toolkit/live-conversations.md).
- Keep your stubborn ten from last week. This week, every phrase you use naturally in a test counts as a tick.

## Day 7: light day

**30 minutes only.** Go back to the very first kind of podcast you listened to in Week 1: a casual American podcast with two people chatting. Search for "two friends podcast chat" or "casual conversation podcast", and choose an episode you've never heard. Listen while you walk.

Your one task: **notice how much you understand now**, and how much of it you could say yourself. Each time you hear a phrase you've practised (a connector, a time-buyer, a reaction, a hedge), smile. You'll probably hear dozens. Six months ago, most of them were passive vocabulary. Now they're yours.

Then do something nice for yourself. You've practised two hours a night, six nights a week, for six months. That's around 290 hours of speaking practice. Rest.
